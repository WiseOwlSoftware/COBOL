# PB5811 - at --std 85 an indexed READ of a record whose OCCURS DEPENDING ON count is past its maximum sets status 34 and a permanent error, a 2023 rule 1985 does not have

**What is wrong.** An indexed READ of a record whose OCCURS DEPENDING ON count, once the record lands, would make the record longer than the file's maximum sets I-O status 34 at `--std 85`, runs the USE procedure, and leaves a permanent error that fails the next valid READ (`A001: 34`). 1985's indexed I-O status list has no 34 at all (its permanent errors are 30, 35, 37, 38 and 39), and a permanent error is one "that precluded further processing of the file". `§1985:IX.4.5.4 GR18` governs record length: an over-long record is truncated and even then "the READ statement is successful"; a record within the file's sizes is plain success. The record read is 14 characters in a 7..15 file, so 1985 requires 00 and no USE procedure; the ODO count 9 > 5 only makes later references to the table undefined (`§1985:VI.5.8.4 GR2b`). The compiler sets 34 (the producer of `§2023:9.1.13.6 GR4b`, with no edition switch), runs the USE procedure, and the permanent-error latch then fails the next valid READ. 2023 prescribes the 34; 1985 requires 00 for both READs. Cause (a), wrong at 85 only. Harm: wrong_answer. The checker's confidence: high. I ran `odord.cob` at `--std 85` myself: it printed `USE-RDF 34`, `R2 34 CNT 9`, `USE-RDF 34`, `R1 34 CNT 9 T AB`. Not on any of the seven files changed between 5e49d46678 and d7d3f99539. Pointer only, not checked here: the reader says 2002 and 2014 lack the rule too.
