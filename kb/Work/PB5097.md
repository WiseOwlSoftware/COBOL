# PB5097 - a queued group is not a stalled group

**What happened.** At 21:45 on 2026-11-09 the loop mailed two STALLs: `STALL w20261973g (PB5081, PB5091): no progress for 29
min on running (29 min since it began)` and the same for w20261973h (PB5071). Wave 20261973 carries eight groups, A to H
(`scratch/wf-args-w20261973.json`), at `concurrency` 6. Groups A to F each had a scratch directory; G and H had none,
because the rolling wave had not yet pulled them from its queue. Neither group had begun, so neither could have stalled.
The operator checked this by hand and closed both mails as false.

**Mechanism.** `progress.py` `progress` (scripts/orchestrator/progress.py#progress) treats every dispatch-ledger group
with no report as `running` and dates it from the ledger's `at`, which `dispatch_ledger.record_dispatch`
(scripts/orchestrator/dispatch_ledger.py#record_dispatch) stamps once for the whole wave, when the wave is planned or
rendered, not when a worker starts the group. `group_activity` finds nothing for a group that has not started, so its idle
time equals its age, and past `STALL_IDLE_MIN` it is mailed as a STALL. The same count inflates the PROGRESS line's
`groups running` (9 at 21:45, with at most 6 workers). This is the third false-STALL mechanism after PB4918 (the review
clock read a DONE report's mtime) and PB4944 (a long agent was read as a stopped one).

**Expected.** A group the rolling wave has not started is reported as `queued`. It is neither counted as running nor
mailed as a STALL, and its clock starts when a worker picks it up. That needs a start signal the loop can read without
git: for example, `wf_rolling_wave.js` writing a marker in the coordination directory when it dispatches a group's
implementer, which `group_activity` and `progress` then read. Add a self-test case to `progress.py`: a wave of more groups
than its concurrency, with the unstarted groups reported as queued.

## Two more silences the same night (2026-11-09 22:05-23:45)

The operator checked every STALL of wave 20261973 by hand. All ten mails were false, and two more mechanisms beyond the
queued group account for them:

1. **A gate's test phase writes nothing to its redirected log.** Groups A (22:05) and F (22:15) were 20 or more minutes
   into the whole-population test phase. 431 and 274 files of their worktrees changed in the 3 minutes before the check, but
   the gate log (`scratch/w<wave><letter>/gate*.log`) stays silent from "the whole population" until the verdict, and
   `group_activity` reads only the coordination directory.
2. **A gate waiting for a slot writes only when its place in line moves.** Groups B and E (22:55), and C (23:35), were in the
   gate-slot queue (`E:\COBOL\.git\cobol-gate-slots`, cap 3). C's gate2 log reads `took slot 2 of 3 (ticket 5112, waited
   1601.2 s)`: 26.7 minutes in the queue with no line written after `next in line`. `group_activity`'s docstring holds that
   "a waiting gate writes its log, so a slot wait is not silence", which is true only while the queue moves.

A third case, a DONE report written while its implementer still gates (group A at 23:25), is PB4875. The same 1601 s
wait is also a throughput measurement: six implementers and the Mythos lane share three gate slots.

**Expected, extended.** The gate leaves a heartbeat in the coordination directory: a line in its log, or a touched file,
at a fixed interval while it runs its tests or waits for a slot. `group_activity` reads that file as an act. The self-test
adds a gate in its test phase and a gate in the slot queue, each past `STALL_IDLE_MIN` with a fresh heartbeat, and
neither is mailed as a STALL.
