# PB2017 — OPEN: operator spacing (§8.7.1) is never checked; the separator diagnostic misleads

Found by the adjudication of battery #88's differential flip `run_misc:156` (DEVLOG 1883), reproduced by the orchestrator
on tree `6e4b5deb0` with `--std 2023`. Citation checked: `cite.py --check 8.7.1 "shall be preceded by a space and followed
by a space"` is OK.

1. **Missed diagnostic (under-rejection).** §8.7.1: the arithmetic operators are COBOL characters "that shall be preceded by
   a space and followed by a space". `COMPUTE X=1*2` compiles with no diagnostic at `--std 2023`. The closing note of
   PB1394, PB1441 and PB1728 (all landed) names §8.7.1 as a future case for `SeparatorRule.Violations`, and no open note
   carries it.
2. **Misleading advice.** With `DECIMAL-POINT IS COMMA`, `COMPUTE X = 1 *,1` is rejected by COBOLNET2631 with "the comma ...
   is not a separator ... write a space after it". Under that clause the comma is the literal's decimal point
   (§12.3.7.4 14) a); §8.3.3.3.2 3) allows it as the leftmost character), so the violated rule is §8.7.1 at the `*`.
   Following the advice yields `COMPUTE X = 1 *, 1`, which is conforming, compiles clean, and computes 1 times 1 instead of
   the intended 0,10. `SeparatorRule.IsDecimalComma` (`src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Parsing/SeparatorRule.cs`, about lines
   121-127) treats a comma before a digit as a decimal point only after a space, `+`, `-`, `(` or a digit, so a comma after
   `*`, `/` or `**` takes the wrong branch.

One mechanism covers both: implement §8.7.1 in `SeparatorRule.Violations` and have it report the `*,1` shape under that rule.
Controls that already behave: `1 * ,1`, `1 + ,1`, `1,5 * 2` and `(1 + ,5) * 2`. The fix needs its goldens (positive and
negative, per edition: the operator-spacing rule is the same in every edition) and a sibling sweep of the other operators
(`+ - * / **`, and the unary signs).
