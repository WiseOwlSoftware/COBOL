# PB2017 — OPEN: operator spacing (§8.7.1) is never checked; the separator diagnostic misleads

Found by the adjudication of battery #88's differential flip `run_misc:156` (DEVLOG 1883), reproduced by the orchestrator
on tree `6e4b5deb0` with `--std 2023`. Citation checked: `cite.py --check 8.7.1 "shall be preceded by a space and followed
by a space"` is OK.

1. **Missed diagnostic (under-rejection).** §8.7.1: the arithmetic operators are COBOL characters "that shall be preceded by
   a space and followed by a space". `COMPUTE X=1*2` compiles with no diagnostic at `--std 2023`. The closing note of
   PB1394, PB1441 and PB1728 (all landed) names §8.7.1 as a future case for `SeparatorRule.Violations`, and no open note
   carries it.
2. **Misleading advice.** With `DECIMAL-POINT IS COMMA`, `COMPUTE X = 1 *,1` is rejected by COBOLNET2631 with "the comma ...
   is not a separator ... write a space after it". Under that clause the comma is the literal's decimal point
   (§12.3.7.4 14) a); §8.3.3.3.2 3) allows it as the leftmost character), so the violated rule is §8.7.1 at the `*`.
   Following the advice yields `COMPUTE X = 1 *, 1`, which is conforming, compiles clean, and computes 1 times 1 instead of
   the intended 0,10. `SeparatorRule.IsDecimalComma` (`src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Parsing/SeparatorRule.cs`, about lines
   121-127) treats a comma before a digit as a decimal point only after a space, `+`, `-`, `(` or a digit, so a comma after
   `*`, `/` or `**` takes the wrong branch.

One mechanism covers both: implement §8.7.1 in `SeparatorRule.Violations` and have it report the `*,1` shape under that rule.
Controls that already behave: `1 * ,1`, `1 + ,1`, `1,5 * 2` and `(1 + ,5) * 2`. The fix needs its goldens (positive and
negative, per edition: the operator-spacing rule is the same in every edition) and a sibling sweep of the other operators
(`+ - * / **`, and the unary signs).

## Landed (wave 1071 group G)

**Re-probed first** on the wave tree (`77f10dfb2`, its own `cobol.exe`, `--std 2023`): `COMPUTE X=1*2.` compiled with no
diagnostic, and under DECIMAL-POINT IS COMMA `COMPUTE X = 1 *,1.` drew COBOLNET2631 ("write a space after it"). Both
claims reproduced; the controls `1 * ,1`, `1 + ,1`, `1,5 * 2` and `(1 + ,5) * 2` behaved as the note says.

**One mechanism, two defects.** `cite.py --check 8.7.1 "preceded by a space and followed by a space except that no space
is required between a left parenthesis and a unary operator or between a unary operator and a left parenthesis"` OK;
`8.3.5 "that may be used anywhere the separator space is used"` OK; `8.3.3.3.2 "may appear anywhere within the literal
except as the rightmost character"` OK (a literal may begin with the decimal point).
1. **§8.7.1 is now asked, as COBOLNET2993** (the same descriptor as §8.7.3 and §8.7.4; its description and IsoSection
   widen to the three clauses, so no new code was spent). It cannot be decided from the token run like its siblings:
   whether a `+`/`-` is binary, unary or no operator at all (a literal's own sign, `LINE PLUS`) is grammar. So
   `SeparatorRule.ArithmeticOperatorViolations(text, tree)` reads the parse tree (`addOp` under `additiveExpression`,
   `mulOp`, `POWER` binary; `addOp` under `unaryExpression` unary unless `ArithmeticFormationRules.SignIsPartOfLiteral`)
   and judges the characters through the one `OperatorSpacingMessage` verdict (unary: a left parenthesis may stand where
   the space does; a comma or semicolon followed by a space counts as the space, §8.3.5 2)). It runs once per unit tree
   in `Frontend.LexAndParse` and once per directive fragment (`DirectiveDiag.ScreenLiterals` became `ScreenFragment`:
   `>>DEFINE X AS 2*3` is held to the rule too).
2. **The misleading advice.** `IsDecimalComma` now treats a comma that begins a literal after `*`, `/`, `=`, `<` or `>`
   (the operator characters, beside the existing `+ - (`) as the decimal point, so 2631 no longer advises the
   meaning-changing `1 *, 1`; the operator's missing space is the one report.

**Sibling sweep.** The other arm is the directive lane (fixed, above); the other operators are the same arm
(`+ - * / **` and the unary signs, goldens below). The relational symbols `= < >` state no spacing rule in §8.7.5, so
they are not asked. A corpus-wide compile of every enabled conformance program, the NIST programs and the 25 others found
three genuine §8.7.1 violations in test sources, corrected to the conforming spelling: `2023/pb2966_...:139` (`-W2`),
`2023/pb1641_...:70` (`(-FUNCTION SQRT(16))`) and NIST `IF117A.cob:783` (`(A * G)/ 1000`, one padding space moved; the
same precedent as the PB528 edits to IX109A/IX110A). D-ARGEXPR's `USING N -1` is still the expression `N - 1` and is now
refused for its missing space (docs/CONFORMANCE.md updated). The landing's Linux whole-population gate found two more
in COBOL embedded in xUnit sources, which the corpus compile does not reach: `WideRefModDiagnosticTests` E4 `X(-A:1)` and
K5 `X(1:-(N))` (a unary operator after the reference modifier's colon: a separator, §8.3.5 7), but not the space §8.7.1
requires). The finisher respelled them `X(- A:1)` and `X(1: -(N))` and pinned the colon case in
`OperatorSpacingDiagnosticTests.UnaryOperatorInAReferenceModifier_IsOneDiagnostic`.

**Goldens.** Positive `85/pb2017_arithmetic_operator_spacing` (every conforming spelling, expected values computed from
§8.8.1.2 by hand before the run). Negatives `pb2017-binary-operator-no-spaces`, `-no-space-after`,
`pb2017-unary-operator-no-space-after`, `-operator-in-subscript`, `-operator-touches-comma-decimal-point`, each at
85/2002/2014/2023 (the clause carries no edition qualifier). Unit `SeparatorRuleTests` (tree rule and the comma);
Conformance `OperatorSpacingDiagnosticTests` (one diagnostic, no 2631, the conforming spellings, the directive lane).
