# PB5952 - a COPY or REPLACE statement's words are not coloured in the HTML listing

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Preprocessor/CopyProcessor.cs` (`ExpandCopiesOneLevel`, `ApplyReplaceStatements`: where each
statement is found), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Preprocessor/TextStageTrace.cs` (`BeginInclusion`: records the COPY statement's first
and last LINE only), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Listing/SyntaxSpanBuilder.cs` (`Mark`).

**Found by** PB5949 (2026-11-19), which colours the source from the compiler's own reading of it (DESIGN-listing.md section 7.2): the
reference format's areas and discarded comments, and the lexer's tokens as the parse read them at the edition in force. A COPY
statement and a REPLACE statement are compiler-directing statements the text stages process and REMOVE (§2023:7.2.3.1: "The COPY statement incorporates library text"; §2023:7.2.4.1: "The REPLACE statement modifies text"): the
lexer never reads their words, and the trace records only the lines a COPY statement stands on (`TextInclusion.CopyFirst`,
`CopyLast`), no column, and nothing of a REPLACE statement's place. So `COPY PAYREC REPLACING ==A== BY ==B==.` prints uncoloured.
Classing it from its text would be the pattern grammar PB5949 decided against.

**Shape of the fix:** the text stages record each compiler-directing statement's words as they read them (the COPY statement's
keyword, text-name, library-name, REPLACING operands and pseudo-text; the REPLACE statement's), each with its physical point
(`MappedText.PointAt` at the word's offset), into the trace only when it records (`TextStageTrace.Records`); `SyntaxSpanBuilder` places
them like the tokens, as reserved words, user-defined words (text-names, library-names), literals and pseudo-text. A drift test: every
listed line that holds a COPY or REPLACE statement has spans over its words.
