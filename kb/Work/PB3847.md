# PB3847 — cite.py labels a clause's closing paragraph as its last numbered rule

**Sites:** `scripts/spec/cite.py#rule_path`, `scripts/spec/cite.py#_label`, `scripts/spec/cite.py#self_test`

## The defect

CLAUDE.md rule 1 makes `cite.py --check` the authority for every citation, so the label it prints is copied into code
comments, goldens, notes and the DEVLOG. For a paragraph that closes a clause (printed at the left margin after the
clause's last numbered rule, and belonging to the clause, not to that rule), `rule_path` walks back to the nearest
column-0 label and reports it:

```
$ python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 14.5.3.3 "Any phrase encountered is the next phrase of the most-recently preceding unterminated statement with which that phrase may be syntactically associated"
OK  §14.5.3.3 4)  (Implicit scope termination)
```

The rendered page (PDF page 566, read for PB3830) prints this sentence unindented after list item 4) b): it is the
closing paragraph of §14.5.3.3, and PB3830 had to cite it as "§14.5.3.3, not §14.5.3.3 4)" by hand, against the tool.
PB308 already taught `rule_path` that an unlettered paragraph at a sub-item's indentation is the PARENT rule's trailing
paragraph; the same mistake one level up (a column-0 paragraph after the last rule) is not handled.

## Acceptance

- The checker tells a rule's own continuation paragraph from the clause's closing paragraph by the transcription's
  structure (re-derive it from `specs/ISO_COBOL.md` and the rendered page, never from this one case), and the
  §14.5.3.3 sentence above reports `§14.5.3.3`.
- Every other clause whose closing paragraph follows a numbered list is swept: list each one the new reading changes,
  and confirm on its rendered page that the new label is right (a label that changes wrongly is a regression).
- A `self_test` arm pins the closing-paragraph case beside PB308's trailing-paragraph case, seen RED before the fix.
- Fix the root cause, never simply making the diagnostic pass; stay consistent with ourselves (PB308's rule and this
  one are one reading of the printed structure).
