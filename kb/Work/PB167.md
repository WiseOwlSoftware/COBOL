# PB167

§8.8.1.5.4 r2e lets the implementor choose the equivalent expression for a non-integer exponent but
constrains the development: "Operands used in the development of that value shall be in SDIDI form."
CobolDec.Pow's non-integer arm develops through IEEE binary64 (`Math.Pow` over `ToDouble`), delivering
~17 correct digits where a decimal128 development carries 34 — `FUNCTION EXP10(0.5)` parts ways with the
SDIDI value at the 17th significant digit (pinned by exp_standard_decimal_eae P17). PB145 (landed
2026-08-29) BOUNDED the approximation: a base or result outside binary64's range now routes through the
`PowByLogs` decomposition (no more silent zeros or spurious overflows on SDIDI-range operands), the past-
loop-bound integer escape is magnitude-aware, and CONFORMANCE.md documents the binary64 development as the
current determination. What remains is the r2e shall itself.

**The question (one decision):** fund a 34-digit SDIDI-carried transcendental core (exp/ln over CobolDec,
correctly rounded — a real numerics work item: argument reduction, a guard-digit series or table-driven
core, proofs at the tie cases), or ratify the documented binary64 development as this implementation's r2e
latitude permanently? ⛔ ~~GnuCOBOL computes the same construct through C `pow` (binary64) — the survey
favors ratifying~~ — **THAT SENTENCE IS FALSE AND IS STRUCK, NOT DELETED** (2026-08-31; see the ANSWERED
section below): the survey of 2026-08-30 found GnuCOBOL develops this through 2048-bit GMP as
e^(z·log x), with an `mpf_sqrt` shortcut at exponent ½ — not C `pow`. It is kept struck because it is the
premise the question was posed on, and a reader needs to see that the premise, not the reasoning, was
what failed. The shall's plain text favors the decimal core, and so does the survey once corrected.
Rows GR-8.8.1.5.4-2/-3 stayed PARTIAL until decided.

## ANSWERED — owner decision D-C, 2026-08-30; implemented 2026-08-31 (the arithmetic wave)

**The decision: FUND the SDIDI development. binary64 is NOT ratified.**

⛔ **The question above rested on a FALSE premise and it is corrected here rather than left standing.** It says
"GnuCOBOL computes the same construct through C `pow` (binary64) — the survey favors ratifying". The survey
(2026-08-30) found otherwise: GnuCOBOL develops this through 2048-bit GMP as e^(z·log x), with an `mpf_sqrt`
shortcut at exponent ½ — not C `pow`. The same false sentence stood in CONFORMANCE.md §7 and has been replaced
there too.

### What landed

ONE development, `exp(operand-2 × ln operand-1)`, on `CobolDec` Add/Sub/Mul/Div only — serving BOTH r2e sites,
which used to be two different approximations of the same value (`Math.Pow` on the non-integer arm, a
`Math.Log10` decomposition in `PowByLogs` for the past-loop-bound integer escape; both now deleted).

- **ln**: split the decimal exponent off the significand (exact — a decimal carrier's decimal exponent needs no
  arithmetic), fold the mantissa into [√⅒, √10) by comparing m² against 10, then three exact `Sqrt` steps and
  the odd-only atanh series on z = (u−1)/(u+1), |z| ≤ 0.072. `ln 10` is not a transcribed constant: it is the
  same series over the same reduction, so there is nothing to mistype and nothing to re-derive.
- **exp**: reduce by n·ln 10 so the 10^n factor is an EXACT decimal exponent shift — the entire decimal128
  exponent range is reached without a single rounding — then the Taylor series on the |r| ≤ ½·ln 10 remainder.
- **|operand-2| = ½ → `FUNCTION SQRT(operand-1)`**, and r3's `1 / (operand-1 ** ½)` at −½. SQRT is the one §15
  function whose standard-decimal value the standard fixes EXACTLY (§15.84.4 r2), so `b ** 0.5` and
  `FUNCTION SQRT(b)` now agree BY CONSTRUCTION. They disagreed before, from the 17th significant digit.

### Wording — what is claimed, and what is not

**An r2e-conforming SDIDI development. NEVER "34 correct digits."** §8.8.1.5.2 rounds every operation to 34
digits, so a development of N operations carries the accumulated rounding of all N.

⚠ **This paragraph originally read "measured ≈31 correct significant digits … degrading as
|operand-2 × ln operand-1| grows and for a base very near 1 — … inherent to the equivalent expression rather
than to this coding of it", and it was wrong in three ways** (kb/Work PB269, found by this wave's own review
fleet and fixed in the same change set): the digits were understated, one of the two named drivers was NOT
inherent, and "for a base very near 1" hid a collapse to ZERO correct digits rather than a gentle degradation.
The near-unit loss was manufactured in `LnReduced`'s three-square-root reduction — `Sub(u, One)` cancelled away
the whole of b−1 — and the series over δ = m − 1 removes it.

**The determination, measured** (an independent 34-digit emulation of `CobolDec.cs`, validated against this
note's own pinned goldens, against 120-digit truth):

* **≈33 correct significant digits** for a moderate exponent — 33.00–33.58 over the six
  `CobolDecPowDevelopmentTests` rows, 30.87 the floor over a 400-case randomized in-range sweep (the binary64
  predecessor reached ≈17);
* degrading as **≈ 33 − log₁₀|operand-2 × ln operand-1|** once that product passes 1 — 32.15 digits at 7.1,
  31.12 at 69, 30.11 at 693, 28.78 at 13 863. THIS driver is inherent to the equivalent expression: an exp
  development turns ABSOLUTE error in its argument into RELATIVE error in its result.
* The near-unit-base driver is **gone**, not reduced: 1.00001 ** 1000000 went 26.6 → 32.4 correct digits,
  (1 + 10⁻³³) ** 10³⁰ went 3.0 → 33.2 (it had answered a flat 1), a 31-digit near-unit base ** 10¹⁴ went
  18.5 → 29.1.

CONFORMANCE.md §7, the `CobolDec.cs` r2e banner and the GR-8.8.1.5.4-2 inventory note publish these same terms.

### Cost

⚠ **This paragraph said "three BigInteger square roots per non-integer exponentiation" and the general arm
performed SIX** — three for ln 10 and three for ln b, `Ln` calling `LnReduced` again on the reduced mantissa,
so the two are separate developments and not one shared reduction (kb/Work PB268). ln 10 is a pure function of
the INTERMEDIATE ROUNDING mode, of which §11.9.11 offers four, so it was a constant being re-derived
unboundedly; it is now developed once per mode.

**Corrected:** ~140–210 decimal128 operations and THREE BigInteger square roots per non-integer
exponentiation on the first call in a mode, three and none thereafter — and a NEAR-UNIT base costs no square
root at all, since `LnReduced`'s δ arm has none (PB269). Measured 73 ms for a program performing ten of them
including process start, before the caching. `**` with a non-integer exponent is rare enough that clarity wins,
on the same reasoning the file already records for its division loop.

⛔ **PROHIBITED is deliberately NOT cached.** ln 10 has no exact SDIDI form, so §11.9.11.2 r3d requires the
development to RAISE EC-SIZE-TRUNCATION every time it is asked for; the raise IS the observable, and a cached
value would answer where the rule requires an exception.

### One deliberate behaviour change, and the standard asks for it

Under `INTERMEDIATE ROUNDING IS PROHIBITED` a NON-INTEGER power now raises EC-SIZE-TRUNCATION where the
binary64 core did not. §11.9.11.2 rule 3 d (`cite.py --check` PASSED): *"If the PROHIBITED phrase is specified
and an intermediate value cannot be represented exactly in SDIDI form, the EC-SIZE-TRUNCATION exception
condition is set to exist and the results of the operation are undefined."* Every step of the r2e development
is an inexact 34-digit operation over an irrational result, so the prohibition applies — and `FUNCTION SQRT`
already behaved this way for an inexact root, so the two are now consistent rather than divided. The old core
escaped the rule by accident: it computed in IEEE double and crossed into the SDIDI through ONE exact
`FromDouble`, so nothing inexact ever happened *in SDIDI form* for the prohibition to see. An INTEGER power
still computes (r2a–r2d is exact multiplication). Corpus census before landing: **no existing case combines
PROHIBITED with a non-integer exponent** — the three PROHIBITED cases that use `**` at all
(`2014/pb129_remainder_no_phrase`, `2014/pb129_store_discipline`, `2023/pb74_sdidi_store_size_error`) all use
integer exponents. Pinned going forward by `2023/pb167_sdidi_exponentiation_prohibited`.
