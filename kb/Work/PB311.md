# PB311 — a green test pinning the wrong rule

⚠ **A row currently recorded state=OK / verdict=CONFORMS**, `test-ref=conformance:2023/pb65_date_windowing`.

## The two wordings, and they differ materially

* `cite.py --check 15.23.3 "The sum of the year at the time of execution and the value of argument-2 shall be less
  than 10000 and greater than 1699."` → **OK §15.23.3 6)** — DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD keys on the **execution year**,
  *even when argument-3 is written*.
* `cite.py --check 15.25.3 "The sum of the values of argument-2 and argument-3 shall be less than 10000 and
  greater than 1699."` → **OK §15.25.3 6)** — DAY-TO-YYYYDDD keys on **argument-3**.

## What the golden actually contains

The only r6-shaped line in `tests/conformance/2023/pb65_date_windowing.cob` is

```cobol
COMPUTE D7 = FUNCTION DAY-TO-YYYYDDD(85365, 9000, 1995)     *> R6VIOL=
```

argument-2 + argument-3 = 10995 — **§15.25.3 r6's sum, not §15.23.3 r6's**. Its DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD lines are
`(851003,10,1900)` and `(851003,10,0)`, neither of which tests r6. Nothing in that golden violates or even reaches
§15.23.3 r6's own condition.

## The consequence

An implementation that applied the DAY rule's formula to `DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD` — using argument-3 in place of the
execution year for the r6 window check — would **pass this golden and violate §15.23.3 r6**, because with an
explicit argument-3 the two sums are different numbers. §15.23.3 r6 has **no witness anywhere in the corpus**.

## The witness it needs

argument-2 chosen against the **execution** year with argument-3 written explicitly and far from it, so the DATE
sum and the DAY sum disagree. `tests/conformance/2002/l1_windowing_omitted_arg3` (landed by the golden lane) already
stages the execution year in `CY` and is the natural home for it.

Found adjacent to the golden lane's ten date/integer rows; `AR-15.23.3-6` is not one of them, and a state=OK row is
not a golden-writing pass's to reopen — hence a register note.
