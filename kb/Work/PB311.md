# PB311 — a green test pinning the wrong rule

⚠ **A row currently recorded state=OK / verdict=CONFORMS**, `test-ref=conformance:2023/pb65_date_windowing`.

## The two wordings, and they differ materially

* `cite.py --check 15.23.3 "The sum of the year at the time of execution and the value of argument-2 shall be less
  than 10000 and greater than 1699."` → **OK §15.23.3 6)** — DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD keys on the **execution year**,
  *even when argument-3 is written*.
* `cite.py --check 15.25.3 "The sum of the values of argument-2 and argument-3 shall be less than 10000 and
  greater than 1699."` → **OK §15.25.3 6)** — DAY-TO-YYYYDDD keys on **argument-3**.

## What the golden actually contains

The only r6-shaped line in `tests/conformance/2023/pb65_date_windowing.cob` is

```cobol
COMPUTE D7 = FUNCTION DAY-TO-YYYYDDD(85365, 9000, 1995)     *> R6VIOL=
```

argument-2 + argument-3 = 10995 — **§15.25.3 r6's sum, not §15.23.3 r6's**. Its DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD lines are
`(851003,10,1900)` and `(851003,10,0)`, neither of which tests r6. Nothing in that golden violates or even reaches
§15.23.3 r6's own condition.

## The consequence

An implementation that applied the DAY rule's formula to `DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD` — using argument-3 in place of the
execution year for the r6 window check — would **pass this golden and violate §15.23.3 r6**, because with an
explicit argument-3 the two sums are different numbers. §15.23.3 r6 has **no witness anywhere in the corpus**.

## The witness it needs

argument-2 chosen against the **execution** year with argument-3 written explicitly and far from it, so the DATE
sum and the DAY sum disagree. `tests/conformance/2002/l1_windowing_omitted_arg3` (landed by the golden lane) already
stages the execution year in `CY` and is the natural home for it.

Found adjacent to the golden lane's ten date/integer rows; `AR-15.23.3-6` is not one of them, and a state=OK row is
not a golden-writing pass's to reopen — hence a register note.

**Code site (located 2026-09-27).** `tests/conformance/2023/pb65_date_windowing.cob`; also `src/Cobol.Net.Runtime/Intrinsics/CobolDate.cs#DateToYyyymmdd`, `tests/conformance/2023/pb65_date_windowing.out` (tests). the row's test-ref golden carries only the DAY-TO-YYYYDDD (§15.25.3 r6 argument-3) sum line, no DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD execution-year r6 case; the implementation delegates DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD to YEAR-TO-YYYY's argument-3 window check

## Landed (wave 1033 E)

**Re-probe: the code was right; the witness was wrong.** `DateToYyyymmddAt` delegates to `YearToYyyyAt`, so the DATE
r6 sum is argument-2 + argument-3. The premise above (a literal clock-year reading of §15.23.3 r6) does not survive
§15.23.1, which DEFINES the year at the time of execution as argument-3 (`cite.py --check 15.23.1 "Argument-3
specifies the year at the time of execution."` is OK). With argument-3 omitted, r5 supplies the real year, so the
two readings coincide; with it written, the written value IS the function's year. The row's PB65 notes had already
re-derived this reading, and the earlier text of this note ("would violate §15.23.3 r6") is superseded by it.

**Fix = the missing witness, on both arms.** `tests/conformance/2002/pb311_date_day_windowing_r6_bounds` (manifest
enabled) pins both bounds of DATE-TO-YYYYMMDD and DAY-TO-YYYYDDD with an explicit argument-3 far from any clock year:
(-400,2100) legal at 1700, (-401,2100) violation, (8299,1700) legal at 9999, (8300,1700) violation, and (7000,3000)
a violation where a clock-keyed sum would be legal, with EC-ARGUMENT-FUNCTION checking ON. Unit mirrors run under a
pinned 2026 clock (`CobolDateWindowingTests.DateToYyyymmdd_R6_*`, `DayToYyyyddd_R6_*`), so a clock-keyed
implementation fails them. Rows `AR-15.23.3-6` and its sibling `AR-15.25.3-6` gained these witnesses (batch
`w1033e-pb311-date-day-r6-witness`). The `DateToYyyymmdd` doc comment now states the derivation instead of
"resolved to the delegated form".
