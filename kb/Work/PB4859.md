# PB4859 - MEDIAN with a binary64 middle: beside an exact middle it takes the exact mean of the float's SDIDI lift on DISPLAY and COMPUTE, and with both middles binary64 on DISPLAY it keeps the SDIDI mean while the renderer's all-binary64 arm returns the binary64 mean

**What the report says (two leads, one note).**

R2-2 LEAD (low): `FunctionValueField.cs:233` a binary64 middle beside an exact one takes the exact mean of the float's SDIDI lift on DISPLAY and COMPUTE alike (no channel split); DOC-A.1-92b says binary64. Left: changing only DISPLAY would create a split; the fix is in the renderer's MedianExact arm (`IntrinsicRenderer.cs`, about line 1385), not in `FunctionValueField.Median` alone, or the channels split.

New defect 1: DISPLAY MEDIAN with both middles binary64 keeps the SDIDI mean of the approximations while the renderer's all-binary64 arm returns the binary64 mean (PB4827 family); site `FunctionValueField.cs` Median.

**Not probed.** The second lead (both middles binary64) was left not probed by the reporting finisher; the clerk probed neither.

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Runtime/Values/Numeric/FunctionValueField.cs` (`FunctionValueField.Median`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/CodeGen/Emit/IntrinsicRenderer.cs` (`IntrinsicRenderer MedianExact arm, about line 1385`).

Filed from review leads of waves 20261884 and 20261885 by the enqueue clerk; the lead's claim is the reviewer's, not adjudicated here.

**Fix (landed, wave 20261887).** Re-probed with `F`, `G` FLOAT-LONG 0.1 and 0.2 and `M PIC 9V9(20)` = 0.5: `DISPLAY MEDIAN (F G)` showed the SDIDI mean `.150000000000000008326...` where `COMPUTE` stored the binary64 mean `0.15000000000000002220` (the second lead); `DISPLAY MEDIAN (F M)` showed `.30000000000000000277...` and `COMPUTE R` stored `0.30000000000000000277`, the exact mean of the float's lift, where DOC-A.1-92b (a binary64 operand: binary64) owes the double 0.3 (the first lead, both channels). Root: the mean's operands are the two MIDDLE values, and which they are is a run-time fact the exact lane's body did not use: `MedianExact` took the exact mean of the middles' lifts whatever their kind, and `FunctionValueField.Median` kept the SDIDI mean for two binary64 middles only. A first attempt that sent every even list with a binary64 argument to the float lane broke `pb65_selection_mixed_carriers` T12 (`MEDIAN (F1 N1 N2 N2)`: the middles are fixed-point, so the exact mean owed 999999999999999998.0, and the double lost it), which is what taught that the middles decide. Now `MedianBinary64Exact (mode, bool[] binary64, xs)` takes each argument's binary64-ness (the renderer's `Binary64FlagList`, parallel to the exact values; a float item's expansion carries no mark) and forms the binary64 mean when a middle is binary64 and `MedianExact` otherwise; the renderer sends every native MEDIAN with a binary64 argument that reaches the exact lane to it (it was: an all-binary64 list over a run-time count), and `FunctionValueField.Median` forms the double mean when a middle is a float. Golden `pb4859_even_median_beside_a_binary64_argument_is_the_binary64_mean` plus its `_below_2002` refusal.
