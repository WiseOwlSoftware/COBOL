# PB5767 - MOVE CORRESPONDING at 85 freezes a subscript read from redefined storage

**Sites:**
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Procedure/Verbs/CorrespondingBinder.cs` (`CorrespondingBinder.Bind`,
  `CorrespondingBinder.CorrAccess`: the per-pair arm below 2002 re-walks the group's path per pair, but the path carries
  the statement-level subscript temporaries)
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/SubscriptBinder.cs` (`SubscriptBinder.MaterializePosition`: a subscript the direct
  walk cannot read, here a data-name in a redefined record, becomes a statement pre-op)
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Bound/StatementBinder.SubscriptSegments.cs` (`StatementBinder.BindPositionTemporary`)
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Bound/BoundCorresponding.cs#PairIdentification`, `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/CodeGen/Verbs/CorrespondingEmitter.cs#Emit`, `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/CodeGen/UnitEmitters.cs` (the fix: the 85 MOVE identification steps run before each pair)

**Rule.** §1985:VI.6.19.4 GR1: "The results are the same as if the user had referred to each pair of corresponding
identifiers in separate MOVE statements", and GR2: "Any length evaluation or subscripting associated with identifier-2 is
evaluated immediately before the data is moved to the respective data item" (both `cite.py --check` OK). So at 85 each
pair evaluates its own subscripts, and the outcome must equal SOME order of the separate MOVEs. The later editions REQUIRE
the freeze: §2002:14.8.24.3 GR6 and §2023:14.9.25.4 GR11 add "except that if subscripting is specified for identifier-3 or
identifier-4, the subscript value used is that resulting from the evaluation of the subscript at the start of the
execution of the statement" (both `cite.py --check` OK). So this is an 85-arm defect only; 2002 and 2023 are right to
freeze.

**What PB5185 and PB5105 cover.** PB5185 (landed) gave 85 its own arm: `CorrAccess` with `perPair` (`DialectLevel < 2002`)
anchors no group local, so every pair re-identifies the group and a plain member subscript is read per pair (witness
`pb5185_move_corresponding_subscript_per_pair`; `work-a/r5-corr.cob` prints `CORR2 27` at 85, `22` at 2023). PB5105
(landed) is the operand-description screen (level 66, USAGE INDEX), not identification. Neither reaches a subscript that
binds through the D18 position route: a data-name whose storage is redefined is not read by the resolver's direct walk,
so `MaterializePosition` hoists it into a `__subexpr` temporary stored ONCE by a statement pre-op before the first pair,
and every pair's access reads that temporary.

**Repro** (re-run 2026-11-15 on this branch's build, `registrar-1985-reread/log-misc.txt` and `log-corr2002.txt`,
`log-corr2023.txt`):

- `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/op-20261115/open-rows-1985/refute-a/m-send-two.cob`: `T = "22111911"`, `I OF R = J OF R = 1`,
  `MOVE CORRESPONDING S (I OF R, J OF R) TO R`, with `01 RX REDEFINES R`. At 85: `CORR    22`; the separate MOVEs give
  29 (order I, J) or 12 (J, I). The same program without the REDEFINES (`m-send-two-ctl.cob`) prints `CORR    29` at 85.
- `refute-a/m-recv-two.cob` (receiving side, the subscripts KA, KB in a record overlaid by `01 TX REDEFINES T`): at 85
  `CORR    [22111111]`; separate MOVEs give `[21111211]` or `[12211111]`.
- `refute-a/m-two-sub-rr.cob` (one subscript, `01 RX REDEFINES R`): at 85 `CORR 23A` where the separate MOVEs give
  `SEP  21B`; `m-two-sub-redef.cob` (the table redefined, not R) gives `CORR 21B`.
- At 2002 and 2023 every one of these, the control included, prints the frozen result (`CORR    22`, `[22111111]`,
  `CORR 23A`): the rule those editions state.

The generated code (`runs/misc/m-two-sub-rr-001/m-two-sub-rr.g.cs` lines 88-91) stores `__subexpr10_L19` from
`_redef_R` once and reads it in all three pair moves.

**Expected** (85): the result of one order of the separate MOVEs. **Actual:** the statement-start value, matching none.
Harm: a wrong answer, silently, in a program whose only difference from a correct one is an unrelated REDEFINES.

**Fix direction (root).** At 85, a pair's access must evaluate its subscripts at that pair. Either `CorrAccess`'s
`perPair` arm re-binds the subscript segments per pair (so a materialized position gets one pre-op per pair, sequenced
before that pair's move), or the position route reads redefined storage directly. Sweep the other CORRESPONDING verbs
(ADD and SUBTRACT CORRESPONDING share `CorrAccess`: `pb5185_subtract_corresponding_subscript_per_pair` is the plain-member
witness) and add an 85 case with a redefined subscript record for MOVE and SUBTRACT.
