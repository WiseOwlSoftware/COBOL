# PB1912 — orchestrator context cap and the loop clock

## Question

An outside post (Scott Ernst's "loop clock", with a reply from Moonveil AI) argued that the hidden cost of a large fleet
is the orchestrator re-reading its own context on every steering call, and that teams that ship cap what the
orchestrator may remember and force summaries up the chain. Our 2026-10-01 telemetry (`usage_report.py`, all sessions
that day) put the orchestrator's thread at about 10 % of spend (about 300 k cache-read tokens per call, 505 calls) and
the harness's own `agent_summary` calls at about 12 %.

## Experiment 1 — cap the return payload

Hypothesis: the structured return and the lander's final text are the largest agent-written text that enters the
orchestrator's conversation, so capping them shrinks the orchestrator's later per-turn read without losing information
(the report files and the DEVLOG keep the detail). Baseline (workflow output files, bytes): wave 1001 31,864; wave 1002
27,210; the PB1907 survey 93,847 (a read-only survey, not the rolling wave). Change: `IMPL_SCHEMA.summary` at most 900
characters, `leads` at most 6 of at most 500 characters, the implementer prompt and the lander's final text (at most 25
lines) say so. Measure on the next rolling wave: the same file sizes, and whether any agent failed schema validation
because of the caps. Result and decision go into a `docs/rearchitecture/evidence/fleet-optimization/` record when the
wave has run.

## Experiment 2 — the loop clock

`scripts/telemetry/loop_clock.py` draws one workflow run from its transcripts (arc per agent, red marks at gate reds).
Open question it should answer: the orchestrator's own per-turn context size against turn number, and the share of
wall time spent waiting on gate slots, the serial lander and CI. Not yet drawn: the orchestrator rings, refuter
overturns and lander drops.

## Not yet looked at

`agent_summary` (the harness's progress summaries of subagents, about 12 % of the 2026-10-01 spend): whether a setting
reduces it.
