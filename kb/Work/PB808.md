# PB808 — OPEN — the last un-swept ON/OFF figure note still states the inverted reading

Filed 2026-09-09 by the registrar from the PB794 implementer's report (§10, N3), which was never filed as a
note. ⛔ **`specs/` is a submodule and was NOT modified** — this note records the defect, its measurement and
its repair text so the change can be made in the submodule's own change set.

## The defect, verbatim

`specs/ISO_COBOL.md:4045`, the figure note under §7.3.18.2:

> **Figure notes (LISTING directive general format).** `LISTING` and `OFF` are underlined in the printed
> standard (required words). ⚠ `ON` is **not** underlined in the printed figure, although 7.3.18.3 general rule
> 2 makes `>>LISTING ON` the default and general rules 4 and 5 treat `ON` as a specifiable phrase; **the missing
> underline appears to be a typesetting omission in the standard** and is reproduced here as printed. **The
> braces are a plain required choice — exactly one of `ON` or `OFF` shall be selected.** No choice indicators
> are present.

The glyph facts in the first sentence are right. The two bolded conclusions are wrong, and they are the part a
reader acts on.

## What the standard makes of a bare ON

`cite.py --check 5.2.3 "They are shown in uppercase and not underlined in general formats"` → **OK, §5.2.3**:

> Optional words are reserved words or context-sensitive words. **They are shown in uppercase and not
> underlined in general formats.** They may be written to add clarity when the clause or phrase in which they
> are defined is written in the source unit.

`cite.py --check 5.2.6.3 "braces"` → **OK, §5.2.6.3**:

> Braces, { }, enclosing a portion of a general format indicate that the syntax element contained within the
> braces or one of the alternatives contained within the braces shall be explicitly specified or **is
> implicitly selected. If one of the alternatives contains only optional words, that alternative is the default
> and is selected unless another alternative is explicitly specified.**

So the ON alternative consists of exactly one optional word, which makes it the implicitly selected default:
**a bare `>>LISTING` is conforming source and means `>>LISTING ON`.** The general rules say so in their own
words — `cite.py --check 7.3.18.3 "specifying or implying the ON phrase"` → **OK, §7.3.18.3 4)**:

> 4\) If OFF is specified, source lines shall not be listed until a LISTING directive **specifying or implying
> the ON phrase** is encountered, with the exception that another LISTING OFF directive shall be listed.

and `cite.py --check 7.3.18.3 "specified or implied"` → **OK, §7.3.18.3 5)**: *"If ON is specified or
implied…"*. ⛔ **"Implying" and "implied" are the omitted-optional-word case, and they have no other referent**
— there is no other way to imply a phrase you did not write. The note's own premise (GR2 makes `>>LISTING ON`
the default) is therefore evidence AGAINST its conclusion: the default exists precisely because the word may be
omitted.

`cite.py --check 7.3.18.3 "LISTING ON"` → **OK, §7.3.18.3 2)**: *"The default LISTING directive is
'>>LISTING ON'."*

## The repair (for the submodule's own change set — do NOT apply here)

Replace the note at `specs/ISO_COBOL.md:4045` with the wording its six siblings already carry:

> **Figure notes (LISTING directive general format).** `LISTING` and `OFF` are underlined in the printed
> standard (required words); `ON` is printed **without** an underline, so per 5.2.3 it is an OPTIONAL word: the
> ON alternative may be selected with the word omitted. The braces therefore do not force a written choice —
> writing `OFF`, writing `ON`, or writing neither all satisfy them, omission being equivalent to ON, which per
> 5.2.6.3 is the alternative implicitly selected because it contains only optional words. That is the
> implied-ON case 7.3.18.3 general rules 4 and 5 presuppose when they say "specifying or implying the ON
> phrase" and "specified or implied", and it is why general rule 2 can name `>>LISTING ON` as the default. No
> choice indicators are present (measured: `figure_geometry.py 108` reports 0 delimiter stems).
