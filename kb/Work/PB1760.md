# PB1760 — a per-file encoding attribute; LINE SEQUENTIAL defaults to UTF-8 (owner R51)

Design: `docs/COBOLNET_FILES_DESIGN.md` D29 (as corrected). The two sections "Work" and "Revisit before starting" below are the ORIGINAL PLAN; where they differ from "Landing" (notably: U+FFFF is a MEMBER of the text set, not refused) the Landing is what was built.

## Original plan (superseded where it differs from Landing)

- A file ENCODING attribute (default UTF-8 for LINE SEQUENTIAL; ISO 8859-1 one byte per position for the fixed
  record organizations unless CODE-SET says otherwise), carried by the connector; `FileCharacterSet` becomes the
  Latin-1 case of it and `LineSequentialCharacterSet` (Annex A.1 item 115) states which characters a text line may
  hold in UTF-8 (control characters and the noncharacter U+FFFF stay refused with '71').
- DISPLAY / ACCEPT and the standard streams: UTF-8 (verify what `StandardStreams` does today — measure first).
- Line length and the record-size rules: a LINE SEQUENTIAL record of n CHARACTERS has a variable BYTE length; the
  §14.9.30.4 GR14/15 truncation and status-06 rules are in CHARACTER positions — decide and document, do not inherit.
- BOM policy on read (accept and strip) and write (none), and invalid UTF-8 on read (status 09 vs replacement — the
  spec decides; derive before coding).

## Original "revisit before starting" (done — see Landing)

R47 said "never re-ask" for the Latin-1 default; R51 supersedes it for line sequential files only, in the owner's words
of 2026-09-29. Update R47's note (superseded-by R51) in the landing commit, plus docs/CONFORMANCE.md DOC-A.1-31 and
DOC-A.1-115, and the golden(s) that pin the refusal of a character above U+00FF on a line sequential file.

## Landing (2026-09-29)

**Measured first.** `StandardStreams.EnsureUtf8` already made stdout/stderr UTF-8 (PB59/PB899), so DISPLAY needed no
change; nothing else in the text path assumed Latin-1 except the LINE SEQUENTIAL record channel.

**The encoding attribute.** `FileConnector.MediumEncoding` (enum `MediumEncoding` in `FileCharacterSet.cs`): `CodeSet`
when a CODE-SET clause is present, else `Utf8` for LINE SEQUENTIAL, else `SingleByte` (8859-1 + the storage-byte law,
`FileCharacterSet`). Derived from the two facts it is made of (the CODE-SET conversion and the organization), never
stored. `FromMedium`/`ToMedium` dispatch on it; the framing below stays a byte channel (one char per byte), so the
line scan, `_lineByteOffset`, START and REWRITE anchors did not change.

**The determinations (docs/CONFORMANCE.md DOC-A.1-115), each derived:**
- The set with no CODE-SET: the Unicode scalar values from U+0020 in BOTH record-area classes (a surrogate pair is one
  character; HIGH-VALUE U+FFFF is a member — it round-trips as EF BF BF). ⚠ This DEPARTS from this note's proposal to
  keep U+FFFF refused: refusing it would make `MOVE HIGH-VALUES TO REC` / WRITE a '71' on legal source, GnuCOBOL
  writes 0xFF there, and Unicode Corrigendum #9 makes noncharacters legal in interchange. Outside: C0 controls and an
  unpaired surrogate (no UTF-8 form).
- Invalid UTF-8 on READ: §14.9.30.4 GR16 decides the STATUS ('09', the READ is successful); what the record area
  holds for a byte that is no character is latitude: each such byte decodes to the lone surrogate U+DC80+byte
  (lossless, and outside the set, so '09' falls out of the ONE predicate and a WRITE/REWRITE of it is '71'); GnuCOBOL,
  the next authority, transfers the bytes unchanged.
- BOM: a UTF-8 BOM opening the file is accepted and skipped (and a REWRITE of the first line keeps it); none is ever
  written.
- Sizes: §14.9.30.4 GR14/GR15 and NOTE 4 in the record area's units (characters), so ten `é` fill `PIC X(10)`;
  §14.9.35.4 GR17 b)/c) in BYTES of the physical line (the replacement is in place): a REWRITE whose UTF-8 is longer
  than the replaced line is '44', a shorter one is space-filled to its byte length.
- A national record area travels as the UTF-8 of its CHARACTERS (pairs decoded first).

**Goldens**: `2023/pb1760_line_sequential_utf8_bom_and_non_utf8_bytes` (BOM, a Latin-1 byte -> '09', REWRITE -> '71');
`2023/pb690_line_sequential_byte_image_2023` re-derived from R47's refusal to UTF-8 (U+20AC '00', a lone surrogate
'71', REWRITE '00' at equal bytes and '44' at more); `2023/pb1542_code_set_line_sequential` S1-IN is ORD 196 (the
UTF-8 lead byte X'C3', written with no CODE-SET). Unit: `FileBoundaryTests.LineSequentialCharacterSet_IsTheUtf8ScalarValues_FromSpace`,
`FileBoundaryTests.LineSequentialEncoding_IsUtf8_WithAByteEscape`.

**For the next implementer:** `src/Cobol.Net.Runtime/IO/LineSequentialEncoding.cs` is the UTF-8 codec (with the byte
escape); `LineSequentialCharacterSet.HasCharacterOutside` is the one '09'/'71' predicate (codeSet null = UTF-8);
`SequentialConnector.NextFrame` owns the BOM skip and the byte anchors; `RewriteLine` compares medium bytes.
