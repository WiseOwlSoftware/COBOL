# PB5873 - a self-test arm asserted a monotonic reading the operating system does not promise

**Sites:**
- `scripts/self_tests.py#self_test` (the arm "this host's processors are readable, and their total ticks advance")
- `scripts/self_tests.py#HostCpu` (`describe`: the busy share between two readings)

## What happened
The gate of branch `records-1985-code-reads-2` (run `000003-20261116T183751Z`, 2026-11-16) was RED on SELF-TESTS: one arm
of `self_tests.py --self-test`, `this host's processors are readable, and their total ticks advance: got False, want
True`. The arm took two `HostCpu.sample()` readings a few microseconds apart (around `sum(range(200_000))`) and required
`second.total >= first.total and second.idle >= first.idle`.

## Cause
On Windows `HostCpu.sample` reads `GetSystemTimes`, which moves once a timer tick and sums per-processor counters. On the
busy 32-processor host, measured the same hour: 23 of 20,000 back-to-back pairs of readings had a smaller second reading,
idle and total both; on the idle host the same loop found none in 60,000. So the arm was a flake under load, and its idle
half is not even true in principle: an idle count need not advance when every processor is busy.

## Fix (landed with the branch that met it)
The arm reads twice a tenth of a second apart (several timer ticks: the total advances by the processors' whole elapsed
time, far past any step back) and asserts only that the total advances. `HostCpu.describe` clamps an idle count that
stepped back to zero idle (a fully busy host), so a busy share never reads above 100 %, with an arm of its own.
`python scripts/self_tests.py --self-test`: ALL GREEN.
