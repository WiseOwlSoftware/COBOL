# PB4911 - bare citations survive in the tooling, unrefused

**What was seen.** PB4887 phase 2 (PR 1270, 440ca75f81) rewrote the tree's citations into the edition form and made a bare
section-sign citation a refusal. Its rewriter deliberately left the tooling's string literals alone (they can be data that
the tool's own logic reads), and the phase 2 brief asked for the tooling's citations to be rewritten by hand. That hand pass
did not reach every file. `scripts/spec/gen_ledger.py` still prints 4.2.1 and 4.2.16 after a bare section sign (clauses of the standard:
`cite.py --check 2023:4.2.16` passes) on lines 12, 916, 1096 and 1236, and the published conformance ledger (v331) shows
them bare. The citations audit (`audit_code_citations.py --check`) is GREEN on that tree, so it does not reach those lines.

**Measured** (a read-only sweep at 440ca75f81: every tracked `.py`, `.ps1`, `.js`, `.sh`, `.yml` and `.md` under `scripts/`,
`tools/`, `.github/` and `.claude/`; each bare citation yielded by `citation.scan(line, bare=True)` and kept by
`citation.bare_standard(line, c)`, without the file's own-section set): 372 lines. The top files are `citation.py` (43),
`audit_annex_a1.py` (33), `audit_code_citations.py` (27), `inventory_schema.py` (22), `extract_rule_catalog.py` (22),
`audit_grammar_optional_words.py` (17), `figure_options.py` (17), `scripts/arch/r2_collect.py` (15), `wf_r2_review.js` (12),
`differential_fuzz.py` (9) and `gen_ledger.py` (8). The count is an upper bound. It includes the parser's and the audits'
own docstring examples of the bare form, test data that plants a bare citation on purpose, and design-doc sections cited
with no document named on the line (`work.py`'s example of a document's section 8.7, written with a bare section sign). So each line needs a decision.

**The fix.**
1. Decide every line: a citation of the standard becomes the edition form; a document's section names its document; an
   example of the refused bare form, or planted test data, is marked as such in the way the audit can read (one marker,
   documented in `citation.py`'s docstring). No hand-kept list of exempt lines.
2. Make the citations audit reach the tooling, string literals included, with that marker the only exemption, so the
   next bare citation in a script or a published page fails the gate. A planted self-test case proves the audit refuses a
   bare section-sign 4.2.16 inside a script's string literal.
3. Two leads from the phase 2 branch (w20261892a) share this root, the screen's reach:
   - The document screen reads one line. A document name ending one comment line, with its section number opening the
     next, is not screened, so the number is read as the standard's (`citation.names_another_text` gets `line`, not the
     previous line). The finisher fixed 7 such sites by moving the name.
   - Design-doc sections cited bare outside the architecture-review family, with no document named on the line, may
     have been rewritten as 2023 citations. There is no mechanical signal. A heading-word mismatch filter (scratch
     `w-citation2/w20261892a-fin/mismatch.py`: 4,882 hits, mostly genuine) is too noisy to drive a fix by itself; it
     can rank candidates for the decision in (1).
4. Regenerate the ledger, so the published page carries the edition form.

**Sites:** `scripts/spec/audit_code_citations.py` (`main`, the scope), `scripts/spec/citation.py` (`names_another_text`, `bare_standard`), `scripts/spec/gen_ledger.py` (`main`), `scripts/spec/audit_annex_a1.py` (whole: its bare citations), `scripts/spec/inventory_schema.py` (whole: its bare citations), `scripts/spec/extract_rule_catalog.py` (whole: its bare citations).

## Landing (wave 20261894, group A)

- **The scope.** `scripts/spec/citation_corpus.py#tooling_files` (with `#tooling`, `TOOLING`, `TOOLING_SUFFIXES`): the
  text files under `scripts/`, `tools/`, `.github/` and `.claude/`. `scripts/spec/audit_code_citations.py#phantom_scan`

  leader (a Markdown quote's `>` included), a string literal's prefix and quote, gap punctuation or earlier members of

  screen also knows an interpolated document name (`f"{BINDER_DESIGN} §3.6"`) and the Unicode Standard.
