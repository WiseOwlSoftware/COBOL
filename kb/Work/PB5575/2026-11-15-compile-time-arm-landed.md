**2026-11-15, the compile-time arm of this note landed; the run-time arm stays open here.**

Landed (wave 20262043, group C): `CompileTimeArithmetic.For` selects THREE modes. `Standard2002` (new) is the 2002 standard intermediate data item: operands and every `+ - * /` result truncated toward zero to 32 significant digits (`CobolDec.TruncateToSignificantDigits`, over the engine's `CobolRounding.Truncation`), magnitude 10^-999 through 10^1000 - 10^968 (`CobolDec.AdjustedExponent`), a value outside it refused with the §2002:8.8.1.3.1.1 range named. `StandardDecimal` (the former `Standard`) is 2014's "as if ARITHMETIC IS STANDARD-DECIMAL" (§2014:7.3.5.2 GR1). `SystemDecimal` is 2023's. The 2014 edge is `DialectBehavior.CompileTimeArithmeticIsStandardDecimal` (VCR row 7.77).

Re-probe on the new build, `--std 2002`: the note's pk repro printed `00009` before and prints `00000` after; `2 / 3 * 3` printed 2 before and 1 after; `--std 2014` still prints `00009` and `00002`. Rows closed (inventory-2002 store, batch `pb5575-ct-arith-2002`, GAP 3297 to 3290): the seven compile-time rows in `closes_rows`. The 25 rows left in `inventory_rows` are the RUN-TIME engine: `ARITHMETIC IS STANDARD` statements, comparisons (31-digit rounding, §2002:8.8.1.3.1.3), BY VALUE arithmetic arguments, SEARCH, the first-exception order, and the intrinsic functions' arguments and returned values.

**Why the run-time arm was not taken in this change (a scope estimate, not a ceiling).** The run-time standard engine is the one SDIDI decimal engine (`ArithmeticModes.IsDecimalEngine(Standard)`), emitted by `NumericRenderer.DecBinary` as `CobolDec.Add/Sub/Mul/Div(…, IntermediateMode)` at 34 digits with the decimal128 range (`CobolDec.Round34Wide`, `Clamp`), and every function lane reads it. The 2002 model needs a different engine PARAMETER (32 digits, truncation per operation, 10^-999 range, 31-digit rounding at a comparison, a function argument without an equivalent expression and a move without ROUNDED) threaded through the emitter, the intrinsics and the comparison lowering. That is its own mechanism and its own redesign (a precision/range model on the engine, with the two shipped models as instances), so it is the next slice of this note. `CobolDec.TruncateToSignificantDigits` and `AdjustedExponent` are the first two members that slice reuses.

**Lead found on the way (2014 lane, not this note).** `§2014:D.16.1` and its rounding sentence say the implicit intermediate rounding "is TRUNCATION ... unchanged from previous editions" and that truncation is the default of standard-decimal arithmetic, while this compiler's 2014 compile-time and run-time standard-decimal use NEAREST-AWAY-FROM-ZERO (the 2023 default, §2023:11.9.11.2 GR3 a). `conformance-suite/cases/_unassigned/pb1592_ct_arith_standard` now accepts at 2014 only, and its 2014 expectations (`D1=002`, `TIE=10010`) rest on the 2023 default; a 2014 adoption read should decide them.
