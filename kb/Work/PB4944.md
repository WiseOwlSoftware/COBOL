# PB4944 - a STALL should mean "stopped", not "long"

**What happened.** On 2026-11-07 the loop mailed about a dozen STALL notices across the operator's fix lane (waves
20261905, 20261911) and Mythos's 1985 campaign (w20261903a, w20261907a twice, w20261912a). The operator and Mythos checked
each by hand, and every one was a healthy agent: committing, gating, or waiting up to 15 minutes for a gate slot. The
1985 campaign's implementers run 60 to 150 minutes on large work (hundreds of figure verdicts, 139 page repairs), and the
review of a large branch takes 20 to 25 minutes. A notice that fires on every healthy unit stops being read, so a real
stall would be missed.

**Two signals in one message.** `scripts/orchestrator/progress.py` (`progress`) raises STALL when a group passes
`RUNNING_STALL_MIN` (60) or `REVIEW_STALL_MIN` (15) elapsed minutes. The 60 is the owner's sizing rule (2026-10-23: "no
wave may take hours; one chain link per wave, hand off at ~60 min"), so passing it is a real finding, but it is a finding
about the SIZE of the work. A stuck agent is a different finding, and it needs action now. One word covers both today.

**Proposal.**
1. **STALL** means no progress: no commit on the branch, no write to its gate log or report, and no tool call in the
   wave's agent state for N minutes. Proposed N = 20, longer than the slot wait seen today (17 minutes at worst), so a
   gate waiting for a slot does not count as stalled.
2. **OVER-SIZE** means a group is past the owner's 60 minutes while still moving. It goes in the PROGRESS line and in a
   per-wave count, not as one mail per group, and the planner's sizing reads it (a campaign whose groups always run over
   is split smaller).
3. A review stall counts from the implementer's return (PB4918), and a large branch's review limit scales with its
   diff size. Alternatively, it falls under rule 1's no-progress test.

**Decided (the owner, 2026-11-07, through Mythos): "yes, a long campaign like the 1985 audit gets its own limit".** It
is the operator lane's to implement here. A campaign's limit is DATA OF THE CAMPAIGN, never a hand-kept list: a
field on the cluster lead's note (for example `running_limit_min:` in its frontmatter, validated by `work.py check`),
read by the over-size count and by the planner. A campaign without the field keeps the owner's 60 minutes
(`RUNNING_STALL_MIN`), and the fix lane always does. Rules 1 and 3 above are tooling and needed no decision.

**Implemented (w20261923c, 2026-11-08).** `progress.py`: a STALL is a group (running, or DONE and awaiting review) with no act in the coordination directory for `STALL_IDLE_MIN` (20) minutes (`group_activity`: its scratch directory, the scratch files and reports named for it, and for a DONE report the worktree's last act); OVER-SIZE is a still-moving group past `RUNNING_LIMIT_MIN` (60) or its campaign's `running_limit_min:`, or a DONE report awaiting review past `REVIEW_LIMIT_MIN` (15), counted in the PROGRESS line per wave and never mailed. `work.py`: `running_limit_min:` on a cluster's lead note (`running_limit_problems` in `check`, `campaign_running_limit`, the one reader). No campaign lead note carries `running_limit_min:` yet (the 1985 audit's lead is the campaign lane's to set: the field is the campaign's data, and this lane sets no other lane's). The loop reads no git, so a commit shows only through the STATUS.md and gate log the checkpoint rewrites.

**Implemented, the planner half (w20261928a, 2026-11-08).** A count per tick is forgotten by the next unit, so the history is per GROUP, not per wave: `scripts/orchestrator/group_sizes.py` (new) keeps `group-sizes.json` in the coordination directory, one entry per dispatch-ledger group seen running (its notes, its campaigns from `work.note_campaigns`, its limit, the longest run seen, and `over`, sticky once a tick counted it over-size; a stall is not over-size), written by `progress.py --record` on every supervisor tick (`orchestrate.ps1` passes it) and kept 30 days after it was last seen, because the ledger drops a group when it lands. The planner reads it: a `--cluster` wave asks `group_sizes.campaign_cap`, and when the campaign's latest 5 recorded groups (at least 3) all ran over, the group size is `limit // median(minutes per note)`, at least one note and never above the turn cap (`clusters.notes_cap`); `plan_wave.py` prints the reason, passes the cap to `campaign_clusters` and to the partition (`PlanState.group_size`), and an overflow's reason names the measurement (`overflow_wait`). One recent group that fitted keeps the turn cap, the owner's "always run over". The fix lane keeps the turn cap. `work.campaign_running_limit` now reads its campaigns through `note_campaigns`, the one membership rule.

## Landing

- `scripts/orchestrator/group_sizes.py#record` / `#campaign_cap`: the ONE writer and reader of `group-sizes.json`; a new
  sizing rule reads `read()`, never the ledger (it drops landed groups). Trap: the history is per group slug, so a tick
  re-records idempotently; never key it by wave.
- `scripts/orchestrator/progress.py#progress`: its `running` list is what `record` takes; a running item carries
  `campaigns` and `limit` (from `wait`), a review wait does not.
- `scripts/orchestrator/plan_wave.py#PlanState.group_size`: the measured cap and why; `overflow_wait(g, s, fate)` takes the
  PlanState now, so a reason always names the cap the partition used.
- `scripts/spec/work.py#note_campaigns`: the one campaign-membership rule; `campaign_running_limit` reads through it.
