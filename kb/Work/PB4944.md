# PB4944 - a STALL should mean "stopped", not "long"

**What happened.** On 2026-11-07 the loop mailed about a dozen STALL notices across the operator's fix lane (waves
20261905, 20261911) and Mythos's 1985 campaign (w20261903a, w20261907a twice, w20261912a). The operator and Mythos checked
each by hand, and every one was a healthy agent: committing, gating, or waiting up to 15 minutes for a gate slot. The
1985 campaign's implementers run 60 to 150 minutes on large work (hundreds of figure verdicts, 139 page repairs), and the
review of a large branch takes 20 to 25 minutes. A notice that fires on every healthy unit stops being read, so a real
stall would be missed.

**Two signals in one message.** `scripts/orchestrator/progress.py` (`progress`) raises STALL when a group passes
`RUNNING_STALL_MIN` (60) or `REVIEW_STALL_MIN` (15) elapsed minutes. The 60 is the owner's sizing rule (2026-10-23: "no
wave may take hours; one chain link per wave, hand off at ~60 min"), so passing it is a real finding, but it is a finding
about the SIZE of the work. A stuck agent is a different finding, and it needs action now. One word covers both today.

**Proposal.**
1. **STALL** means no progress: no commit on the branch, no write to its gate log or report, and no tool call in the
   wave's agent state for N minutes. Proposed N = 20, longer than the slot wait seen today (17 minutes at worst), so a
   gate waiting for a slot does not count as stalled.
2. **OVER-SIZE** means a group is past the owner's 60 minutes while still moving. It goes in the PROGRESS line and in a
   per-wave count, not as one mail per group, and the planner's sizing reads it (a campaign whose groups always run over
   is split smaller).
3. A review stall counts from the implementer's return (PB4918), and a large branch's review limit scales with its
   diff size. Alternatively, it falls under rule 1's no-progress test.

**Decided (the owner, 2026-11-07, through Mythos): "yes, a long campaign like the 1985 audit gets its own limit".** It
is the operator lane's to implement here. A campaign's limit is DATA OF THE CAMPAIGN, never a hand-kept list: a
field on the cluster lead's note (for example `running_limit_min:` in its frontmatter, validated by `work.py check`),
read by the over-size count and by the planner. A campaign without the field keeps the owner's 60 minutes
(`RUNNING_STALL_MIN`), and the fix lane always does. Rules 1 and 3 above are tooling and needed no decision.

**Sites:** `scripts/orchestrator/progress.py` (`progress`, `render`, `RUNNING_STALL_MIN`, `REVIEW_STALL_MIN`, `stall_lane`), `scripts/spec/work.py` (`frontmatter_errors`, `FRONTMATTER_ERRORS`).
