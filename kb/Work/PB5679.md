# PB5679 - the rule lineage runs forward

**The owner's words, verbatim.**
1. 2026-11-14: "2002 spec covers 2002 rules. It should not matter to the 2002 rules whether or not 2023 removes/adds/changes a clause. That only affects 2023 rules".
2. 2026-11-15: "Note that the same argument for the 2002 rules applies equally to the 1985 rules."
3. 2026-11-15: "Conceptually, each version's rules, syntax, and semantics are independent. However, as the language designers needed to maintain some level of compatibility, unless explicitly decides not, later version rules can be thought of as including a prior version's construct as is, including with additions, deletions, and alterations, explicitly removing the prior version construct, or adding an entirely new construct."
4. 2026-11-15, on the sentence "an older rule may inherit a newer rule's verdict": "It seems it should be the other way around. A newer version can inherit an older versions construct as the newer version didn't remove or change it."

**What the backward keying cost.** Every stored change record sits on an older row and names 2023, skipping the
editions between: 2,260 1985 records and 704 2002 records, all `edition: 2023, bridge: true` (counted from
`tests/version-matrix/inventory-1985/` and `inventory-2002/`). The endpoint comparison was wrong for 16 of 100
word-identical 2002/2023 pairs in the first blind sample and 13 of 100 (plus 3 doubtful) in the second
(DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md section 10.5). The 1985 records are many to many: 451 of the 1,373 KEPT records name
two or more 2023 rules, and 437 of the 1,180 2023 rules they reach receive two or more records, so no "same requirement"
reading of them can hold rule for rule.

**The decisions (the design is DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md section 10.7).**
- L1. A row closes on a verdict and a witness of the kind its rule needs that runs at its own edition (its own
  `test-ref`, PB5675 mechanism B), whatever the lineage says.
- L2. The unit is the LINE: one rule carried as is through consecutive catalogued editions. An altered rule starts a
  line and names what it altered; a new rule starts a line; a removed rule ends one.
- L3. The record lives on the successor (`from`: relation, predecessor rules, basis); a removal is derived from the
  successor's change list; no older row names a newer row.
- L4. The successor's own change list is the first authority for a relation other than as-is; an as-is hop is recorded
  only from a read of both rules in their own terms, attacked by a refuter.
- L5. One adjudication per line, held by a member adjudicated on its own text, read where it was made (2023's stays on
  2023; new work starts at 1985); each member closes only with the line's verdict OK and its own witness; two holders that
  disagree are red; the view counts the rows closed through a later holder.
- L6. Every 1985 row carries its own verdict, permanently and on purpose: once PB5675 is on main the writer refuses every
  new 1985 KEPT record because 1985 has no candidate pairing (0 of 2,274 rules), and this design keeps that refusal. The
  1,373 stored KEPT records are replaced by own verdicts, each from a read in 1985's own terms, before the switch.
- L7. Candidates, screen, read and import take a (predecessor, successor) pair; a hop over an edition whose pairs are
  not complete is a skipping hop naming it, listed owed a split (never red) once they are.
- L8. The migration states every edition's GAP before and after and lists every row whose state moves.
- ONE FORMAT (lead decision 2026-11-15 on this design's review, CLAUDE.md rule 4): at every landed head the tooling
  reads and writes one lineage record format. The forward record, the line's state in both languages, the forward
  derivation of the edition gates, and the migration of EVERY stored older-row record land as one change, the switch
  (PB5682), which deletes `next` with its reader and writer. The stages before it work in today's format.

**2014 (owner, 2026-11-15, relayed by the operator's mail 20261115-112411).** He asked "We also need to do 2014, don't
we?" and then said "If so, we can skip it until the other three editions are complete but should plan for it". So 2014
is planned, not started: its place is between 2002 and 2023 (2002 to 2014 to 2023); until 2023, 2002 and 1985 are at
zero GAP, the 2002-to-2023 hop stands in, a skipping hop naming 2014. The switch and every stage before it need no 2014
catalog: no precondition of the switch names 2014.

**What it supersedes.**

  for new work. Its adjacency (one record per neighbour pair) is L7.

**What the code showed, and how each point was settled (lead, 2026-11-15).**

  the same change; no second format.
- L6's "the writer already refuses them all": not true on main today; true once PB5675 lands, because 1985 has no
  candidate pairing. Settled as the permanent design.
- The registry "already this shape": dropped. Lines and construct windows meet only at edition edges; the EDITION GATES
  audit is that check, so no separate drift step.
