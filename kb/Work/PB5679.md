# PB5679 - the rule lineage runs forward

**The owner's words, verbatim.**
1. 2026-11-14: "2002 spec covers 2002 rules. It should not matter to the 2002 rules whether or not 2023 removes/adds/changes a clause. That only affects 2023 rules".
2. 2026-11-15: "Note that the same argument for the 2002 rules applies equally to the 1985 rules."
3. 2026-11-15: "Conceptually, each version's rules, syntax, and semantics are independent. However, as the language designers needed to maintain some level of compatibility, unless explicitly decides not, later version rules can be thought of as including a prior version's construct as is, including with additions, deletions, and alterations, explicitly removing the prior version construct, or adding an entirely new construct."
4. 2026-11-15, on the sentence "an older rule may inherit a newer rule's verdict": "It seems it should be the other way around. A newer version can inherit an older versions construct as the newer version didn't remove or change it."

**What the backward keying cost.** Every stored change record sits on an older row and names 2023, skipping the
editions between: 2,260 1985 records and 704 2002 records, all `edition: 2023, bridge: true` (counted from
`tests/version-matrix/inventory-1985/` and `inventory-2002/`). The endpoint comparison was wrong for 16 of 100
word-identical 2002/2023 pairs in the first blind sample and 13 of 100 (plus 3 doubtful) in the second
(DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md section 10.5). The 1985 records are many to many: 451 of the 1,373 KEPT records name
two or more 2023 rules, and 437 of the 1,180 2023 rules they reach receive two or more records, so no "same requirement"
reading of them can hold rule for rule.

**The decisions (the design is DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md section 10.7; L5 and L6 as revised on its review).**
- L1. A row closes on its own verdict and a witness of the kind its rule needs that runs at its own edition (its own
  `test-ref`, PB5675 mechanism B), whatever the lineage says.
- L2. The unit is the LINE: one rule carried as is through consecutive editions. An altered rule starts a line and
  names what it altered; a new rule starts a line; a removed rule ends one.
- L3. The record lives on the successor (`from`: one hop per predecessor edition, with relation, predecessor rules,
  basis); a removal is an entry of the successor's change-list data; no older row names a newer row.
- L4. The successor's own change list is the first authority for a relation other than as-is; an as-is hop is recorded
  only from a read of both rules in their own terms, attacked by a refuter.
- L5 (revised). No row's state is computed from another row's state or verdict, in either direction. Every row holds
  its own verdict record and own-edition witness. A line shares the analysis, never the verdict: an own verdict may be
  RECORDED on a line-mate's adjudication by the writer, naming its source, when every as-is hop between is read-backed
  and the row's witness runs at its edition. The LINE CONSISTENCY audit turns line-mates whose verdicts differ in kind
  red unless the non-conforming one's notes name the edition-dependent code.
- L6 (revised). Every row of every edition carries its own verdict at the switch. Before it, each stored 1985 KEPT
  record is confirmed in place by a read in 1985's own terms (read record plus own test-ref) or re-recorded through the
  existing path with the gate list unchanged; nothing is withdrawn. Once PB5675 is on main the writer refuses a new
  1985 KEPT record with no candidate pairing, and 1985 has none (0 of 2,274), so the import's pairing check for such an
  edition takes the stored record's targets.
- L7. Candidates, screen, read and import take a (predecessor, successor) pair. The skipped editions of a hop are every
  edition strictly between its ends, computed; a hop that skips a held edition is owed a split, never red.
- L8. The switch changes no row's state, proven row by row.
- ONE FORMAT (CLAUDE.md rule 4): the forward record, the state function that reads no other row, the LINE CONSISTENCY
  audit, the forward rule for gate edges and the migration of EVERY stored older-row record land as one change, the
  switch (PB5682), which deletes `next` with its reader and writer and keeps no copy of the retired `to` or `reason`.
- A DIFFERENCE found later (a definition or governing clause) is a trigger of the pair's screen table; every as-is hop
  it flags is red until its read answers it. The read record gains `examined` (the definitions and clauses relied on).

**2014 (owner, 2026-11-15, relayed by the operator's mail 20261115-112411).** He asked "We also need to do 2014, don't
we?" and then said "If so, we can skip it until the other three editions are complete but should plan for it". So 2014

a 2002-to-2023 hop stands in with 2014 among the editions it skips. The switch and every step before it need no 2014
catalog: no precondition of the switch names 2014.
