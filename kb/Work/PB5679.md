# PB5679 - the rule lineage runs forward

**The owner's words, verbatim.**
1. 2026-11-14: "2002 spec covers 2002 rules. It should not matter to the 2002 rules whether or not 2023 removes/adds/changes a clause. That only affects 2023 rules".
2. 2026-11-15: "Note that the same argument for the 2002 rules applies equally to the 1985 rules."
3. 2026-11-15: "Conceptually, each version's rules, syntax, and semantics are independent. However, as the language designers needed to maintain some level of compatibility, unless explicitly decides not, later version rules can be thought of as including a prior version's construct as is, including with additions, deletions, and alterations, explicitly removing the prior version construct, or adding an entirely new construct."
4. 2026-11-15, on the sentence "an older rule may inherit a newer rule's verdict": "It seems it should be the other way around. A newer version can inherit an older versions construct as the newer version didn't remove or change it."

**What the backward keying cost.** Every stored change record sits on an older row and names 2023, skipping the
editions between: 2,260 1985 records and 704 2002 records, all `edition: 2023, bridge: true` (counted from
`tests/version-matrix/inventory-1985/` and `inventory-2002/`). The endpoint comparison was wrong for 16 of 100
word-identical 2002/2023 pairs in the first blind sample and 13 of 100 (plus 3 doubtful) in the second
(DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md section 10.5). The 1985 records are many to many: 451 of the 1,373 KEPT records name
two or more 2023 rules, and 437 of the 1,180 2023 rules they reach receive two or more records, so no "same requirement"
reading of them can hold rule for rule.

**What this decision lands.** The doctrine (D1 to D6), the requirements a design of the change of record format must
meet (Q1 to Q6, in PB5682 with their acceptance cases), and the open questions as notes (PB5692, PB5682's design,
PB5693). It designs no switch: two review rounds of design-lineage-forward showed every concrete mechanism written for
it broken by real records (the many-to-many 1985 KEPT records, the general-format gates, the edges at 2014). The design is
DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md section 10.7.

**Decided.**
- D1. A relation is recorded on the SUCCESSOR and names its PREDECESSOR, with one of the owner's four relations, on the
  authority of the successor's own change list checked by a read in both editions' own terms. No record, tool message
  or document says an older row inherits from a newer one.
- D2. A row closes on its own record and a witness that runs at its own edition; no state function reads another row.
  PB5675 is the first half; the rest arrives with the switch.
- D3. A verdict's PROVENANCE is recorded and visible in every view: `adjudicated` (the code was judged against this rule
  at this edition) or `shared` (taken from a named line-mate's adjudication through read-backed as-is relations, with
  this row's own witness). A shared verdict is never presented as adjudicated. Where the line-mates a row could share
  from disagree in verdict, nothing is shared: the row needs its own adjudication.
- D4. 1985 is the earliest edition: its rows end with ADJUDICATED verdicts of their own.
- D5. One record format at every landed head; the change of format is ONE change (the switch, PB5682); nothing is
  withdrawn before it; no copy of the retired content survives it.
- D6. 2014 is planned only. The owner, 2026-11-15 (relayed by the operator's mail 20261115-112411), asked "We also need
  to do 2014, don't we?" and then said "If so, we can skip it until the other three editions are complete but should
  plan for it". It slots between 2002 and 2023; nothing before the switch, and no precondition of it, needs 2014's
  catalog.

**What it supersedes.**
- PB5574 decision 2 ("No edition is linked between 1985 and 2023, ever"; every older edition's record names 2023) as the
  target structure. Its decisions 1, 3, 4 and 5 and its 2002 reads stand. Dated file beside PB5574.
- PB5583 (the tooling of PB5574 decision 2): its target, `next_held` the newest edition for every older one. Dated file
  beside PB5583.
- PB4991's placement of the record on the older row (`next`) and its chain state (a KEPT row taking its targets' state).
  Its one record per pair of editions stands. Dated file beside PB4991.

**The steps** are this note's cluster (`python scripts/spec/work.py next --cluster PB5679`): PB5680 (the 1985 read
sample), PB5683 (the 1985 adjudications, D4), PB5682 (the switch: its requirements, its design open), the open analyses
PB5692 and PB5693, and, planned only under D6, PB5681 and PB5684.
