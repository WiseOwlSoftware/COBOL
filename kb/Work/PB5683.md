# PB5683 - the 1985 own verdicts and the neighbour-pair reads, before the switch (PB5679 step 2)

**Sites:** `scripts/spec/audit_batch.py` (`import_reads`), `scripts/spec/lineage_reads.py` (`reads_dir`, `import_records`,
`load`, `main`), `scripts/spec/lineage.py` (`candidates`, `candidates_document`): the pair on the read-record path (the
read records named by both years, `lineage-reads-2002/` renamed `lineage-reads-2002-2023/` with every caller), composition
as a candidate signal, and `lineage_reads.py coverage --switch`. The rest is read records and `record_verdicts.py`
batches.

**What is stored.** 2,260 1985 records, all `next` into 2023 over a bridge: KEPT 1,373, CHANGED 564, REMOVED 323. The
1985 candidate pairing against 2023 pairs 0 of 2,274 rules.

**In today's format, no second one** (section 10.7.6). This step changes no record format:
- each 1985 KEPT rule is read in 1985's own terms (sized by PB5680's measured rate), and its row is recorded with its own
  verdict and no `next` through `record_verdicts.py --edition 1985` (withdraw, then record), the form the 2002
  own-verdict rows already have, until the 1985 KEPT count is 0;
- the same read decides the rule's relation to its 2002 candidates (as-is, altered with `change`, or no counterpart):
  first the word-identical pairing of 1985 with 2002, then composition (the 1985 KEPT records and the 2002 pairing meet
  on 1,100 pairs: 864 1985 rules, 648 2002 rules), then routing and wording. The decision is a tracked read record of
  the pair 1985:2002, evidence only;
- the 887 CHANGED and REMOVED rules, and every rule a proven gate cites, are read at the pair their switch needs
  (section 10.7.6 precondition 2), also as read records.

**Done when.** `inventory_view.py --check` shows 1985 `KEPT` 0; `lineage_reads.py coverage --switch` prints nothing
missing; each landing shows `edition_gates.py --list` at base and head with the same proven gates (any that moves listed)
and each held edition's GAP before and after.

**Rows.** None claimed here; each landing's batch names its rows.
