# PB5822 - a chained row closes on a derivation of its own

**Sites:** `scripts/spec/lineage.py#state_of`, `#unwitnessed` and `#next_errors`;
`tests/version-matrix/inventory-schema.json` (`next.verdict-fields`);
`scripts/spec/inventory_schema.py#Derivation.refusals`; `scripts/spec/record_verdicts.py#validate`;
`scripts/spec/audit_derivations.py`; `scripts/spec/lineage_reads.py#bare_kept`;
`tests/WiseOwl.Cobol.Tests.RepoDrift/SpecTraceabilityInventoryDriftTests.cs#StateAt`;
`docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md` sections 1.1, 10.2 and 10.6.

## What is wrong today

Seven decision notes (kb/Work PB5756, PB5757, PB5758, PB5779, PB5783, PB5791, PB5792) and one more the mop-up
registrar files hold that 23 rows of the 1985 inventory state nothing a program can observe. Twenty of the 23 are
chained rows: their change record is KEPT, because the next edition states the same requirement.

A chained row closes today on one thing only. `lineage.state_of` returns OK when its chain is closed and
`Schema.has_spec_derived_test(row)` holds, and `StateAt` in the C# engine says the same. The schema lists
`derivation` among `next.verdict-fields`, so `lineage.next_errors` refuses a derivation on a KEPT row. The notes
say each row "closes only as an own 1985 verdict with a DRV record". No tool can record that without replacing a
true KEPT record by a false CHANGED one. So the 1985 inventory cannot reach zero GAP, whatever is written.

The seven 1985 derivations on main (`docs/conformance/8/DRV-1985-*.md`) are all on REMOVED rows, which hold a
verdict of their own. The case of a chained row was never designed.

## The decision

1. **A chained row's own-edition evidence is ONE of two things:** a spec-derived conformance-suite test-ref of its
   own (kb/Work PB5675, unchanged), or a derivation of its own under section 1.1 of the conformance-review design.
   The derivation is keyed `DRV-<the row's own rule-id>`, takes one of the schema's arms, is checked against the
   row's OWN edition's catalog, is signed, and an argued arm carries an UPHELD refuter record (kb/Work PB5373).
   Nothing about an arm, a signature or the register changes.
2. **It stands in for the witness and for nothing else.** The chain must still be closed: every rule the row
   carries into is OK at the next held edition with a band that names this edition. For a chained row that is what
   the second refusal of section 1.1 means (a derivation "explains why no test can exist, not why a DIVERGES is
   acceptable"): its verdict is its chain's, so the refusal reads "its chain is not closed".
3. **It is never inherited.** A derivation on the rule a row carries into closes that rule at its own edition. It
   does not close the older row. The older row's rule is judged by its own edition's text (owner 2026-11-14: "2002
   spec covers 2002 rules"), and an arm can be true at one edition only: Annex A.2 is a list of the 2023 text, and
   what this implementation can reach differs by `--std`. A target that closed on a derivation is a pointer to where
   the argument may be found, as a target's cases are a pointer to candidate witnesses.
4. **The first refusal of section 1.1 is unchanged.** A chained row that carries a spec-derived test-ref of its own
   takes no derivation.
5. **One predicate.** The state rule, the writer and the audit ask one function whether a chained row's derivation
   stands, in each engine, and the parity test compares the two engines' refusal codes on a chained row as it does
   on a newest one (kb/Work PB315).

## What was rejected

- **Re-record the row as CHANGED with a verdict of its own.** The requirement is the same, so the record would
  state a difference that does not exist, and the chain would lose a true link.
- **Let the target's derivation close the older row.** Point 3.
- **A new kind in the lineage evidence table.** That table says what a DIAGNOSIS witnesses. A rule nothing can
  witness has no case of either kind. kb/Work PB5783's row (its one observable consequence is a refusal) is a
  question for that table and stays with its note.

## What this does not decide

- Each row's arm and argument. An adjudicator drafts them, a refuter attacks every argued one, and a registrar
  records them after this lands.
- kb/Work PB5791's two rows are CHANGED rows whose catalog text lacks the trailing sentence that makes them
  undefined. They hold a verdict of their own and close through the existing path once the trailer is attached.
- kb/Work PB5792's row has no record at all. It takes its change record first.

## Lead nobody has checked

Fourteen of the twenty chained rows carry into a 2023 rule that closed on a TEST, not on a derivation. If no
program can witness the 1985 rule and the 2023 rule is the same requirement, the 2023 rule's test deserves the same
attack: can it fail on the rule? The table is `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/my-20261115/unwitnessable/rows.txt`.
