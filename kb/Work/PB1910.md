# PB1910 — the overlapping-MOVE corners D-OVL2 did not measure

Found by wave 1002 group D (PB1907's overlapping-MOVE part) and filed at the train-1002 landing.

## What is open

§14.6.10 1) (Annex A.2 item 36; §4.4 2)) leaves an overlapping MOVE's result undefined. Group D measured GnuCOBOL 3.2
and adopted its two DISPLAY corners (D-OVL2: JUSTIFIED RIGHT leading pad and numeric DISPLAY zero-fill, both written
before the sender is read). Two more libcob behaviors were seen in the source but not measured or adopted:

1. **PACKED and numeric-edited receivers** are also prepared before the sender is read
   (`cob_move_display_to_packed` memsets the receiver). An overlapping DISPLAY sender into a PACKED receiver would
   then read the cleared bytes. Code site: `src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Binding/Bound/MoveOverlap.cs` `Classify` (add a
   classifier arm, an `OverlapPrefillKind` and an `MoveEmitter.EmitOverlapPrefill` arm; `MoveOverlapDriftTests`
   holds the shape). Probe: run a PACKED receiver overlapping a DISPLAY sender through cobc under WSL (recipe in the
   wave-1002 scratch `w1002d/run_cobc1.sh`).
2. **A signed sender's sign is re-punched after the move**, so a fully overlapped result can be a negative zero in
   libcob (`100p`) where ours stores a positive zero. Documented in D-OVL2 as not adopted.

The owner's choice for PB1907 is "Follow GnuCOBOL, except WRITE length", so each measured difference is either
adopted or recorded in D-OVL2 with the reason it is not; item 2 needs that reason checked against the choice.

## Re-filed as a defect (analyses sweep, 2026-10-23)

Adjudicated read-only by an Opus cobol-adjudicator on main e7cf38ce4; accepted by the Mythos session.

Item 1 is a measured difference: an overlapping MOVE from a numeric DISPLAY item into a PACKED-DECIMAL receiver that redefines part of it. libcob prepares (clears) the packed receiver before it reads the sender, so the sender reads the cleared bytes: with AD PIC 9(6) VALUE 123456 and AP PIC 9(5) PACKED-DECIMAL redefining its first 3 bytes, MOVE AD TO AP gives 00456 in GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 and 23456 here; with BP redefining its last 3 bytes, 23300 versus 23456 (the non-overlapping MOVE gives 23456 on both). §14.6.10 1) makes the result undefined, so the owner's PB1907 latitude decision (DEVLOG 1836: follow GnuCOBOL wherever it measurably differs) sets the expected result as libcob's. Fix: a PACKED arm in MoveOverlap.Classify (today line 75 returns None for any non-DISPLAY receiver) with an OverlapPrefillKind for the packed pre-clear and its MoveEmitter.EmitOverlapPrefill rendering, MoveOverlapDriftTests extended, the pb1907_overlap_move golden gaining PK lines measured against cobc, and D-OVL2 rewritten. Numeric-edited receivers were measured in both head and tail shapes and agree with libcob: no change. Item 2 is mis-attributed and needs only a doc correction in the same change: libcob stores a negative-signed zero for MOVE -1000 TO S9(3) with or without overlap (a sign-of-zero representation difference, invisible to DISPLAY and to comparison), so D-OVL2's 'a signed sender's sign is re-punched after the move' sentence should say that libcob keeps the sender's sign on a zero result in every MOVE and that WiseOwl COBOL stores a positive zero; whether that general representation should follow libcob belongs with the SIGN encoding documentation, not with overlap. Flag set wrong_answer to match the register's classification of rule-1 latitude deviations (PB1540, PB1664: bytes wrong against the latitude determination, not an ISO violation).

Evidence: Measured 2026-10-23, e7cf38ce4 vs GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 (WSL cobc -std=cobol2014), probes/pb1910/ovl.cob + ctl.cob: a PACKED-DECIMAL receiver REDEFINING the head of a 9(6) DISPLAY sender holding 123456 gives ours 23456, libcob 00456; over its tail ours 23456, libcob 23300; non-overlapping control 23456 on both. Numeric-edited receivers (head and tail) agree (3456). The negative-zero item is NOT an overlap effect: MOVE -1000 TO a separate S9(3) stores a negative-signed zero in libcob (00p) and a positive one here (00{), DISPLAY +000 and IF < 0 false on both.
