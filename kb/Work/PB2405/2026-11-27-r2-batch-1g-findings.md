## R2 batch 1g (pin 9e65164eb3a9): 1 finding on this note

### `data-binding-6--architecture--f1#1` (finding, extract, votes upheld/upheld/upheld)

**Finding:** The ONE operand category reader and the ONE integer-operand classifier live inside IntrinsicResultType, so PB2405 would move two general bound-operand classifiers into compiler.bound under an intrinsic-function name

**Rule:** Single responsibility and naming by responsibility (DESIGN-architecture-review §8.2 layout and naming; DESIGN-architecture-review §5.3; dimensions.md 1 one mechanism per job, a type named for what it owns): IntrinsicResultType is documented as THE ONE READER of IntrinsicSig.Result (IntrinsicResultType.cs:128-152), yet it also owns IntrinsicResultType.OperandCategory (the ISO/IEC 1989:2023 8.5.2.1 category of any bound operand, lines 276-348) and IntrinsicResultType.IsIntegerOperand(BoundOperand)/(BoundExpr) (the integer classifier every statement rule that says shall be an integer asks, lines 407-472)

**Scenario:** Measured on the pin: IntrinsicResultType.OperandCategory has 24 call sites in 14 files and IsIntegerOperand 28 in 16 files, of which most are not intrinsic: MoveBinder, SetBinder, ConditionBinder, OperandClassScreen, StatementValidation, MoveTable16, CollatingModel (CollatingSelection), ControlFlowBinder (PERFORM TIMES), ControlFlowBinder.PerformVarying, CommunicationBinder (SEND WITH), StringUnstringBinder, SequentialIoBinder, RelationComparability, VersionConformancePass, CodeGen ObjectComputerEmit.cs:115 and ConditionRenderer.cs:996. A maintainer fixing a category bug in a MOVE or relation screen must know to open the intrinsic result-type file (PB401 records that a second copy, CollatingSelection.OperandCategory, grew exactly because the reader was not where the question is asked), and PB2405 lists IntrinsicResultType among the intrinsic rule family it moves to compiler.bound, so after that wave the general operand classifiers would sit in compiler.bound under the intrinsic name and the next reader of operand class keeps finding them by grep, not by layout

**Target:** Extract the two classifiers into one bound-operand classification type in compiler.bound (for example OperandClass with Category(BoundOperand) and IsInteger(BoundOperand) / IsInteger(BoundExpr)), beside the bound nodes they switch over; IntrinsicResultType keeps only Resolve, CarrierFacts and IntegerRowArguments and asks the new type; every caller (the 14 and 16 files above) and every doc-comment cref (PicInfo.cs:421,641, NumericLiteral.cs:54, MoveTable16.cs:176) changes in the same wave; fold it into PB2405 so the intrinsic family moves without the general classifiers

**Finding sites (the pin's):** src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/IntrinsicResultType.cs:276-348 (IntrinsicResultType.OperandCategory); src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/IntrinsicResultType.cs:407-455 (IntrinsicResultType.IsIntegerOperand(BoundOperand)); src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/IntrinsicResultType.cs:457-472 (IntrinsicResultType.IsIntegerOperand(BoundExpr))

The finding is about this note's own target: PB2405 moves the rule families an emitter reads to compiler.bound, and the ONE operand category reader and the ONE integer-operand classifier sit inside IntrinsicResultType, so the move as written would carry two general bound-operand classifiers (24 and 28 call sites, most of them not intrinsic) into compiler.bound under an intrinsic-function name. It is recorded here and not filed apart. The members are still declared in IntrinsicResultType.cs on the tree after the pin. The sites are the pin's.
