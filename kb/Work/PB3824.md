# PB3824 — a merge made under the old drivers is judged by the new ones

**Sites:** `scripts/merge_drivers.py#is_drivers_merge` (line 548), `#merged_tree` and `#attributes_tree`;
`scripts/prune_worktrees.py#mechanical` and `#unreviewed_since` (the review stamp's and `enqueue.py land`'s coverage rule).

## Measured

`is_drivers_merge` is "exactly the merge's own definition": it recomputes the merge of the two parents with the drivers
and attributes the registry (plus the first parent's `.gitattributes`) gives TODAY. A merge of main that an in-flight branch
made BEFORE PB3727 landed used the old attributes (`merge=union` on a PublicAPI baseline that both sides changed). The
union's result differs from the set driver's, so the recomputed tree differs from the committed one and the merge reads as
hand-resolved: the reviewer must read it, though it is mechanical. The cost is bounded to branches cut before the landing, and
repeats whenever a driver is added or changed (PB3825 is the other half).

## Acceptance

- Decide and record which: (a) accept, listing the affected branches once (`prune_worktrees` survey) and stamping them by
  review; or (b) `is_drivers_merge` also accepts the tree the drivers of the commit's own first parent would have made
  (the registry as of that commit), so the rule reads the merge's own history. Say why in DESIGN-orchestrator-loop.md 16.9.
- A `merge_drivers.py --self-test` arm builds a merge under a registry without the driver and asserts the chosen answer.
