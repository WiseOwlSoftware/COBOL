# PB4885 - --std=1985 claims the high subset and all four optional modules

**The standard's terms** (ANSI X3.23-1985, paragraph 1.5, page I-6, PDF page 26). The language is 11 modules: seven required (Nucleus, Sequential
I-O, Relative I-O, Indexed I-O, Inter-Program Communication, Sort-Merge, Source Text Manipulation) and four optional (Report
Writer, Communication, Debug, Segmentation). Each module has levels. Three subsets are defined:
- the **high subset** is "all language elements of the highest level of all required modules": level 2 of each, except
  Sort-Merge, which has only level 1;
- the **intermediate subset** is level 1 of all seven required modules;
- the **minimum subset** is level 1 of Nucleus, Sequential I-O and Inter-Program Communication.
Paragraph 1.5.2: "A conforming implementation of Standard COBOL must fully support any of the three subsets ... and may include none,
all, or any combination of the levels of the optional modules." A box drawn in the text marks what is unique to level 2 (PDF
pages 23 and 147).

**The decision (owner, 2026-11-06: "yes").** `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` documents `--std=1985` as a conforming implementation of the
HIGH subset of X3.23-1985, together with every level of all four optional modules. Because the claim is the high subset, every
boxed (level-2) element must be accepted, and no level-1 restriction that a boxed rule lifts may be enforced.

**Before the claim is written** (as part of PB4850's Connect step, once the 1985 transcription exists with its boxes):
- Check each optional module's highest level against the compiler, element by element, using the reconciled 1985 file and its
  boxes. For each element that is not supported, cite an open note. Debug triggers are open now: PB4759 (cd-name), PB4810
  (data-name), PB4811 (file-name). So is the message control and communication work.
- The claim says what is supported. Every element still open is listed as an exception that names its note, until the note
  lands. A claim the compiler does not meet is not written.
- An optional subset flagger (warnings for elements above a chosen subset, the FIPS-style flagger that
  `docs/VERSION_TEST_MATRIX_DESIGN.md` lists as future strictness work) is not part of this decision. It would be a separate
  owner decision, and the 1985 box list (PB4850's `boxes.json`) is its data.
- Whether ISO/IEC 1989:2002 kept subsets and levels is checked against the 2002 text (PB4813) before anything is written
  about it.

**Sites:** `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` (whole: the 1985 conformance statement).
