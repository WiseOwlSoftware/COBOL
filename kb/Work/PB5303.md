# PB5303 - a wave's concurrency is the gate cap in force, not the cap less the holds at planning time

**Found.** PB5100 derived a wave's concurrency as the gate cap less the slots held and the tickets waiting at the moment
the planner ran. An implementer codes far longer than it gates (PB4888), so the slots held when a wave is planned say
nothing about the slots free when its gates arrive.

**Witness.** Wave 20261999 was planned at concurrency 1 (`E:\COBOL-coord\scratch\wf-args-w20261999.json`) from a busy
moment. It ran 8 compiler groups serially and starved 7 groups (about 25 notes) for 216+ minutes. Mythos's wave 20261998 was
planned at 1 for the same reason. The operator had to stop the unit and steer a re-plan at 3 (wave 20262000).

**Decision (Mythos, 2026-11-11 05:28).** The wave's concurrency is the gate pool's cap in force (3), never fewer than 1. The
cap already bounds the gate queue: the slot queue serializes the gates themselves.

**Fix.** `plan_wave.py#wave_concurrency` returns the cap in force; its printed reason says so. The `max_groups` clamp is
unchanged (it limits groups, not workers). `test_plan_wave.py` pins it: cap 3 with 2 held and 1 waiting gives 3, which the
old formula answered 1. The workstream skill, its rolling-wave and cost-law references, `wf_rolling_wave.js` and
DESIGN-test-build-ci §3.14.6 state the new rule.
