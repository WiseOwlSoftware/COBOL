⛔ **The two are different rules and this row is only the first of them.** §13.10.3 SR2 is ADMISSION — *where* a
constant-name may be written. §13.10.4 GR1 is SUBSTITUTION — *what it means* once written. [[PB614]] records
about eighteen code comments that attribute the substitution to SR2; an adjudication of this row that cites the
substitution behaviour as its evidence would be the nineteenth. **This row's evidence is about ADMISSION: does the
grammar let the word be written there at all.**

## Why the row's shape is the finding

SR2 is not a rule about one construct. Its subject is *"anywhere that a format specifies a literal"* — so a
CONFORMS verdict on it asserts something about **every literal position in every general format in the standard**,
and the two sibling admissions (§8.3.3.6.3 SR1 for figurative constants, `--check` OK; §8.3.3.6.2 Format 7 for
symbolic-characters) ride the same positions. That is a claim no hand-enumerated sample can support:
`feedback_measure_the_selectors_complement` — a survey is evidence about what it looked at, never about what it
did not. It is also the exact shape CLAUDE.md rule 5 names: where a hand-maintained list would go, a structure
belongs.

`literal` and no `dataReference`/`identifier`/`arithmeticExpression`/`cobolWord`/`unaryExpression`/`functionCall`/

count, because an alternative's sibling frequently sits on the next line. Nobody has done the rule-level pass.

## What verdict the row can carry, and when

1. **Today: not CONFORMS, and not blank either.** The honest verdict is `PARTIAL` with the two violating positions
   named in `notes` and `code-location` pointing at `CobolExpressions.g4:494` (the narrow rule) beside the nine
   files above — the row's state stays GAP, so the denominator does not move on an unfinished claim.
2. **After PB732 lands (train 19)** one violating position closes; the row is still PARTIAL, because PB778's three
   remain.
3. **After PB778 + PB568 land together** the two known holes are closed and the row becomes claimable — but
   `CONFORMS` at that moment would still be a claim about positions nobody enumerated. ⭐ **The verdict this row
   should eventually carry is one a GENERATED check defends**: the rule-level twin of the heuristic above, run as
   a drift test — *every `.g4` operand written as bare `literal` where the printed general format names a literal
   is a defect* — with a cited exemption list for the positions where the format writes something other than
   `literal` (`character-1` is the exemplar, per [[PB568]] and §13.18.40.4 GR14). Then `test-ref` names the drift
   test rather than a sample of programs, and the CONFORMS is defended against the next grammar edit rather than
   against the last one. `feedback_green_gates_arent_evidence`: prove the check red on `CobolData.g4:451` before
   the fix lands, and green after.
4. **`editions`**: the EDITING phrase is a COBOL-2023 introduction (`SR-13.18.40.3-8`'s adjudication records
   Annex E.3.3 item 19), but §13.10.3 itself and the VALUE positions apply from 2002 — the constant entry is not a
   2023 feature. So the row's editions are `2002,2014,2023` for the clause and the 2023-only EDITING hole is a
   position inside it, not a per-edition split of the rule.
