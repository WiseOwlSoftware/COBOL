# PB1446 — OPEN

## The rule (every citation re-run by the registrar through `cite.py --check`)

> The decimal point is treated as an assumed decimal point, and may appear anywhere within the literal except as the rightmost character

`python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 8.3.3.3.2 "…"` → `OK  §8.3.3.3.2 3)  (Fixed-point numeric literals)`

## Refuter evidence — row `GR-8.3.3.3.2-4` (PARTIAL)

PARTIAL — The value half fails in one operand shape: under DECIMAL-POINT IS COMMA a leftmost-comma literal preceded by a space and an integer (`1 ,5`) is valued as 1.5, the value of characters OUTSIDE the literal, instead of 0.5. Sign, scale, size counting (leading zeros, trailing fraction zeros), the 18/31-digit edition ceilings and the integer-literal refusals were all re-probed and hold.

**Code site.** src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/CodeGen/Emit/EmitCore.cs#EmitText.TryUnscaledParts; src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Common/NumericLiteral.cs#NumericLiteral.Normalize; src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Binding/EditionContext.cs#EditionContext.CheckDigitCapacity; src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Binding/IntrinsicResultType.cs#IntrinsicResultType.IsIntegerOperand

## Refuter evidence — row `SR-8.3.3.3.2-3` (PARTIAL)

PARTIAL — The one-point and not-rightmost halves hold for both '.' and ',' (including `(1.)`, `1.+ 2`, `(1,)`). But the 'anywhere, including leftmost' half fails for the comma under DECIMAL-POINT IS COMMA: the leftmost-comma literal `,5` is not kept as its own literal when a space and an integer come before it. The parser fuses `1 ,5` into an interior-point literal 1,5 (value 1.5), so a wrong answer is produced and illegal source is accepted.

**Code site.** src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolLexer.g4#DEC_BODY; src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolExpressions.g4#numericLiteralCore; src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/CodeGen/Emit/EmitCore.cs#EmitText.TryUnscaledParts

## What the fixer owes

Find where DECIMAL-POINT IS COMMA re-lexes the comma (the DPC token re-classification) and make it respect the separator: a comma preceded by a space begins a NEW character-string (§8.3.5), so `1 ,5` is two operands (or a separator-comma + `5` — derive which from §8.3.5 before fixing). Every other shape the adjudicator probed holds (one point, not-rightmost, leftmost `,5`/`+,5`, 31-digit ceilings); pin them with a golden next to the fix. Check PB1230's constant-entry normalization against the same spelling.
