# PB4769 — a prerequisite owes the harm of the note it unblocks

**What was wrong.** `work.actionable` (`next`) lists open, unblocked notes that carry a harm flag and are not
`process_only`. The fix lane's population (`clusters.ready_notes_view` handed to `fix_clusters.py`, which skips
`.agent-fleet.json`'s `skip_flag: process_only`) reads the same stored flags. A prerequisite has no harm of its own: it
changes nothing a user sees until its dependent lands. The MCS slices of PB4700 show the effect. PB4734 (the 85/2002
engine entry) and PB4739 (the 2023 platform-assumption tests) are `process_only: true`. Yet PB4736 (a wrong answer: the
`mcs.lock` flock hole) waits on PB4734 and PB4735, and PB4743/PB4744 (rejected legal source) wait, through PB4740 to
PB4742, on PB4739. Neither slice appeared in `next` and neither was planned, so every harmed note behind them would have
waited for ever. The operator found it on 2026-11-05 when wave 20261875 took PB4740 and left the other two ready slices.

**As landed.** `work.inherited_harm(items)` is the one reading: for every open, half or `status: blocked` note with a
harm flag (not `owner`: the owner has not decided that work yet; not `process_only`), it walks `blocked_by` through the
open blockers and gives each one the dependent's harm flags (kind upstream inherits nothing).
`with_inherited_harm` applies the result, setting the flags, `process_only: false` and `harm_inherited`. `actionable` and
`view_notes` read the notes through it, so `unharmed` stays the exact complement of `next`.
`clusters.ready_notes_view` copies a ready prerequisite into the planner's view with the owed flags written into the
copy's frontmatter (`owing`). The register's own file is never edited: the harm is derived, never stored.

**Effect on the register at landing.** Nine notes owe harm, all in the MCS chain (PB4734, PB4735, PB4739 to PB4745). The
two that are ready, PB4734 and PB4739, join `next` (17 to 19 actionable), and the fix lane plans them.

**Witnesses.** `work.py --self-test` covers a process_only note blocking a crash, which joins `next` and leaves
`unharmed`; a `blocked` harmed note's prerequisite, which joins; and an `owner` one's, which does not.
`test_plan_wave.py` (538 checks) covers a chain's ready root, which joins the view owing the harm; a waiting link and a
dependent-less process_only note, which do not; and the register's file, which is untouched. The docs that define `next`
(`docs/DOC_INDEX.md`, the session-start skill) say so.
