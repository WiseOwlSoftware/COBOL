# PB3570 — a numeric literal moved to a group: its characters, sign included

## What the fuzz run saw

`SIGN` signature, 6 programs, first fz00177: `MOVE -456 TO group` stores the literal's characters, `-45`. This is kb/Work PB1113's
determination, also used by CALL (BY CONTENT to a group formal) and INVOKE.

### The probe (the B1 adjudicator's, verbatim: `adj\B1\probes\litgrp\litgrp.cob`)

```cobol
IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
PROGRAM-ID. LITGRP.
DATA DIVISION.
WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
01 G.
   05 G1 PIC X(3).
01 E PIC X(3).
01 N PIC S99 VALUE -15.
PROCEDURE DIVISION.
MAIN-PARA.
    MOVE -15 TO G
    DISPLAY "LIT-GROUP [" G "]"
    MOVE -15 TO E
    DISPLAY "LIT-ELEM  [" E "]"
    MOVE N TO G
    DISPLAY "ITEM-GROUP [" G "]"
    MOVE N TO E
    DISPLAY "ITEM-ELEM  [" E "]"
    STOP RUN.
```

Output on judge e7cf38ce4: `LIT-GROUP [-15]`, `LIT-ELEM  [15 ]`, `ITEM-GROUP [1N ]`, `ITEM-ELEM  [15 ]`.

## The rules (each re-run through `cite.py --check`, OK)

- §14.9.25.4 4): a move to a group "is treated exactly as if it were an alphanumeric to alphanumeric elementary move, except
  that there is no conversion of data from one form of internal representation to another". An `S99` sender sends its overpunch
  image (`1N`).
- §14.9.25.4 6) (OK): "When an alphanumeric, alphanumeric-edited, national, or national-edited data item is a receiving operand,
  alignment and any necessary space filling shall take place as defined in 14.6.8 …"; its item a) drops the operational sign for
  an elementary receiver, as the `PIC X` line shows.
- §14.9.25.4 4) gives a numeric literal no internal representation to preserve, so the adjudicator recorded LATITUDE: the
  standard does not say what a literal sent to a group stores.

## Why it is a decision

The determination (characters, sign included) is not in `docs/CONFORMANCE.md`. CLAUDE.md rule 1 says that, where the spec leaves
latitude, the precedence is the ISO spec, then GnuCOBOL, then IBM or Micro Focus; GnuCOBOL drops the sign (as WiseOwl does for an
elementary `PIC X` receiver). The choice is an owner question, to be asked by the session, not decided by the clerk. Whatever is
decided, the rule belongs in CONFORMANCE.md with a golden.

## Owner question (bare, wording by the clerk from the sources)

Should a numeric literal moved to a group keep its sign character (today's PB1113 determination, `MOVE -15 TO G` stores `-15`),
or drop the sign as GnuCOBOL does and as WiseOwl does for an elementary alphanumeric receiver?

## Owner decision (2026-10-23)

Drop the sign, as GnuCOBOL does (AskUserQuestion, attended Mythos session). A numeric literal sent to a group item stores its digits
only: `MOVE -456 TO G` stores `456` in the first three positions. Rule 1's precedence for implementor latitude applies: §14.9.25.4 4)
(`cite.py --check 14.9.25.4` → OK 4)) gives a literal no internal representation to preserve, the standard is silent on what a literal
sent to a group stores, and GnuCOBOL drops the sign. PB1113's determination (the literal's characters, sign included) is superseded.
An elementary `PIC X` receiver already drops the sign by §14.9.25.4 6) a) (`--check 14.9.25.4` → OK 6)), so the group move now
agrees with it.

## Fix

- Site: `src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/CodeGen/Emit/OperandText.cs` `NonElementaryMoveSender`, the literal arm: today the fallthrough
  `_ => AsString(op, num, deSign: false)` keeps the sign for a numeric literal; the literal arm sends the digits only. The same
  channel serves CALL BY CONTENT to a group formal (`CallEmitter.cs`) and INVOKE, so the one change covers all three; the
  implementer sweeps every caller of `NonElementaryMoveSender` and every INVOKE argument path for a sibling that renders a literal
  with its sign.
- Golden to require (expected values from the rule above, not copied output): `MOVE -456 TO G`, `MOVE +456 TO G` and `MOVE 456 TO G`
  (an unsigned literal) each store `456`; a decimal literal (`MOVE -4.56 TO G`) stores its digits only, decimal point and sign
  dropped, per the same documented rule (the implementer confirms the digit string against GnuCOBOL's behavior and records it); and the
  CALL BY CONTENT to a group formal and INVOKE siblings with a signed literal. Keep the PIC X receiver line (`MOVE -15 TO E` gives `15 `)
  and the item-to-group line (`MOVE N TO G` sends the overpunch image `1N`) unchanged, as the contrast in the probe above.
- `docs/CONFORMANCE.md`: write the row WITH the fix, not now: the group-move latitude (a numeric literal sent to a group stores its
  digits only), the §14.9.25.4 4) citation, and the GnuCOBOL precedence.
- Delete the PB1113 sign-keeping assumption wherever it is written in the emitter comments in the same change (no shim).

## Landed (2026-10-24, wave 20261024 group C) — the digits only, through the one sending codec

**Re-probe (own build, Debug).** The note's probe printed `LIT-GROUP [-15]`, `LIT-ELEM  [15 ]`, `ITEM-GROUP [1N ]`, `ITEM-ELEM  [15 ]`
as filed; `MOVE -456 TO G` stored `-45`, `MOVE +456 TO G` `+45` and `MOVE -4.56 TO G` `-4.`. The CALL lane (`CALL ... AS NESTED USING BY
CONTENT -456` into a plain-group formal) and the INVOKE lane stored the same signed text (`[-456 ]`, `[-4.56]`).

**Fix.** `OperandText.NonElementaryMoveSender` gained an arm for `BoundNumericLiteral { FunctionValue: false }`: the digits of the
literal as written, no sign, no decimal point (`LiteralDigits`; a floating-point literal sends the digits of its SIGNIFICAND as
written: `1.5E+3` sends 15, as cobc 3.2.0 does - review R1-1 corrected the first landing, which expanded the exponent). A function value folded to a literal keeps its signed rendering, like
its unfolded twin (a numeric function's value sent to a group is a separate question, filed as a lead in the report). The three
channels that send a literal into a group now read that one arm: MOVE (unchanged callers), `CallEmitter`'s known-group-formal literal
arm (it spelled `CsLiteral(n.Text)` itself) and `OoEmitter`'s group arm, whose binder used to hand a numeric literal to a group formal
through the string channel (`BoundInvokeArg.StringLiteral = numLit.Text`); it now rides the numeric channel for every formal and the
emitter builds the operand. D-B4 in `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` records the determination, the GnuCOBOL precedence and what it does not
cover; the PB1113 sign-keeping comments in `CallEmitter`, `OoBinder` and `OoEmitter` are rewritten (no shim: the old spelling is gone).

**Goldens.** `wiseowl/cases/14.9.25-MOVE/pb3570_numeric_literal_group_move` (all four editions: the behavior is the same at 85),
`wiseowl/cases/14.2-Procedure-division-structure/pb3570_numeric_literal_group_formal` (CALL AS NESTED and INVOKE, 2002+) and its
negative `..._below_2002` (refused at 85, COBOLNET0900). Drift pins: `GroupMoveSenderChannelDriftTests`.

**For the next implementer.** `src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/CodeGen/Emit/OperandText.cs#NonElementaryMoveSender` owns what any channel sends into a plain group: the numeric-literal arm (`LiteralDigits`) is the digits rule, the last arm reads the usage codec `RepresentationOfText`; reuse them, never render a literal for a group with `CsLiteral(text)`. `CallEmitter.cs#ArgCarrierText`'s `BoundNumericLiteral when a.Formal is { IsGroup: true }` arm and `OoEmitter.cs` (the group arm of the INVOKE argument chain) are the two other readers. The trap: a group formal that is a bit or national GROUP (GROUP-USAGE) is an elementary item for the MOVE and takes `MoveEmitter.RecordValue`, while `01 X USAGE NATIONAL. 05 ...` is a plain group. A numeric FUNCTION value sent to a group still keeps its signed rendering (open question, see the wave report).
