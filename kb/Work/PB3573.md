# PB3573

## Scenario

Review of PB3513 (PR #432), lead R1-1 (low, from `E:\COBOL-coord\scratch\reports\w1157f-PB3513-report.md`).

`src/Cobol.Net.Runtime/Values/Numeric/CobolNum.DeviceDecimal.cs` `ParseDeviceDecimal` (branch `worktree-wf_37d4041f-6f7-6`,
about line 244 at review) counts every integer digit up front, then drops the high-order surplus
(`skip = count - integerCap`) from the front of the run. A non-digit that precedes a dropped digit is rejected, while
GnuCOBOL 3.2.0, whose rules the doc comment says the reader reproduces exactly, skips the non-digit while dropping the
surplus digits. The accepted text differs for a window such as a junk character ahead of an over-wide digit run.

## Fix shape

Probe GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 (cobc under WSL) with a window carrying a non-digit before a surplus high-order digit, make the
reader skip it the same way, and add a golden. Per rule 1 the precedence is ISO, then GnuCOBOL: the ISO spec leaves the
ACCEPT conversion of invalid text implementor-defined, so GnuCOBOL decides. Land after PR #432 (PB3513).

**Sites:** src/Cobol.Net.Runtime/Values/Numeric/CobolNum.DeviceDecimal.cs:102 (ParseDeviceDecimal); tests/Cobol.Net.Tests.Unit/NumericDisplayDecimalTests.cs (Accept_skips_a_non_digit_that_stands_ahead_of_a_dropped_high_order_digit, Accept_skips_a_non_digit_ahead_of_a_dropped_digit_in_a_binary_item_only_past_its_wide_first_stage); tests/Cobol.Net.Tests.Conformance/AcceptDifferentialTests.cs (Device_NumericReceiver_SkipsANonDigitAheadOfADroppedHighOrderDigit); docs/CONFORMANCE.md (DOC-A.1-1).

## Landed (wave 20261500, 2026-10-25)

**Re-probe.** Reproduced on the wave's own build with `cobc -x -std=default` against `cobol.exe` over one program
(`PIC 9(2)V99` given `Y123`, `PIC S9(2)V99 SIGN LEADING SEPARATE` given `-X123`, `PIC S9(3)V99 COMP` and `COMP-3` given a
non-digit and 39 integer digits): GnuCOBOL gave 23.00, -23.00, +678.00, +678.50 where WiseOwl COBOL gave zero for every one.
The note named the character-form arm; the binary and packed arm had the same defect behind a wider threshold.

**Mechanism.** GnuCOBOL's `cob_move_alphanum_to_display` counts the integer digits, then advances over CHARACTERS until the
surplus digits are consumed, so a non-digit ahead of the last dropped digit is never examined. A character-form item
converts into itself (surplus = digits past its own integer positions); a binary or packed item converts through an
intermediate 38 integer digits wide (measured: `X` plus 38 digits is invalid, `X` plus 39 is accepted and keeps the low
digits), then the move truncates the high-order end. `ParseDeviceDecimal` now has the same two stages: the first-stage
capacity is the item's integer positions for a character-form item and `DeviceReadDigits` for the others, the surplus
characters are skipped by position, and the second stage cuts the integer to the item's positions.

**Sweep.** `ParseDeviceDecimal` is the only reader of the decimal device form; the `digits` form (`ParseDisplay`) never
rejects a character, and the emitter has no second conversion. The NATIONAL arm shares the character-form path.

**For a successor.** `CobolNum.DeviceDecimal.cs#ParseDeviceDecimal` owns the rule; the trap is that the first-stage
capacity differs by byte form, so a "skip the junk" fix that ignores it would accept `12X345` into a COMP item, which
GnuCOBOL refuses.
