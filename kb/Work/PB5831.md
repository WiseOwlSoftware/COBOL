# PB5831 - a sixth derivation arm: unfalsifiable under a documented determination

**Sites:** `tests/version-matrix/inventory-schema.json` (`derivation.arms`);
`scripts/spec/inventory_schema.py#Derivation._arm_refusals`;
`tests/WiseOwl.Cobol.Tests.RepoDrift/SpecTraceabilityInventoryDriftTests.cs#DerivationStands` and the parity
fixture; `docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md` section 1.1 (the table of arms and the list of
refusals).

## The case no arm states

`§1985:VI.5.13.4 GR3` says SYNCHRONIZED LEFT "specifies that the elementary item is to be positioned such that it
will begin at the left character position of the natural boundary in which the elementary item is placed", and GR4
says the same of SYNCHRONIZED RIGHT and the right position. The 1985 text ties natural boundaries to the computer
(`§1985:VI.5.13.1`: "alignment of an elementary item on the natural boundaries of the computer memory") and names
byte boundaries among them (`§1985:IV.4.3.7`). Which boundary is appropriate for an item it leaves to the
implementor: `§1985:VI.5.13.4 GR8b` speaks of an item that "does not terminate at an appropriate natural boundary",
and GR8 calls the clause hardware dependent. WiseOwl COBOL's determination, documented in the conformance register
(`docs/conformance/7/DOC-A.1-197.md`), is that every byte position is a natural boundary.

Under that determination every placement of an item begins at the left position, and ends on the right position,
of a one-character boundary. No behaviour of a compiler can make GR3 or GR4 false, a wrong one included. So no test
can fail on either rule.

Two independent refuters tried to write the witness and could not, and both found the stated facts true. Both also
held that the arm drafted for the rows does not state the case:
- `unpopulatable-antecedent` claims the rule's antecedent is false in every reachable state. Here the antecedent
  (SYNCHRONIZED LEFT is written) is reachable; what is unreachable is a state that makes the consequent false.
- `indistinguishable-consequent` naming GR1 was tried next and refused: GR1 is broken by an overlap of items and
  GR3 is not, so a program can tell the two apart.
- `definitional` is not open: the rule's subject is the implementation.

## The decision

A sixth arm, `unfalsifiable-under-determination`.

| | |
|---|---|
| What it claims | A term of the rule is the implementor's to settle (the standard says so), this implementation's determination of that term is documented in the conformance register, and under that meaning of the term alone the rule's requirement is true of every state a compiler could produce, so no program can fail on it. |
| Names cell | The register key of the determination, alone: a section 7 item (`DOC-A.1-<n>`) or a section 3 determination (`D-<NAME>`). |
| How it is checked | REVIEWED ARGUMENT, shape-checked: the key must be a row of the conformance register (the reader the DOC anchors already use; no second parser). The argument has a reader: it is an argued arm, so a `claude:` signature needs an UPHELD refuter record (kb/Work PB5373). |
| What the argument must carry | The words that leave the term to the implementor, cited; the determination of the term, quoted from the register; why every behaviour satisfies the rule under that meaning alone; and that the derivation is CONTINGENT on it. |

**The bound that keeps it from closing a rule that merely holds.** Every rule a correct compiler implements is true
in every state that compiler reaches. That is not this arm. The test is whether ANY behaviour, a defective one
included, could make the rule false while the MEANING OF THE TERM stands. Only the term's meaning is held fixed:
the determination may fix what the term means, never the behaviour under test. A register entry often states
behaviour beside the term (DOC-A.1-197 says every byte position is a natural boundary, and also that the compiler
"adds no slack bytes"); the behaviour half is not held fixed, or the arm would admit every rule the entry promises
to keep. `§1985:VI.5.13.4 GR8b` (implicit FILLER) is the example the arm's refuter gave: holding "adds no slack
bytes" fixed would make it unbreakable, yet a compiler that added slack would break it and the case `g85_f09_02`
witnesses it. If a broken compiler could violate the rule under the term's meaning alone, a program can witness it,
and the row takes a test. The refuter's job on this arm is to describe such a behaviour.

**Why a new arm and not a wider old one.** Section 1.1 gives one fact one arm, and each arm's check reads what its
claim names. Widening `unpopulatable-antecedent` to "no reachable state falsifies the consequent" would make its
Names cell state two different kinds of closed set, and would drop the one thing this case can check mechanically:
that the determination it rests on exists in the register.

## What this does not decide
- The two rows are recorded by a registrar, on an UPHELD refuter record under this arm, after this lands.
- Whether other rows fit. None is claimed here.
