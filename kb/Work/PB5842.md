# PB5842 - three gaps of the code-read store the first full import met

**Sites:** `scripts/spec/code_reads.py#reader_problems` (the citations of a read), `#main` (the commands), and its
`plan`; `scripts/spec/code_read_store.py`; `scripts/selftests/spec/code_reads/test_code_reads.py`;
`docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md` section 10.6.1.

## What happened
A registrar imported the 185 slices of the 1985 code reads (1,106 rows) on 2026-11-16.

1. **Six slices were refused whole** (`1985-013`, `-035`, `-042`, `-089`, `-114`, `-153`: 36 rows, eleven of them
   rows the rulings then could not be recorded on). In each, one read cites a rule of 2023 or 2002 beside the
   1985 rule, for example `1985-GR-VI.5.4.4-2: citation '§2023:13.18.8.4 GR3' is of 2023, not of the rule's own
   text ([1985])`. A read whose defect is the same at 2023 (the readers' cause "b") has every reason to cite the
   2023 rule, and its record even has an `at-2023` field for it. The importer admits only the rule's own edition
   (and the edition its catalog citation names, and the 1989 amendment).
2. **Two imported reads name a code location that does not resolve in the tree**, and the gate's
   `SpecTraceabilityInventoryDriftTests.EveryCodeReadLocation_ResolvesInTheTree` is red on them:
   `1985-GR-VIII.2.3.4-5` (`FileControlBinder.cs#Bind`: the member is no longer in that file) and
   `1985-SR-XI.4.1.3-2` (a path that does not exist). A read is held verbatim and no command removes one, so the
   branch cannot be made green without editing a read by hand, which the store forbids.
3. **The planner lists rows that need no code read.** After the import `plan --edition 1985` still owes 253 rows;
   17 of them are chained rows closed on a derivation of their own (kb/Work PB5822): a rule with no observable
   obligation, already adjudicated at its own edition by an argued and refuted derivation.

## The decisions (the Mythos lane, 2026-11-16)
1. **A third acceptance.** A citation of ANOTHER held edition is accepted in a read. It is parsed and checked
   against its own edition's text exactly as a citation of the rule's edition is (a citation that does not check is
   still refused). A read must still carry at least one citation of the rule's own edition, of the edition its
   catalog citation names, or of the 1989 amendment: a read that cites only another edition judged nothing by its
   own text (owner 2026-11-14: "2002 spec covers 2002 rules") and is refused, naming the rule.
2. **A command that withdraws a read:** `withdraw --edition YEAR RULE --reason TEXT`. It removes the rule's
   record through the store's writer and prints what it removed; the row is then owed a read again and `plan`
   lists it. It refuses a rule with no record and an empty reason. It is for a read that is defective as a read (a
   location that resolves nowhere), never for a read someone disagrees with: that is `adjudicate`.
3. **`plan` leaves out a chained row whose own derivation stands** (the one predicate, `lineage.derivation_refusals`,
   through the state the view computes: a KEPT row that is OK with no test-ref of its own). Such a row is not owed
   a read, and the view's count of rows owed follows `plan`.

## What the fix carries
Planted tests that could each fail: a read citing its own edition and another one is imported and both
citations are checked; a read citing only another edition is refused; another-edition citation that does not check
is refused; `withdraw` removes a record, lists the row in `plan` again, refuses an unknown rule and an empty
reason; `plan` omits a derivation-closed chained row and keeps a chained row whose derivation does not stand.
Section 10.6.1 states the three (the acceptances become three; the commands gain `withdraw`; the planner's rule).
