# PB2350 — the token-stream and parse-tree differential (DESIGN-architecture-review §4 item 3)

**Wave kind:** instrument (not a restructuring wave; kind analysis, like PB2116). **Model:** Opus.

**Not yet built.** DESIGN-architecture-review §4 says item 3 is the next step: the token-stream and parse-tree differential for grammar changes, whose first consumers are PB2113 and PB2114. It needs a capture of the lexer's token stream and the parse tree's shape over the same population. That is a second observation per case on the same enumerator the PB2116 oracle uses (a `tokens` mode of the host), not a second population.

**What to build:** the PB1715 token-stream form (kb/Work PB1715: a replay to identical token streams, comparing type, channel, offsets, line, column, text, errors and mode) plus a parse-tree shape differential, over every lexer and parser input the suites use, with the comparison script a grammar wave runs and a drift test that pins the enumerator and the recorded baseline's well-formedness, as PB2116 does for the emitted-C# oracle.

**Why it is the gate of every grammar wave** (§8.4, "Kept, each with its pin"): the token-stream and parse-tree differential is the oracle of EVERY grammar wave, so no grammar wave runs before it exists (§8.7: "A grammar wave needs §4 item 3 built"). Its waiting customers: PB2143 (DISPLAY's inline sending set, blocked by 83 differing error trees; the differential's first customer, landing only with it), PB2333 (the `CobolParserCoreBase` predicate split, a grammar wave), the grammar waves PB2113 and PB2114 name as consumers, and PB2147's corpus sweep, whose input population becomes the differential's (§8.4).

**Blocked by:** PB2118 (§8 binds nothing until the owner approves it). The notes that name this one in `blocked_by` (R1 Draft 3): PB2333 (the predicate split), PB2410 (the grammar regroup, with its "no rule name in two fragments" assertion) and the grammar defects PB2143 (its first customer, landing with it), PB2144, PB2145 and PB2146.
