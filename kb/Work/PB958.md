# PB958 — OPEN — one character, in the clause a fixer reads to derive a bound

## Measured

```
$ grep -n "π" specs/ISO_COBOL.md
30076:1\) The returned value is the approximation of the arccosine of argument-1 and is greater than or equal to zero and less than or equal to π.
30178:1\) The returned value is the approximation of the arctangent of argument-1 and is greater than **−**π/2 and less than +π/2.
32863:The PI function returns a value that is an approximation of π, …

$ grep -n -- "-p/2" specs/ISO_COBOL.md
30148:1\) The returned value is the approximation of the arcsine of argument-1 and is greater than or equal to –p/2 and less than or equal to +p/2.
```

Three occurrences of π in the whole transcription, and ASIN's is not one of them. §15.8.4 (ACOS, `π`) and
§15.11.4 (ATAN, `π/2`) bracket §15.10.4 (ASIN, `p/2`) within a hundred lines, so the convention is the
transcription's own, measured on the two neighbours. The printed standard cannot be re-rendered here to
confirm — `specs-private` is not initialised in this worktree (`git submodule status` → `-1dc65bb…`) — but the
arcsine codomain is [−π/2, +π/2] and both siblings spell it, so the reading is not in doubt; the RENDER is
still owed by whoever repairs it (CLAUDE.md rule 1).

## Why it matters more than a typo

§15.10.4 rule 1 is the clause a fixer reads to derive ASIN's expected value — [[PB952]] is a live example, and
its returned-value analysis had to quote this line. A bound written `p/2` reads as an identifier, not a
constant: a reader who takes it literally derives a bound from a data-name that does not exist, and a reader
who silently "corrects" it has re-derived the standard from memory, which is the one thing the transcription
exists to prevent. `feedback_fix_transcription_errors_immediately` — the repair is owed on discovery, and the
reason it is a NOTE rather than a one-character edit in this pass is the coupling below, which was measured,
not assumed.

## ⛔ THE REPAIR IS NOT A HAND EDIT — it moves a rule-digest under a live verdict

`scripts/spec/build_inventory.py:44` computes `rule_digest(text)` per catalog rule, and `:115-127` is explicit
about what a changed digest means:

```python
    drifted = [r["rule-id"] for r in out
               if (p := prior.get(r["rule-id"])) and p.get("rule-digest")
               and p["rule-digest"] != r["rule-digest"] and any(r[k] for k in CARRIED)]
    …  "⛔ n CARRIED VERDICT(S) LANDED ON CHANGED RULE TEXT — re-adjudicate each through a record_verdicts batch"
```

`RV-15.10.4-1` is adjudicated, so repairing the character changes its digest and the rebuild reports a carried
verdict standing on text that is no longer there. The change set is therefore: repair the transcription →
`extract_rule_catalog.py` → `build_inventory.py` → a `record_verdicts.py` batch that re-states
`RV-15.10.4-1`'s verdict against the repaired text (the verdict itself does not change — π/2 and p/2 bound the
same set of reals; what changes is which text it was adjudicated against) → the drift/inventory Unit leg.
⚠ Whitespace and markdown escapes are already squashed out of the digest (`_SQUASH`), so no other row moves.

## The sibling sweep the fixer owes

Every mathematical symbol the OCR could have flattened to a Latin letter, not just π — the sweep is over the
clauses that state numeric codomains and constants (§15.8, §15.10, §15.11, §15.27 E, §15.73 PI, and the
arithmetic-expression clauses of §8.8.1). [[PB291]] is the standing note for the same class one level down (107
