# PB3380 — a successor merges its predecessor before the predecessor's review is done, and nothing merges the fixes forward

**Landed (2026-10-18, by PB3381, PR #270, on main at de7082e19).** The rolling wave no longer starts a successor from
its predecessor's implementer-DONE head. A successor waits until the predecessor is SETTLED (its review loop ended: CLEAN,
FINDINGS, STOPPED, NO-RESULT or STALE, or it returned unfinished), then merges the predecessor's FINAL branch (the review
finisher's, when one ran: the reviewed head). Every link's review fixes therefore reach every later link, and the
predecessor is no longer held to land through its successor: reviewed CLEAN, it lands at once. The workstream
`SKILL.md` "Same-file successors" bullet states the new rule and carries no chain caveat.
`scripts/orchestrator/test_rolling_wave.py` case 2 pins one link (the successor waits for the whole review loop and
merges the review finisher's branch). The three-link chain test this note asked for was not added; each link's merge is
the same step. The double answer of an UNANSWERED predecessor's findings that PB3381's review left open is PB3404.

Filed by the operator (account 2) at the Mythos lane's request, 2026-10-18 09:05 PDT. The Mythos lane's evidence is
`E:\COBOL-coord\scratch\w1118\MYTHOS-stop-w1118-20261018-0903.md`. Grepped `kb/Work` for `successor` with
`predecessor` and `review`. PB3067 and PB3267 are the branch SURVEY's side: a successor that contains a FINDINGS

pre-review head.

**The stated design** (`.claude/skills/workstream/templates/wf_rolling_wave.js`, the SAME-FILE SUCCESSORS comment
above `held`):
- A group with `after:` runs once its predecessor has returned, merges the predecessor's branch, and orients from its
  report.
- "The predecessor's branch is then HELD from landing and lands through the successor's branch, which contains it (so
  the successor's review, over origin/main...HEAD, reviews it too)."

**What wave 1118 did** (the Mythos lane's PB3330 engine core, `wf_14a1e62b-326`: A PB3331 → A2 PB3332 → A3 PB3333 →
A4 … as one `blocked_by` chain):
1. A returned DONE at `a01f381d5`. A2 started at once and merged A at `a01f381d5`.
2. A's branch was reviewed anyway, on A's own branch, with review finishers:
   - r1: 5 findings, fixed by `7ee8d408d`;
   - r2: 2 findings, fixed by `c336ab17f`;
   - r3: 1 low finding, at the round cap.
3. None of those fixes reached A2: A2 returned DONE at `0614ebe91`, built on A's original head.
4. A3 merged A2 at `0614ebe91` the same way. A4 had started.
5. A2's own review found the gap ("the branch does not carry PB3331's branch-review fixes", high), plus two defects of
   its own.
6. The lane stopped the wave at 09:03 and re-sequences one link at a time by hand.

**The mechanism** (read from the code by the operator, 2026-10-18 09:30):
1. `worker()` reviews a finished group only `if (!hasSuccessorQueued(g.letter))`. A predecessor with a queued
   successor is put in `held` unreviewed (`onResult`), as the comment says.
2. When the successor returns, `onResult` runs
   `if (g.after && held[g.after]) { … if (!landable(r)) await landAlone(p) }`.
   `landable(r)` needs `review === 'CLEAN'`. A successor that is itself HELD (A2, with A3 queued) was never reviewed,
   so it is never landable at this point.
3. So at every middle link, `landAlone` reviews and fixes the predecessor on the predecessor's own branch (A's r1-r3).
   Meanwhile the held successor still carries the predecessor's unreviewed head, and the next successor merges that.
   The fallback cannot tell "the successor failed" from "the successor is held for its own successor".
4. The public base's `skills/agent-fleet/references/rolling-wave.js` (brent-tools 1.22.0) does not have this defect.
   It starts a successor only after its predecessor's review loop has ended (CLEAN, FINDINGS at the round cap, or a
   review or finisher that did not finish), merges the predecessor's FINAL head and tells the successor how the review
   ended. Nothing is held, so no middle link falls back to a predecessor-alone landing and no successor builds on an
   unreviewed head.

**Harm.**
- In a pair, the successor's reviewer catches the gap and its finisher merges the fixed predecessor: one extra round.
- In a chain, every later reviewer restates every unfixed predecessor finding.
- A later finisher that runs before the predecessor's finisher returns fixes the same defect independently. That
  gives divergent fixes on two branches, a conflict at the next merge, and a re-review of all the merged content at
  every link.
- The cost grows with chain depth, and every campaign `blocked_by` chain produces exactly this shape.

**The bar for the fix.** One model, stated in the script and pinned by a test. Either:
- a successor starts only from the predecessor's REVIEWED head: the wave waits for the predecessor's review loop
  before releasing the successor; or
- the predecessor is held unreviewed, as the comment says, and reviewed only through the successor, with no
  independent review loop on the held branch.

Whichever is chosen, `plan_wave.py` plans a chain deeper than a pair as successive waves, one link each with its landing
between, or proves the in-wave form converges. A test drives a three-link chain through a FINDINGS review on the first
link and checks that the third link's branch carries the first link's fixes.
