# PB5675 - one own-edition evidence rule for every older edition

**The owner's words.** 2026-11-14, on 2002: "2002 spec covers 2002 rules. It should not matter to the 2002 rules whether or not 2023 removes/adds/changes a clause. That only affects 2023 rules". 2026-11-15 about 09:55 PST: "Note that the same argument for the 2002 rules applies equally to the 1985 rules." The same morning, the model: "Conceptually, each version's rules, syntax, and semantics are independent. However, as the language designers needed to maintain some level of compatibility, unless explicitly decides not, later version rules can be thought of as including a prior version's construct as is, including with additions, deletions, and alterations, explicitly removing the prior version construct, or adding an entirely new construct."

**What is wrong today.** PB5597 made a row of an older edition close only on evidence produced at its own edition, and put the rule behind a per-edition switch (`own-edition-evidence` in `tests/version-matrix/inventory-schema.json`, read as `inventory_schema.Edition.own_evidence`), set for 2002 alone, so that the 1985 records written before the rule were not refused while they were being measured. The inventory therefore counts 1985 by a weaker rule than 2002: a 1985 row recorded KEPT is closed whenever its 2023 targets are closed. Measured on main 97f1e70b45 (`lineage_reads.py own-edition --edition 1985`, a static reading of the suite's case headers and the inventory): of 1,373 stored KEPT records of 1985, 570 have a witness of the kind the rule needs at `--std 85` for every target, 232 for some targets and not all, 571 for none. The probe's "1985: 32 GAP" is true only under the weaker rule.

**Decision (the Mythos lane, under the delegation of 2026-11-10; the owner's sentences above are its ground).**
1. There is ONE evidence rule and no edition names it: the test the 2002 path applies today (`lineage_reads.kept_problems`: the record's targets, EACH witnessed at the row's own edition by a suite case of the kind the rule needs) applies to every edition that inherits rows. The switch, its schema key, `Edition.own_evidence` and every branch on it are deleted (CLAUDE.md rule 4: no second path).
2. A stored KEPT record that fails the evidence part of that test is not a closed row: its derived state is GAP, at every edition, computed in the one place a row's state is derived, and the views count such rows separately from the rows that never had a verdict (`inventory_view.py --summary`, the session probe's `invent` line, the generated burn-down).
3. The writer refuses a NEW KEPT record that fails the test, at every edition.
4. No stored record is deleted and no lineage target is thrown away by this change: the targets are the pointers the 1985 read and the band widening need.
5. The gate stays green on the stored 1985 records at the landing, and an evidence LOSS stays loud at every edition: a row that is closed at a landing's base and is no longer closed at its head, with no recorded reason, fails a check; it never just adds one to the GAP.
6. A drift test keeps it true for the next edition: a schema entry cannot carry an evidence switch, and a synthetic older edition gets the rule by construction.

**What the figures become.** 2023 and 2002 do not move (every 2002 KEPT record was written under the rule). 1985 goes from 32 GAP to 32 plus the stored KEPT rows that fail the test: 571 fail it for every target and 232 more for at least one, so about 835 if the 232 fail as the rule is written; the landing states the measured figure. This is the count becoming true, not a regression, and the plan, the DEVLOG entry and this note say so in those words.

**What the opened rows need (the facts file's count by cause, for the 571 with no witness at all).** 327 have a case whose band excludes 85 and could be widened; 11 a refusal test that could be widened; 90 have a best case that cites a rule which does not exist at 85; 85 have only a refusal or flag at 85 where the rule needs an accepting or behaviour case; 58 have no suite case. `lineage_reads.py widen` plans only from read records, and 1985 has none, so the order is: this decision; then the read of the inherited 1985 rows in 1985's own terms (the 2002 pipeline with a 1985 brief), which is what makes widening and KEPT possible; then witnesses of their own for what the read moves off "covered".
