# PB3373 — a literal after the computer-name misattributes the computer-name

Filed 2026-10-18 from wave 1119 group A's report (`E:\COBOL-coord\scratch\reports\w1119a-PB3370-report.md`, leads) and the
"Not closed here" paragraph of `kb/Work/PB3370.md` (landed). Pre-grep: only PB3370 mentions it.

## What happens

`SOURCE-COMPUTER. XYZ "LIT".` and `OBJECT-COMPUTER. XYZ "LIT".`: `genericClause` opens only at an IDENTIFIER, so the
name-less reading is the only viable parse and XYZ is reported as a configuration paragraph or as an OBJECT-COMPUTER
clause, with a false COBOLNET0900 or COBOLNET3734 at 85. Harm: a misattributed diagnostic (the source is refused).
Whether the form is legal COBOL is not judged here; the implementer reads §12.3.5 and §12.3.6 before any fix.

## Repro

```cobol
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. P09.
       ENVIRONMENT DIVISION.
       CONFIGURATION SECTION.
       SOURCE-COMPUTER. XYZ "LIT".
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           STOP RUN.
```

## Landed (w1126b, 2026-10-18) — the error production opens at a literal; the unsigned integer and the figuratives are the parse layer's

**Spec.** ISO §12.3.5.2 prints `SOURCE-COMPUTER. [ computer-name-1 ] .` and §12.3.6.2 the OBJECT-COMPUTER clause set:
a literal stands in neither, so the source is illegal at every edition and the defect is the attribution. (A
computer-name is a system-name, §8.3.2.3.1.)

**Re-probe (worktree build, before).** `SOURCE-COMPUTER. XYZ "LIT".` drew COBOLNET1971 'XYZ' is not a paragraph of the
CONFIGURATION SECTION (plus COBOLNET3734 at 85); `OBJECT-COMPUTER. XYZ "LIT".` drew COBOLNET1970 'XYZ' is not a clause
(plus a false COBOLNET0900 at 85); the integer forms `XYZ 12` the same. Siblings in the other closed formats: a literal
at a residue position (`FD F "LIT".`, `SPECIAL-NAMES. ... "LIT".`, `SPECIAL-NAMES. "LIT".`, `SOURCE-COMPUTER. "LIT".`,
`OBJECT-COMPUTER. XYZ PROGRAM COLLATING SEQUENCE IS A "LIT".`) drew COBOL0308 "A data-name is expected here, not a
literal" — false, the IDENTIFIER in the expected set was the error production's own left edge — and a literal as a
configuration or identification paragraph or an I-O-CONTROL clause a bare COBOL0001; `01 X PIC X "A".` a missing-period
guess. A figurative after the name (`OBJECT-COMPUTER. XYZ ZERO.`, `SOURCE-COMPUTER. XYZ ZERO.`) misattributed XYZ the
same way, through the run's `literal` tail. Scratch `w1126b\mkprobes.sh`, `sibprobes.sh`, `tailprobes.sh`, `ocprobes.sh`.

**Fix.** (1) Grammar: `genericClause : (IDENTIFIER | residueLiteral) (IDENTIFIER | residueLiteral | INTEGERLIT)*`, with
`residueLiteral` every one-token literal but the unsigned integer (ClosedFormatDriftTests derives the set from
`nonNumericLiteral` and `numericLiteralCore`). The name's reading is viable at the literal, the two readings are
ambiguous, and the tie goes to the name. The integer does not open the run because it may be the next data description
entry's level-number (§8.3.2.2.13) after a forgotten period; the tail keeps it, because `SEGMENT-LIMIT IS? integer`'s
text predicate needs a viable residue reading of `FOO 12` (measured: without it `OBJECT-COMPUTER. XYZ FOO 12.` was a
NoViableAlt at FOO). The tail is tokens, not `literal`: the figurative is a reserved word and goes to the parse layer.
(2) ClosedFormatPass quotes the run's first token, word or literal. (3) CobolErrorStrategy: the 0g arm and its Sync take
a literal token as they take a reserved word (`OBJECT-COMPUTER. 12.` is named), `IsLiteral` is one ATN-read set
(`residueLiteral` plus INTEGERLIT; it was STRINGLIT, INTEGERLIT and DECIMALLIT), and hint 15 yields to a structural
diagnosis. (4) VersionConformancePass: an error-production clause is no OBJECT-COMPUTER clause (no false COBOLNET0900).
No new code (COBOLNET4484-4486 returned).

**Goldens.** Negatives at 85 2002 2014 2023: `12.3.5-SOURCE-COMPUTER/pb3373-source-computer-literal-clause`,
`12.3.6-OBJECT-COMPUTER/pb3373-object-computer-literal-clause` (COBOLNET1970 naming "LIT"). Positive at 85:
`12.3.7-SPECIAL-NAMES/pb3373_literal_lists_beside_the_residue` (ALPHABET, CLASS and level-88 literal lists beside the
widened residue keep their clause). Unit: `ClosedFormatReservedWordTests.LiteralResidue` (13 formats and classes),
the 85 theory, the level-number control, two figurative cases; `ClosedFormatDriftTests.TheResidueLiterals_*`.

**Not closed here (reported as a lead).** The residue's unsigned integer is the one token whose right reading depends
on the format: a level-number can follow only the data division's lists. One production cannot express that, so
`SOURCE-COMPUTER. XYZ 12.` and `OBJECT-COMPUTER. XYZ 12.` still read XYZ as the residue, and `01 X PIC X FOO` then
`05 Y PIC X.` (period forgotten) still reads `FOO 05 Y` as one run and Y's PICTURE as X's second (COBOLNET2423).

**For the next implementer (w1126b):**
- `src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolExpressions.g4#genericClause` / `#residueLiteral` — the head and tail token
  sets are each load-bearing: an INTEGERLIT head swallows the next entry's level-number after a missing period, an
  INTEGERLIT-free tail turns `SEGMENT-LIMIT`'s text predicate into a NoViableAlt at the decision's first token, and a
  `literal`-rule tail reads a figurative after the computer-name into the run.
- Trap: a semantic predicate on an alternative that ends up the SOLE syntactic survivor throws NoViableAlt at the
  decision's START token (ANTLR's accept-state predicate evaluation), so a residue shape can never be excluded by a
  predicate; the exclusion has to be syntactic.
