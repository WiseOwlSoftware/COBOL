## ⛔ Owner decision (2026-09-28 ~17:00 PDT)

Asked for "the best long-term, commercial-product, decades-long supportability solution", then: "do the design doc and
work now. let's get onto the right solution. It may make up for lost time over future runs".

Decided: fix the ROOT CAUSE, the false coupling, rather than choose (a) or (b) as stated. Three layers:
1. **Restructure the monolithic static registries.** DiagnosticCatalog, DiagnosticDescriptors and ConstructRegistry
   become modular, data-driven tables that do not all initialize together, so adding or changing one entry no longer
   touches every test that ever loaded the registry.
2. **Record the impact map at ENTRY granularity.** A test that looks up one entry depends on that entry; a test that
   enumerates the registry depends on all of it. This is exact by construction, not a narrowing heuristic. Maps are
   recorded automatically per main commit (PB1709).
3. **Keep the whole suite as the permanent backstop.** The lander and CI always run everything, and the full run is
   made cheaper (sharding; the compiled-program cache, PB985).
Option (a), capping concurrent whole-assembly implementer gates, is the INTERIM only, until layers 1 and 2 make
selection precise.

The design goes FIRST, per CLAUDE.md rule 2: `docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-test-build-ci.md` §3.5 (the diagnostic
registry) and section 3.13 (the impact map), plus the construct-registry design doc per `docs/DOC_INDEX.md`. It then gets
an adversarial design review, and implementation follows the approved design.

## ⛔ Second owner decision (2026-09-28 ~18:05 PDT): the cap outlives layers 1–2

The PB1708 architect MEASURED the premise, and it was partly wrong. Registry work alone barely narrows selection:
over 20 replayed clusters, the median goes from 9,236 to 9,180 of 9,274. Even perfect method-level precision has a
median of 6,404, because bundled clusters touch core files that most tests reach. The time is instead in the full
run: the continuity cells take 53 % of 18,104 Conformance test-seconds, NistDifferential 18 % and the corpus runner
11.5 %, plus long poles such as one 219 s test.

Asked whether the interim gate cap should become long-lived, the owner chose "Keep cap until measured". The cap on
concurrent whole-suite implementer gates stays, and retires only on the measured criterion in DESIGN-test-build-ci.md section 3.14.6: median
implementer selection under 25 %, or a cold whole-Conformance leg under 6 min. The priority is making the full
suite faster (continuity-cell sharing, the long poles), and PB1712 (the under-selection found by the same
measurement) comes first.

## ⛔ Fourth owner decision (2026-09-28 ~18:50 PDT): PIVOT to "order, don't skip"

Two independent adversarial reviews rejected precise test SELECTION. The first rejected the design with 7 BLOCKING
findings. The revision closed 5, and the re-review found 2 still open plus 3 new BLOCKING. Each cycle found another
way the single-process, memoizing compiler hides dependencies from the map:
- values memoized into static collections' contents (`CobolKeywordTokens.Probed`, `CollationKeyCache`,
  ConditionalWeakTable caches);
- edition state cached in the front end, where the recorder cannot reach it.
Any such hole lets selection SILENTLY skip a failing test. And even perfect method-level precision has a median of
about 69 % of the suite.

The owner chose "Pivot: order, don't skip":
1. **Implementers always run the WHOLE suite, ORDERED by impact likelihood**, so reds surface first. The map only
   prioritizes and never skips, so it is sound by construction.
2. **Invest in making the whole suite fast:** the long poles (M7); a SAFE redesign of continuity-cell sharing (M6;
   the re-review's edition-caching finding applies to it); sharding.
3. **Keep the gate cap (M2).**
4. **Shelve the registry restructuring (M3/M4/M5/M8/M10) as a speed measure.** Revisit it later on engineering
   merit only.
5. **PB1712** (the under-selection) cannot bite once the map never skips; it stays documented.

**The pivot's design** (2026-09-28): `DESIGN-test-build-ci.md` section 3.13 (the map as an ordering) and section 3.14 (the
two-leg whole-population gate, its population check, fail-fast, whole-suite speed, the cap), with the implementation
plan in section 3.14.9: M6 (the ANTLR lexer's DFA never caching past a predicate — measured as the continuity cells'
wall), M7 (the table-value odometer's 64 M-element fill), M11 (the order plan; deletes the selection, closing PB1712),
M14 (one population check for the gate, the battery and CI), M12 (the in-assembly leg filter and its environment
handshake), M2 (the FIFO cap) and M13 (the ordered gate, its lander/implementer modes, and the deletion of the filter
interface with every caller). Evidence: `docs/rearchitecture/evidence/impact-map-pb1708/` (README names every input
not in the repository).

**The pivot design's review (2026-09-28).** An adversarial review returned 2 BLOCKING and 9 NON_BLOCKING findings;
the revision resolves all eleven in the design doc. BLOCKING: (1) the in-assembly leg filter keyed on the environment
alone, so a stray variable could make the battery run a third of Conformance and report GREEN — now a three-variable
handshake whose partial forms turn every case into an execution error (a throwing framework constructor was
MEASURED to fall back silently to running everything), a scrub in every other caller, and one population check that
the battery and CI run too; (2) the plan deleted `impacted_tests.py`'s filter output in M11 while its callers were
rewired only in M13 — now M11 deletes only the narrowing and M13 deletes the interface with every caller. The
measurements the review asked for are `b3` (extended), `b7` and `b8`. Two product defects the design found (M6, M7)
await kb/Work ids from the orchestrator (agents never allocate ids).

⚠ **The third decision (source generator) is WITHDRAWN.** The re-review found its recorded premise false (the other
generators' inputs) and that it would create two mechanisms for one job (rule 4). It was approved on the
