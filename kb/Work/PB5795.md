# PB5795 - at --std 85 a word that is an implementor-name is accepted as the computer-name of SOURCE-COMPUTER and OBJECT-COMPUTER

**The checker rates this medium, and why.** The disjointness is stated of the implementation's name sets rather than as a syntax rule on the program: `SOURCE-COMPUTER. CONSOLE.` names no computer-name of an implementation whose computer-names must exclude its implementor-names, but the ruling turns on that inference. The checker finds no reading under which an implementation whose computer-names include its implementor-names conforms. Harm: under_rejects (minor).

**What is wrong.** `SOURCE-COMPUTER. CONSOLE.` and `OBJECT-COMPUTER. SWITCH-1.` compile and run at `--std 85` in a program that also uses the same word as an implementor-name (`c3.cob`: `CONSOLE IS MYCON` in SPECIAL-NAMES). `§1985:VI.4.3.3 SR1` and `§1985:VI.4.4.3 SR1` make the computer-name a system-name, and `§1985:IV.4.2.2.1.2` makes the three types of system-names (computer-name, implementor-name, language-name) disjoint within an implementation. The compiler accepts any non-reserved COBOL word as a computer-name (DOC-A.1-127, "any spelling"), so its own implementor-names (CONSOLE, SYSIN, SYSOUT, SYSERR, C01, CSP, SWITCH-n, UPSI-n, and the code-names ASCII and EBCDIC) are both. Whatever set of computer-names the implementor defines must exclude its implementor-names, so the word must be refused at `--std 85`. At 2023 the disjointness is restricted to device-name, feature-name and switch-name (`§2023:8.3.2.3.1`; computer-name is not among them), so the acceptance is right at 2023: cause (a), wrong at 85 only. The reserved-word, length and character parts of the reader's record stand (the refuter: all 350 1985 reserved words are refused).
