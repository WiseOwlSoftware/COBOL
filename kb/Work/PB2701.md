# PB2701 — the landing check stops a wave's first train on the wave's own later groups

**Found by:** the wave-1042 unit (2026-10-08 10:00 PDT), reading train 1042's push-main log.

## What happened

Train 1042 (C PB2496, F PB2507+PB2640+PB2641, B PB2587, D PB2660+PB754, A PB1722+PB1952) was gated GREEN on every
leg: Windows whole population run `20261008T161520Z-b26f2c` (Conformance 11,248/11,248, Unit 32,776/32,776,
Characterization 36/36), the Linux gate GREEN, the oracle attributed and re-recorded. Its head `3c5887e51` (DEVLOG 1939)
is in `E:\COBOL\.claude\worktrees\wf_eb1fc3a8-908-9`. `bash scripts/push-main.sh` exited 3 at the landing check
(`E:\COBOL-coord\scratch\w1042L\push-main.log`) with four stops:

- three on worktrees `wf_eb1fc3a8-908-5`, `-908-7` and `-908-8`: wave 1042's own groups E, G and H, which were waiting
  for the second train (1042b). Each stop reads "dispatched earlier … It is PB1096, PB1136, PB1149, PB1253 work by its
  dispatch ledger". Those four ids belong to neither group; they lead the sorted note list of a `hand` entry in
  `E:\COBOL-coord\inflight-groups.json` recorded at 08:21 PDT with about 130 notes, PB2119 among them, and 990
  declared files.
- one on branch `train1041-dropped-d-pb2169` (PB2169, the Delete campaign, wave 1041), sharing `IntrinsicBinder.cs`.

The workflow then started train 1042b (E, H, G). Its lander took the free lease, gated (one red in H, fixed in the
train), and returned SPLIT when `STOP-loop` appeared at 09:58 PDT; nothing landed.

## Mechanism

`scripts/orchestrator/landing_check.py` `identity()` identifies an in-flight worktree that no train manifest lists by
the dispatch-ledger groups "whose declared files its actual diff meets", takes the UNION of their notes and the
MINIMUM of their `at`. The ledger holds very wide `hand` entries: 05:53 (15 Delete-campaign notes, 174 files), 07:51
(wave 1042's own 24 notes again, 504 files, recorded at the Workflow launch) and 08:21 (about 130 notes, 990 files).
Almost any diff meets one of them, so a fix-lane worktree inherits R3 notes (which defeats the "fix lane against fix
lane: a train merges them" exemption) and the earliest `at` (which makes it "dispatched earlier"). Wave 1042's E, G and
H were in no train manifest yet when train 1042 pushed (train 1042b's manifest was written after), so they were
identified this way and stopped their own wave's first train.

The PB2169 stop is a circular wait. PB2679 says to re-land PB2169 once w1039B (PB1722) and w1039F (PB2496, PB2497)
are terminal. Train 1042 is that work, and the landing check makes it wait for PB2169 to land first.

## Fix direction

- Identify a worktree by what dispatched it before falling back to declared files. A rolling wave's group worktree is
  known from the wave's dispatch-ledger entry for its letter (`inflight-groups.json` has `wave` and `letter`; the
  Workflow names the worktree `wf_<run>-<n>`). Never take the union of every group whose declared set it meets: bind
  it to the one dispatch that created it, or treat it as unbound.
- Stop recording a whole fleet's union as one `hand` entry at launch (the 07:51 entry duplicates wave 1042's eight
  groups as one 504-file set). A ledger entry's declared set should be the size of one group.
- Treat every group of the landing's own wave as a member, not as other work, so a wave's trains never stop each other
  (same-file groups within a wave are already ordered by `after:`).
- Break the PB2169 cycle explicitly: PB2679's re-land condition and this check must agree which side lands first. The
  dropped Delete branch is the one to rebase, because it is a one-member deletion and the fix-lane train is gated.
- Drift tests: a plant where a wave's first train shares a file with the same wave's held group passes; a plant where
  a 900-file `hand` entry overlaps a fix-lane worktree does not make that worktree R3.
