# PB6036 - joined sub-items in the 2023 transcription

**Defect.** The printed page of §2023:13.18.38.3 lists SR25 a) to d), and §2023:14.5.3.3 GR1 lists items a) to c); the
transcription in `specs/ISO_COBOL_2023.md` joins the later sub-items into the text of an earlier one, so the rule
catalog the citation checker reads knows only the first ones. `python scripts/spec/cite.py --check
"§2023:13.18.38.3 SR25d" "is subordinate to an entry with a LINE clause"` answers `FAIL [no-sub-item]: ... its
sub-items there are SR25a, SR25b, SR25c` (run 2026-11-21 on train-1985-c). The train's citation canonicalization
(PB4976/PB6000) therefore rewrote the right citations as prose that no audit reads, and left one in the old spelling.

**Sites:** `specs/ISO_COBOL_2023.md` (13.18.38.3, 14.5.3.3), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/ReportSectionBinder.cs` (`ReportOccursOf`, `AbsoluteColumnClauseIn`, `SubordinateToLineClause`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs` (`EndsAStatementTheRecoveryLeft`, `EndsLeftStatementsAtAContainingStatementsPhrase`, `BeginsAStatement`, `ContinuesAStatementTheRecoveryLeft`, `TakenByALeftStatement`, `Recover`)

- `ReportSectionBinder.cs:2279` (a message: "SR25c / SR25 d)") and `:2981` (the same in a summary); `:3001` writes
  SR25 "(the 'is subordinate to an entry with a LINE clause' leg)".
- `CobolErrorStrategy.cs:191` and `:362` write "§2023:14.5.3.3 GR1, its item c"; the siblings at `:238`, `:275`,
  `:312` and `:929` write "§2023:14.5.3.3 4) b)" / "4))", a form the checker does not parse.
- `kb/Work/PB1224.md`, `kb/Work/PB565.md` cite "SR25 d)" in prose.

**Fix.** Repair the transcription of the two rules against the rendered pages (`scripts/render-spec-page.py`), regenerate
the rule catalog, then restore each site to its checked citation (`§2023:13.18.38.3 SR25d`, `§2023:14.5.3.3 GR1c`,
and whatever designator the printed page gives "4) b)"), each through `cite.py --check`. Sweep the transcription for
other joined sub-items the same way (a catalog rule whose printed page has more lettered items than it lists).
