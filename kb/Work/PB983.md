## Rule

The transcription's figure notes, measured from the printed page: §14.9.13.2 range-expression — "`IN` is **not**
underlined, so it is an optional word per 5.2.3"; §13.18.63.2 Format 3 — "`IS`, `ARE`, `IN`, `WHEN`, `SET`, and `TO` are
not underlined"; Format 5 — "`IS`, `ARE`, and `IN` are not underlined". (The CLASS clause's `IN alphabet-name-4` in
§12.3.7.2 IS underlined — that one stays required.) The fixer renders the three printed pages
(`scripts/render-spec-page.py`) before relying on the notes, per CLAUDE.md rule 1.

- `python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 5.2.3 "optional"` → OK §5.2.3 (Optional words)

With IN optional, `"M" THRU "A" AL` is a range whose collating sequence is alphabet AL's; the binder already
resolves the IN form (PB843: `BindRangeHigh` reads a trailing `IN <alphabet>` and binds through
`ReferenceResolver.WithoutTrailingSuffix`). Without IN, a bare trailing word after a range's high bound is either an
alphabet-name (the phrase) or — in EVALUATE — the start of the next object; only the symbol table decides, the same
§8.3.2.2 principle PB843 applied (`TrailingInPhraseDriftTests` pins the IN form's grammar shape).

## What the fixer owes

1. The three range productions take `IN?` alphabet-name, decided by the resolved symbol (alphabet-name) and never by
   ANTLR alternative order; a drift test pins all three formats together (one rule written once, memory
   `one_rule_one_place`).
2. Re-verdict FMT-14.9.13.2 (CONFORMS today) and the VALUE format rows.
3. COBOLNET1639 (and every diagnostic that echoes a qualified reference) renders the reference with its connectives
   spaced — one reference-text renderer, not `ctx.GetText()` per site; sweep `GetText()` inside diagnostic messages.
4. Goldens: EVALUATE at 2002 and VALUE Format 3 at the edition that introduced the alphabet phrase; a negative for a
   trailing word that is not an alphabet-name.

## Landed (wave 51 group D)

Re-probed on the implementer's own build (base train 48 `0abe2c80f`, which already carries PB843): `h843b.cob`
COBOLNET2072, `v88.cob` COBOLNET1639 and `q1639.cob` `'WS-CINNOGRP'` all reproduced; after the fix `h843b` prints
`IN`, `v88` prints `MID`, `q1639` reads `'WS-C IN NOGRP' does not uniquely identify …`.

- **EVALUATE — by position.** `valueRange : valueOperand (THRU|THROUGH) valueOperand (IN? cobolWord)?`. After a
  complete right-hand operand only ALSO, WHEN or a (reserved) verb can follow, so a bare word is alphabet-name-1;
  its TYPE is still checked by the one resolver.
- **VALUE formats 3/5 — by symbol.** `DataBinder.RangeAlphabetPhraseOf` is the ONE reader of the clause's phrase: the
  written `IN word`; a LAST bare operand (after a literal-2) naming a declared alphabet (§8.3.2.2 — one word, one
  type); and PB843's OTHER ARM, found by the sweep: over a constant-name operand (`VALUE "P" THRU K IN AL`,
  §13.10.3 SR2) the qualification loop claimed `IN AL` (COBOLNET1902). The operand is registered in
  `_alphabetPeeledOperands`, which `BareValueOperandWord` — the one literal-position accessor — consults.
- `TryResolveRangeAlphabet` asks the word's TYPE first, so a stray word after a range draws COBOLNET1997 rather than
  COBOLNET1998. `(IN IDENTIFIER)?` in `valueClause` became `(IN cobolWord)?`.
- The cosmetic sibling: `DataBinder.WrittenText` is the one reference renderer (now safe on an incomplete node and
  collapsing line breaks); the 68 `{dref.GetText()}` diagnostic interpolations and the three ReferenceResolver
  `text` locals now use it.
- `TrailingInPhraseDriftTests`: `valueClause` joins `valueRange` as a symbol-consumed trailing-IN rule, and a new
  fact pins the IN-optional shape of both clauses together.
- Golden `2002/pb983_range_alphabet_in_optional` (both clauses, both spellings, the constant-name arm, no PCS so
  every leg is the opposite of the native answer); negative `pb983-range-trailing-word-not-alphabet` (COBOLNET1997,
  all editions). Rows re-witnessed (both were already CONFORMS over the IN spelling only): FMT-14.9.13.2,
  FMT-13.18.63.2.
