**The question, per site:** the grammar's own comments give two reasons. (a) A WORD that is a user-defined word in the
earlier edition (§8.9 reserved-word lists differ by edition: RETRY, VALID, APPLY, COMMIT, LOCATION): the superset parse
would otherwise misread a legal earlier-edition program that uses the word as a name, so the read decides word status,
which no post-bind gate can do. (b) `validationClause` is gated by edition, not by word status (its grammar comment, `CobolDeclined.g4:89-91`, is not a citation of the standard): that reads like a construct gate — below 2002 the clause falls to `unrecognizedClause`
(COBOLNET1941) instead of being recognized and refused by name (COBOLNET1708) or named by its introduction edition.

Adjudicate them with the rest. **The pin this note lands with its verdict** (DESIGN-architecture-review §8.4): the grammar drift test APPLIES the rule — an edition read is a superclass member whose body reads `Edition` or calls another edition read, to a fixpoint, and a site is any predicate or action body of the parser grammars, or any parser listener, that calls one — and compares what it computes with the set this verdict records, per file, so a read under any new name, in a predicate, an action or a listener, fails until the verdict records it. **Widened again by R1 Draft 5 (refuter 4, F6):** the parser is one of three stages that read the edition. The lexer reads it through the fields `TokenRetypes.PrimeLexer` primes from it (`Parsing/TokenRetypes.cs:47-52`: `SetCaseMappingEdition(edition.Year)`, `SetReservedNonDataNames(ReservedNonDataNames(edition, …))`): `ApplyEditionSpelling` (`Grammar/Core/CobolLexer.g4:136-150`, the Annex C spelling of a word at the edition, reading `_caseMappingEdition`; called from `NextToken`, `:318`) and `PreviousTokenCouldBeDataName` (`:63-64`, whether a reserved word's '(' may open a subscript, reading `_reservedNonNames`; called from the reference-paren action, `:222`). The rule the drift test applies is therefore §8.4's widened one: edition STATE is the parser's `Edition`, an `EditionInfo`, or a lexer field primed from one; an edition read is a member of the parser superclass, the lexer's `@members` or the retype stage that reads edition state or calls another read, to a fixpoint; a site is any parser or lexer predicate or action body, lexer override, parser listener or retype-stage member that calls one. Adjudicate the lexer and retype reads with the rest (both are word status: a word's spelling at the edition, and whether the edition reserves it).
