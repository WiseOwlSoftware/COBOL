# PB362 — the boundary the pc space already knows and nothing consults

**Rows:** `SR-14.9.49.3-3`, `SR-14.9.49.3-4`.

## The programs

`<lane3 scratch>/out-b1/probe/sr34/`:

* `sr3.cob` — a declarative paragraph DP1 containing `PERFORM NONDECL-P.` and `GO TO NONDECL-P.` where NONDECL-P
  is a nondeclarative paragraph → compiles at `--std 2023` with **zero diagnostics**.
* `sr4.cob` — a nondeclarative paragraph containing `GO TO DP1.` where DP1 is a declarative paragraph →
  compiles with **zero diagnostics**.

## The rules

> SR3: Within a declarative procedure, there shall be no reference to any nondeclarative procedures except in a
> RESUME statement

> SR4: Procedure-names within a declarative section may be referenced in a different declarative section or in a
> nondeclarative procedure only with a PERFORM statement

Both `--check 14.9.49.3` → **OK**. So both statements in `sr3.cob` are violations, and `sr4.cob`'s GO TO is a
violation while a PERFORM of DP1 would be legal. §4.2.2 requires a compile-time indication.

## What the compiler does

A full search of `src/Cobol.Net.*` for a declarative-boundary predicate finds exactly two consumers of
`ProcedureTableBuilder.EntryPc` in any verb binder — `EcBinder.cs:114` and `:137` — and **both implement RESUME's
own §14.9.33.3 SR1/SR3**, a real clause answering a different question. `ControlFlowBinder.BindGoTo` and
`BindPerform` go straight to `Table.ResolveProcedure` with no boundary test.

**Worse, the comments assert the unrestricted behaviour while citing the rule that restricts it.**
`ProcedureTableBuilder.cs:124-125`: *the declarative sections share the ONE pc space, entered only via the USE
dispatch or an explicit PERFORM/GO TO — SR4*; `CodeGen/DispatchEmitter.cs:92`: *entered only via `__RunUse` or an
explicit PERFORM/GO TO (SR4)*. SR4 is precisely the rule that forbids the GO TO those comments describe as the
design.

## The fix shape

Both checks are cheap and structural given the existing pc space: `EntryPc` already partitions declarative pcs
from nondeclarative ones, and `ParaSections` already gives each pc its owning section. So

* **SR3** = bind cursor `< EntryPc` AND resolved target `>= EntryPc` AND the statement is not RESUME;
* **SR4** = resolved target `< EntryPc` AND (bind cursor `>= EntryPc` OR a different declarative section) AND
  the statement is not PERFORM.

Write them once in `ResolveProcedure`'s callers — the ONE funnel every procedure-name reference passes through —
rather than per verb, so a future verb taking a procedure-name inherits the rule. Correct the two comments in the
same change set.

**Code sites:** `ControlFlowBinder.cs#BindGoTo` (line 219), `#BindPerform` ·
`ProcedureTableBuilder.cs#ResolveProcedure`, lines 124-125 · `DispatchEmitter.cs:92`.
