FOR: section 7.3.10.1, section 7.3.10.4 GR3 "shall no longer be reserved or restricted in any way", section 4.2.10 3) "additional reserved words" (all cite OK), plus two the first report missed: D.12.1 (the directive lets a programmer "conform to previously existing implementor extensions") and D.12.3 (UNDEFINE "can be useful when a change of compiler that includes new reserved words that conflict with existing code") - the collision itself. AGAINST: section 8.3.2.4.1 defines the term by the section 8.9 list; section 4.2.10's remedy is documentation (D-RW1). Pinned: `UNDEFINE 'COMP-3'` and `UNDEFINE 'JSON'` both draw COBOL1623 at 2023. Evidence correction: `01 COMP-3 PIC X.` does NOT draw COBOL0313 (COMP-3 is consumed as USAGE of an implicit FILLER, COBOL0881); `01 JSON PIC X` does. Recommendation: include the 15 extensionReserved words in the SR3 population, with goldens for UNDEFINE 'JSON' / 'COMP-3' each followed by the word as a data-name, recorded in D-RW1; if excluded, COBOL1623 must say the word is reserved as a nonstandard extension (section 4.2.10) that the directive cannot modify. Either way COBOL1623 (and COBOL1501, see the gap-pass note on COBOL1501 and RemovedByDirective) must stop calling a word the compiler reserves "not a reserved word". **Owner question 1** (GAP-REVIEW.md section 5.3): "Does section 7.3.10.3 SR3's 'reserved word' include the section 4.2.10 implementor additions (JSON, XML, COMP-3, ENTRY and the other 11 extensionReserved words), so that `>>COBOL-WORDS UNDEFINE "JSON"` is legal?"
