# PB5462 - the NIST console-output basis is a third normalizer no parity table holds, and the shell's COMPUTED= mask covers one operand a line

**Where it comes from.** The branch review of the PB5460 fix (wave 20262015 group A, reviewer `w20262015a-review-r1`, head c54a114f67, verdict CLEAN) recorded two leads that the fix did not take up: R1-1 (low) and R1-2 (info) in `E:\COBOL-coord\scratch\w-mythos-pb5460\review-w20262015-a.jsonl`. The implementer's report (`E:\COBOL-coord\scratch\reports\w20262015a-PB5460-report.md`) names the first as a lead of its own. Everything below is the reviewer's and the implementer's reading of the code, a claim until the fixer re-reads it.

**R1-1: a third normalizer.** `tests/WiseOwl.Cobol.Tests.Conformance/NistRunner.cs`, `NistOutputs.StdoutBasis` (near l.187), and its shell twin in `scripts/guard-verdict.sh` (`guard_files_apply`) normalize a NIST program's console output by a rule that differs from `NistRunner.Normalize` / `guard_normalize_into`, and its comment still calls it "the acceptance basis". PB5460 put `Normalize` and `guard_normalize_into` under one table of parity cases, `tests/nist/normalization-cases.json`; this pair reads no table. Two differences the reviewer names:
- A `(stdout)` that differs from its golden only by a NUL byte scores MATCH in the shell arm, which drops the NUL, and red in the C# arm.
- An overprint `MARKER<spaces><CR>text` keeps the spaces before the CR under this basis; under `Normalize` it does not.

The implementer adds that this basis drops every CR and does not mask `COMPUTED=`.

**R1-2: the mask.** `scripts/guard-verdict.sh` near l.122: the sed that masks `COMPUTED=` has no `/g`, so it masks the first operand on a line, while the C# `ComputedOperand` masks every one. No parity case has two operands on a line, and no golden under `tests/nist/valid` contains `COMPUTED=` today, so the mask changes nothing in either arm at present.

**Harm.** No wrong verdict is known. The harm is PB5460's again, waiting: two arms that claim one rule and are held to none, so the nightly's CLI guard and the in-process gate can disagree about a program whose output carries a NUL, an overprint or two computed operands, and the disagreement shows as a red nobody can act on.

**Shape (the fixer decides from the code; CLAUDE.md rule 5).** Every normalizer of NIST output that either arm applies reads the one table, `tests/nist/normalization-cases.json`, with the console-output basis as its own named rule in it when its rule must differ, and a case with a NUL, an overprint and two `COMPUTED=` operands on one line. A comment that says "the acceptance basis" names the rule it applies.
