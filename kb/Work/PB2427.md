**Found by:** the R1 (PB2118) landing brief. `scripts/orchestrator/plan_wave.py` `next_wave()` returns one past the
highest wave number seen in the reports directory and in `\b[Ww]aves? (\d{3,})` matches of the DEVLOG head. Note ids
have one allocator (`scripts/orchestrator/alloc.py`); wave numbers do not. The attended session's one-off dispatches
(wave 1031's R1 drafts, wave 1033's adjudications) pick a number by hand, and until their report exists the planner
can issue the same number.

**Harm:** two waves with one number share report names, dispatch-ledger keys and `--release <wave>` targets; a release
or a report read then acts on the wrong wave.

**Fix direction (rule 5):** `alloc.py wave` allocates wave numbers from the same locked counter mechanism as ids, and
`next_wave()` calls it (no second derivation; rule 4). A test allocates twice concurrently and gets two numbers.
