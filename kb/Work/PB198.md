# PB198 — LANDED — the standard-binary decline, and the ground it actually rests on

> ⚙ **Authority:** the owner protocol of 2026-08-30 ("confirm to what the other model COBOL implementations do")
> plus a **unanimous** survey — no shipping COBOL implements standard-binary arithmetic, which the standard
> itself records. Plan item **D-A**; supersedes the open halves of [[PB67]] and [[PB146]].

## The decision

`ARITHMETIC IS STANDARD-BINARY` is **not claimed**, at every edition that has it, and use of it is a **hard
compile error** (COBOLNET0806).

## ⛔ The ground was recorded wrongly, and the correction is the substance

`docs/CONFORMANCE.md` §2 row 2 read *"Obsolete feature (A.3 NOTE 1)"*. **Obsolescence licenses nothing.** Annex
F.2's own preamble:

> "A conforming implementation shall support obsolete language elements **except for elements that are also
> optional or processor-dependent**." (`cite.py --check F.2 …` → OK)

So the exception is doing all the work, and the exception is **processor-dependence**:

> **A.3 item 2** — "The ARITHMETIC IS STANDARD-BINARY clause in the OPTIONS paragraph is dependent on the
> capabilities of the processor." (→ OK, §A.3 2)

and **§4.2.6** is what makes declining it conforming: the decision to claim support "is within an implementor's
discretion" (→ OK), the absence "shall be specified in the implementor's user documentation" (→ OK, and §2 row 2
is that documentation), and a compile-time warning mechanism is required. The obsolescence is corroboration,
never the licence. Every citation here was `--check`ed for this note.

## REVISITABLE — by the standard's own words

F.2 item 3 is the STANDARD-BINARY item ("These features have not been implemented as of the writing of this
revision by any COBOL provider") and adds: *"Unlike other obsolete features, it is intended that interest in
these facilities will be **reevaluated** during the next revision of standard COBOL and before any final
decision is made on whether or not to remove them from the next revision."* The decline is therefore recorded as
revisitable rather than permanent. ⚠ The remark had been cited as **Annex E** on row `RV-15.27.3-2`, and the
sentence as quoted there was F.2 **item 5** — the VALIDATE facility. Repaired 2026-08-31 (DEVLOG 1410).

## Hard error, not the 1560 warning band — the asymmetry, explained

§4.2.6 ¶3 requires a warning and adds "The implementor is not required to produce executable code when
unsupported processor-dependent language elements are used" (→ OK), so both severities conform and the choice is
ours to explain. The 1560 band (SCREEN, MCS, COMMIT/ROLLBACK, VALIDATE) warns because those facilities are
**additive** — the program still means what it says with them inert. Standard-binary is **pervasive**: §8.8.1.4.1
makes it "a method of evaluating an arithmetic expression, an arithmetic statement, the SUM clause, and certain
integer and numeric functions" (→ OK), in effect for the whole unit, so ignoring the clause would run the entire
program under a different arithmetic than the source selected and the divergence would surface as **wrong
values**, not as a missing facility. Recorded in `CONFORMANCE.md` §1.

## The rows: ONE determination, applied by a DERIVED predicate

**Sixteen** catalog rules are conditioned **solely** on the declined mode — **10 named by their TEXT and 6 by
their CLAUSE** (§8.8.1.4, titled *Standard-binary arithmetic*, whose rules never repeat the phrase:
`GR-8.8.1.4.2-1` reads "Any operand of an arithmetic expression that is not already in SBIDI is converted into
SBIDI form"). They carried **four different verdicts** — NOT-IMPLEMENTED (9), blank (2), NEEDS-OWNER-DECISION
(4), CONFORMS (1) — four answers to one question. All sixteen now carry `DOCUMENTED-NON-SUPPORT` from this
determination; GAP **3656 → 3641**.

⛔ **Selected, not listed.** The predicate lives in `inventory-schema.json` under `derived-verdicts.
standard-binary-only` as DATA, read by both the batch generator and the C# drift test
(`DerivedVerdictDriftTests.NoStandardBinaryConditionedRow_Diverges`), so one rule is written down once.

**The predicate was wrong twice before it was right, and both errors are recorded in the schema** because they
are the shape of error it will attract again:
1. *Word-sense, not mode-sense.* "standard-binary" also names a FLOAT-BINARY **usage** (§11.9.8, §13.18.63);
   the first draft selected `SR-11.9.8.3-1/-2/-3`, which have nothing to do with arithmetic.
2. *Over-broad exclusion.* Excluding any mention of "standard decimal" dropped `AR-15.43.3-3`, whose only such
   mention is "standard decimal floating-point **usage**" — a usage again. It IS solely mode-conditioned.
3. And requiring a trailing "arithmetic" after standard-decimal let `GR-14.7.7-3` and `GR-14.9.26.4-3` in,
   whose text reads "standard-decimal **or** standard-binary arithmetic".
4. ⛔ **Text is not the only way a rule is conditioned.** All three drafts above were text-only, and every one of
   them missed the six rules of **§8.8.1.4** — the clause whose TITLE is the mode. They were caught not by
   re-reading the predicate but by checking it against what [[PB146]] had listed by hand: PB146 named seven
   §8.8.1.4 rows and the predicate had selected one. A hand list is not authority, but a hand list the derived
   selector disagrees with is a signal worth chasing. The selector gained `requires-sections`.

📏 **The estimate was wrong and is corrected** (CLAUDE.md rule 5): the plan said "~20 rows carrying five
different states". Measured: **41 rules mention** standard-binary across five states — that is where the
estimate came from — but mentioning is not being conditioned on. **16 are conditioned solely on the mode**
(10 by text, 6 by clause), across four states. The remaining mode-naming rules keep their own verdicts: they
have reachable content under a mode we DO support, and stamping them would over-claim.
