# PB5852 - at --std 85 a MOVE whose truncated result is zero stores a positive sign where the 1985 rule places the sending operand's sign

**Ruling: UPHELD (a), harm wrong_answer.** The row `1985-GR-VI.6.19.4-4` (a lead raised by the refuter of slice 1985-191) does not close on its inherited verdict. `MOVE -100` to `S99 SIGN LEADING SEPARATE` stores `+00`. The 1985 rule places the SENDING operand's sign, so `-00` is due; the compiler makes a zero result positive. **Checker's confidence: medium** (the 2023 wording ties the sign to the value; an implementor might argue that negative zero is not a value, but the 1985 sentence names the operand, not the value). Cause (a): wrong at 85 only. Severity MAJOR: a wrong-answer defect is at least MAJOR.

**The checker's evidence (rulings.json, copied).** 1985 places the sending operand's sign: `§1985:VI.6.19.4 GR4b1` says that "the sign of the sending operand is placed in the receiving item" when a signed numeric item is the receiving item. -100 (literal or S999 item) is negative; truncation keeps 00; so the receiving item holds negative zero: `-00` (LEADING), `00-` (TRAILING), and the negative overpunch for a plain S99. Observed `+00`, `00+`, `0{` (positive), at 85 and 2023; a non-zero result keeps its sign (S1 -1 gives `-01`), so the compiler normalizes a zero result to positive. Cause (a), not (b): at 2023 the rule reads that "the sign of the numeric value shall be represented in the receiving operand" (`§2023:14.9.25.4 GR6d.2.a`), which ties the sign to the value moved (zero after truncation) and so admits the positive zero; 1985 ties it to the sending operand. That the 2023 wording yields positive zero is the checker's reading of 2023, given only for the cause.
