# PB5120 - at --std 85 INSPECT with both BEFORE and AFTER on one operand searches for the BEFORE delimiter only from the AFTER boundary, so crossed or repeated delimiters (AYAAXAA, AYXAAYA) count 2 where 1985 counts 0

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Runtime/Verbs/CobolInspect.cs#Region` (lines 75-101: the forward BEFORE search is `text.IndexOf(before, start)`, `start` being the AFTER-derived position, at every edition; its comment reads "ENCOUNTERED FROM the AFTER-derived start")

**Mechanism.** Both phrases are measured on the whole content in 1985: each participates only in its own portion (BEFORE: from the leftmost position up to the first occurrence of the delimiter; AFTER: to the right of the first occurrence), and the position of that first occurrence is determined before the first cycle. Where BEFORE's delimiter occurs left of AFTER's, or earlier than the first AFTER match, the two portions do not intersect and no cycle is eligible. The compiler measures BEFORE from the AFTER-derived start (the 2023 wording, §2023:14.9.22.4 GR9, "first occurrence encountered"). Writing the phrases in the other order also gives 2, and REPLACING ALL "A" BY "Z" and CONVERTING "A" TO "Z" with both phrases turn AYAAXAA into AYAAXZZ where 1985 leaves it unchanged. The other twelve lines of the draft match. The rule's change record calls the two editions' regions "the same intersection", which this result contradicts.

**Found by.** The 1985 witness-drafting pass (kb/Work PB4995 item 3): each rule below is a witness program drafted from the 1985 text alone, run at `--std 85`, and its result differs from what the text requires (outcome MISMATCH). Harm and observed text are the record's; nothing here was re-derived.
