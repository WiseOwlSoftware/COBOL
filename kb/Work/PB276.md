# PB276 — a negative that could not fail for its own rule

Found by the arithmetic-wave review fleet over the D-B/D-C/PB194/PB195 working tree, 2026-08-31, and fixed in the change set that landed the wave. Every number below was re-derived independently by the implementer (a 34-digit emulation of `CobolDec.cs` validated against the shipped `pb167` goldens, and 80–120-digit `Decimal` truth) BEFORE the confirming run, and then confirmed by it.

`CorpusRunnerTests.EnabledNegativeCase_RejectsWithItsDiagnostic` reads a one-line `.err` and calls
`AssertHasDiagnostic`, which is `all.Any(d => d.Contains(expected))` — one match over the whole diagnostic set,
no count, no per-line anchoring. With both lanes in one file the VALUE-clause entries F1–F4 (diagnosed by
`DataBinder`'s `FitsBinaryFloat` screen, a DIFFERENT rule) always produced a COBOLNET1661, so the three
PROCEDURE-DIVISION literals contributed nothing to the verdict.

**Fixed by splitting**: `pb99-floating-literal-range.cob` keeps the VALUE-clause entries and now has no
PROCEDURE-DIVISION literal at all; `pb99-floating-literal-range-statement.cob` carries the three statement
entries and declares no VALUE. Each lane can now fail alone.

The two inventory rows are re-pointed with it. `SR-8.3.3.3.3-3` (the implementor-defined exponent RANGE) cites
both negatives and its notes are rewritten to the post-D-B determination — one decimal128 range for every
statement position, in every arithmetic mode, replacing the per-position binary64/decimal128 split the old note
still published. `GR-8.3.3.3.3-5` is the exact-VALUE law and asserts no diagnostic, so the negative was removed
from its `test-ref` entirely: it was never a witness for that rule.
