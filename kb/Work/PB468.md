# PB468 — OWNER — three answers taken, six questions still to ask

The owner asked, on 2026-09-04: **is there a more efficient process that completes the compiler to 100 % — the
PHASE-14 traceability inventory at zero GAP — more quickly?** Efficiency was defined as rows closed *per day*
**and** *per token*, **without weakening the definition of DONE** (`DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md` §1).

Three proposals were written independently (throughput-first, cost-first, risk-first) and reconciled into one
synthesis measured on the tree. The whole record — the data brief and the synthesis, verbatim — is
`docs/rearchitecture/evidence/PROCESS-REVIEW-2026-09-04.md`. The measurement itself is `kb/Work/PB469`.

## The three answers, verbatim, taken 2026-09-04 by bare question

> **Q1.** "May the agent that assigns a row's verdict also author that row's witness, given a mechanically checked
> pre-run predicted expectation and an independent blind replicate?"
>
> **A1. NO: keep the lanes separate.**

> **Q2.** "May every agent be hard-capped — 160 turns read-only, 220 for an implementer — splitting the work rather
> than extending the transcript?"
>
> **A2. YES.**

> **Q3.** "May one lander carry five clusters in one landing, one commit per cluster inside it, never more than one
> landing per lander transcript?"
>
> **A3. YES.**

## Q1 — what was declined, and the measured reason it was still the right call

The synthesis's central recommendation is a pipeline it names *"one derivation per rule"*: **S2** has one agent
read the rule, locate the code, write a pre-run predicted expectation, author the witness and run it, so the
verdict *falls out* of the run; **S4** replaces both of today's refuters with a blind replicate that sees only the
rule text and a copy of the `.cob`. Its case is real and it is measured: adjudication emits a verdict with **no
evidence**, and the golden lane later re-reads the same rule and the same code to produce that evidence — ⓜ batch
b1 adjudicated 184 rules for a GAP delta of −15 (8 %), b2 adjudicated 292 for −32 (11 %), and b2 took the
CONFORMS-but-untested band from 9 rows to 55.

**It is declined, and adjudication stays verdict-only.** The reason is independent derivation of the evidence. An
agent that has already located and read the implementing code has already formed an expectation *from the code*;
a witness it then authors is checked against that same single derivation. Both guards the synthesis offers — the
pre-run predicted expectation and the blind replicate — check the *record* of that one derivation rather than
supplying a second one. Two lanes, each reading the standard for itself, produce two derivations, and the
measurement says that second derivation earns its cost: ⓜ **every adjudication batch so far overturned some
CONFORMS verdicts, and always downward** (synthesis §1.2 A4; batch-2 measured 2 of 4 overturned on `set-p3`). The
double derivation the synthesis diagnoses as the root defect is therefore assessed as the **price of the
guarantee**, not as waste to be removed.

⚠ Recorded as **the analysis, not adopted process.** The synthesis's S2/S4, its `wf_witness.js`, its
`witness_gate.py` and its blind-replicate disagreement band are not the project's pipeline. Everything in the
synthesis that does not depend on S2/S4 stands.

## Q2 and Q3 — what was adopted, and the law that priced them

The cost law was the strongest finding of the review and it reproduced independently: ⓜ
**`tokens ≈ 0.115·T + 0.00031·T²`**, refitted as `0.1154·T + 0.000309·T²` over **n = 239 agents**. It is quadratic
in turns, which is what makes a long transcript expensive: ⓜ **18 of 239 agents ran ≥ 250 turns and burned 39 % of
all 5.62 B tokens**, and re-modelled at a 150-turn cap the same turns cost **45 % less**.

* **Q2 — the caps.** 160 turns for read-only work, 220 for an implementer; a job that will not fit is **split, not
  extended**. ⓜ lander-3 carried 4 landings in 794 turns for **295.9 M**; the same turns split into four fresh
  ~198-turn landers model at **140 M — 53 % less**.
* **Q3 — the landing train.** A landing is ~90 % fixed cost, so ⓜ **10.4 M per cluster at k = 1 against 5.1 M at
  k = 5**, and 4.0 minutes of lander per cluster against 9.8. Five clusters, one build, one union gate filter, one
  commit per cluster inside the landing so a red bisects by cluster, **one landing per lander transcript**. Past
  six clusters the token curve is flat and the gate-attribution risk is not.

## Adopted alongside them without an owner decision, because they change no definition

* **One mechanism per implementer.** ⓜ A mechanism costs ~150 turns and an implementer's fixed cost is only ~70,
  so the quadratic eats the amortization immediately: the **second mechanism in one transcript costs 44.6 M, more
  than a whole fresh implementer at 40.3 M**. Two only when the notes share one code site *and* one gate filter —
  in which case the register's own clustering rule says they were one mechanism.
* **A free implementer slot is filled within the turn it frees**, from `work.py next`. ⓜ Three implementers ×
  6 dispatch-hours ÷ 45 min per mechanism = ~24 mechanisms/day of capacity; the measured fix lane delivered ~16 in
  six days — **under 15 % utilization of a cap the owner had already authorized**.
* **The comprehensive battery runs in a worktree cut at the batch head.** Its phase 2 rebuilds, so a battery on
  main freezes the lander ⓜ ~45 min ≈ 1.5 landings ≈ 7 clusters. The cadence (one battery per accumulated batch)
  does not change.
* **Read-only fleets keep probing a pinned worktree**, and the ledger refresh after every GAP-moving landing stays.

These are written into `.claude/skills/workstream/SKILL.md` §2 and §3, `templates/implementer-brief.md`, the new
`templates/lander-train-brief.md`, and the 160-turn cap plus an optional `deferred` return in
`templates/wf_lane3_refute.js`, `wf_lane1_draft.js` and `wf_lane1_validate.js`.
