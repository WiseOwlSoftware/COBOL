# PB272 — the exact `**` arm keyed on spelling

Found by the arithmetic-wave review fleet over the D-B/D-C/PB194/PB195 working tree, 2026-08-31, and fixed in the change set that landed the wave. Every number below was re-derived independently by the implementer (a 34-digit emulation of `CobolDec.cs` validated against the shipped `pb167` goldens, and 80–120-digit `Decimal` truth) BEFORE the confirming run, and then confirmed by it.

Pre-existing — `EmitCore.UnscaledLit` returned a `Real` NumX for any E-form text long before D-B, and
`10 ** 30.0` failed the same guard on `e.Scale == 0` with no float literal anywhere. What the wave added was the
CLAIM that a literal's notation never changes the arithmetic, which made the split worth fixing rather than
merely noting.

## Measured

```
COMPUTE R = 10 ** 30       1000000000000000000000000000000   (both)
COMPUTE R = 10 ** 3.0E+1   1000000000000000071935427891953  →  1000000000000000000000000000000
COMPUTE R = 10 ** 30.0     1000000000000000071935427891953  →  1000000000000000000000000000000
IF 10 ** 30 = 10 ** 3.0E+1              NE  →  EQ
IF 10 ** 30 = 10 ** 30.0                NE  →  EQ
```

## The fix

`NumericRenderer.IntegerValuedLiteral` normalizes a numeric LITERAL operand of `**` to a scale-0 Int128 whenever
its algebraic value is an integer the native carrier can hold — trailing zeros past the point are notation, not
scale. §8.3.3.3.3 rule 5 makes a floating-point literal's value *"the algebraic product of the value of its
significand and the quantity derived by raising ten to the power of the exponent"* and nothing more, and
§8.3.1.2's fixed-point form is likewise a value: 30, 30.0 and 3.0E+1 are ONE operand.

Not applied under a standard arithmetic mode (there every operand is an SDIDI by §8.8.1.5.2 r1 and this arm does
not exist), and not applied past `Int128` (the exact arm's own carrier cannot hold it, and the Dec lane it
otherwise reaches is the right home). Pinned by `pb156_float_literal_exact_native`'s IPOW-F / IPOW-E / IPOW-D
legs; the pin was proved to fail by disabling the normalization and re-running.
