# R41

Asked 2026-09-23 09:35 PDT as a bare question after the owner observed that "the overhead seems huge for the actual
progress we make on fully implementing a conformant compiler". Answer: **Adjudication first** (offered: rebalance
~50/50 [recommended], adjudication first, keep defects-first).

## The evidence the question carried (measured, DEVLOG 1663)
- Waves 45–57 (81 implementer/finisher transcripts): implementers + finishers were ~89 % of the fleet's 3.09 B tokens,
  landers 8 %, registrars 2 %, batteries 0.4 %. Inside an implementer, codebase search + read was 46 %.
- They closed ~216 inventory rows — roughly 13 M tokens per row — against ~2.6 M per row measured for adjudication
  (lane plan, 2026-09-01).
- GAP stood at 2,060 of 4,348 rows, of which ~1,991 had never been adjudicated; the fix lane works the ~139
  known-defective rows and the register, and each wave's leads re-grow the register (registrar #13 filed 39 notes).

## What it decides
1. The daily quota goes MOSTLY to the adjudication lane (`templates/wf_lane3_adjudicate.js` + `wf_lane3_refute.js`,
   pinned worktree, checkpoint per rule) until the unadjudicated mass is mapped.
2. The fix lane continues only for defects that give a user a WRONG ANSWER or a CRASH (`work.py next` rank, harm
   flags `wrong_answer` / `crashes`). Rejects-legal-source, under-rejects and silent-only defects wait.
3. Work already in flight when the decision was made (wave 58) finishes and lands; nothing is killed.
4. Supersedes [[feedback defects-to-zero-first]] (2026-09-13). The 2026-09-01 lane plan's cost figures are the
   baseline to re-measure against on Opus 5.5.
