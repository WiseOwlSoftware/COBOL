# PB4920 - the socket transport's accept lifecycle

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Runtime/Messaging/SocketTransport.cs` (`SocketTransport.AcceptServerAsync`, `SocketTransport.AcceptBellAsync`, `SocketTransport.Dispose`, `SocketTransport.Abort`)

**Lead R1-3 (low).** `AcceptServerAsync` and `AcceptBellAsync` end in `catch (Exception e) when (e is SocketException or ObjectDisposedException
or OperationCanceledException)` and read every `SocketException` as "the listener has closed". A genuine accept failure (resource exhaustion, an
aborted handshake the OS reports as an error) therefore ends the loop with no post and no log, while the listening socket stays open and its file
stays published: a requestor then connects, is never accepted, and waits. Expected: only the listener's own close (`ObjectDisposedException`,
`OperationCanceledException` from `listener.Stop`, or a `SocketException` raised after `Stop` is cancelled) ends the loop quietly; any other
accept failure is posted as a transport fault (the way `PeerLink.PumpAsync` posts `LinkLost` for every fault) and the listener is withdrawn
or the loop resumes. Observed: the loop exits silently.

**Lead R1-6 (low).** `SocketTransport.Dispose` closes the links before the listeners, and neither it nor `Abort` stops an accept that is already
past its `await`: that accept can still hand `NewLink(accepted)` a socket and `Post(new LinkAccepted(...))` after the links were closed, leaving a
link nothing disposes. Expected: stop the listeners first (cancel `listener.Stop`, dispose the socket), then close the links, and an accepted
socket that arrives once the transport is disposed is closed at once. Observed: the order is links then listeners.

No repro was run by the clerk; both are the reviewer's reading of the code at the sites named (reviewer lines 181, 282 and 300 at the reviewed head).
