# PB616 — OPEN — one level short, eleven times, audit green

Found 2026-09-05 by the `PB248` implementer, which corrected its own comment and left the inherited sites;
re-derived on this tree for this note.

## The measurement

```
python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 12.3.7 "DECIMAL-POINT IS COMMA"          → FAIL (the heading clause carries no such text)
python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 12.3.7.4 "The character written in numeric literals to represent the decimal separator shall be the comma"
OK  §12.3.7.4
```

Sites at the time of the sweep: `DataBinder.cs:2280,3114,3230`, `DataBinder.Constants.cs:233`,
`DataBinder.Switches.cs:211,220,227,231`, `ExpressionBinder.cs:920`, `NumericLiteral.cs` — each a comment or
message of the form `§12.3.7 GR14a`. The number is the SPECIAL-NAMES paragraph heading; its general rules are
§12.3.7.4, and "GR14a" is the ordinal inside that subclause. Every site inherited the spelling from the first.

## Why the audit did not see it

`audit_code_citations.py`'s phantom scan asks whether the clause EXISTS (§12.3.7 does) and its construct
agreement asks whether a construct is keyed to its own clause; neither asks whether a clause spelled with a
rule ordinal actually HAS rules of that kind — §12.3.7 is a heading with sub-clauses, and "GR14a" under it names
nothing. `PB591` (prose `§X GR<n>` references) and `PB597` (a wrong rule NUMBER inside a correct clause) are the
same family; this is the third face — a correct rule number under a clause one level too high — and the
cheapest to detect: a clause cited with `GR`/`SR` that has no `.3`/`.4` sub-clause content of its own.

## Fix shape

Sweep the eleven sites to `§12.3.7.4 GR14 a)` with each `--check`ed (one commit, the messages first); add the
"rule ordinal under a heading-only clause" check to the audit family PB591/PB597/PB607 describe, fired once
against a fixture line reading `§12.3.7 GR14a` before it is trusted.
