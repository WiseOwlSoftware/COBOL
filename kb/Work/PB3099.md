**The derivation.** A group without GROUP-USAGE is an alphanumeric group (§13.18.29.4 GR3), so every rule that
measures it measures alphanumeric character positions: LENGTH (§15.50.4 r3), a reference modifier's positions
(§8.4.3.3.4 GR1, GR5 a)), a group MOVE (§14.9.25.4 GR4, an alphanumeric to alphanumeric move with no conversion),
a BY REFERENCE group formal (§14.8.2.2 rule 1, bytes), and DISPLAY, whose conversion to the device "is defined by the
implementor" (§14.9.11.4 GR1) over the operand's positions. GR8 makes a national character "a uniform size equal to or
a multiple of" an alphanumeric one and leaves the multiple to the implementor; this compiler's determination is two
bytes, UTF-16BE (D-N1, DOC-A.1-33, DOC-A.1-209). So in an alphanumeric group a national position IS two alphanumeric
positions, in every channel, and `01 G. 05 N PIC N(2). 05 A PIC X(2).` is six positions with `A` at positions 5 and 6.
SR12 names the shapes a usage-national item can take, and GR8 speaks of usage, not category: a numeric,
numeric-edited, boolean or national-edited item of usage national occupies the same two bytes per position as a
category-national one. The GROUP-USAGE NATIONAL NOTE predicts the alternative exactly ("Without the GROUP-USAGE
NATIONAL clause, the content of such a group item would be treated as category alphanumeric, possibly leading to
corruption or invalid handling of data") and names the remedy, a national group (GR2 b)).

**Verdict.** The determination PB327 took on 2026-09-05 is what the text derives. It is spec-derived, not
owner-signed, and DOC-A.1-57 stands as written. The owner is not asked.

## GnuCOBOL does the same (measured)

GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 (its USAGE NATIONAL "unfinished" by its own warning) sizes the same group at ten bytes because it
stores `N(2)` in eight, and still: `LENGTH(G)` = `BYTE-LENGTH(G)` = the sum of the members' bytes, `G(3:2)` indexes
bytes, `MOVE G TO W` copies bytes, and `MOVE "0A0B0C" TO G` fills bytes left to right. The unit of an alphanumeric
group is the byte there too.

Two sites remain, and they share one mechanism: a byte count that decides "national" by the item's CATEGORY, so a
usage-national item of another category (SR12's boolean, numeric, numeric-edited) falls into the one-byte arm.
`OperandText.AsStorageImage`'s reference-modified arm keys on `rmp.Category is PicCategory.National`; a boolean
slice keeps category boolean (§8.4.3.3.4 GR6, kb/Work PB73) and is serialized one byte per position. Every other
reader of the unit (`DataItem.ElementaryByteWidth`, `RefModPlace.PositionBits`, `RecordLayout.IsNationalClass`)
already keys on the USAGE.

## D-N8 — a group sender into a national dynamic-length receiver (recorded in docs/CONFORMANCE.md §3)

§14.9.25.4 GR4 makes the move a byte copy, and §8.5.1.10.4 makes the receiver's new length "the length of new
content". A three-byte sender leaves a national receiver with an odd byte count the model cannot hold. The fixed
receiver `FN PIC N(3)` already completes its last position with the alphanumeric-move space fill GR4 prescribes
(bytes 41 42 43 20 20 20). The dynamic receiver takes the same copy and the same completion: content 41 42 43 20,
two positions, `LENGTH(DN)` = 4 (§15.50.4 r6, bytes). The current six is a character conversion GR4 forbids.
GnuCOBOL 3.2 has no dynamic-length items, so nothing in precedence speaks.
