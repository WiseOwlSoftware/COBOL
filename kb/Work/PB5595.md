# PB5595 - CURRENCY SIGN IS X"40" is refused at --std 2002 (and 85) by a rule that first appears in the 2014 text

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/SpecialNamesBinder.Currency.cs:170-172` raises SR19 when `bare.Hexadecimal`,
with no edition gate.

**Repro** (`E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/op-20261114/sample-k0/probes-2/cs.cob`):

```
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. CSP.
       ENVIRONMENT DIVISION.
       CONFIGURATION SECTION.
       SPECIAL-NAMES.
           CURRENCY SIGN IS X"40".
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01 E1 PIC @@@9.99.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE 12.5 TO E1
           DISPLAY "[" E1 "]"
           STOP RUN.
```

At `--std 2002`, 2014 and 2023 the pinned compiler
(`E:/COBOL-wt/coderead85-tree-d/src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Cli/bin/Debug/net10.0/cobol.exe`) says: `error COBOL0890: CURRENCY SIGN:
a hexadecimal currency literal requires the PICTURE SYMBOL phrase (ISO §2023:12.3.7.3 r19)`.

Expected at 2002: the program compiles. X"40" is one character ('@' in an ASCII-based compile-time set), which §2002:12.2.6.2
SR19's excluded list does not name. The refusal is right at 2014 and 2023.

**Citations** (each run through `python scripts/spec/cite.py --check`, verdict OK):
- §2014:12.3.6.2 SR19: "If literal-7 is an alphanumeric or national literal in hexadecimal format, the PICTURE SYMBOL phrase shall be present in the CURRENCY SIGN clause" (label mode; this is the rule's first appearance);
- §2023:12.3.7.3 SR19: the same words;
- §2002:12.2.6.2 SR18: "Literal-7 shall be an alphanumeric or national literal that is not a figurative constant.";
- §2002:12.2.6.2 SR19: "If the PICTURE SYMBOL phrase is not specified, literal-7 shall consist of a single character".

§2002:12.2.6 has no rule naming hexadecimal literals (`grep -c hexadecimal` over the 400 lines of the clause gives 0).

**Fix:** gate SR19 from 2014. Sweep the same file's other 2014+ rules (SR22's wording, SR26, NOTE 1) for edition gates,
and check what 1985 says of the CURRENCY SIGN literal.
