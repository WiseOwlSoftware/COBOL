# PB689 - LANDED 2026-09-05 - sixty rules the denominator never counted

Found 2026-09-05 by the `PB329` implementer (report §"New defects" N2) while looking for §14.9.51.4 GR23's
inventory row to close - there was none. Its scan reported `14.9.51.4 spec ordinals 1-42, count 42 | inventory
max 16, count 16`, with interior headings `['## SEQUENTIAL FILES', '## 15 Intrinsic functions']`. The note said
"at least 26 rows in this clause alone, and the implementer measured only three clauses". Measured over the whole
spec, it was **sixty rows across five clauses**.

## The mechanism

`scripts/spec/extract_rule_catalog.py` closed a rule block at ANY markdown heading:

```python
if ANY_HEADING.match(line) and not HEADING.match(line):
    flush(); cur = None; continue
```

That line is not arbitrary - it exists because §16.2.2.2, the last rule block in the standard's body, otherwise
ran straight through `## Annex A (normative)` and turned every `N.` line in Annexes A-F into a fake rule (601
phantoms). But the standard also prints **in-clause GROUP LABELS** inside its rule lists - `SEQUENTIAL FILES`,
`RELATIVE FILES`, `INDEXED FILES`, `FORMAT 3`, `ALL FORMATS`, `FORMATS 1, 2 AND 4` - and the transcription had
rendered NINE of them as `##` headings. Every rule after such a label was lost.

The same label had a second, opposite defect: read as PROSE (which is what the 209 correctly-transcribed plain
ones were) it was spliced onto the END of the preceding rule's text. Seven catalog rows read
`"... is set to '44'. RELATIVE FILES"` - the exact contamination `FURNITURE` exists to prevent.

## The audit - `scripts/spec/audit_catalog_coverage.py` (new)

The catalog's completeness critics all ask *"did this block yield SOMETHING?"*, and a block that yields 16 of 42
passes every one of them because 16 is not zero. The new audit COUNTS instead: per clause, the column-0 ordinal
lines the transcription prints against the rows the catalog holds, plus every unnumbered interior heading inside
a numbered clause. Measured over the whole spec on the pre-fix tree - **9 interior headings, 5 clauses, 60 rules**:

| clause | printed | catalog | short by | interior heading(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| §14.9.51.4 WRITE | 42 | 16 | **26** | `## SEQUENTIAL FILES` |
| §14.9.1.4 ACCEPT | 29 | 12 | **17** | `## FORMAT 3` |
| §8.3.3.6.4 figurative constants | 10 | 3 | **7** | `## FORMAT 1` `## FORMAT 2` `## FORMAT 3` `## FORMAT 4` |
| §13.18.38.4 OCCURS | 18 | 13 | **5** | `## FORMAT 4` |
| §14.9.22.4 INSPECT | 28 | 23 | **5** | `## FORMAT 3` `## FORMAT 4` |

Nothing else in the spec disagrees: every other rule-block clause holds exactly the ordinals it prints. The
audit's `--check` is non-zero on either finding.

## The transcription question, answered page by page

Rendered and measured in the PDF text layer, never by pattern. **Every one of the nine is printed in BODY TYPE**
- Cambria 10.7pt, flags=4, at the rule-number margin - typographically indistinguishable from the rule text
above and below it, where a real clause heading is Cambria,**Bold** 11.7pt flags=20 (`14.9.51.4  General rules`,
PDF p817):

| label | PDF page | printed folio | typography |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SEQUENTIAL FILES` §14.9.51.4 | 820 | 790 | Cambria 10.7 f=4 @ x0=63.49 (GR16 above: x0=63.50; GR17 below: x0=63.49) |
| `FORMAT 1..4` §8.3.3.6.4 | 146 | 116 | Cambria 10.7 f=4 @ x0=63.50 - and `FORMAT 5/6/7` in the SAME list are plain lines |
| `FORMAT 4` §13.18.38.4 | 463 | 433 | Cambria 10.7 f=4 @ x0=80.02 |
| `FORMAT 3` §14.9.1.4 | 609 | 579 | Cambria 10.7 f=4 @ x0=80.03 |
| `FORMAT 3`, `FORMAT 4` §14.9.22.4 | 680 | 650 | Cambria 10.7 f=4 @ x0=63.47/63.49 - and `FORMAT 2` in the same list is plain |

So the standard prints NO heading there, and the transcription's own convention already agreed: **209 of the 218
labels were plain column-0 lines**, including `RELATIVE FILES` and `INDEXED FILES` sixty lines below the offender
in §14.9.51.4 itself. Two of the five clauses carried BOTH forms inside ONE rule list, which is the proof it was
transcription noise rather than structure. All nine were demoted to the plain form (byte-preserving; the file is
CRLF, 2 930 156 -> 2 930 129 bytes, CRLF count unchanged).

## The structural fix (rule 5) - one line kind, not one more heading exception

⛔ **The in-clause group label is now its own LINE KIND in the extractor**, skipped in ONE place ahead of both the
heading branch and the buffering branch, so the truncation and the contamination cannot come back independently.
It is **keyed on SHAPE, not on a vocabulary** - a list of label spellings is the hand-maintained-list
anti-pattern - and the shape was DERIVED by measurement: 301 all-caps standalone lines inside rule blocks, 37
distinct, **every single one a group label, zero false positives**. The optional `#` prefix is matched, so a
transcription that heading-ifies one is absorbed rather than obeyed, and every such absorption is REPORTED
(fatal under `--check`) instead of silently tolerated.

Proven independent of the transcription repair: run against the UN-repaired spec, the fixed extractor still
harvests all 4 371 rules AND exits 1 naming all nine labels.

## The gate - `tests/Cobol.Net.Tests.Unit/CatalogCoverageDriftTests.cs` (new)

Per clause, the ordinals the transcription prints must equal the rows the catalog holds - no segmentation
reasoning, no vocabulary, two counts that have to agree - plus zero unnumbered interior headings. It re-derives
the printed count from `specs/ISO_COBOL.md` itself rather than running the extractor, so it can contradict the
code that built the catalog. `TheseChecks_ActuallyFail_OnAFabricatedSpec` fires both predicates against inputs
built to break them (truncation, inflation, a heading-ified label, and an annex heading that must still end a
clause). **Fired RED once before the fix**, naming all five clauses and their exact deficits.

## Two downstream artifacts moved with it — and one of them CORROBORATES the finding independently

The full Unit suite caught both; neither was predicted, and both were regenerated rather than adjudicated.

- **`tests/version-matrix/annex-a2-undefined.json` (`extract_annex_a2.py`).** Annex A.2's undefined-element
  list cross-references rules BY NUMBER, and three of its citations had been resolving to nothing because the
  rule-ids did not exist: item 21 cites INSPECT GR21, item 65 cites WRITE GR19, item 66 cites WRITE GR25. ⚠
  **This is an INDEPENDENT WITNESS to the missing rules** — the standard's own annex was pointing at rules the
  catalog did not have, and the resolver had been silently dropping the references rather than failing. Three
  rule-ids gained, 64 of 66 items now resolving.
- **The `screen-handling-only` derived verdict (`derive_verdict_batch.py` → `record_verdicts.py`).**
  `DerivedVerdictDriftTests` requires every rule that selector reaches to carry `DOCUMENTED-NON-SUPPORT`,
  because Annex A.4.2 ACCEPT/DISPLAY screen handling is Not claimed (`docs/CONFORMANCE.md` §5, §4 item 4). The
  twelve new §14.9.1.4 rows are ACCEPT's format-3 screen rules, so twelve rows took the derived verdict; the
  other 156 records in the batch were already at it and re-applied as no-ops. The verdict is recorded WITHOUT
  a test-ref — the 156 standing rows are witnessed by `conformance:negative/a42-accept-screen-*`, and whether
  those negatives also witness these twelve is an adjudication this landing did not make, so the twelve stay
  GAP and `kb/Work/PB260` keeps the witness debt for the whole selected set. **GAP unchanged at 2 853.**
