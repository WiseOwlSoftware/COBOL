# PB381 — the citation audits' population stops at three segments

Recorded by the PB379 implementer (2026-09-03) as a "known limit, recorded not fixed", and moved here because a
limit written inside a landed note is a defect no work list can see (CLAUDE.md rule 8).

`audit_code_citations.py` and `audit_doc_citations.py` both admit a clause number only when it has at least two
dots (`§8.8.4`, `§14.9.25.3`). The floor exists for one reason: a design doc's own section numbers (`§4.2`,
`§0.5`) look like clauses and are not the standard. The cost is that every genuine two-segment citation —
`§15.2` (Types of functions), `§13.16` (Data description entry), `§8.6` (Scope of names), `§9.1` (Files) — is
invisible to both audits: a phantom `§15.9` would not be reported, and a quotation filed under `§15.2` is never
checked against §15.2's text. PB379 repaired `docs/CONFORMANCE.md`'s `(item 5, …)` into `(ISO §15.2 item 5, …)`
and that repaired citation is exactly one the audit cannot see.

## What closes it

Widen the floor to two segments, guarded the way the sibling already guards prose that is not the standard: the
`NOT_THE_STANDARD` predicate (a `§` that resolves to a heading of the FILE it sits in, or sits in a file whose own
headings are numbered) decides "this repo's section" versus "the standard's clause", rather than the dot count.
Then re-run both audits over the tree; every new finding is either a phantom to repair or a self-reference the
guard should have caught — say which. `--self-test` legs for a two-segment phantom, a two-segment misfiling, and a
two-segment self-reference in a numbered doc. Prove the failure branch by planting a phantom `§15.9` and watching
it counted.
