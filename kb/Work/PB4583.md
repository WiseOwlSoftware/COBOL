# PB4583 — the turn cap is a sentence in a brief, not a limit of the runner

**Sites:** `scripts/fuzz_triage.py#turn_cap` (the cap a role gets), `scripts/fuzz_triage_briefs/common.md` ("Turn cap <<TURN_CAP>>. At the cap: write the report, return `SPLIT`" — the cap reaches the model as prose only), `.claude/workflows/fuzz-triage.js` (the Workflow that ran nights 7 to 9, whose adjudicator subagents carried `maxTurns` 160 according to step 1). The runner that drove night 10's sessions is not in the repository: night 10 ran as standalone API sessions (14 adjudicator sessions of 18 to 224 turns, nine refuter sessions), a different driver from the Workflow's, kept outside the tree.

**Observed.** Under the Workflow (nights 7 to 9) the adjudicators were subagents with `maxTurns` 160, a limit the runner enforces. Night 10's adjudicator slices ran as API sessions, and three passed the cap: 193, 224 and 200 turns, at a cost of $67.75 for the adjudicators and $19.27 for the refuters. The brief told them to stop at 160 and write the report; nothing stopped them, and the cost of a session that ignores its brief is unbounded. The overrun is silent: no record says a slice exceeded its cap unless someone counts turns afterward (step 1 did).

**Fix shape.** Enforce the cap in the driver: count the assistant turns of each session and end the session at `turn_cap(role)` with the `SPLIT` instruction injected as the last message, recording the overrun as a failure in the run's checkpoint. Track the driver in `scripts/` (one tool for both the Workflow and the API path) so the cap, the stop files and the cost meter come from `fuzz_triage.py` and not from a copy outside the tree.
