# PB5482 - at --std 85 a user-defined word spelled as a COPY text-name or library-name of the same source program is accepted (COPY IX beside INDEXED BY IX): 1985 puts text-names and library-names in their own disjoint sets of user-defined words

**Mechanism.** "Within a given source program, but excluding any contained program, the user-defined words are grouped into the following disjoint sets" (§1985:IV.4.2.2.1.1), library-names and text-names among them, and "All user-defined words, except segment-numbers and level-numbers, can belong to one and only one of these disjoint sets". The COPY-equivalence of §1985:XII.2.4 GR1 states the order of compilation; it does not take the COPY statement's words out of the source program, and ISO 2002 confirms the 1985 rule by lifting it: "Text-names and library-names are now system-names, instead of user-defined words, and do not have to be different from user-defined words" (§2002:F.2, item 135). The preprocessor resolves text-names and library-names and they never reach the one word-type funnel `DataBinder.CheckOneTypePerWord`, so an index-name (SR14), a data-name or any other user-defined word spelled as one compiles. The rest of SR14 conforms (a repeated index-name COBOL6863; an index-name that is also another kind of word COBOL2692). The disjoint-set rule of IV.4.2.2.1.1 has no row of its own in the 1985 catalog, so this note claims the SR14 row and names the wider sibling (u-dn) for the fixer's sweep.
