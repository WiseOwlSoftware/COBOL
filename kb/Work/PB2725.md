**Landed (wave 20262143, group A, 2026-11-23).** Re-probe at c3eb4a6a05: the four tokens were listed in four grammar rules
(`nonNumericLiteral`, `concatOperand`, `allLiteral` and `residueNonNumericLiteral`), and 30 hand-written lines in 10 files read
them by accessor (`.STRINGLIT()` and its three twins), two of them with `CobolLiteral.DecodeHex` beside `Decode`. The
VersionConformancePass reads the note names are gone (kb/Work PB5416 moved the literal gates to `LiteralScreen.GateFormat`).
The fix: one `quotedLiteral` rule, named by the other four (`residueNonNumericLiteral` deleted); one reader,
`Parsing.QuotedLiteral` (it lives in `Frontend/Parsing`, not `Frontend/Common`, because it reads a generated parser
context and `Frontend/Common` depends on no parser type); the class and format by the written prefix through
`CobolLiteral.FormOfToken`, which shares the prefix-to-class map with `ClassOf`; and `ConcatFolder.CategoryOf` as the one
class-to-category map (also used by `FoldAllLiteral` and `BoundAllLiteral.Of`). `QuotedLiteralDriftTests` pins the one
rule and the absence of the accessors. Oracle: `=== ARCH-ORACLE: IDENTICAL — 23447 cases (baseline c3eb4a6a0597, new
09c8fea4aea6-dirty) ===`. The lexer-level token tables (`LiteralTokens.CategoryOf`, `SourceTextClassifier`) map token
types, not parse nodes, and stay: they are the lexer's own view, read before any tree exists.
