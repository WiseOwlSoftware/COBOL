**The rule, at 2023 (the citation the note lacked).** `§2023:8.3.5 SR2`: "The COBOL characters comma and semicolon,
immediately followed by a space, are separators that may be used anywhere the separator space is used" (cite.py OK). 2023
drops 1985's "Except when the comma is used in a PICTURE character-string"; the exception never reached this case, since a
comma the string contains is never followed by a space (`§1985:IV.4.2.1 SR9`: "PICTURE character-strings are delimited
only by the separators space, comma, semicolon, or period", cite.py OK). 2002 (frozen; no case, no verdict): the same words
stand as rule 2 of `§2002:8.3.2` ("immediately followed by a space, are separators that may be used anywhere the separator
space is used", cite.py OK), so 2002 admits the form too, and the fix, edition-blind, gives it.

**The fix, two halves of one rule in `CobolLexer.g4`'s PICMODE.** (1) `PIC_WS` skips the separator space in each of its
spellings, `( [ \n] | [,;] [ \n] )+`: before, it took only spaces and line ends, and PIC_STRING took the `,` or `;` as the
string's first character. Longest match decides it: `, ` is two characters and PIC_STRING stops after one. (2) In
`PICTURE IS, X(2)` the match `IS,` outran PIC_IS's `IS`, so PIC_STRING won it; its action (now the member
`OnPictureString`, the old inline action) gives back the trailing separator as before and, when what is left is the word
IS, hands the token to `OnPictureIs` (the IS keyword once, a second IS the character-string, kb/Work PB5213). `PIC ,999`
is unchanged: no space follows the comma, so it is the string `,999` and prints `,005` for 5.
