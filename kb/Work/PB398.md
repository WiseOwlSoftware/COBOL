# PB398 — EVALUATE's selection-object figure lost two alternatives in transcription to grammar

## 1. The mechanism

`evaluateWhenItem` (`src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolControlFlow.g4:196-201`) is exactly four
alternatives — `valueRange | valueOperand | condition | ANY` — and the printed general format of 14.9.13.2
stacks **ten** alternatives inside one select-exactly-one brace. Two of them were dropped on the way into the
grammar, and neither drop is a missing convenience: each one makes a whole family of rules unreachable.

**(a) partial-expression-1 has no alternative at all.** None of the four can begin with a bare relational
operator, a class condition with its identifier removed, or a sign condition with its identifier removed —
which is precisely the shape 14.9.13.3 SR5 *defines* a partial-expression to be. `EVALUATE WS-N WHEN > 5` is
not an exotic dialect extension; it is the first shape SR5's own list names and the ordinary way to write a
threshold arm, and it dies in the parser. Five rules fall on this one absence — SR5 (the definition), SR8 (the
rewrite), 14.9.13.4 GR4 a) 2. (the execution semantics), SR7 d) (the correspondence restriction plus the
"would a conditional expression result" well-formedness test) and SR6 e) (the subject reclassification that
applies only when the object *is* a partial expression). It also leaves one of Table 15's nine rows
permanently unreachable: `EvaluateOperandCombinations` carries a `PartialExpression` row
(`src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Binding/EvaluateOperandCombinations.cs:26`) that no operand can ever classify into —
a dead lookup, and therefore an unverified one.

**(b) range-expression's trailing `[ IN alphabet-name-1 ]` phrase is absent.** `valueRange` is transcribed as
`valueOperand (THRU | THROUGH) valueOperand` (`src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolExpressions.g4:37-39`)
with the phrase simply gone. The IN phrase is the **only** way a program can say which collating sequence
orders an alphanumeric or national range; without it 14.7.8 rule 2 leaves that ordering "defined by the
implementor". So the omission both rejects legal source and removes the user's only lever over an otherwise
implementor-defined result. 14.9.13.3 SR3, whose entire content is a constraint *on* that phrase, has no code
site to live at.

## 3. Expected vs observed — RE-MEASURED ON HEAD (89044aa0), both parts

    $ cobol.exe p01-partial.cob --run          # 01 WS-N PIC 9(3) VALUE 7. EVALUATE WS-N WHEN > 5 ...
      p01-partial.cob(8,19): error COBOL0001: no viable alternative at input 'WHEN>'
      p01-partial.cob(8,19): error COBOL0001: cannot parse construct near '>'

Expected: SR5 classifies `> 5` as a partial-expression; SR7 d) permits it against an identifier subject; SR8
rewrites the pair to condition-2 `WS-N > 5` with the subject treated as TRUE; GR4 a) 2. evaluates it. WS-N is
7, so the first WHEN is selected and the program prints `GT5`. Observed: two parse errors, no executable.

    $ cobol.exe p03-in-alphabet.cob --run      # ALPHABET MY-ALPHA IS STANDARD-1; WHEN "A" THRU "M" IN MY-ALPHA

      p03-in-alphabet.cob(12,32): error COBOL0307: unexpected 'IN'. A period may be missing ...
      p03-in-alphabet.cob(13,16): error COBOL0001: unexpected 'DISPLAY'
      p03-in-alphabet.cob(14,14): error COBOL0307: unexpected 'WHEN'. A period may be missing ...
      p03-in-alphabet.cob(16,12): error COBOL0306: unexpected 'END-EVALUATE' ...

Expected: SR3 permits alphabet-name-1 here (both literals are class alphanumeric and STANDARD-1 defines an
alphanumeric collating sequence); GR2 delegates to 14.7.8, whose rule 2 makes MY-ALPHA the ordering; `"C"`
collates between `"A"` and `"M"`, so the program prints `IN-RANGE`. Observed: a parse error at `IN` and a
four-diagnostic cascade behind it — itself evidence that the failure is in the parser, not a later screen.

## 4. The code site and the shape of the repair

- `src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolControlFlow.g4:196-201` — `evaluateWhenItem`, four alternatives.
- `src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolExpressions.g4:37-39` — `valueRange`, no trailing IN phrase.
- `src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Binding/Procedure/Verbs/EvaluateBinder.cs:149` — `BindWhenItem`, no partial-expression arm.
- `src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Binding/Procedure/Verbs/EvaluateBinder.cs:215-231` — the `valueRange` arm, which has
  no alphabet parameter to carry even if the grammar produced one.
- `src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Binding/Procedure/Verbs/EvaluateBinder.cs:116-134` — `ObjectOperandKind`, which never
  returns `EvaluateObjectOperand.PartialExpression`.

**Do not fix this as three separate implementations.** SR8 *is* the mechanism: splice the selection subject in
front of partial-expression-1 to form condition-2 and treat the subject as TRUE. Add one alternative to
`evaluateWhenItem` and **one arm in `BindWhenItem` that performs the SR8 rewrite**, and GR4 a) 2. (evaluate the
resulting conditional expression), SR7 d) (well-formedness — the rewrite either produces a conditional
expression or it does not) and SR6 e) (the subject becomes an identifier) all become computable from that one
rewrite instead of needing three checks. For the IN phrase, thread the alphabet through `valueRange` into
`BoundRangeMembership`/`CheckedRelational` as a collating-sequence parameter rather than a boolean — the same
parameter the VALUE clause's THROUGH phrase needs, since 14.7.8 says the two share this specification.

## 5. The sibling sweep the fixer must do

- **`valueClauseRange` is the twin** (`CobolExpressions.g4:42-44`) and 14.7.8's opening sentence says its
  specification "applies to THROUGH phrases specified in the VALUE clause and the EVALUATE statement". If the
  IN phrase is added to `valueRange` only, the two ranges diverge on the one rule that says they must not.
- The alphabet must reach `CobolString.ThruMember` and `ConditionBinder.CheckedRelational`, which today order
  by the implementor default; a range under a locale-associated alphabet takes LC_COLLATE (14.7.8 rule 2).
- Landing the partial-expression alternative **makes two Table 15 rows live for the first time**
  (`Partial-expression`, and `Boolean-expression` once PB400's length test lands). `ScreenPairing`
  (`EvaluateBinder.cs:86-95`) declines whenever either classifier returns null, so the new arm must also teach
  `ObjectOperandKind` to return `PartialExpression`, or the row stays dead and the drift test stays green over
  a column nothing can reach.
- Edition gate: partial-expression and the national class are 2002 additions; the IN alphabet-name phrase
  likewise. Adding a grammar alternative changes what parses at `--std 85`, so run the edition-gate sweep.

## 7. LANDED — one rewrite, one range-alphabet resolver, and two dead lookups made reachable

Both halves are repaired, and both are repaired as the structural move the note asked for.

**(a) partial-expression-1 is a CONDITION WITH ITS LEFTMOST OPERAND MISSING, and SR8's splice is §8.8.4.12's
subject insertion.** `ConditionBinder.BindPartialExpression` seeds the `AbbrevCarry` — the holder that already
carries §8.8.4.12.4 GR1's "last preceding stated subject" through an abbreviated combined relation — with the
selection subject, and the leading `partialComparison` consumes it. That is why `WHEN > 5 AND < 10` needs no code
of its own: SR7 d) licenses the compound form and GR1 already knew how to read the tail. The grammar is a
four-rule spine (`partialExpression` / `partialXorExpression` / `partialAndExpression` / `partialComparison`)
whose TAILS are the very sub-rules the condition tiers use, so the two spines cannot accept different operators;
`PartialExpressionSpineDriftTests` EXTRACTS each condition tier's tail from the grammar and requires the partial
tier's to be identical, because a hard-coded expectation would rot the first time a connective is added. The rule
is deliberately NOT folded into `comparisonExpression`: that rule is shared by every IF / PERFORM UNTIL / SEARCH,
and DEVLOG-621 is the measured cost of widening it. §14.9.13.3 SR5's class and sign shapes are bound by the SAME
bodies the WRITTEN forms use — `BindClassConditionOn` / `BindSignConditionOn`, extracted for this — so §8.8.4.4.3
SR4/SR8's boolean-operand guard, the SR2 LOCALE refusal, the §12.3.7 user-class arm, §8.8.4.4.4 GR3 a)'s coded-set
arm and §8.8.4.7.3 SR1's operand closure have no second copy that could drift from the first.

**(b) The `IN alphabet-name-1` phrase resolves at ONE site for BOTH clauses**, because §14.7.8 opens by governing
both in one sentence. `DataBinder.TryResolveRangeAlphabet` enforces both sentences of SR3 (COBOLNET1998 the
operand-class restriction, COBOLNET1999 the national-vs-alphanumeric class match — including §12.3.7.4 Table 6's
coded-set-only alphabets, whose collating column is empty; COBOLNET1997 a word that declares no alphabet) and
registers the carrier. **The phrase OVERRIDES the implementor default, PROGRAM COLLATING SEQUENCE included** —
rule 2's two arms are exclusive — which the golden pins with a range written `IN` an identity alphabet inside a
program whose PCS reverses the alphabet. It governs the RANGES only: a singleton condition-name value is compared
by §8.8.4.5.3 GR2's ordinary relation rules, so `RenderMembershipTest` takes two collate arguments and the golden
lists a singleton beside a range in one clause to pin the split.

**The VALUE clause's twin was a SILENT WRONG ANSWER, not a missing feature.** `valueClause` has parsed
`(IN IDENTIFIER)?` since kb/Work PB695 and DROPPED it: measured on this tree before the fix, `88 X VALUE "M" THRU
"A" IN AL` under `ALPHABET AL IS "ZYXW…A"` answered NO for the value "C" that AL's ordering puts inside the range.
It now carries `Condition88.Alphabet` through the same resolver. The SR3 screens run only where a THROUGH phrase
is actually written — that is what the rule is about — so `88 CN VALUE 1 IN AL1 WHEN SET TO FALSE 0`, conforming
source this corpus already pins, keeps compiling.

**A dead category reading, found by the sweep.** `CollatingSelection.OperandCategory` (the renderer's
`StringCategoryOf`, moved here so the binder cannot grow a second copy) answered NULL for a numeric literal, which
`ForComparison`'s alphanumeric fall-through then read as an ordinary group. Harmless in the renderer — §8.8.4.2.5
makes a numeric operand against an alphanumeric one an alphanumeric comparison anyway, and two numeric operands
never reach the string branch — but NOT harmless once a binder asks the class of a range: `WHEN 1 THRU 9 IN AL`
answered "alphanumeric" and slipped past SR3 entirely. §8.3.3.3.1: "Numeric literals are of the class and category
numeric."

**The carrier is a per-TYPE field, not an inline expression.** A WHEN pair is re-analysed on every execution of
its EVALUATE, so rendering `new AlphanumericCollation(new ushort[256]{…}, …)` inside the condition would allocate a
256-entry table per evaluation. `DataBinder.RangeCollations` allocates one `static readonly CobolCollation` per
named alphabet per runtime module, collapsed onto `__COLLATE` / `__COLLATE_NAT` when the alphabet IS the program
collating sequence and omitted entirely for an identity sequence; the bound tree carries the COBOL NAME and the
renderer maps it, so no generated identifier travels in a bound node. ⚠ The two collapsed cases first returned
EARLY without recording themselves, and the renderer's name lookup then missed — silently reinstating the very
default the phrase overrides. Measured (`A=OUT` where `A=IN` was derived), then fixed by recording every accepted
alphabet.

**Rows.** `SR-14.9.13.3-3`, `SR-14.9.13.3-5`, `SR-14.9.13.3-8` NOT-IMPLEMENTED → **CONFORMS**;
`SR-14.9.13.3-6` and `SR-14.9.13.3-10` (transferred here by PB400) PARTIAL → **CONFORMS** (GAP 2561 → 2556).
`GR-14.9.13.4-4` stays **PARTIAL** and its residue CHANGED: a) 2. is realized, and what remains is
boolean-expression-2 written with B-OPERATORS — staged grammar residue belonging to another construct, not to
this note. Table 15 now has no unreachable row: the Partial-expression row's four `Y` cells are measured by the
positive golden and its two blank cells by two negatives, each a COMPILE-time COBOLNET1634.

**Editions — the one judgment an owner may want to overturn.** No introduction gate fires, so the constructs parse
at every `--std`. The inventory records the rows 2002+, but that edge is INFERRED from the feature history: this
repo holds only the 2023 text, whose Annex E records no EVALUATE change. Gating on an unproven edge would REJECT
LEGAL SOURCE if the inference is wrong; not gating under-rejects at worst, and the surveyed implementations accept
the constructs in every dialect (the 2026-08-30 standing answer). Stated in the goldens' own headers and in
`docs/COBOLNET_CONDITIONS_EXCEPTIONS_DESIGN.md`, so it is overturnable in one line.

**Residue, named and NOT closed.** (i) `IN` is also the qualification connective, so over an identifier-4 both
readings are complete parses and ANTLR's greedy suffix loop takes the qualifier — `WHEN WS-LO THRU WS-HI IN AL`
still reports COBOLNET1639, exactly as before this landing. Flipping the grammar's preference would make the
opposite legal reading unreachable instead; only the resolved SYMBOL separates them (§8.4.1), which needs a
`ReferenceResolver` able to re-resolve without a trailing qualifier. (ii) A BARE user-defined class-name or
alphabet-name object (`WHEN MY-CLASS`) is indistinguishable from identifier-2 and still binds as identifier-2; the
`IS`-led spelling reaches the partial rule. Both are reported as their own mechanisms.
