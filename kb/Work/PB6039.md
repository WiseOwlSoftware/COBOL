# PB6039 - the floating-point literal's digit limits are 2023's at every edition

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/SpecialNamesBinder.cs:99` (`NormalizeNumericLiteral`),
`src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Common/NumericLiteral.cs:129` (`CheckFloatingPointForm`). The first holds the form check and
its messages, which name only the 2023 rules; the second the 36-digit and 4-digit limits.

**The rule.** §2002:8.3.1.2.2.2 rule 2: "The significand shall be 1 through 31 digits in length" and rule 3: "shall have
a maximum of three digits and no decimal point" (`cite.py --check "§2002:8.3.1.2.2.2" "The significand shall be 1
through 31 digits in length"`: OK). §2014:E.3 item 20 raises them to 36 and 4, the figures §2023:8.3.3.3.3 SR2/SR3
print. The floating-point literal is a 2002 introduction (construct row `floating-point-literal-2002`), so 1985 has no
such literal and is not affected.

**Repro.** `MOVE 1.2345678901234567890123456789012E1 TO W` (32 significand digits) with `01 W COMP-2`:
`run_probe.py --std 2002 --compile-only` exits 0 (`E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/w20262132h/fp32.cob`, run directories under
`E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/w20262132h/probes/`); the 2002 text refuses it. Same at 2014 (exit 0, correct there).

**Fix (owed).** The two limits are a function of the edition, in one place (the edition behaviour table or the form
check taking the edition), each message citing its own edition's rule (`EditionInfo.CiteRule`); a positive case at 2014
and a negative at 2002 for each limit.
