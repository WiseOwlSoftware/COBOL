**Fix.** The `integerLiteral` rule (`src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolExpressions.g4`) parses a sign written as
the literal's leftmost character (`{signedIntegerAhead()}? (PLUS | MINUS)`, the predicate in `CobolParserCoreBase`: the sign,
then the integer's digits, contiguous, §2023:8.3.3.3.2 SR2), and the one integer-n screen, `IntegerOperandPass.VisitIntegerLiteral`,
refuses it ONCE with the new COBOL7737 (`DiagnosticCatalog.IntegerOperandSigned`), citing the rule of the edition in force:
§1985:III.2 p142 at 85, §2002:5.4 item 1, §2014:5.5 item 1, §2023:5.5 SR1 (each `cite.py --check` OK). The zero refusal
(COBOL2386) now cites the same per-edition rule (`IntegerOperandRules.IntegerRule`; it cited §2023 at 2002 and 2014). Every
reader of the operand's value reads its digits (`IntegerLiteralParts.Digits`, Frontend), so a refused `-3` binds as 3 and
draws no cascade; messages quote the operand as written (`WrittenSource.Of`).

(the `reportRelativeSign` alternative is first; 1,100 report cases green). Goldens moved from the spec: pb721 (two),
