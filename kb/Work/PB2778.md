# PB2778 — the unit's stall watcher outlives its workflow

Found 2026-10-08 by the operator during the wind-down the owner asked for (a Claude Code update restart). Owner: "why
does the loop take so long to wind down ... what is the loop productively doing?"

## Measured (campaign unit, wave 1043, session b8bd95d4, supervisor pid 698540)

- 13:27 the unit started `stall_watch.py <wf_2ca5e648-fef dir>` in the background, as `scripts/orchestrator/units/wave.md`
  step 16 says ("run stall_watch.py beside it as the skill says").
- 14:32:51 STOP → STOP-UNIT + the loop's fleet stop. 14:38 the workflow's 10th agent returned (journal: 10 starts, 10
  results). 14:40:35 the unit wrote and schema-validated its handoff (split) and printed its final summary.
- The unit did not exit: `orchestrate.ps1` closes the unit's stdin only when the result is seen, `$bgTasks -eq 0`, and the
  unit has been idle `-IdleCloseSeconds` (20 s; the condition near line 328). The watcher was a live background task.
- 14:44 the operator killed the watcher; the unit ended at 14:44:43 ("exit 0, handoff split") and the loop with it.
  Left alone, the watcher's own IDLE exit (`--idle 600`: no agent in flight for 600 s, counted from 14:38) would have
  ended it at about 14:48, so the cost is up to ten idle minutes per unit that ran a fleet, at every wind-down and every
  normal unit end.

`stall_watch.py`'s own docstring already says what to do: "A workflow that finished normally delivers its completion
notice first; stop the watcher then, or its IDLE exit is one harmless false wake-up." The unit templates do not say it.

## Fix

1. `scripts/orchestrator/units/wave.md` (and every unit template or skill step that starts the watcher: the workstream
   skill's MANDATORY-PRACTICES and the campaign unit) — on the Workflow's completion notice, stop the watcher (TaskStop)
   before anything else, and never write the handoff while one runs.
2. The supervisor as the guarantee: once the unit's handoff is written and validated and the only background tasks left
   are stall watchers, treat the unit as idle (or tell it to stop them), so a template that forgets cannot hold the loop.
3. Sweep: any other background poller a unit starts (a CI wait, a gate wait) has the same shape; name each and its exit.
