## Observation (not verified)
PB1393 moved the §8.3.3 literal length rule into one token screen, `CobolLiteral.LengthViolation`
(`src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Common/CobolLiteral.cs`, called from
`src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Validation/LiteralScreenPass.cs#Screen`). It enforces 8,191 character positions at every
`--std`: ISO/IEC 1989:2023 §8.3.3.2.3 SR1 ("shall be less than or equal to 8,191 alphanumeric character
positions", `cite.py --check` OK). The implementer recalls that ANSI X3.23-1985 limited a nonnumeric literal to 160
characters, but the repository holds no 1985 text to verify it.

## To decide
Establish the COBOL-85 limit from `docs/VERSION_CHANGE_REFERENCE.md` or a 1985 source. If it was 160, the screen
under-rejects a 161–8,191 character literal at `--std 85`. The fix would be an edition-keyed bound on the one
constant (`CobolLiteral.MaxLiteralPositions`) plus a VCR row, not a second check. Repro: a 200-character
alphanumeric literal in a MOVE compiled with `--std 85` compiles clean today.

## Re-filed as a defect (analyses sweep, 2026-10-23)

Adjudicated read-only by an Opus cobol-adjudicator on main e7cf38ce4; accepted by the Mythos session.

The literal length cap is not edition-keyed: CobolLiteral.MaxLiteralPositions = 8191 is applied at every --std, so a 161..8,191-position alphanumeric literal is accepted at --std 85 and --std 2002 where those editions cap it at 160 (GnuCOBOL 3.2 cobol85.conf / cobol2002.conf literal-length: 160; cobol2014.conf 8191; ISO 2023 Annex E lists no change, so 2014 already had 8,191). Precedence: the repo holds no 1985/2002 text; the owner's R61 method (GnuCOBOL per-standard dialect files) dates the edge at 2014. Fix as the note says: one edition-keyed bound beside CobolWordRule's word-length ceiling (an edition-parameterized LengthViolation, read by LiteralScreen and by ConcatFolder.ResultLengthViolation so the concatenation result shares it), a VCR row (limit 160 through 2002, 8,191 from 2014), the constructs.json row, goldens: 160 accepted / 161 rejected at 85 and 2002, 8,191 accepted at 2014. Sibling: CobolWordRule already gates word length; numeric-literal 18 digits at 85 is already gated (COBOLNET0802).

Evidence: Re-run: a 200-position alphanumeric literal in MOVE compiles clean at --std 85 and 2002 (the 19-digit numeric literal in the same probe IS rejected at 85, COBOLNET0802). GnuCOBOL 3.2's per-standard dialect files (the R61 method, VCR rows 7.27/7.28): literal-length 160 in cobol85.conf AND cobol2002.conf, 8191 in cobol2014.conf; 2023 Annex E does not list the limit, so 2014 = 2023 = 8,191.

Rule: §8.3.3.2.3 1) (2023: 8,191; checked by the note); 1985/2002 limit from GnuCOBOL 3.2 dialect files per kb/Work R61's method
