(`src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Common/CobolLiteral.cs`, called from

characters, but the repository holds no 1985 text to verify it.

# PB1615 - LANDED (wave 20261501 E)

## Landed 2026-10-25 (wave 20261501 E, with PB1601)

**Re-probe (w20261501e build).** A 200-position alphanumeric literal in a MOVE (fixed form, continued) compiled clean at `--std 85`, 2002, 2014 and 2023 (probe `l1.cob`; `l161.cob`, `l8191.cob` likewise); 8,192 positions drew COBOLNET0814 at every edition. The note reproduced.

**Edition evidence (derived, VCR row 7.41).** `cite.py --check` OK for 8.3.3.2.3 1), 8.3.3.4.3 1), 8.3.3.5.3 1) and 8.8.3.2 2) (all print 8,191; the 2023 text). The repo holds no 1985, 2002 or 2014 text, so the edge follows the method of owner decision kb/Work R61: GnuCOBOL 3.2's per-standard dialect files carry `literal-length` 160 in `cobol85.conf` and `cobol2002.conf` and 8191 in `cobol2014.conf`; Annex E lists no change, so 2014 = 2023. The row states the derivation so it can be overturned in one line.

**Fix.** The bound is the edition's, in ONE place: `CobolLiteral.MaxPositions(dialectLevel)`, its edge read from the constructs row `literal-length-8191-2014` through `ConstructRegistry.IsAvailable` (so the number and the version matrix that samples it cannot disagree; no `>= 2014` copy). `CobolLiteral.LengthViolation(raw, dialectLevel)` and `SyntaxViolation(raw, dialectLevel)` (asked by `LiteralScreen.Screen` with the unit's edition) and `ConcatFolder.ResultLengthViolation(folded, dialectLevel)` (asked by `ConcatFolder.Fold`, so the `&` expression, the concatenated ALL literal-1 and the literal they equal agree) all read it. A relaxation, not an introduction (the word-length ceiling's posture): the longer literal draws COBOLNET0814 / COBOLNET1545, an error also under `--permissive`, with a message naming the edition and where 8,191 begins; above 8,191 every edition refuses. The old public `MaxLiteralPositionsText` property is replaced by `MaxPositionsText(dialectLevel)` (every caller changed; no shim). The fast path for short literals now skips the registry entirely under a quarter of the lowest bound and keeps the BX exception (four boolean positions per digit).

**Goldens.** `8.3.3.2-Alphanumeric-literals/pb1615_literal_160_positions` (the boundary, accept 85-2023), `pb1615_literal_over_160_positions` (200 positions, accept 2014-2023, names the construct) and the negative `pb1615-literal-above-160-below-2014` (COBOLNET0814 at 85 and 2002). Unit and conformance tests pin every class (alphanumeric, hexadecimal, boolean, boolean-hex, national), the `&` expression and the concatenated ALL literal-1 at 160/161 for 85 and 2002 and at 2014. `FixedFormLayout.Source` gained a `classic` mode (the 1985 hyphen-in-column-7 continuation, no `>>` directive) because the floating continuation the helper used is itself a 2002 introduction and could not carry an 85 test.

**Siblings swept.** `grep` of `8191|8,191|MaxLiteralPositions` over `src` and `tests`: the other readers are the number's documentation (`CobolIntrinsics.Text.cs`, which explicitly says a returned value is NOT bounded by the literal maximum) and the 2023-edition tests (`LiteralScreenTests`, `DirectiveLiteralScreenTests`, `NationalBooleanLiteralTests`, which compile at 2023). A scan of `conformance-suite/cases` and `tests` for literals longer than 160 positions found only comments and one free-form line-length case accepted at 2023. Numeric literals (18 digits at 85, COBOLNET0802) were already edition-keyed. Directive-operand literals reach the same screen (a directive is a 2002 introduction, so their bound is the 160 / 8,191 of 2002 / 2014).
