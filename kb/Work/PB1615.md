Filed 2026-09-26 by the **train-62 lander** from the w61g implementer report (PB1393), lead 1.

## Observation (not verified)
PB1393 moved the §8.3.3 literal length rule into one token screen, `CobolLiteral.LengthViolation`
(`src/Cobol.Net.Frontend/Common/CobolLiteral.cs`, called from
`src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/Validation/LiteralScreenPass.cs#Screen`). It enforces 8,191 character positions at every
`--std`: ISO/IEC 1989:2023 §8.3.3.2.3 SR1 ("shall be less than or equal to 8,191 alphanumeric character
positions", `cite.py --check` OK). The implementer recalls that ANSI X3.23-1985 limited a nonnumeric literal to 160
characters, but the repository holds no 1985 text to verify it.

## To decide
Establish the COBOL-85 limit from `docs/VERSION_CHANGE_REFERENCE.md` or a 1985 source. If it was 160, the screen
under-rejects a 161–8,191 character literal at `--std 85`. The fix would be an edition-keyed bound on the one
constant (`CobolLiteral.MaxLiteralPositions`) plus a VCR row, not a second check. Repro: a 200-character
alphanumeric literal in a MOVE compiled with `--std 85` compiles clean today.
