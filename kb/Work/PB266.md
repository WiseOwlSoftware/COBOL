# PB266 — r3 written in two places out of four

Found by the arithmetic-wave review fleet over the D-B/D-C/PB194/PB195 working tree, 2026-08-31, and fixed in the change set that landed the wave. Every number below was re-derived independently by the implementer (a 34-digit emulation of `CobolDec.cs` validated against the shipped `pb167` goldens, and 80–120-digit `Decimal` truth) BEFORE the confirming run, and then confirmed by it.

## The rule

`python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 8.8.1.5.4 "When operand-2 is less than zero, the result shall be
equivalent to the evaluation of the arithmetic expression"` → **OK**, and
`--check 8.8.1.5.4 "(1 / (operand-1 ** FUNCTION ABS (operand-2)))"` → **OK**.

r3 is an EQUIVALENCE, not implementor latitude: r2's latitude is scoped *"When the value of operand-2 is greater
than zero"*. Under §8.8.1.5.2's per-operation 34-digit rounding the mandated expression is a DIVISION whose
divisor is the same development at |operand-2| — one extra correctly-rounded division, and no negated exp
argument — so it is a different value from `exp(-|p| × ln b)`. That the standard means this at digit granularity
is settled internally: r2d spells `((o1*o1)*(o1*o1))` rather than `(((o1*o1)*o1)*o1)`, a distinction that exists
only because each operation rounds.

## What the code did

`Pow` spelled r3 for a within-bound integer exponent (`Div(One, acc)`) and `PowCore` spelled it at exactly −½
(`Div(One, Sqrt(b))`). The other two arms — the general non-integer one and the past-loop-bound integer escape —
carried the SIGN INSIDE the exponent handed to `PowCore`.

| expression | computed | r3 owes |
|---|---|---|
| `2 ** -0.25` | 0.8408964152537145430311254762332146 | …2332141 |
| `7 ** -0.125` | 0.7840842766892245016410129437845561 | …7845559 |
| `10 ** -0.3` | 0.5011872336272722850015541868849460 | …8849459 |
| `3 ** -1.5` | 0.1924500897298752548363829268339865 | …8339863 |
| `2 ** -2.5` | 0.1767766952966368811002110905262118 | …5262117 |
| `1.0000001 ** -600001` | 0.9417644422330979028651558768075958 | …075964 |
| `1.0000001 ** -500000` | agrees | (the within-bound arm) |

The last two are the loop bound at 500 000, and the discontinuity across it was the second violating arm.

## Measured, before and after (probe `za1r3p.cob`, `--std 2023`, `ARITHMETIC IS STANDARD-DECIMAL`)

```
                                              BEFORE   AFTER
IF 2 ** -0.25     = 1 / (2 ** 0.25)             NE      EQ
IF 7 ** -0.125    = 1 / (7 ** 0.125)            NE      EQ
IF 2 ** -0.5      = 1 / (2 ** 0.5)              EQ      EQ    (the -1/2 short-circuit)
IF 1.0000001 ** -600001 = 1 / (…** 600001)      NE      EQ
IF 1.0000001 ** -500000 = 1 / (…** 500000)      EQ      EQ
COMPUTE DLT = (2 ** -0.25 - 1/(2 ** 0.25)) * 10 ** 33
                                 into 9V9(4)   05000   00000
```

The last line matters: the divergence was NOT confined to a relation. An SDIDI subtraction of two near-equal
values is exact, so an ordinary cancellation lifted the 34th digit into a `DISPLAY`.

## The fix — one place

r3's reciprocal is hoisted to the top of `Pow`, over `PowMagnitude(b, |operand-2|)`. Nothing below ever sees a
negative exponent; `PowCore`'s −½ twin is deleted because r3 now reaches it through the same one division.
Deliberately the shape that makes the next arm automatic rather than the smallest diff (CLAUDE.md rule 5).

## Consequences pinned in the same commit

* An out-of-range NEGATIVE exponent now takes its EC name from r3's own expression, because the operation that
  leaves the range is the INNER power: `2 ** -600000` → EC-SIZE-OVERFLOW, `0.5 ** -600000` → EC-SIZE-UNDERFLOW.
  Before the hoist they answered the other way round. (`pb145_pow_range`'s R3OVER / R3UNDR legs.)
* `(-1) ** -10^20` now reaches its answer through a real `Div(1, 1)`, whose pre-scaled quotient normalizes to
  (10³³, −33). Same VALUE; §8.8.1.5.2 leaves the representation to the implementor, so
  `MinusOne_ToAnEvenNegativeExponentPastLongRange_IsPlusOne` compares by `CobolDec.Compare` — the precedent its
  own file's `TinyBase` case already set.

## ⚠ r3 is an IDENTITY requirement, not an accuracy one

The true 34-digit 2^−¼ is 0.8408964152537145430311254762332149. The old direct form was 3 ulp out; r3's
reciprocal form is 8. Honouring the rule makes this case slightly LESS accurate, which is precisely why the fix
had to be the literal expression and never a tolerance.

## The verdict flip that could not fail

`GR-8.8.1.5.4-3` was flipped PARTIAL → CONFORMS in the same wave on a note whose BOTH sentences were false, and
none of its five pins could reach a violating arm: pb167's two negative legs and
`MinusHalfExponent_IsR3sReciprocalOfTheSquareRoot` are all −½ (the one exponent `PowCore` short-circuited),
pb145's `B ** NN` and `MinusOne_…PastLongRange` are base −1 (the |b| = 1 parity shortcut), and
`HalfPastTheLoopBound_IsUnderflow_NotOverflow` uses a POSITIVE exponent. The row's note and pins are rewritten
with the fix.

Reach the finding added: §15.35.4 r1 makes `FUNCTION EXP10`'s equivalent expression `(10 ** argument-1)`, so
EXP10 with a negative non-integer argument inherited the same divergence through the same arm.
