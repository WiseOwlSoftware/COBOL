## The blind spot, demonstrated

`scripts/spec/cite.py --check 14.9.25.4 "When an unsigned numeric item is the receiving item, the absolute value
of the sending value is used, and no operational sign is generated for the receiving item."` → **OK**. The clause
is right and the text is really in it, so a comment reading `§14.9.25.4 GR8 — "the absolute value"` passes
`--check`, passes `audit_code_citations.py --check` (which screens the clause against the CONSTRUCT the comment
names) and passes `audit_doc_citations.py --check` (which screens a QUOTED fragment against the clause it is
filed under). **The GR number is never compared to anything.** Both audits report 0 on this tree while every site
below ships.

## The fix

Two halves, and the second is the one that matters.

1. Correct the eleven sites.
2. ⛔ **Extend the audit to the sub-rule level** so the next one is impossible rather than merely fixed. Where a
   citation carries a `GR<n>` / `SR<n>` (or `rule <n>`) alongside a quoted fragment, resolve the fragment to its
   OWN numbered item within the clause and compare — `cite.py` already reports that path (it printed
   `§14.9.25.4 6) 1.` for the fragment above, which is how the mismatch with `GR8` becomes decidable). ⚠ Its
   nested-list numbering is imprecise (three round-3 refuters each hit a label that renders one level off), so
   the comparison has to tolerate depth while still refusing a different TOP-level item — `GR8` vs `6) …` is a
   top-level disagreement and must go red; `6) 1.` vs `6) d) 2. b.` must not.

A guard at that level is also what would have kept R10's sweep honest: the note recorded a sweep, and no gate
could tell that it had covered 5 of 14.

**The audit.** `audit_code_citations.py` gains QUOTE-RULE: where a rule designator (`GR8`, `SR 3`, `rule 6`, a chain
`GR4/GR6/GR9`, a range `GR12-13`) stands beside a quotation, the quotation is resolved to its top-level rule with
`cite.py`'s own `rule_path` and the designator's number is compared with it. Only the top-level number is compared
(`6) 1.` and `6) d) 2. b.` are one rule). The first draft paired a quotation with the nearest chain and accused 63
sites, of which 5 were real; the measured calibrations that bring it to zero false positives on the tree are the
unambiguous-pairing rule (exactly one chain in reach, punctuation only between), the parenthetical-cite rule for a
chain after the quotation, the rule that a quotation filed under another clause just before it is that clause's,
elisions and phrases under three words, banner lines (`FORMATS 1 AND 2` heads the NEXT rule, and the path walk files
it under the last), and the draft/other-edition veto `OTHER_TEXT` (the 2002 post-ballot text numbers its rules
differently, by design). Each calibration is a silent case in `ORDINAL_SELF_TEST`, taken from the tree, next to the
two firing shapes (quote before the cite, quote after it) and the depth-tolerance case.

**Real sweep yield.** The audit's own first run on the tree found a ninth live shape that no note listed:
`kb/Work/PB206.md` quoted SR10's bit-group sentence as "sentence 3 of SR5" (SR5's sentence 3 is the national-group one;
SR10 is the boolean twin). Corrected.

**Left unfixed and why.** A citation with no quotation (`§14.9.25 GR8 — an unsigned receiver stores the magnitude`)
cannot be checked mechanically, because there is no text to resolve; the five such sites in this note were fixed by
hand, and the audit would not have found them. That limit is the audit's, and the sweep for the next occurrence is
the grep in the report: `GR8` beside `unsigned|magnitude|absolute` is now empty.
