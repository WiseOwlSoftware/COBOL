# PB39 — LANDED 2026-08-08 — 326 catalog rule-ids did not match the standard's own rule numbering, because a NESTED list mis-segmented the top-level one

> ⚙ **flags:** process

> **Found while admitting §9.3.8.2.3 (PB29's multi-sublist slice): the manifest predicted 8 rules and the
> extractor harvested 12.** The clause's raw ordinal sequence is `1,2,3,4,5, 1,2,3, 6,7,8,9` — nine top-level
> rules with a THREE-ITEM NESTED LIST inside rule 5's item c), which the transcription flattens onto column 0.
> `extract_rule_catalog.py` opens a new sub-list on the backwards jump (correct) and then **never returns to the
> parent**, so top-level rules 6–9 are filed as `GR-9.3.8.2.3-L2.6 … -L2.9` instead of `-6 … -9`.
>
> ⛔ **THIS IS PRE-EXISTING AND WIDESPREAD — 40 sub-lists, 326 rules, 8% of the catalog.** The signature is a
> sub-list whose ordinals are not contiguous-from-1. Worst cases: `SR §12.3.7.3 L7: [1,2,3,4,5, 18…32]` (fifteen
> top-level rules mis-filed), `GR §14.9.10.4 L2: [2, 8…21]`, `GR §12.3.7.4 L3: [2…6, 8…19]`.
>
> ⚠ **THE COUNT IS NOT AFFECTED, SO THE DENOMINATOR IS SOUND** — every rule is harvested exactly once and 3,981
> is right. What is wrong is the rule's IDENTITY: `SR-12.3.7.3-L7.18` *is* "§12.3.7.3 syntax rule 18", and nobody
> reading the standard would guess that id. For a project whose deliverable is spec↔code traceability, an id that
> does not match the standard's own numbering is the defect, not a cosmetic one.
>
> ⛔ **ATTEMPTED 2026-08-04 AND REVERTED — AND THE ATTEMPT FOUND THE MECHANISM.** A parent STACK (pop back when
> an ordinal continues the parent's run) is **not sufficient**, and that is proven rather than suspected: it took
> the mis-segmentation from 40 sub-lists / 326 rules to 23 / 121, and then **mis-split §15.68.3**, whose raw run
> is `1\) …5\) , 1. …7. , 6\) 7\)` — nine top-level rules around a SEVEN-item nested one. No arithmetic on the
> ordinals can tell the nested `6.` from the top-level `6\)`; the stack pops on the nested `6.` because it happens
> to equal parent-last + 1. A second cut using the delimiter promoted the nested pair and demoted the real one,
> i.e. inverted the clause. Both reverted; the catalog is byte-identical to its committed state and `--check` is
> clean.
>
> ⭐ **THE MECHANISM IS THE DELIMITER FORM, AND IT IS IN THE SOURCE.** Read from the raw transcription:
> §15.68.3's top-level rules are written `1\)` `2\)` … and its nested list `1.` `2.` … — the `ORDINAL` regex
> accepts BOTH, which is exactly why they collide. ⚠ **The form is per-BLOCK, not global**: the transcription
> uses `1.` as the TOP-LEVEL form in §8.8.3.2, §11.9.11.2, §13.18.24.3 and §14.9.30.3 (the `ORDINAL` comment
> records that history), so the rule is *"whatever form this block OPENED with is its top level; a change of form
> opens a nest, a return to it closes every open nest"*. Small and evidence-backed — but it must be re-measured
> against §15.68.3, §13.18.60.4, §12.3.7.4 and §14.9.13.4, whose raw runs are recorded in DEVLOG 1167.
>
> **THE MIGRATION** is unchanged and still tiny: ⚠ **It renumbers 326 ids,
> so it needs a migration** — but the exposure is measured and tiny: **only 2 adjudicated rows** use a
> mis-segmented id, `AR-15.68.3-L3.6` and `AR-15.68.3-L3.7`, which are §15.68.3 rules 6 and 7 — the NUMVAL-C
> digit caps PB33/PB34 landed the same day. Everything else is an untouched GAP row that the next
> `build_inventory` run would simply re-key.

### ✅ LANDED 2026-08-08 — the delimiter form decides, with TWO evidence rules the mechanism note did not have

> **The fix is in `extract_rule_catalog.py`: a stack of open levels `[form, sublist, last-ordinal]` replaces
> the backwards-jump arithmetic.** The DEVLOG-1167 delimiter rule alone was measured INSUFFICIENT before
> implementing — simulated over the whole transcription it demoted 32 correct top-level ids in §13.18.40.3
> alone — and the two missing evidence rules were read out of the transcription, not invented:
> · **Form drift**: a run may CHANGE delimiter form mid-list (§13.18.40.3 writes SR 1–5 as `N.` and SR 6–37 as
>   `N\)`; also §7.2.3.3, §10.7.3, §13.18.14.3, §13.18.62.4). An ordinal that strictly continues an open
>   level's run (n = last+1) belongs to that level regardless of form — form-matched levels win when two
>   continue with the same n (§9.3.6's paren parameter list vs its dot nest, both ending at 3).
> · **Flattened nested tails**: the transcription indents the HEAD of some nested lists and flattens the TAIL
>   to column 0 (§14.9.13.4 GR4a: `1\.–3\.` indented, `4.–6.` at column 0). A column-0 ordinal continuing the
>   most recent INDENTED run is that nested list's tail, not a rule. ⭐ **This found SIX rules the CURRENT
>   catalog filed as top-level that are not rules at all** — `GR-14.9.33.4-3`, `GR-14.9.37.4-3`,
>   `RV-15.30.3-3`, `RV-15.31.3-3`, `RV-15.95.4-5`, `RV-15.95.4-6` — all demoted to `L2.*`; §15.95.4's own
>   heading is SINGULAR ("Returned value rule") and corroborates. In §14.9.33.4/§14.9.37.4 the id `-3` now
>   names the standard's real rule 3, which had been sitting in a sub-list.
> **THE RESULT, all measured:** 3,981 rules before and after (the count contract held) · **257 ids changed**
> (239 promotions L→top, 6 demotions top→L, 12 sub-list rekeys) · migration applied by TEXT-KEYED diff of the
> old catalog against the new (rule text is untouched by segmentation, so it is the stable key) · **4
> adjudicated rows migrated, not the 2 this note predicted** — `RV-15.30.3-3` (DIVERGES) and `RV-15.31.3-3`
> (PARTIAL) were adjudicated in the R-wave AFTER this note was written; re-measuring the exposure before
> migrating is what caught them · build_inventory carried **421/421 verdicts, 0 added, 0 removed** · GAP
> 3,903 unchanged · `SpecTraceabilityInventoryDriftTests` 10/10.
> **THE DRIFT GUARD:** every column-0 ordinal must be EXPLAINED (level continuation · indented-run tail ·
> same-form restart · list opening at 1); an unexplained ordinal is reported per-clause and **fails
> `--check`**. Zero on the whole transcription, and **proven to fail** by injecting a `77\)` into §15.68.3
> before being trusted (exit 1, names the clause and ordinal).
> ⚠ **RESIDUE FILED AS PB55:** row-ification of nested items is transcription-layout-determined — a flattened
> nested item becomes a catalog row while its indented sibling is absorbed into the parent rule's text
> (§14.9.13.4's L2 holds items 4–6 of a six-item list whose items 1–3 are invisible). Pre-existing, count-safe,
> and a DENOMINATOR-class question, so it is its own note, not a silent rider on this fix.
