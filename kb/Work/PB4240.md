## Gap pass addendum (2026-10-31)

Found by the Mythos front-end review, gap pass, 2026-10-31 (GAP-REVIEW.md; artifact https://claude.ai/artifact/KaxkLe85tfZBZ3nZ1tDGTs). G5-7 (CORRECTED; the gap pass REVERSES this note's fix direction - the earlier text above is kept as filed). Under section 8.1.3.2 4) ("Extended letters in user-defined words are subject to the following rules") the Annex C fold governs user-defined words only; directive keywords match through 3) a) on basic letters alone. So the accepted U+0130 `>>IF`/`>>END-IF` are the OUTLIERS: the fix is to stop folding directive keywords, not to fold DEFINED. E.2 14) (deletion of mapping (0131,0069)) confirms such mappings fold user words, so the reading "never fold a basic-letter-target mapping" is refuted. The residual defect (diagnostics printing the folded spelling) is the gap-pass note on COBOL0901/COBOL0001 and U+0130/U+212A. **Owner question 2** (GAP-REVIEW.md section 5.3): "Confirm the reading that section 8.1.3.2 GR4's fold (Annex C, including the two basic-letter-target mappings 0130->i and 212A->k) governs user-defined words only ... and directive keywords are matched on basic letters alone?" Recommended: yes. Evidence: `G5/u130-*.cob`, review-G5.md.

**Owner decision, 2026-10-31 (Mythos session, answering GAP-REVIEW.md section 5.3 question 2):** the section 8.1.3.2 GR4 fold (Annex C, including 0130 to i and 212A to k) governs user-defined words only. `DİSPLAY` is neither the DISPLAY verb nor a legal data-name, and directive keywords match on basic letters alone: `>>IF` and `>>END-IF` are brought in line. This confirms the reversed fix direction.

## Review round 3 (wave 20261815 B, its second finisher, 2026-11-02): the declaration screen asks the USER-WORD question

**Finding R1-1 (BLOCKING), reproduced** on the round-2 build: `REPOSITORY. FUNCTION ALL INTRINSIC.` with `01 <U+0130>NTEGER PIC 9 VALUE 7.` compiled and
ran (it printed 7). Re-keying `IntrinsicCatalog` by `CobolKeywords.Comparer` made `DataBinder.DeclareUserWord` stop folding: it handed the WRITTEN user
word to the catalog (`FunctionWord.OfWrittenWord`), but a user-defined word is compared in the Annex C fold (ISO §8.1.3.2 4) b)), so
the data-name IS the user word INTEGER, which §8.3.2.1 5) / §12.3.8.3 SR12, SR13 forbid within the REPOSITORY scope (COBOL1649), as the basic spelling is.

**Fix.** `FunctionWord.OfUserWord` (the third and last factory) folds the user-defined word and then applies the directive to it; `DeclareUserWord`
is its one caller. A function name written as a keyword (`OfToken`, `OfWrittenWord`, `IntrinsicBinder`, `ConditionBinder`) still matches on the basic
letters. Sweep: every `IntrinsicCatalog.TryGet` caller (DataBinder.TryIntrinsicOfThisCompilation, IntrinsicBinder:130/618/631, ConditionBinder:269,
FunctionDeviceText:66 take a token-derived keyword; VersionConformancePass:173 folds first) and every `OfWrittenWord` caller (ReferenceResolver:2976, a
keyword-omitted function call, which is a function-name question) were read; the declaration funnel was the one user-word site.

**Lead R1-2 (fixed in the file touched anyway).** `CobolWordsMap.Is` documented itself allocation-free but called `Resolve`, whose pass-through now builds
the keyword spelling. `Denoted` is the one place GR2-GR4 are applied, before any spelling; `Resolve` spells its pass-through, `Is` compares the unspelled word
on the basic letters.

**Witnesses.** Goldens in `cases/8.1.3-COBOL-character-repertoire/`: `pb4240-all-intrinsic-name-spelled-with-dotted-capital-i-as-user-word` (SR13) and
`pb4240-named-intrinsic-name-spelled-with-dotted-capital-i-as-user-word` (SR12), COBOL1649 at 2002, 2014, 2023.
