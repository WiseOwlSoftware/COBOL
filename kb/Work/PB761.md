# PB761 — NATIONAL THROUGH — a decline whose citation answers a different question

Found 2026-09-06 in the PB728 implementer's report (`New defects (b)`), filed as **analysis** rather than a
straight defect because it carries two claims that resolve differently: the citation is misfiled (a fact,
verified below) and the stage is dischargeable (a strong reading that needs one build to confirm).
Verified against main `3d75a747`.

## ⛔ Harm flag — a deliberate departure from the filing brief, with the reason

The brief allocated this note `process-only`. **It is not process-only, and the flag is set from what was
verified.** §13.18.63.3 SR29 bans the THROUGH phrase for a **boolean** subject and for no other category; SR31
and SR32 both discuss national THROUGH literals as an ordinary case; so a national THROUGH range is **conforming
source**, and `Edition.Error(...)` at `DataBinder.cs:2594` refuses to compile it. That is
`rejects_legal_source: true` by the register's own definition, and marking it process-only would keep it out of
`work.py next` — the exact disappearance `feedback_measure_the_selectors_complement` records as having hidden
nine harmful items for months. The severity label stays **MINOR** (the construct is niche and the refusal is
LOUD — no wrong answer can escape it); the register ranks on what the defect DOES, not on the label, and this
one stops a conforming program from compiling.

⭐ **The code comment already concedes the point**: *"A national THROUGH range is **spec-legal**"*. The decline
is an implementation-capacity statement wearing a syntax rule's citation.

## What the standard prints — every clause re-derived with `cite.py --check`, none inherited

- `--check 13.18.63.3 "Alphabet-name-1 may be specified only when the literals specified in the THROUGH phrase
  are of class alphanumeric or national"` → **OK §13.18.63.3 31)**. The full rule reads:

  > 31) Alphabet-name-1 may be specified only when the literals specified in the THROUGH phrase are of class
  > alphanumeric or national. If literal-2 and literal-3 or literal-5 and literal-6 are of class national,
  > alphabet-name-1 shall reference an alphabet that defines a national collating sequence; otherwise,
  > alphabet-name-1 shall reference an alphabet that defines an alphanumeric collating sequence.

  ⛔ **This is a rule about the `IN alphabet-name-1` PHRASE** — when it may be written, and which kind of
  alphabet it must name. It says nothing about implementability, and it is not even reachable in the probe that
  triggers the diagnostic (`nat88c.cob` writes no `IN` phrase at all). It is a real clause answering a different
  question — `feedback_a_real_clause_can_answer_a_different_question` exactly, and the reason it survived is
  that `--check` PASSES on it: the text is genuinely there. **A citation that verifies is not thereby the right
  citation.**
  ⭐ Note the inversion: SR31 is the strongest evidence AGAINST the decline it is cited for. It contemplates
  national THROUGH literals as an ordinary, supported case and legislates the alphabet they order under.
- `--check 13.18.63.3 "If the category of the subject of the entry is boolean, the THROUGH phrase shall not be
  specified"` → **OK §13.18.63.3 29)**. **The only categorical THROUGH prohibition in the clause, and it names
  boolean alone** — which is what makes the national form legal source rather than merely undiscussed.
- `--check 13.18.63.3 "The manner of determining the range of values specified by the THROUGH phrase is
  described in 14.7.8"` → **OK §13.18.63.3 32)**. **This is the rule the stage actually stands in front of**:
  the obligation the compiler is declining to meet is §14.7.8's range determination for a national subject, and
  SR32 is the pointer to it. If the stage survives, it is SR32 + §14.7.8 that it should cite — never SR31.

## Is it dischargeable? The evidence, and the one thing that must still be built

**For:**

- `__COLLATE_NAT` and the `NationalCollation` machinery have been live since **[[PB678]]**.
- `ConditionRenderer.cs:72` already renders the national arm of a THROUGH range —
  `IsNationalOperand(n.Left) || IsNationalOperand(n.Lo) ? ctx.NatCollateArg : ctx.CollateArg` — routed through
  `RuntimeApi.ThruMember` (`:73-74`), which is §14.7.8's inverted-range / EC-RANGE-INVALID behaviour. The
  renderer is waiting for source the binder refuses to hand it.
- PB728's report reached the same conclusion independently from its own build.

**Against, or at least still open:**

- ⛔ The report's own reservation was *"discharging it properly pulls in SR31's `IN alphabet-name-1` phrase,
  whose grammar support I did not measure."* **That reservation is now answered, and the answer is [[PB555]]**
  (open): the grammar **does** parse the phrase (`CobolData.g4:621-624`), and `Condition88`
  (`Condition88.cs:11-25`) does not MODEL it — its whole state is `Name`, `Parent` and
  `List<(string Low, string? High)> Values` — so `BindCondition` **discards** alphabet-name-1 and literal-4 and
  every downstream consumer is structurally unable to see them. So it is a **model** gap, not a grammar gap, and
  PB555 owns it together with SR27's literal-4 screen as ONE restructuring.
- ⭐ **Therefore the discharge SPLITS cleanly, and does not need PB555 to start.** A national THROUGH range with
  **no** `IN` phrase orders under the national PROGRAM collating sequence, which `ctx.NatCollateArg` already
  supplies — that half can be discharged on its own. The `IN alphabet-name-1` half arrives with PB555's
  restructuring and not before; declining *that* form is what a staged diagnostic is legitimately for, and it
  would cite SR31 **correctly**, for the first time.

## Landed 2026-09-06 (landing train 18) — the stage is DISCHARGED

The landing order this note asked for is the order that happened: [[PB728]] + [[PB575]] came in first (train 18
row 1, reconciled onto [[PB741]]'s pair classifier), and their arrival is what forced this note to a decision
rather than an interim. Row 1 had extended the stage to the GROUP shape as its "honest interim", so the two
shapes would at least agree — but PB741's landed golden
`tests/conformance/2002/pb728_level88_group_usage_collating` asserts the national GROUP THROUGH range COMPILES
AND ANSWERS CORRECTLY under `ALPHABET REV-NAT FOR NATIONAL IS N"ABC"`. Extending a "recognized but not yet
implemented" stage over that turned a landed golden RED, which is the sharpest possible statement that the
stage is false. Making the two shapes agree by DECLINING BOTH would have been a capability regression and a new
refusal of conforming source; making them agree by discharging is the root fix.

**MEASURED, before deciding, with the guard disabled and the tree rebuilt** (`scratchpad/train-18/probe/`):

```

natthru_inv.cob  ALPHABET REV-NAT FOR NATIONAL IS N"CBA"   GRP=N  ELM=N

The GROUP-USAGE NATIONAL subject and its elementary `PIC N(3)` twin answer alike, and BOTH flip with the
national alphabet — so §8.8.4.2.9's national program collating sequence is genuinely consulted and the range is
not passing on the native UTF-16 ordinal. `ConditionRenderer` had been emitting
`CobolString.Compare(read, lo, __COLLATE_NAT) >= 0 && … <= 0` for this shape since [[PB678]] put `__COLLATE_NAT`
live. **The stage was refusing source the compiler already implemented.**

**The citation finding is confirmed mechanically**, both spellings re-run on this tree:

```

                            THROUGH phrase are of class alphanumeric or national"     -> OK  §13.18.63.3 31)

                            of the range"                                             -> OK  §14.7.8 2)

SR31 governs WHEN the `IN alphabet-name-1` phrase may be written and WHICH kind of alphabet it may name; §14.7.8
rule 2 is what actually governs the range. The stage cited the first for an obligation only the second could
have carried — `feedback_a_real_clause_can_answer_a_different_question`, exactly.
