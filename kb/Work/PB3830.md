# PB3830 — which statement does the NOT ON SIZE ERROR phrase belong to

- The other five programs of the signature (fz03631, fz04237, fz04490, fz03657, fz05296) are correct refusals: an
  unterminated IF in an inline PERFORM (the PB351 family), COMPUTE ON SIZE ERROR inside a WHEN, and a second ON SIZE
  ERROR after the phrase was already used.

## The two readings

1. **The adjudicator's** (§14.5.1, checked): "Wherever 'imperative-statement' appears in the general format of a
   statement, 'imperative-statement' refers to one or more imperative statements ended either by a separator period or
   by any phrase associated with that general format". NOT ON SIZE ERROR belongs to SUBTRACT's general format, so it
   ENDS imperative-statement-1. `ADD 1 TO D` is then an imperative statement and the program is legal at every
   edition (this is the 1985 phrase order).
2. **WiseOwl's own** (the diagnostic text of COBOLNET2796, emitted by `ProcedureFormatPass`): an ADD written without
   END-ADD "is a conditional statement", and a conditional statement may not stand where an imperative-statement is
   required. ADD's own general format also has `NOT ON SIZE ERROR`, so the inner ADD CAN take the outer phrase, and
   which statement the phrase binds to is the question. The grammar's `statementBlock : statement+` lets the inner
   arithmetic statement's optional `arithmeticOnSizeError` take it greedily, and `VisitStatementBlock` then refuses the
   conditional operand.

## What the owner decides

Whether §14.5.1's phrase-ending rule makes the outer reading mandatory, so that the grammar must bind an inner
conditional's trailing phrase outward whenever the outer statement has not yet used it. If yes, the fix reaches the
whole class of nested phrase-bearing statements (SUBTRACT, MULTIPLY, DIVIDE, ADD and COMPUTE, and the AT END, INVALID
KEY and ON EXCEPTION families), not these two. The citation check:
`python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 14.5.1 "ended either by a separator period or by any phrase associated with that general format"` is OK.
