# PB5349 - lineage.candidate_key compares the catalog subject verbatim, so the 1985 catalog's subjects with an article (THE INSPECT STATEMENT) never pair with 2023's (inspect statement): the committed 1985 pairing pairs 0 of 2274 rules even where the text is identical; and the pairing document's _comment still says normalized means cite.norm

**Sites:**

- `scripts/spec/lineage.py` (`candidate_key`, `candidates_document`)

- **R1-2 (lineage.py:435).** `candidate_key` compares `rule["subject"].strip().lower()` verbatim. The 1985 catalog spells subjects with the article (THE INSPECT STATEMENT) where 2023 and 2002 do not (inspect statement), so the committed, gated 1985 pairing pairs 0 of 2274 rules, even where the text is identical. (PB5256's note reads the 0 as "none is word for word a 2023 rule"; the subject mismatch alone would also give 0.)
- **R1-3 (lineage.py:478).** The `_comment` that `candidates_document` writes into every committed `spec-lineage-candidates-<year>.json` says `normalized` means `cite.norm`: words only, but the PB5256 R1-3 fix made the pairing `pairing_text` (typography only; every bracket, brace, bar, case and number kept).

**Fix direction.** Strip a leading article from the subject in `candidate_key` (a table of the 1985 spellings, not a regex that could eat a real word), regenerate the 1985 pairing and read what pairs; correct the `_comment`; regenerate every committed pairing file in the same change (`lineage.py --candidates --check`).

**Source.** Review leads R1-2 and R1-3, low, not blocking at round 1.
