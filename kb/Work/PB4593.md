`E:/COBOL-coord/inflight-groups.json`, recorded 2026-11-04 08:29. Its round-1 branch `worktree-wf_09e5ef0b-71c-4` was
stamped `Review: FINDINGS` (R2-1, R2-2). Mythos's finisher answered them on a NEW branch, `worktree-wf_09e5ef0b-71c-7`
(it merged 71c-4), which went DONE at 10:41:33 and was reviewed CLEAN at `1ff5fe8c6`. At 10:40:52 the operator loop's wave
unit started ("the DECISION row is decided FINISH and plan_wave.py plans its finisher for R2-1/R2-2") and dispatched
`w20261862a` (lane `operator`) for PB4573. That finisher fixed the same two findings again on
`worktree-wf_acba161d-4d1-1` (`b576d7e03`, gate GREEN, its own review FINDINGS 1), about 25 minutes of an implementer and a
reviewer spent on finished work. The ledger now holds two PB4573 claims, `w20261860a` (mythos) and `w20261862a` (operator).

**The mechanism to find.** Two guards each should have stopped the plan, and neither did:
1. The IN FLIGHT arm of `plan_wave.py` (the PB3311 comment: "a second finisher would redo the first one's fix") holds a
   note only while the branch the newest report names is `owned`. The live work was on 71c-7, a branch that CONTINUES
   71c-4 under a new name, so 71c-4 read unowned and its FINDINGS read unanswered.
2. A dispatch of another lane that names the note (`held_by_dispatch`, "applies in BOTH lanes: a held note waits") did not
   hold PB4573, although `w20261860a` was in the ledger. Find out why: whether a dispatch whose report exists counts as
   returned, or whether a finisher pred bypasses the hold.

**Fix at the root.** A note another lane's ledger entry names is never planned by this lane until that entry is released
or its branch is on main; and a branch that merges a FINDINGS branch carries its ownership (the replay rule of PB3170,
applied to the IN FLIGHT arm). Add a test_plan_wave.py case for each: a mythos-lane claim with a FINDINGS branch and a live
continuing branch plans nothing.
