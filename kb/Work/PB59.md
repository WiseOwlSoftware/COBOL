# PB59 — OPEN — CONVERT/BASECONVERT/DISPLAY-OF/NATIONAL-OF: the Repertoire/hex subsystem hard-codes Latin-1, trims what it must not, and screens nothing

> ⚙ **flags:** wrong-answer, silent · **28 inventory rows** (triage 2026-08-09, probe+refute confirmed; the
> per-row mechanisms live in the triage record and each row's inventory notes)

> **One subsystem, three mechanism families** (`CobolIntrinsics.Text` + `BindConvert` +
> `IntrinsicBinder.CheckRepertoireArgs`):
> 1. **`Repertoire`'s `toNational || c <= 0xFF ? c : sub` hard-codes an 8-bit Latin-1 identity** and
>    short-circuits `toNational` FIRST, so NATIONAL-OF never substitutes (its computed `sub` is dead), the
>    substitution ordering inverts on the DISPLAY-OF path, and the UTF-16 repertoire commitment is
>    contradicted at exactly one site. The `Sub()` EC raise sits inside the dead arm.
> 2. **The value paths trim/normalize before parsing**: `BaseConvert` and `HexDigitsToBytes` open with
>    `Trim()`, admitting zero-digit and space-embedded arguments into the value rules; no bit-level
>    padding step exists before `ToHex`; CHAR still bounds its domain by the 256-entry `AlphabetBind`
>    table with the pre-PB3 fallback, and the alphanumeric `CollatingTable` carries no representative
>    array while its national twin does — a two-arm asymmetry.
> 3. **CONVERT and CONCAT have no bind-time screens at all**, and `BindConvert` harvests keyword-shaped
>    arguments position-blind.
> Silent wrong answers on legal source (conversions produce wrong bytes/characters) — top harm band with
> PB56/PB60/PB65.
