- **R9-2: suppression attributes, and a hole in the obvious fix.**
  - The first version reported WOCA0003 as a NotConfigurable analyzer diagnostic on any `[SuppressMessage]` or

  - A planted test then showed that an attribute naming WOCA0003 itself, on the member, silenced it.
  - Reporting it with no location instead was silenced by a target-less `[assembly: SuppressMessage("x", "WOCA0003")]`.
  - So no analyzer diagnostic can be the refusal: Roslyn applies global suppressions to every analyzer diagnostic.
  - **The decision:** the refusal is `SuppressionRefusal`, an incremental generator in the analyzers' assembly. It writes a
    `#error` (CS1029, a compiler error that no suppression, pragma or NoWarn reaches) for each such attribute.
  - The theory covers the full name, a `using` alias, an id in a constant, and the unconditional form.
  - Self-suppression is covered too: `[assembly: SuppressMessage("x","WOCA0003")]` and `("x","CS1029")` beside member-level
    attributes all still fail.
  - Real build: a Compiler plant with an alias, a const id and the two assembly-level suppressions failed `dotnet build` with two
    CS1029. The textual SuppressMessage arm of `AnalyzerWiringDriftTests` is deleted. The PB id allocated for a residual note
