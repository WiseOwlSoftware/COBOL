# PB275 — a column with two conventions and one sentence

Found by the arithmetic-wave review fleet over the D-B/D-C/PB194/PB195 working tree, 2026-08-31, and fixed in the change set that landed the wave. Every number below was re-derived independently by the implementer (a 34-digit emulation of `CobolDec.cs` validated against the shipped `pb167` goldens, and 80–120-digit `Decimal` truth) BEFORE the confirming run, and then confirmed by it.

`Farthest` is a STRICT UPPER DECADE BOUND — the largest f for which every magnitude the caller can present below
10^f is in range — because the caller's own quantity (`intDigits + fm.MaxExp`) is an exponent the magnitude is
strictly below. That is why the SDIDI rows read 6145: decimal128's maximum is (1 − 10⁻³⁴)·10^6145, i.e.
9.999…E+6144 with 34 nines. Binary64 reads 308 rather than 309 for the mirror reason — its maximum leads with
1.797, so 9.99E+308 is NOT representable and 309 would admit it.

⚠ **The column is read under two conventions**, which is what makes a single sentence impossible:
`IntrinsicBinder`'s float-EDITED arm supplies the strict upper bound, its float-ITEM arm the carrier's EXACT
magnitude exponent ({28, 38, 308}). Nothing separates them today — §13.18.40.4 GR13 b caps the exponent part at
four digit positions and §13.18.40.3 SR15 the significand at 36, so the edited arm's reachable values are
confined to [9,45] ∪ [99,135] ∪ [999,1035] ∪ [9999,10035] and 6144/6145 (and 308/309) are behaviourally
interchangeable. That is now written beside the table, with the consequence spelled out: if the ITEM arm ever
gains a TRUE decimal128 carrier row it must be written 6144, not 6145.

⛔ No row changed, and the original finding's proposed 309 for binary64 would have been a regression. The boundary
pictures it offered as verification (`PIC 9(1)E+6144` and friends) are not writable at all — §13.18.40.4 GR13 b
restricts the exponent part to `+9`…`+9999`.
