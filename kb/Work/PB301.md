# PB301 — LANDED — the screen-word sweep, now a derivation rather than a list

Found by the sweep step of the **A.4.2 screen-handling** landing (kb/Work PB260): *grep for any screen word the
lexer reserves that could break a legal 85/2002 program using it as a user word.*

## The rule

Three clauses decide every case, and all three were re-derived at source and `cite.py --check`ed on 2026-09-06:

- **§8.3.2.1 rule 1** — "Reserved words shall not be used as user-defined words or system-names." (`OK §8.3.2.1 1)`)
- **§8.3.2.1 rule 3** — "Context-sensitive words may be used as user-defined words and system-names in contexts
  other than the language construct in which they are defined." (`OK §8.3.2.1 3)`)
- **§8.9** is the reserved-word list, **§8.10** the context-sensitive-word table (`OK §8.9`, `OK §8.10`).

So a lexer token is **not** a reservation. Declining an optional module (Annex A.4.2, `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` §5)
licenses refusing the module's *constructs*; it licenses nothing at all about the module's *words*.

## The measurement — the whole screen surface, 22 words

§8.10's word column carries exactly fifteen screen words and §8.9 carries seven, and the join of both against
`CobolLexer.g4`'s single-literal tokens is the screen surface's whole word population. It is no longer written
down: `ScreenFacilityConstructDriftTests.ScreenSurfaceWords()` derives it from `CobolScreen.g4` plus the
`crtStatusClause` / `cursorClause` rules of `CobolSpecialNames.g4`, and `EveryScreenSurfaceWord_…` runs it
against all four editions (22 × 4 = 88 legs).

## The fix

All twenty-two are `nameSlot` rows in `tests/version-matrix/cobol-words.json` — the single generated source for
`cobolWord` and `_dataNameTokens`. The seven §8.9 words are **reservation-gated**, and since kb/Work PB693 that
gate is no longer a hand-set flag: `scripts/gen-cobol-words.ps1` derives it from `reserved-words.json`, emitting
`cobolWord` alternatives under `{userWordHere("W")}?` and their `reservedGatedWord` twins under the inverse, so
the DECLARATION still parses where §8.9 reserves the word and the §8.9 funnel answers with COBOLNET0901 naming
the rule instead of a raw `COBOL0001 no viable alternative`.

A second arm was needed, and it is the arm a name-slot-only fix would have missed: `specialNameEntry`'s
`crtStatusClause` / `cursorClause` alternatives are gated on `{reservedHere("CRT")}?` / `{reservedHere("CURSOR")}?`.
Without that, `SPECIAL-NAMES. CURSOR IS FOO.` at `--std 85` — a legal COBOL-85 **implementor-switch entry** —
parsed as the screen module's CURSOR clause and drew a screen-facility verdict for a program with no screen in it.

## The drift gate, and the hole the failing-branch proof found

The eight `[InlineData]` rows that used to name five words at two editions were evidence about the five they named
and silent about the seventeen they dropped. They are gone; the population is derived. Proving the new gate can
FAIL (feedback_green_gates_arent_evidence) found that it could not, in one direction: the guard on the SPECIAL-NAMES
half was `surface.Length > screen.Length`, an OR over two rules that `cursorClause` alone satisfied, so **renaming
`crtStatusClause` left every screen test GREEN** while CRT silently left the population — and the `>= 20` floor
cannot see 22 become 21. Closed structurally rather than with a second length check:

- each external rule is scraped **by name** and each must be found;
- `EveryScreenRefusalSite_IsClassified` derives the funnel's rule set from `ScreenFacility`'s own `Report*`
  context-parameter types plus its clause table and requires every one to land in a named class, so a screen
  refusal added for a rule in a third file fails instead of carrying its words outside every scrape.

Three failing branches were then exercised and reverted: dropping `SECURE`'s `nameSlot` row (the population fact
names SECURE), renaming `crtStatusClause` (two tests red), and dropping `cursorClause` from the surface list (the
classification names it).

## What is NOT this note's, and where it went

The same join over the **whole** §8.10 table finds 22 further context-sensitive words that are lexer tokens and
not `cobolWord` alternatives (`AWAY-FROM-ZERO`, `CAPACITY`, `RECURSIVE`, `YYYYMMDD`, …). They are legal
user-defined words outside their one construct and are parse errors today (`01 CAPACITY PIC X.` at `--std 2023`
is still a raw COBOL0001, re-measured 2026-09-06). They are **class 4 of [[PB655]]**, whose sweep measured the
whole lexer against §8.9 (99 hits, four classes) and whose own title records that it supersedes this note's
complement. They are not held open here as well: one register, one owner.

For the same reason inventory row **SR-8.3.2.1-3** (context-sensitive words as user words) stays GAP — PB655
class 4 is what closes it. **SR-8.3.2.1-1** (reserved words barred from user-defined-word AND system-name slots)
is CONFORMS as of this landing.
