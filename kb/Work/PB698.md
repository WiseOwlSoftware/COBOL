# PB698 — LANDED — a repair that moves the denominator

Found 2026-09-05 by the `PB352`/`PB354` implementer (report §"New defects" a), which fixed the indentation,
regenerated `docs/rearchitecture/spec-rule-catalog.json`, measured `rule_count` 4311 → 4309 — the rows
`SR-14.9.41.3-L2.2` and `-L2.3` that its own note declares — and reverted both edits, because a CI-owned register
is never an implementer's side effect (rule 8).

## The facts

`cite.py --check 14.9.41.3 "It has the same class, category, and usage as that record key"` → `OK 6) b) 1.`, and
the same path for item 3.'s text, because items 2. and 3. are outdented at the page break (render PDF page 785:
both are indented under b) in the printed standard). The catalog's extractor reads the indentation: item 1. is
folded into SR6's text, items 2. and 3. become standalone "L" rows. Either state is internally inconsistent
(one sub-item folded, two separate); making it consistent moves the GAP denominator in one direction or the
other. Same family as [[PB689]] (the rule catalog's structure decides what a row IS — PB689's interior-heading
repair is the same kind of correction, taken deliberately on a quiet main with the step recorded).

## What settles it

Decide ONCE, for the whole catalog, how a sub-lettered/sub-numbered syntax rule is rowed (one row per numbered
rule with sub-items folded, or one row per leaf) and make the extractor enforce that shape regardless of
indentation accidents; then repair the transcription's indentation (rule 1: fix on discovery), rebuild the catalog
and the inventory on a quiet main, and record the denominator change with its reason — as PB689's landing does.
Until then the sub-items are cited in code and notes by their current row ids and verbatim text, both stable.
Fold this into PB689's follow-up rather than dispatching it alone.

## LANDED 2026-09-06

### The shape question was already answered by the owner

The note asks to "decide ONCE ... how a sub-lettered/sub-numbered syntax rule is rowed". That decision exists and
is the owner's: [[PB55]], **2026-08-08, "THE RULE IS THE UNIT"** — nested numbered items are the parent rule's
BODY, not rows. Nothing here re-decides it. PB55 also schedules its full *absorption wave* (the ~200 `L` rows of
lists the transcription flattened whole) for AFTER the defect burn-down, and that wave did **not** run here.
What ran is PB55's own step 1 restricted to the **tails** — the page-break accidents PB698 names — plus the
guard that stops an indentation accident from deciding a row's identity ever again.

### What was measured, before anything was changed

`extract_rule_catalog.py --check` reported **23 flattened tail items in 14 clauses** (§8.4.6.2.1 · §9.3.6 ·
§12.3.7.4 · §13.18.54.4 · §14.9.10.4 · §14.9.13.4 · §14.9.20.4 · §14.9.25.4 · §14.9.33.4 · §14.9.37.4 ·
§14.9.41.3 · §15.30.3 · §15.31.3 · §15.95.4). Each was located on the canonical PDF and its **ordinal glyph's
x0** compared with that page's level ladder. Every one is printed one level right of its block's top-level rule
ordinal. Two decisive pages: **961**, where `c)` sits at x0 97.56 and items `5.`/`6.` at 115.05; and **652**,
where the top-level `5)` sits at 63.49, `b) c) d)` at 81.0, and items `4. 5. 6.` at 98.5. §14.9.41.3's own pair
is at 115.05 on page 785 against a body margin of 80.04. **23 of 23 INDENTED — no item was a top-level rule.**

### The repair, and the proof that the tool is not the one deciding

All 23 lines were re-indented to their printed level in `specs/ISO_COBOL.md`, each taking the exact leading
whitespace and ordinal-escaping of its own preceding sibling (three spaces, `N\.`), CRLF preserved, byte-checked.
Then:

* the **pre-PB698 extractor**, run on the repaired transcription, produces a catalog **byte-identical** to the
  patched extractor's. The tool and the transcription now agree, and the 4371 → 4348 move is the *printed
  structure*, not a tooling opinion.
* `cite.py --check 14.9.41.3 "It has the same class, category, and usage as that record key"` now answers
  **`6) b) 2.`** (it answered `6) b) 1.` for both items before), and item 3 answers `6) b) 3.` — the defect this
  note was opened for.
* the extractor keeps the fold as a **net**: a column-0 ordinal continuing an indented run is buffered into its
  parent rule and REPORTED, fatal under `--check`. It catches nothing today; it will catch the next outdent.

### The denominator, and the adjudications that had to move with it

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| catalog rules / inventory rows | 4371 | **4348** (−23) |
| GAP | 2820 | **2807** (−13) |
| resolved | 1551 | **1541** (−10) |

⛔ **The rebuild is only safe while a rule-id names the same rule.** It does not: `L<sublist>.<n>` is POSITIONAL,
so removing a sub-list shifts every id below it. Measured here: **34 ids disappeared, 11 appeared, and 6
adjudicated rows kept their id while their TEXT changed underneath** — e.g. `GR-14.9.20.4-L2.2` carried PB415's
PARTIAL for "The REPLACING phrase is specified…" and would have carried it onto "If the category of the
receiving-operand is object-reference…". Nothing in `build_inventory.py` could see that: `added`/`removed`
compare ids, and `subject` is the rule BLOCK's subject. So:

* every old row was mapped onto the new catalog **by text**, and the migration applied as batches:
  `pb698-rekey` (10 adjudicated rows moved to the id that now carries their text) and `pb698-fold` (9 parent
  rows absorb the 18 sub-item rows' code locations, tests and forensic notes, each tagged with the printed path
  `cite.py` gives it today);
* one verdict genuinely changed: **`RV-15.95.4-1` CONFORMS → PARTIAL**, because the folded `1) b) 5.` was
  PARTIAL. A parent row cannot read CONFORMS over an obligation that is not met — the obligation did not change,
  it stopped being counted in a row of its own;
* `build_inventory.py` now stamps a **`rule-digest`** on every row and reports (fatal under a new `--check`) any
  carried verdict that landed on changed text. It is silent on this rebuild by construction — the prior rows had
  no digest to compare — which is exactly why this landing states its migration explicitly instead of trusting
  the guard it installs. It was proved to FIRE before being trusted.

20 `kb/Work` notes' `inventory_rows` were re-keyed in the same change set, along with the references in
`RecordLayout.cs`, `docs/CONFORMANCE.md`, `inventory-schema.json` and `DerivedVerdictDriftTests.cs`.
