# PB291 — a hyphen that is not a hyphen, in the artifact every predicate reads

Found 2026-09-02 by the lane-2a derived-verdict selector work, which needed `\bdata-name-1\b` to mean the CURSOR
operand in §12.3.7 and found the pattern matching nothing in a family of rules that plainly contain the words.
The sibling sweep then found the same shape in the arithmetic operator and the PICTURE symbol.

## Measured on this tree, 2026-09-02

| character | `specs/ISO_COBOL.md` | catalog rules whose `text` carries it | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **U+2011 NON-BREAKING HYPHEN** | 107 | **29** | ⛔ always a defect — it stands where a COBOL word's hyphen belongs |
| **U+2212 MINUS SIGN** | 34 | **14** | ⛔ always a defect — it stands for the arithmetic operator / PICTURE symbol |
| U+2013 EN DASH | 103 | 39 (65 occurrences) | ⚠ MIXED — 64 of 65 are NOT between digits, so most are the operator; 1 is a legitimate range. Needs per-occurrence adjudication |
| U+2014 EM DASH | 1095 | 187 | ✅ the transcription's own prose punctuation. NOT in scope — named here so the next reader does not re-open it |
| U+2010, U+2012, U+00A0, curly quotes | 0 | 0 | ✅ absent |

The 29 U+2011 rules span nine clauses — §14.9.30.3 (11, the whole SR list of one statement), §14.9.39.4 (5),

U+2011, U+2013 and U+2212 together, **79 catalog rules across 47 distinct clauses** carry a look-alike.

## Why it is a defect and not a distinction

These characters print identically to `-`, which is exactly why they survived OCR and review: the rendered page
is right and the CHARACTER is not. But **§8.3.2.1** is explicit about what a COBOL word may contain (cite.py
`--check` OK): *"Each character of a COBOL word that is not a special character word shall be selected from the
set of basic letters, basic digits, extended letters, and the basic special characters hyphen and underscore."*
`integer‑1` spelled with U+2011 is therefore not the word `integer-1` the standard defines, and no COBOL source
can contain it. The same holds for the arithmetic operator and the PICTURE symbol, whose character in the
standard's own character set is the hyphen-minus. This is a typesetting artifact of the PDF's text layer,
carried into the transcription verbatim.

## The harm, and its direction

Every consumer that reads rule text with a pattern over an ASCII hyphen — the `derived-verdicts` selectors, any
future text predicate, a grep by a human or an agent — matches **nothing** in those rules. The failure direction
is **under-selection**: the predicate returns fewer rules than it should, the population looks smaller, and
nothing goes red. `feedback_measure_the_selectors_complement` names this shape exactly — a selector is evidence
about what it RETURNED, never about what it dropped. Before the fold landed, two blank rows
(`SR-13.18.14.3-11/-12`) were invisible to the screen selector rather than deliberately protected.

## What is already done, and what is NOT

**Done (2026-09-02, lane 2a):** both selector matchers — `DerivedSelector` in `scripts/spec/inventory_schema.py`
and `DerivedVerdictDriftTests.Select` — hyphen-normalise the rule text before matching, with a test, so the
selector lane is no longer affected. That is a CONSUMER fold. It is the correct immediate move (a consumer must
be robust to the artifact) and it is **not the repair** — the next consumer written will have the bug again.

**Not done — the repair, which is upstream, in two steps:**

1. **`specs/ISO_COBOL.md`** (private submodule): normalise U+2011 → `-` (all 107) and U+2212 → `-` (all 34), and
   adjudicate the 103 U+2013 per occurrence — an en dash between digits is a range and stays. ⚠ Per
   `feedback_spec_diagrams_render_pdf`, check the general-format DIAGRAMS before a blanket replace: a
   line-breaking hint inside a diagram is the only place the character could be deliberate. The submodule change
   is its own commit and its own gitlink bump.
2. **`docs/rearchitecture/spec-rule-catalog.json`**: rebuilt from the repaired transcription by
   `extract_rule_catalog.py`. A catalog rebuild moves the DENOMINATOR, so it lands with the inventory rebuild and
   a recorded before/after row count — the same landing discipline [[PB284]] carries for its own denominator
   defects. The two are adjacent but distinct: **PB284 is rules the extractor LOST or truncated; this is rules
   the extractor kept faithfully and whose TEXT is wrong.**

**Invariant owed with the repair**, so it cannot come back: a drift check that the catalog text contains no
character from the confusable set (U+2011, U+2010, U+2012, U+2212, U+00A0), and that U+2013 appears only between
digits. Asserting the absence is cheap and is the only form of evidence that stays true as the catalog is rebuilt
— exactly what the consumer fold cannot give, because a fold makes the artifact's defect *invisible* rather
than *impossible*.
