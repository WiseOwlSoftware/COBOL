# PB3393 — a publish cannot be marked while main moves faster than the render

**The site.** `scripts/orchestrator/ledger_state.py`. `state()` computes main's stamp (the last commit on
`origin/main` that touched a ledger input, `gen_ledger.STAMP_PATHS`) and the checkout's stamp. `mark-published`
refuses with "not marked: the checkout … renders the ledger at X, not origin/main's Y" unless the two are equal at
the moment of marking, and when they are, it records main's stamp (kb/Work PB2987: never record a page that does not
describe main as current).

**What happened (2026-10-18, operator).** The procedure is fast-forward, render, publish, mark. Each render
(`gen_ledger.py`) took 6 to 8 minutes on the loaded host, and the merge queue landed several pull requests an hour:
- v126 was published at c5adfe4cd and marked;
- v127 was rendered at b49b80308 and refused, since main had reached 14e0fc4ff;
- v128 was rendered at d689feb2e (ledger stamp 35919bd0b) and refused, since main had reached 0ec6bf172.

Each refusal leaves the publish owed, so the supervisor posts another `publish` notice after the next unit, and the
operator renders again into the same race. The record never says what IS published.

**The fix.** Record what was actually published, and leave the owed state to say whether that is main's.
`gen_ledger.py` writes the stamp it rendered into a sidecar next to the page (`ledger.html`'s `ledger.stamp`), and
`mark-published` records that stamp. `state()` stays as it is: the publish is current only when that recorded stamp
is main's. So PB2987's rule holds, and no page is recorded as describing a stamp it does not show. A publish that
main overtook is recorded truthfully and is still owed, and the next render only has to catch up. A self-test pins
it: render at X, move main to Y, mark; the record says X and the state reads owed. The render's own time (6 to 8
minutes) is a separate question, worth one measurement of where it goes.
