## 2026-11-17: landed — one rule for every written subscript group and reference modifier, at 85 and 2023

**Probed at 85 first.** The triage tagged this NOT-1985; it is in the 1985 edition: Amendment 1 adds the ALL subscript
(§1985:IV.4.3.8.2.3 SR7, "The subscript ALL may be used only when the subscripted identifier is used as a function argument",
`cite.py --check` OK) and applies a reference modifier to each element it names (§1985:IV.4.3.8.4.4 GR3a). On main, at 85 and
at 2023, `MOVE FUNCTION MAX (TE (ALL) OF TT (2:2)) TO R` drew COBOL2363 ("the subscript ALL is written where it is not
permitted") beside the right COBOL2776, and at 85 a cascading COBOL0900 and COBOL0819 from the refused argument;
`TE (ALL) OF TT` drew COBOL2776 alone and `TE OF TT (ALL) (2:2)` ran (`JK`).

**Legality.** The identifier format puts the subscripts after the whole qualified name in both editions (§1985:IV.4.3.8.5.2,
§2023:8.4.2.3.2 Format 1), so the spelling is refused, and COBOL2776 is the diagnostic that names the actual rule.

**The fix.** `IntrinsicBinder.TryBindAllArgument` classifies every written part by one local rule (`Admit`), wherever the
grammar hangs it (a suffix of the name or a part of a qualification): a subscript group is the first and only one, and a
reference modifier stands after it. The arm that refused a qualifier carrying a modifier and no subscripts, even after the
subscript group, is gone. After the fix the three spellings at 85 and 2023 give exactly COBOL2776, COBOL2776 and `JK`.

**Witness.** `tests/WiseOwl.Cobol.Tests.Unit/AllSubscriptQualifierAfterSubscriptTests.cs`: `TE (ALL) OF TT (1:1)` and
`TE (ALL) OF G OF TT (1:1)` are COBOL2776 alone, at 85 and 2023 (the suite's refusal file cannot assert an absence).
