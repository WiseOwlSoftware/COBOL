# PB469 — LANDED — what the burn-down actually costs, measured

The analysis is complete and its evidence file lands with this note:
`docs/rearchitecture/evidence/PROCESS-REVIEW-2026-09-04.md` (the data brief and the three-proposal synthesis,
verbatim). The decisions taken on it are `kb/Work/PB468`. ⓜ marks a figure measured on the tree by the synthesis's
own probe scripts; the probes were written to the session scratchpad, and the numbers they produced are frozen in
the evidence file.

## 1. The published rate is not the earned rate

ⓜ `ledger-trend.json` held 19 points over 18 GAP-moving landings, 08-29 → 09-04: **720 rows closed, mean 40.0,
median 14.0, four landings closing zero.** ⓜ **305 of the 720 (42 %) came from three module-decline witness
landings** (screen handling 152, witness B2 104, witness cluster A 49) and **203 more from the golden lane**
(151 + 52). Strip the declines and the **earned rate is (720 − 305) / 6 = 69 rows/day**, not the 138 headline.
All three proposals found this independently.

⛔ **Plan on zero further declines.** ⓜ `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` confirms A.4.11 Report Writer is *Partial with a
shipped implementation* — there is no fourth module to decline, so the 42 % that carried the headline cannot
repeat. (Whether Report Writer stays claimed is open question 8 in PB468.)

## 2. The evidence is derived twice

Adjudication emits a verdict with **no evidence**; the golden lane later re-reads the same rule and the same code
to produce that evidence. ⓜ b1: 184 rules adjudicated → GAP **−15 (8 %)**. ⓜ b2 (`073d764a`): 292 rules → **−32
(11 %)**, and it took the CONFORMS-but-untested band from **9 to 55** while opening **84 new `kb/Work` notes**.
ⓜ Golden-lane round 2 closed 52 rows with 49 witnesses = **1.06 rows per witness**, against an existence proof of
163 rows on a single test-ref.

**The owner declined the synthesis's fix for this** (a single agent deriving the verdict *and* the witness) —
see PB468 Q1: the second derivation is the guarantee, not the waste. The cost is therefore accepted, and the
number is recorded here so the plan is built on it rather than on the headline.

## 3. The binding constraint is the fix lane, and it was already the fix lane

ⓜ Measured on the tree *after* b2 landed — the fact none of the three proposals could see, because they reasoned
from a pre-b2 count of 245 adjudicated-but-open rows:

* GAP **3022** · un-adjudicated **2517** · **adjudicated-but-open 505** (PARTIAL 233 · DIVERGES 110 ·
  NOT-IMPLEMENTED 105 · CONFORMS-untested 55 · DNS-GAP 2).
* ⓜ `kb/Work` holds **249 open/half notes claiming 487 inventory rows** (1.96 rows/note) — the proposals reasoned
  from ~110 actionable.
* ⓜ **Two adjudication batches (471 rules) opened 169 notes; the fix lane closed ~16 mechanisms in the same six
  days.** The register fills roughly **10× faster than it drains**.
* ⓜ **Fix-lane utilization is under 15 %** of the three-implementer cap the owner had already authorized —
  3 implementers × 6 dispatch-hours ÷ 45 min per mechanism is ~24 mechanisms/day of capacity against ~2.7
  delivered. Implementers were dispatched one cluster at a time, behind landings, behind the evidence lane.

**This is the finding with the largest lever, and it needs no new pipeline** — only a standing queue of
apply-ready contracts and the dispatch rule *a free implementer slot is filled within the turn it frees*
(adopted, PB468).

## 4. Agent cost is quadratic in turns

ⓜ **`tokens ≈ 0.115·T + 0.00031·T²`**, refitted `0.1154·T + 0.000309·T²` over **n = 239 agents** — reproduced to
three significant figures by an independent probe. Consequences, all measured:

| shape | measured |
|---|---|
| long transcripts | ⓜ 18 of 239 agents ran ≥ 250 turns and burned **39 % of all 5.62 B tokens**; re-modelled at a 150-turn cap the same turns cost **45 % less** |
| 2 mechanisms in one implementer | 85.0 M for two vs 40.3 M for one — the **second mechanism costs 44.6 M, more than a fresh implementer** |
| one lander, four landings | ⓜ lander-3: 794 turns, **295.9 M**; as four fresh ~198-turn landers, **140 M** |
| clusters per landing | ⓜ **10.4 M/cluster at k = 1 vs 5.1 M at k = 5**; lander minutes 9.8 → 4.0 |
| the amortization, observed | the golden lander landed **151 rows for 52.9 M = 0.35 M/row**; the PB383 lander landed **2 rows for 7.2 M = 3.6 M/row** — a **10×** spread |

ⓜ All-tokens per closed row today is **9.66 M** (5.62 B / 246 agents / 582 rows), of which **97.6 % is cache
read**; the harness-visible figure is ~2.4 % of it. All-tokens is the accounting that matters because it is what
the weekly limit consumes — and the limit paused the campaign three times in six days.

## 5. ⛔ The softest number, named

**The note yield outside §14 is unmeasured.** ⓜ It is 0.36 notes per adjudicated rule on §14 statements
(b1 + b2: 169 notes from 471 rules), and the only samples on §13 (700 un-adjudicated rows) and §8 (412) are 51 and
34 rows of *declined or definitional* content — 0.97–1.00 CONFORMS, which says nothing about statement-shaped
rules. Nothing else moves the projection as far:

* at a yield of **0.05** the fix lane is **529 mechanisms / 14.3 B / ~22 working days**;
* at **0.36** it is **1155 mechanisms / 31.2 B / ~40 working days**.

**Lane-3 batch b3 is §13.18 data-division clauses, so it measures this directly.** Its registrar must report the
notes-per-adjudicated-rule yield as a first-class number, not as a by-product — that is the single most valuable
measurement the burn-down can make right now.

A second-order effect, stated so it is not mistaken for regression later: any move that makes a rule *run* rather
than be *read* raises the measured defect rate, because a witness that runs finds what a code read misses. That is
CLAUDE.md rules 3 and 4 working as designed, and it lengthens the tail.
