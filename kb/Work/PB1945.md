Filed 2026-10-03 12:15 PDT by the orchestrator, after probing the `cobc` the owner installed in WSL (Ubuntu `apt`, **GnuCOBOL 3.1.2.0**; the repo's corpus pins 3.2, so a 3.2 confirmation is owed).

## What was measured
`>>CALL-CONVENTION <word>` followed by an empty program, `cobc -fsyntax-only -free`, in WSL:
- accepted: `COBOL`, `EXTERN`, `STDCALL`, `STATIC`;
- rejected with `expecting COBOL or EXTERN or STDCALL or STATIC`: `C`, `PASCAL` and an arbitrary word.

## Where this leaves PB1383 (landed in train 1007, DEVLOG 1847)
The landed rule admits only `COBOL`. That agrees with GnuCOBOL on every unknown word, and the implementer's note that GnuCOBOL "was not surveyed (no cobc)" is now answered for that half. The three further names are implementor-defined: §7.3.9.3 GR3 ("may also be used by the implementor to determine other details needed to interact with a function, method, or program", `cite.py --check` OK) leaves what any further name does to the implementor, and PB1383 cites GR2 b) for the name mapping. Admitting them means implementing a mapping to native calls, which is a feature and not a latitude choice, so under CLAUDE.md rule 1 (ISO, then GnuCOBOL) it is open.

## To decide
1. Keep COBOL-only and record in `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` that EXTERN, STDCALL and STATIC are not supported (unsupported means unsupported), or
2. implement them from a design doc, with the call mapping decided first.

**3.2 confirmed 2026-10-03 12:33 PDT** (cobc 3.2.0 built from the repo-pinned tarball, `/usr/local/bin/cobc`, same probe): the result is identical to 3.1.2, so the three names are stable across both releases. 4.0-early-dev is not a reference (its own page says anything may change between builds). What remains is the decision above.
