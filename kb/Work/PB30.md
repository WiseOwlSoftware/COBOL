# PB30 — LANDED — eight more functions screened, every row spec-derived and cited

> ⚙ **flags:** under-rejects
> MAX, MIN, MEAN, MEDIAN, MIDRANGE, MOD, NUMVAL-C and LOWEST-ALGEBRAIC carry argument-class rules that no code
> consults. This is PB1's named residue growing by the clause, exactly as designed — the table is deliberately
> partial and each batch is supposed to extend it. ⚠ Every new row must carry **the ISO clause it was read
> from**: PB1's disaster was wiring 79 *unaudited* declarations into a rejection path and rejecting 12 legal
> corpus programs.


## ⚙ LANDED 2026-08-06

Eight rows, each read at its own clause and `cite.py --check`ed:
`MEAN` §15.60.3 r1 · `MEDIAN` §15.61.3 r1 · `MIDRANGE` §15.62.3 r1 (all `'n'`, and all VARIADIC — their rule
names argument-1 only, which governs every occurrence of the repeated argument, so `Uniform` IS the rule's shape
rather than a widening) · `MOD` §15.64.3 r1 (`'i'`, "argument-1 and argument-2 shall be integers") ·
`NUMVAL-C` §15.68.3 r1/r2 · `MAX`/`MIN` §15.59.3/§15.63.3 (see [[PB31]]) · and `HIGHEST-ALGEBRAIC` /
`LOWEST-ALGEBRAIC` resolved as DELIBERATELY UNSCREENED rather than screened — see [[PB12]] for why that is the
correct disposition and not a deferral.

⚠ NUMVAL-C is one of the eight functions whose bespoke binder RETURNED ABOVE the screen, so its row would have
been dead on arrival without [[PB12]]'s reachability half. Its screen runs BEFORE the §15.68.3 r3 default-currency
injection, deliberately: that operand is compiler-supplied, and screening it would report a class disagreement
about an argument-2 the source does not contain.
