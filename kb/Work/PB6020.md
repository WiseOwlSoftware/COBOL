# PB6020 - the read records could not follow a move of their rule

**Sites:** `scripts/spec/rule_moves.py` (`tracked_copies`, `recorded_moves`, `MOVES_DIR`, `READS`),
`scripts/spec/record_verdicts.py` (the copy of an applied batch), `scripts/spec/lineage_reads.py` (`follow_moves`, `load`),
`scripts/spec/audit_batch.py:1450` (`import_reads`, the refusal that showed it).

**Defect.** Group e of wave 20262131 (PB6001) relabelled five 1985 rows (`1985-GR-XIII.3.10.7-p4/p5` to `-p3/-p4`,
`1985-GR-XIII.3.10.9-p4/p5` to `-p3/-p4`, `1985-GR-XIV.2.2.6-T1` to `-T2`), and the PB5453 import had recorded all five,
so the train applied a MOVE batch through `record_verdicts.py --edition 1985` (store records moved by digest). The
read records `tests/version-matrix/lineage-reads-1985/s-18.json` and `s-99.json` still read the old ids, and a read record
is sealed by its digests, so it cannot be re-keyed. `lineage_reads.py --check` passed, but
`audit_batch.py import-reads --edition 1985` refused every new import: "3 read rule(s) of ... are no rule of the 1985
catalog: ['1985-GR-XIII.3.10.7-p5', '1985-GR-XIII.3.10.9-p5', '1985-GR-XIV.2.2.6-T1']". A move kept no record (only a
split had a tracked copy, PB6002), so nothing could say which reads meant the moved rule.

**Fix.** One tracked copy for both operations: `record_verdicts.py` copies each applied batch that holds a move or a split
to `docs/rearchitecture/spec-moves-<year>/` (was `spec-splits-<year>/`, splits alone; the one file there moved with
`git mv`), and each move carries `reads`, the slices that read its old id when it was applied (read with
`load(superseded=True)`, before a second read takes one out). `lineage_reads.load` renames those slices' reads under the
new ids (`follow_moves`: a batch's moves are one simultaneous renaming, the batches in file-name order) before it resolves
the second reads; a slice the move does not name keeps its ids. The five moves the train had applied are recorded as
`docs/rearchitecture/spec-moves-1985/moves-pb6001.json` (written by `tracked_copies` from the applied batch). The design
doc's section on moves and splits (DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md) says so.

**Tests.** `scripts/selftests/spec/rule_moves/test_rule_moves.py` case 07 (the copy holds moves with their readers;
`recorded_moves` reads it back) and `scripts/selftests/spec/lineage_reads/test_reads.py` case 17 (a renumbering p4 to p3,
p5 to p4 after a slice read both; second reads resolve on the new ids; an unnamed slice keeps its id). Both RED on the
base (`tracked_copies`, `recorded_moves` and `load(..., moves)` do not exist), GREEN on the head.

**Limit.** Batches apply in their copies' file-name order; a later batch that chains on an earlier one's ids must sort
after it (a regeneration names its batch; none chains today).
