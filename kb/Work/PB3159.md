# PB3159 — the license rethink: what the owner decided, what is still open

> flags: process-only · owner decision

## Source

The owner, 2026-10-14 morning, while the operator answered a commercial inquiry that asked to license the test and
sample programs (as compiler test cases, or as AI training data): "We should rethink whether the business source
license is the appropriate one before we have a fully working compiler."

The operator's analysis that morning (session 0d11b7c9): the runtime (`WiseOwl.Cobol.Runtime`) and the generated C# ship
inside every program a user compiles, yet they sit under the same Business Source License as the compiler. Compilers
normally exempt them (GCC's runtime library exception; GnuCOBOL's runtime, libcob, is LGPL). Recommendation:
(1) put the runtime and the generated output under Apache 2.0, or an explicit exception, now; (2) hold the compiler's
own license until the business model is chosen; (3) have a lawyer review it.

The owner, 2026-10-14 ~14:50 PDT (operator session 7e5a828b), verbatim: "1. Agreed. 2. Explain the various license
options, other than a totally custom one. I would like to be able to license the compiler for commerical use."

## Decided

1. **The runtime and the generated output go under Apache 2.0** (or an explicit exception for the output), but
   **NOT YET: it is decided together with the compiler's license.** Before the change the operator raised one
   trade-off, because a push to the public repository cannot be withdrawn: an Apache runtime means a program built
   with WiseOwl COBOL deploys without a fee, and Rocket (formerly Micro Focus) charges for deployment licenses as well
   as developer seats, so it gives up that revenue stream in exchange for adoption (no lock-in). Asked "Put the runtime
   and generated code under Apache 2.0 now, or decide it together with the compiler's license?", the owner answered
   (2026-10-14 ~15:25 PDT): "Decide with compiler license". The runtime stays under BSL 1.1 until then.
2. **The owner wants to license the compiler for commercial use**: an ordinary company using it must buy a license.
3. **Government users are commercial users and must pay** (owner, 2026-10-14 ~15:25 PDT, verbatim: "I want
   government users to be treated as commerical and must pay"). This rules out PolyForm Noncommercial, whose
   "Noncommercial Organizations" clause makes any government institution's use a permitted (free) purpose. PolyForm
   Small Business does not name government, but its free grant covers "your company", defined as "any legal entity,
   sole proprietorship, or other kind of organization that you work for" plus everything under common control; a
   small government body (fewer than 100 people, under 1,000,000 USD (2019) revenue) can read itself into that.
   Only a license whose free grant the licensor writes excludes government outright: the BSL's Additional Use Grant
   (standard license, one sentence of the licensor's own) or a proprietary EULA.

## Open — the compiler's license (the owner's choice)

The options the operator laid out (no custom license), sorted by whether a commercial user must pay:

| License | Free for | Commercial user pays? | Converts to open source? |
|---|---|---|---|
| BSL 1.1 (current) | whatever the Additional Use Grant says (now: non-commercial) | yes | yes, each version after at most 4 years |
| PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 | individuals, education, research, charities, government institutions | yes (businesses) | no |
| PolyForm Small Business 1.0.0 | companies under 100 people and under 1,000,000 USD (2019, CPI-adjusted) revenue | yes (larger) | no |
| Proprietary EULA | only those granted (trials, academic) | yes | no |
| FSL 1.1, Elastic 2.0, PolyForm Shield | everyone except competitors | no | FSL after 2 years |
| GPL/AGPL plus a commercial license | everyone who accepts copyleft | rarely | already open |
| Open core | everyone, for the core | only for add-ons | the core is open |

Facts checked 2026-10-14: the PolyForm Small Business threshold from the official text
(`github.com/polyformproject/polyform-licenses`, `PolyForm-Small-Business-1.0.0.md` § Small Business); PolyForm
Noncommercial's "Noncommercial Organizations" clause names government institutions (SPDX text).

The operator's points: only the first four make a company pay (the morning's FSL suggestion does not fit the stated
goal); copyleft does not reach the programs a compiler produces, which is why GnuCOBOL is free for business use; BSL's
conversion fits COBOL badly, because editions are 9 to 12 years apart (2002, 2014, 2023) and a 4-year-old version
still compiles the current standard; the copies already published on GitHub keep BSL 1.1 and its conversion; a
standard text clears enterprise legal review faster than a custom one; `CLA.md` already permits a separate paid
license. First recommendation: PolyForm Small Business for the compiler plus a paid license for larger organizations,
the runtime under Apache 2.0; PolyForm Noncommercial instead if the public sector should use it free; a lawyer either
way. **Revised after decision 3 (government pays):** PolyForm Noncommercial is out and PolyForm Small Business leaves
a small-government reading open, so the recommendation is the BSL 1.1 with its Additional Use Grant rewritten to name
the free uses (for example individuals, education, evaluation, small companies) and to state that government use is
commercial, with the Change Date at the 4-year maximum; if 4-year conversion is unacceptable, a lawyer-drafted
proprietary license with the same free tiers instead. A lawyer reviews either.
