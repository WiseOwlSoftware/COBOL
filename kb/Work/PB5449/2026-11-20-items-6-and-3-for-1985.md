- **Item 6: built.** The MOVE (`{"rule-id": <new>, "move-from": <old>}`) and the SPLIT (`{"rule-id": <parent>,
  "split": {old-text, carved, rows, recheck}}`) are records of a `record_verdicts.py` batch, their rules
  `rule_moves.py`'s (one module, as `lineage.py` holds the change record's): a move only with the record's text (its
  `rule-digest` is the new id's text now), a chain moving each record once; a split's old text equal to the parent's text
  now plus the carved paragraphs in order, no record for a carved row, the parent's record kept only through an UPHELD
  recheck record (`outcome`, `refuter`, `attempt`, `date`, `digest` of the remaining text) and withdrawn otherwise; the
  older held editions' change records follow (a move renames `next.to` and `next.digests`; a split re-stamps the parent's
  digest unless the `reason` quotes eight consecutive words of carved text, which leaves it STALE). The regeneration writes
  the move batch of a relabelled decided row (`text_units.moves` → `TestResults/rule-moves/<year>.json`) and prints the
  command; the audit's "moved" refusal points there. The design names the operation `record_verdicts.py split`: it is
  a batch record kind, as the withdrawal is, so one writer and one all-or-nothing validation hold every store change.
  Built and self-tested on planted data, not applied (no decided unit moved; no row was carved).
