# PB4397 — the 2023 to 202x delta survey

**Owner decisions (2026-11-01, Mythos session).** (1) The analysis runs after the pipeline redesign lands (PB4323's last
notes, PB4395 and PB4396, are its `blocked_by`). (2) Asked what the analysis should produce (annotate `specs/ISO_COBOL.md`,
a full `ISO_COBOL_2027.md`, a separate delta document, or a delta in the existing edition-delta document), the owner chose
**"Delta in VERSION_CHANGE_REFERENCE"**.

**The source.** ISO/IEC CD 1.3 1989:202x, "Committee Draft International Standard", dated 2026-05-13, 1,323 pages, which
the GnuCOBOL maintainer expects to become the 2026/2027 standard. It is posted free in the GnuCOBOL forum thread
https://sourceforge.net/p/gnucobol/discussion/cobol/thread/65f89f44b0/ (ISO leadership permits sharing working and
committee drafts; the DIS of 2026-08 is restricted to ISO members, and the owner buys it). The local copy is OUTSIDE the
repository: `E:/COBOL-coord/specs-drafts/COBOL ISO_IEC 1989_202x-CD 1.3.pdf`. Extract its text the same way as the
earlier drafts there (`pdftotext -layout`), outside the repository too.

## What the analysis produces

A **change register of CD 1.3 against 1989:2023** in a new section of `docs/VERSION_CHANGE_REFERENCE.md`, marked at its
head as DRAFT and NON-NORMATIVE (the source is a committee draft; the DIS and the final standard may change or renumber
it). One row per change:

- the 2023 clause and the 202x clause it maps to (both; a renumbering is a row of its own class);
- the class: new feature, changed rule, removed or made obsolete or archaic, renumbered, editorial, or **clarifies an
  existing rule** (owner 2026-11-01: "The new version may also clarify an ambiguity in the 1985:2023 spec");
- the compiler impact: the subsystem it reaches (grammar, binding, validation, lowering, runtime, diagnostics, the
  version test matrix's `constructs.json`), and whether a 2023 program could change meaning or validity.

**The clarifies class.** A row where the draft's text settles a question the 2023 text (or an older edition's) leaves
ambiguous, without changing the rule. Each such row names the edition and clause it clarifies and the open `kb/Work` note
it bears on (an implementor-latitude choice in `docs/CONFORMANCE.md`, an adjudication, a GnuCOBOL-precedence decision), so
the note's owner can weigh it. The draft is evidence of the committee's intent, never authority over the published text
(CLAUDE.md rule 1): a clarification that contradicts what the 2023 text plainly says is a CHANGED RULE row instead.

**How far to trust a committee draft.** Before the survey, measure it: compare ISO/IEC CD2.2 1989:202x (2021-07-18, the
late committee draft of the cycle that became 1989:2023; `E:/COBOL-coord/specs-drafts/COBOL ISO_IEC 1989_2023-CD2_2.pdf`,
downloaded with owner approval 2026-11-01) with the published 2023 text over a sample of clauses, and record how much a late
CD changed before publication. Every CD 1.3 row then carries that measured confidence, not an assumed one.

Start from the draft's own change annex (the 202x counterpart of 2023's Annex E), then verify each entry against the
body text and sweep the body for changes the annex does not list. Render a page with the PDF when a general-format
diagram is load-bearing (the OCR'd diagrams were lossy before; CLAUDE.md rule 1).

## What it does NOT produce

- **No change to `specs/ISO_COBOL.md`.** It is the 2023 authority (CLAUDE.md rule 1) and `cite.py --check` validates
  clause numbers against it; draft text there would turn proposed rules into citable authority.
- **No full transcription** of the draft. That waits for the final standard, which then becomes the base spec while 2023
  joins the older editions as a delta (the shape the four editions already use).
- **No implementation work.** A row becomes a `kb/Work` note only on an owner decision to adopt a change early, or once
  the final standard is published. The register is a reference derived from the draft, never a work list (CLAUDE.md rule 8).
