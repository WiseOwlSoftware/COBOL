# PB5803 - at --std 85 pseudo-text that begins a line with two greater-than signs is refused as a compiler directive, which 1985 does not have

**What is wrong.** REPLACE and COPY REPLACING pseudo-text that begins a line with `>>` is refused at `--std 85` (COBOL2572, COBOL2697): the scanner looks for compiler directive lines at every edition, and 1985 has none, so `>>XYZ` is an ordinary text word there. Ruling: UPHELD (a), wrong at 85 only (the same three diagnostics are right at 2023). Harm: rejects_legal_source, misleads_diagnosis (the messages cite 2023 rules). The checker's confidence: high.

**Mechanism (the checker's reading).** 1985 has no compiler directive lines; `>>XYZ` is a sequence of COBOL characters (`>` is in the 1985 character set) bounded by separators, so a text word (`§1985:III.2`, Text Word (3)). Pseudo-text-1 holding it meets SR3 and pseudo-text-2 holding it meets SR4: the programs are legal and print `[OK  ]`. The edition-blind directive-line recognizer makes the line a directive line, the SR3 count drops to 0, and the 2023 screens of the replacement rules fire. Written on the REPLACE line itself (`== >>XYZ ==`) it compiles and runs (the checker's control `rp_ctl.cob`: exit 0, `[OK  ]`, at both editions), so only the line-initial form is hit. The sibling outside the batch's rows, COPY ... REPLACING with the same pseudo-text (`cp_pt1.cob`), is refused at 85 too.

**Sites:**
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Preprocessor/TextWord.cs#TextWordScanner` (recognizes a directive line through `CompilerDirectiveLine.TryParse`, with no edition test)
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Preprocessor/CopyProcessor.cs#ScreenOperandPair` (the SR10 arm at 85)
