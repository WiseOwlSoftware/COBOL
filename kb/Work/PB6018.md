# PB6018 - Weak diagnostics at --std 85: a paragraph header after a sentence without its period is read as one more operand

The procedure-division twin of PB6011. At --std 85, with `MOVE "B" TO A` (no period) followed by `P1.` beginning in area A
(probe `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/w20262131n/src/noperpara.cob`), the parse takes P1 as a second receiving operand of the MOVE, and the
user gets COBOL3735 "'P1' begins in area A" and COBOL1639 "'P1' is not defined" (at 2023: the COBOL1639 alone). The cause is the
missing separator period before the paragraph header: in the COBOL-85 reference format the paragraph-name "starts in area A"
(§1985:IV.7.3.3) and a sentence continued on a later line goes on in area B ("Successive sentences or entries either begin in area
B", §1985:IV.7.3.3), so a word beginning its line in area A and followed by a period (or SECTION) heads a paragraph or section.

PB6011 gave the parse the fact (`CobolParserCoreBase.AreaAOpenings`, `OpensAreaA`) and used it for the data division's integer
operands only (`nextEntryOpensHere`). The fix here is the same mechanism for the word operands of the procedure division: a left-edge
predicate on the rules a data or procedure reference opens with (`dataReference`, `procedureName`, the cobolWord operand rules) that
yields when the word begins its line in area A and writes a header (`LT(2)` a period or SECTION), with a drift test of the sites, and
the hint-14 arm PB6011 added then reports the period. Decide first what `PERFORM` then an area-A `P1.` should say (an operand in
area A is COBOL3735 legal-shape text, the header reading is the reference format's); sweep the conformance goldens at 85 that hold
a procedure-division word in area A. Not done with PB6011: the word operand rules are a far wider set of sites than the four integer
rules, and the brief of wave 20262131 group n scoped the data entry.
