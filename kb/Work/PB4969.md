# PB4969 - sub-list rows share their first list's citation

**Sites:** `scripts/spec/rule_catalog_1985.py#parse` (a row's `citation`), `scripts/spec/citation.py` (`RULE`, `NUMBERED_RULE`,
`parse`: the format has no sub-list part), `docs/rearchitecture/spec-rule-catalog-1985.json`; as landed:
`scripts/spec/citation.py` (`SUBLIST_RULE`, `rule_designator`, `_rule`), `scripts/spec/rule_catalog_1985.py`
(`Catalog.shared_citations`, `parse`'s `flush`), `docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md` §10.1

Measured: 43 citations in the 1985 catalog name two rows each. Example: `1985-GR-IV.4.4.2-1` (sublist 1) and
`1985-GR-IV.4.4.2-L2.1` (sublist 2) are both cited `§1985:IV.4.4.2 GR1`. The ids differ, but any tool, answer or golden
that names the rule by citation lands on two rules. The 2023 catalog has no `citation` field, so the defect is
1985-only. Design §2 ("THE RULE IS THE UNIT") defines sub-list ids but says nothing about their citation.

Expected: decide a citation for a sub-list rule in `citation.py`, the one format and parser. Write it into the catalog's
rows and into `cite.py --check`. Add a drift check that no two catalog rows share a citation.

## Landed

Re-probed: 43 citations named two rows each (2,253 rows; 54 rows in restarted lists). Fix at the root: `citation.rule_designator`
is the one writer of a rule designator, and a restarted list's rule is cited `GRL2.1` (`SUBLIST_RULE`, also read by `parse` and
`cite.py --check`, which canonicalizes `GR L2.1`); `rule_catalog_1985.flush` writes it, the 1985 catalog was regenerated, and
`Catalog.shared_citations` makes the extractor's `--check` fail on two rows sharing a citation (page-cited rows exempt).
Self-tests: `citation.py --self-test`, `rule_catalog_1985.py --self-test`. Design: DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md §10.1.
