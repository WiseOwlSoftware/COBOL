# PB4286 — a cascade COBOL0001 at the period after an operand-slot refusal at a reserved verb

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs:551` (`CobolErrorStrategy.SingleTokenDeletion`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs:569` (`CobolErrorStrategy.BeginsTheNextStatement`, new), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs:198` (`CobolErrorStrategy.Resynchronize`, new; `CobolErrorStrategy.Recover`, `CobolErrorStrategy.EndErrorCondition`), `tests/WiseOwl.Cobol.Tests.Unit/ParseRecoveryDeletionTests.cs`, `conformance-suite/wiseowl/cases/14.9.31-RECEIVE/pb4286-receive-giving-operands-omitted.cob`, `conformance-suite/wiseowl/cases/14.9.31-RECEIVE/pb4286-receive-giving-omitted-next-statement-read.cob`, `docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-frontend-grammar.md` (section 3.11).

## What happens

`RECEIVE FROM W-X GIVING` (the receiver missing) followed by `DISPLAY W-Y.` draws COBOL0001 at `DISPLAY` plus a cascade COBOL0001 at the period. It is identical on main, on the reviewed PB4208 branch and on the fixed one, so it is not in the mechanism of PB4208, PB4218, PB4207 or PB4214. The sibling `ADD 1 TO` + `DISPLAY W-Y.` draws no cascade. Repro: `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/w20261788d/r2/r.cob`.

The site is the CobolErrorStrategy recovery after an operand-slot refusal at a reserved verb that begins a complete next statement: after the refusal the recovery consumes the verb as the operand the missing slot stood for (`IsReportedReservedOperand`), leaving `W-Y.` to be refused at the period.

## Fix

Make the recovery for the RECEIVE GIVING slot resynchronise at the next statement the way the ADD ... TO slot does (find why the two differ: probably a distinct slot production), then sweep every operand slot whose refusal can sit at a reserved verb (`SEND`, `RECEIVE`, `SET`, `ACCEPT`). Golden: the repro with exactly two diagnostics.

## Landed (wave 20261804 group B)

**Re-probe (2026-11-01, base 2ce2e70a2).** The repro drew `(10,12) COBOL0001: unexpected 'DISPLAY'` and `(10,23) COBOL0001:
unexpected '.'`; `ADD 1 TO` + `DISPLAY W-Y.` drew the one. The note's mechanism was wrong: DISPLAY is not
reservation-gated, so no COBOL0901 is made and `IsReportedReservedOperand` never runs. Two mechanisms, one after the
other:

1. **Single-token deletion dropped the DISPLAY.** ISO §14.9.31.2 prints `GIVING identifier-1 data-name-2` (cite.py OK
   "identifier-1 data-name-2"), two unbracketed operands. PB4133's decline asks only whether the token may follow the
   ONE element missing (identifier-1, whose follow is data-name-2), so the deletion acted because W-Y fits
   identifier-1, and the period met the missing data-name-2. The same shape: `RECEIVE FROM` + `DISPLAY W-Y.` (expected
   GIVING at the period), `SEARCH ALL` + `DISPLAY W-Y.` (expected WHEN). ADD's receiver may be followed by a statement,
   which is why ADD drew one. Fix: `SingleTokenDeletion` declines when `BeginsTheNextStatement` (the base deletion would
   act, the token may follow the innermost open `statement` completed in its real context, and it begins a statement
   that is a complete reading, PB3248's test).
2. **ANTLR's same-state guard then consumed the DISPLAY.** With the deletion declined, the missing identifier-1
   resynchronized at DISPLAY, then the missing data-name-2 failed at the SAME `dataReference` state, and the base
   `Recover` consumed one token because it keys "no progress" on the state alone: the next statement was lost into the
   RECEIVE's tail (`... DISPLAY W-Y MOVE 1 W-N.` reported nothing for the MOVE). Fix: `Resynchronize` keys the guard
   on (state, invoking state); a loop failing again still consumes.

The note's golden "with exactly two diagnostics" was a slip: the cascade-free count is ONE (the ADD sibling's).
