# PB4919 - the grammar against the 1985 general formats

**What was measured.** PB4849 gave the optional-word audit a scan arm: it reads the decided 1985 drawings
(`figure_options.read_drawn`). Run as `python scripts/spec/audit_grammar_optional_words.py --edition 1985` over the 213 1985
formats, it reports 36 candidates where the grammar and the 1985 format disagree on whether a word is optional. They fall in
two directions:
- **The grammar demands a word that the 1985 format prints as optional, not underlined.** Examples: `memorySizeClause` SIZE,
  `rerunClause` EVERY and OF, `symbolicCharacterEntry` IS/ARE, `readKey` KEY, `valueOfClause` IS. If the grammar reaches
  these rules under `--std=1985`, legal 1985 source is rejected.
- **The grammar lets source omit a word that the 1985 format underlines, so it is required.** Examples: `useStatement` AFTER
  and PROCEDURE, EVALUATE ALSO, PERFORM VARYING BY, SIGN SEPARATE. Under `--std=1985`, illegal source is accepted.
The full list is in the source report.

**Each candidate needs a decision before any grammar change.** Three questions:
1. Does the 1985 format really print it so? Read it from its rendered page, not from the drawing.
2. Does the rule in question apply under `--std=1985`? Some of these rules are 2023-only, or are gated off for 1985.
3. Is the difference a real 1985-versus-2023 language difference? If it is, the edition gate is the fix. If not, the grammar
   is wrong for every edition. CLAUDE.md rule 1's "falsely restrictive" diagrams cut the other way here: the 1985 drawing was
   decided from the page by PB4849, so it is the authority.

This is part of the 1985 audit (PB4886): each confirmed candidate becomes a 1985 inventory row, or a defect note with a
witness compiled under `--std=1985`. It is filed now so the audit starts with it rather than finding it again.

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar` (whole: the rules named above), `scripts/spec/audit_grammar_optional_words.py` (`main`).
