# PB656 — LANDED — two tables about two facts

## Landed (wave 1084 B)

**The measurement.** Every `constructs.json` row with `removedIn` was joined against `reserved-words.json`. PADDING
was already consistent (PB300 had set `removedIn: 2014` where the flag drops). SEGMENT-LIMIT is the one pair still
flagged reserved at its construct's `removedIn` (2002). A wider sweep found 35 rows whose provenance said
"removed post-85" while their own 2002/2014 flags were set, and one facility half reserved at 2002.

**The decision (CLAUDE.md rule 1 precedence; `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` D-RW3).** The repository holds no 2002/2014
text, so GnuCOBOL decides, and it was surveyed, not recalled (GnuCOBOL 3.2 in WSL, `cobc -std=…`):
- SEGMENT-LIMIT: `-std=cobol2002` refuses the clause ("SEGMENT LIMIT does not conform to COBOL 2002") AND
  `01 SEGMENT-LIMIT` (syntax error, a reserved word); `-std=cobol2014` admits the word. A word's reservation and
  its construct's availability are two facts; nothing in the standard ties one to the other. **No flag changes**;
  VCR row 7.8 and the registry row record it. The note's premise ("a word cannot be reserved by an edition that no
  longer has the construct") is not a rule of the standard and is withdrawn.
- COBOL: `-std=cobol2002` and `-std=cobol2014` refuse `01 COBOL`. **The flag stands; the provenance was wrong.**
  The generator's `$f85 -and -not $f23` branch labelled all 35 such rows "removed post-85"; it now names the last
  reserving edition from the flags it describes.
- SEND, RECEIVE, END-RECEIVE at 2002: the generator cleared r2002 on ISO Annex E.2 item 25, but E.1 scopes Annex E
  to "the previous COBOL standard" (2014). GnuCOBOL's 2002 list reserves them and `cobol2002.conf` marks the
  communication facility obsolete at 2002, the facility whose CD/DISABLE/ENABLE/PURGE/... rows already read
  r2002 true. **r2002 is now true** (the item-25 rule became an ASSERTION on the 2014 list);
  `receive-as-user-word` / `end-receive-as-user-word` now have `introducedIn: 2014`.
- PADDING CHARACTER: `cobol2002.conf` sets `padding-character-clause: obsolete` and `cobc -std=cobol2002` warns
  that it is obsolete, which VCR row 7.20 had named as the one-line change: `obsoleteIn: 2002` (COBOLNET0903).

**Witnesses.** `conformance:2023/pb656_directive_words_as_user_words` (§8.3.2.1 2): §8.12 words as system-names,
data-names, a condition-name, a section-name and a paragraph-name beside >>DEFINE/>>TURN/>>IF);
`negative/pb656-cobol-reserved-through-2014` (85 2002 2014); `conformance:2002/pb656_padding_character_obsolete_2002`;
`negative/user-word-receive` / `user-word-end-receive` now reject at 85 2002 2023. Row SR-8.3.2.1-2 PARTIAL →
CONFORMS (batch `w1084b-PB656`).

Found 2026-09-05 by the `PB300` implementer (report §"New defects" D2), which needed the removal edge of the
PADDING CHARACTER clause and found the two places that state edition facts disagreeing. §8.9 is authoritative for
the 2023 edition only (`OK §8.9`); for 1985, 2002 and 2014 the repository has no text, and
`tests/version-matrix/reserved-words.json`'s `r85`/`r2002`/`r2014` flags are generated (`scripts/gen-reserved-words.ps1`,
[[PB618]]'s subject) from GnuCOBOL's per-standard word lists — which are that compiler's diagnostic lists and can
keep a word reserved past the deletion of the construct that spelled it.

## The measurement to make

Join `tests/version-matrix/constructs.json` rows carrying `removedIn` against `reserved-words.json` on the
construct's leading word(s), and report every pair still flagged reserved AT `removedIn` (SEGMENT-LIMIT / 2002,
PADDING / 2014 are the two known). For each pair, decide from the only evidence available — the 2023 text's Annex
on removed elements where it lists them, the construct's own history in `docs/VERSION_CHANGE_REFERENCE.md`, and
(per [[survey_compilers_on_latitude]]) what the other compilers reserve at that standard — and record the decision
in the VCR row beside the construct, then correct the FLAG (a one-line JSON edit) so the two tables agree; the
generator's input, not its output, carries the correction ([[PB618]]).

## Why it matters

An over-reserved word at an edition is [[PB655]]'s class 3 by another route: `01 SEGMENT-LIMIT PIC X.` at
`--std 2002` is refused on a flag the standard of that year may not support. Consistency between the two tables is
also what lets [[PB655]]'s drift test be written against ONE source of edition truth.
