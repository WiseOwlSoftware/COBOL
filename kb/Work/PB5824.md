# PB5824 - OPEN: is TERMINATE's control break level FINAL (when FINAL is a control) or data-name-1? The 1985 text supports both and the result differs

**Ruling: NOT DECIDED (an open reading).** TERMINATE's control break level: the text supports FINAL (when FINAL is a control) and it supports data-name-1; writing FINAL changes the result at both editions (LINE-COUNTER 3 against 6, identical at `--std 85` and `--std 2023`). The row `1985-GR-XIII.3.16.4-3` stays `undecided`. Harm: wrong_answer under reading B only (not set while the row is open). The checker's confidence: medium. The ruling's note: an open reading, settled by CLAUDE.md rule 1's order, then a conformance entry; GnuCOBOL was not measured (it is not on this host).

**The two readings (the checker's).**
- Reading A (the code): TERMINATE produces every footing, so its highest break level is FINAL when FINAL is a control; with no CF FINAL declared, CF MAJ is below the highest level and its NEXT GROUP is ignored (GR3). Support: FINAL is the highest control (`§1985:XIII.3.7.4 GR1`, quoted below), and the GENERATE text processes only the footings not more major than the highest level of the break (`§1985:XIII.4.3.4 GR6a`, quoted below), so a TERMINATE that produces CF FINAL implies the break is at FINAL.
- Reading B (the refuter's): TERMINATE acts as though a control break had been sensed in the most major control data-name (`§1985:XIII.4.7.4 GR1`, quoted below); FINAL is not a data-name, so the break level is data-name-1 and CF MAJ's NEXT GROUP applies whether or not FINAL is written. Against B: the "as though" clause sits in the sentence about prior control values.
- The text admits both. The NEXT GROUP rule itself is `§1985:XIII.3.16.4 GR3` and the definition of the control break level is `§1985:III.2` (both quoted below).
- What settles it: rule 1's order (what GnuCOBOL does with `-std=cobol85`, measured; failing that IBM Enterprise COBOL or Micro Focus, from their manuals) and a `docs/conformance/` entry for 1985.
