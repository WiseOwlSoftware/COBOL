§8.3.3.1: *"If the opening and closing delimiters are contiguous, the length of the literal is zero, and it
is known as a zero-length literal."* COBOL-85 has no such literal.

## The code site — and why this is a `constructs.json` job, not a MOVE one

The literal is produced by the LEXER; nothing downstream can gate it, because by the time a binder sees it
the token is just a literal of length 0. The work is:

1. a `constructs.json` row for the zero-length literal with its introducing edition (the existing row is
   `ref-mod-zero-length-2023`, a DIFFERENT construct — reference modification with a zero length);
2. an introduction gate at the lexer/token level keyed on that row;
3. `docs/VERSION_CHANGE_REFERENCE.md`;
4. ⛔ **the EDITION-GATE SWEEP** (memory `edition_gate_sweep`): gating a construct breaks every existing
   test that compiles it below the new edition. Enumerate the `85` and `2002` goldens that contain `""` /
   `B""` / `N""` BEFORE landing, not after the gate goes red.

The introducing edition itself has to be DERIVED — §8.3.3.1 as printed in 2023 cannot settle whether the
form entered at 2002 or 2014. Use `docs/VERSION_CHANGE_REFERENCE.md`'s method and the historical revisions,
and record the derivation in the note before writing the row.
