# PB955 — OPEN — the right clause, the wrong question, eight times

## The two clauses

```
$ python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 13.18.40.3 "the number of digit positions described by character-string-1 shall range from 1 through 31"
OK  §13.18.40.3 14)  (Syntax rules)
    14) For data items of category numeric, and for fixed-point data items of category numeric-edited, the
        number of digit positions described by character-string-1 shall range from 1 through 31.

$ python scripts/spec/cite.py --check 8.3.3.3.2 "fixed-point numeric literals of 1 through 31 digits in length"
OK  §8.3.3.3.2   (Fixed-point numeric literals)
    A fixed-point numeric literal is a character-string whose characters are selected from the digits '0'
    through '9', the plus sign, the minus sign, and the decimal point. …
```

Both are real; both `--check` clean; they answer **different questions**. §8.3.3.3.2 bounds a LITERAL written
in the source. §13.18.40.3 SR14 bounds the digit positions a PICTURE describes — the ITEM. A reader who
follows the citation from a sentence about a 31-digit *item* lands in the literal clause, finds a rule about
something else, and either believes the wrong thing or re-derives it. `feedback_a_real_clause_can_answer_a_different_question`
— the failure mode CLAUDE.md rule 1 names, and the one `--check` cannot catch because the quotation is genuine.

## Why neither audit sees it

`scripts/spec/audit_code_citations.py` documents its own reach (`:28-36`): **SUBJECT** fires when *"the cited
clause's catalog subject is a NAMED construct … whose name appears NOWHERE in the citation's context, while the
context DOES name another construct that has a clause of its own"*, and **HEADER** fires on a
definition-header comment naming a construct. §8.3.3.3.2's catalog subject is *Fixed-point numeric literals* —
not a named construct — and the context says *item*, *receiver*, *argument*, which are not constructs with
clauses of their own. So neither check has anything to notice, and `--check` passes because the QUOTED text is
genuinely in §8.3.3.3.2. This note's fix is therefore TWO things, and the second is the durable one:

1. Repair the eight sites (and re-read the one flagged ⚠) so each names the clause that answers ITS question.
2. ⭐ Decide whether the audit can reach this class at all. The candidate is a SUBJECT-CLASS check: when the
   cited clause's subject is a *literal* and the context's noun is *item* / *receiver* / *data item* / *PICTURE*
   (or vice versa), that is a scope mismatch a machine CAN see, unlike the general case. If it cannot be made
   quiet enough to gate on, say so in the note rather than leaving the reader to assume the audits cover it —
   [[PB476]] is the standing note about an audit whose population nobody measures, and this is a hole in its
   REACH, not in its population.
