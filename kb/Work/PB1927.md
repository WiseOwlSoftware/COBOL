# PB1927 — OPEN

Filed by the train-1004 lander from wave 1004 group E's report (`w1004e-PB1226-report.md`).

## Question

§13.18.57.4 GR6 c): an OR PAGE control heading is reprinted after a page advance, provided the advance "did not take
place just before the printing of a control footing at a lower control level". GR7 d) 4. places a footing's upper limit
after the OR PAGE headings at its own level or higher. For a footing at a lower level than an OR PAGE heading the two
point opposite ways. Wave 1004 group E (kb/Work PB1298, which records the conflict in its landed section) follows the
proviso as written.

**Site.** `src/Cobol.Net.Runtime/IO/ReportWriter.cs#PresentOrPageHeadings` (called from `CobolReport.AdvancePage`).
Golden `conformance:2002/pb1298_control_heading_or_page` does not exercise the conflicting case.

## Orchestrator analysis 2026-10-03 (NOT a verdict; the note stays OPEN)
Both clauses were re-run through `cite.py --check` (§13.18.57.4 6) c) 2., OK; 7) d) 4., OK). Reading them again: a footing at a
LOWER level than an OR PAGE heading has that heading at a HIGHER level, and "at the same level as the control footing, or
higher" in GR7 d) 4. includes it, so d) 4. gives the footing an upper limit BELOW the heading's last line. GR6 c)'s proviso
says that heading is NOT reprinted when the advance comes just before such a footing. If the heading is not printed, there is
no "last line of the heading" on the page for the limit to follow. The two can be reconciled by reading d) 4. as applying
only when the heading is on the page, but that is a reading, not text, and I earlier called the rules non-conflicting without
that caveat; that was too confident. **Not closed from the spec.**
**GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 (cobc, WSL) does not settle it either:** it accepts `TYPE CONTROL HEADING ... OR PAGE` syntactically, but my probe
(PAGE LIMIT 4 to 8, three details, then a lower-level footing) never emitted a page advance, so the proviso case was never
reached. Next step: build a probe that forces the advance (explicit FIRST DETAIL / LAST DETAIL), then record the outcome as
an implementor decision in `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` per "a defect in the standard is recorded, never silently coded around".

**Verdict needed.** The reading that reconciles the two rules (cite both), and a golden with a lower-level footing that
forces a page advance.

## Adjudication 2026-10-07 (w1033)

**Spec (cite.py --check OK; the refuter extracted PDF pages 527-528 and confirms the printed text matches the transcription word for word, so it is not a transcription slip).**
- `§13.18.57.4 6) c)` (cite.py path `6) c) 2.`): the OR PAGE heading is printed after each page advance "provided that the page advance did not take place just before the printing of a control footing at a lower control level".
- `§13.18.57.4 7) d) 4.`: for a control footing the upper limit is "the line following the last line of the lowest-level control heading with an OR PAGE phrase at the same level as the control footing, or higher", else the FIRST DETAIL integer; `7) d) 2.` (lower CH: after the next higher-level CH), `7) d) 3.` (detail: after the lowest-level OR PAGE CH); `7)` definition of the upper limit.
- `§13.18.35.4 5) b) 3.` (first body group on a page goes to FIRST DETAIL), `4) b)`, `5) a)`; `§13.18.35.3 6) c)` (absolute lines within limits), `9)`; `§13.18.57.4 6) e) 1.`; `§13.18.16.4 1)-2)` (FINAL is the highest level), `4) a)`.
- The proviso read literally collides with d) 4. in both directions. Read with the referent of "lower" as the HEADING's level ("not reprinted before a control footing when the heading is at a lower level than it"), the proviso and GR7 d) 1.-4. agree: headings of groups still open (footing's level and above) are repeated, those of groups the break closed (below the footing) are not.
- Precedence (rule 1, because the printed sentence is self-contradictory): GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 implements no OR PAGE semantics (`do_page_heading` never reprints one; observed no form feed, a stray `M=1` before `Y=1`); IBM SC26-4301 IBM manual section 3.24.4 rule 1.c: "no CONTROL HEADINGS below the level of the CONTROL FOOTING are printed" (worked example point (C): only those at the same level or above), which is exactly GR7 d) 4. Local copy `PB1927/ibm/c2643014.pdf`, text lines 11294-11440.
- The refuter corrects the decision: Micro Focus (COBOL-IT Report Writer reference) DOES document OR PAGE, in IBM's lineage (its OR PAGE fit rule is word for word SC26-4301 IBM manual section 3.24.2 rule 11); its footing-caused-advance rule could not be retrieved, so it corroborates but does not prove. The BS2000 claim is unevidenced and BS2000 is not in the precedence chain.
- Independent of which reading is chosen: the compiler's own two halves already disagree. `DataBinder.Reports.cs#GroupLimits` implements d) 4. as printed (`orPage.Where(x => x.ControlLevel <= g.ControlLevel)`) while `ReportWriter.cs#PresentOrPageHeadings` implements the proviso as printed; refuter probe `r4` (a program the binder accepts) overprints two groups on one line under the literal proviso.

**Expected.** When a control footing at level F causes the page advance, print the OR PAGE control headings at level F and every higher level, major to minor, after the page heading, and skip those below F.

## Landed (wave 1037 G, with PB2513) — a footing-caused page advance reprints the OR PAGE headings at its level and above

Re-probed on a fresh build before the fix (`pb1927_cf_lower`, `pb1927_cf_higher`, `r4`, `r2` from the adjudication): all four reproduced exactly as recorded.
`ReportWriter.cs#PresentOrPageHeadings`, the `ControlFooting` arm, skipped `ch.ControlLevel < causing.ControlLevel` (the headings MORE MAJOR than the footing); it now skips `ch.ControlLevel > causing.ControlLevel` (the headings BELOW the footing's level, which the footing's own break prints in their turn). ControlLevel is major to minor with FINAL at its index in the CONTROLS list, so a CF FINAL reprints only a CH FINAL OR PAGE. The ControlHeading arm and the detail arm were the other two arms of the same dispatch and were already right (`ch.ControlLevel >= causing.ControlLevel` skip; none skipped).
