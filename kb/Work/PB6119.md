# PB6119 - a separator period where an operand is due still draws the bare COBOL0001 for three statements

PB6113 made hint 0i's operand arm (`AdmittedKinds.Refusal`) name the open statement and the missing operand (COBOL5506) when a separator
period stands where an operand is due. The arm keeps its statement-position guard: a failed prediction whose walk admits every statement
start leaves the bare COBOL0001 ("unexpected .").

**Repro (each draws COBOL0001 or "cannot parse construct near ."):**
```cobol
SET I TO.
COMPUTE N =.
IF N = .
```
**Site.** `AdmittedKinds.Refusal` (statement-position guard); `CobolErrorStrategy.GuessCobolIntent` hint 0i.

**Deliberate and out of scope.** Bare verbs that also read as a paragraph header (`FREE.`, `VALIDATE.`, `RAISE.`, `INVOKE.`, `ALLOCATE.`,
an inline `PERFORM.`) keep COBOL0001 on purpose: a paragraph named FREE is legal source.

**Left by the review.** Review lead R1-3 (low) names `tests/WiseOwl.Cobol.Tests.Conformance/ReservedWordPositionConformanceTests.cs` as a
file the PB6113 change left untouched, without saying what it holds; the implementer's gate logs show its
`ABareVerbSentence_IsAnIncompleteStatement_NeverNamedCobol0901` cases (FREE., RAISE. at 2002/2014) were red before the fix. Re-read that
file against the PB6113 change when this note is worked.

**Landed (wave 20262166 A).** Two causes, both read at `AdmittedKinds.Refusal` and `CobolErrorStrategy.GuessCobolIntent` hint 0i, and a sweep of every
statement verb bare (`VERB.`, 59 verbs, 2023) and of the operand shapes of SET, COMPUTE, IF, EVALUATE and PERFORM.
(1) `SET I TO.`, `COMPUTE N =.`, `IF N = .`, `EVALUATE.`, `PERFORM VARYING I FROM 1 BY.` and `SET I UP BY.` admit the grouping `(` (LPAREN: the
opening of a parenthesized arithmetic expression), and the arm's symbol test (`operand.Symbols.Count > 0`: a symbol means the element before the token
is complete) read it as such; the period arm now names `'('` among the alternatives and no longer treats the grouping paren as completion
(`AdmittedKinds.GroupingParen`). `IF N > 1 AND.` (relational operators after AND) keeps COBOL0001: see the new defect in the report of this wave.
(2) `ACCEPT.`, `SEARCH.`, `DELETE.`, `RECEIVE.`, `SEND.`, `RESUME.`, `UNLOCK.`, `FREE.`, `VALIDATE.`, `RAISE.`, `INVOKE.`, `ALLOCATE.` failed in the prediction that chooses among
the verb's formats (dispatcher `statement`) or between a paragraph header and the verb (`paragraphDefinition`), before any statement rule was entered, so
no statement was open and the period named none. `CobolErrorStrategy.StatementOpenAtAPeriod` now names the verb the parser stands at when it is
a reserved word of the compile edition (§2023:8.9 through `ReservedWords.IsReservedAt`; §2023:8.3.2.1 SR1), so it cannot be the paragraph-name the period would
end. The note's "deliberate" list was wrong for the editions that reserve the verb: `FREE.` at 2002-2023 is no legal paragraph-name; at COBOL-85 (FREE,
UNLOCK, RESUME not reserved) and at COBOL-2014 for RECEIVE and SEND the word remains a paragraph-name and no statement is named. Only inline `PERFORM.` keeps COBOL0001.
R1-3: `ReservedWordPositionConformanceTests` accepted COBOL5506 or COBOL0001 for a bare verb, so it could not tell the fix from the defect; the new
`ABareVerbOrAnOperandStartingWithAParen_AtThePeriod_NamesTheStatement` asserts the named statement per row.
