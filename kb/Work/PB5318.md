# PB5318 - the citation audit checks a 2023 rule number twice, two ways

**Sites:**

- `scripts/spec/audit_code_citations.py` (`spec_ordinals`, `catalog_rule_tops`, `_alias`, `_ordinal_findings`, `quote_rules`:
  the RULE, SUBITEM, QUOTE-RULE and FORMAT arms of the ordinal family, kb/Work PB388, PB597)
- `scripts/spec/audit_code_citations.py` (`resolve_line`, `resolve_scan`: RESOLVE, kb/Work PB5317)

**Mechanism.** kb/Work PB5317 made `cite.resolve` the one checker of every part of a citation in every edition (the rule
list of the cited kind, the ordinal, the sub-item, `Format n`, a quotation inside the rule) and gave the CITATIONS audit its
RESOLVE check, which runs it over every edition-form citation of the repository. The ordinal family predates it and reads
the 2023 standard alone with its own reader of rule counts (`spec_ordinals`) and of quotation paths (`quote_rules` through
`cite.rule_path`). Two mechanisms now decide one question (CLAUDE.md rule 4: one rule in one place).

They also disagree. `_alias` accepts a rule block's clause written as its statement's clause (`§2023:14.9.39 SR17` for
`§2023:14.9.39.3 SR17`: "this repository deliberately writes a block's clause both ways"); RESOLVE refuses it
(`no-rule-list`, naming the subclause), as `cite.py --check` always has, since the region of 14.9.39 ends where 14.9.39.1
begins. RESOLVE's baseline (`scripts/spec/data/cite-resolve-baseline.json`) holds 1,514 such citations (edition 2023,
`no-rule-list`, the message naming the cited clause's subclause or sibling clause), each with its exact repair; the
baseline's other 116 entries are kb/Work PB5333's.

**Fix direction.** Burn those 1,514 entries down with the suggestions RESOLVE prints (a mechanical
rewrite, reviewed), then delete RULE, SUBITEM, QUOTE-RULE, FORMAT and `_alias`, their self-tests moving to
`resolve_self_test`. FORMAT-RULE (a rule under a format's banner) and FORMAT-NAME (a format named by its words) ask
questions RESOLVE does not and stay.
