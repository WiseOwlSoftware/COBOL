# PB5805 - OPEN: may the integer of WRITE ADVANCING carry a written sign at --std 85 (+3 accepted, -0 refused)? Settled by rule 1's order and a 1985 conformance entry

**Ruling: OPEN.** WRITE ADVANCING accepts `+3` and refuses `-0` at `--std 85` (and at `--std 2023`, identically). `§1985:VII.4.7.3 SR5` says integer-1 "may be positive or zero, but must not be negative"; the glossary says an integer is "neither signed nor zero unless explicitly allowed". Whether SR5 allows a written sign is not decided by the text. It is settled by CLAUDE.md rule 1's order (GnuCOBOL measured, then the vendor manuals) and a 1985 conformance entry in `docs/conformance/`. Accepting ZERO is right either way. The row `1985-SR-VII.4.7.3-5` stays `undecided`. Harm: depends on the reading (not set while the row is open): under_rejects under reading A, rejects_legal_source under reading B. The checker's confidence: medium.

**The readings (the checker's).**
- **Reading A** (the amended glossary taken literally, "neither signed nor zero unless explicitly allowed"): SR5 explicitly allows zero, not a sign, so `+3` and `+0` must be refused (under_rejects), and `-0`'s refusal is right.
- **Reading B**: SR5's "must not be negative" presumes a sign may be written, so the value decides; `-0` has the value zero (`§1985:IV.4.2.2.2.2` GR4) and its refusal rejects legal source, while `+3` is right.
- **A third reading** (a minus-signed literal counts as negative) makes the compiler conform.
- **ZERO is settled for the compiler:** a 1985 literal includes a reserved word referencing a figurative constant (`§1985:IV.4.2.2.2`), so ZERO is a numeric literal of value zero, which SR5 allows.
- **What settles the signs:** CLAUDE.md rule 1: GnuCOBOL `-std=cobol85` measured on `+3` and `-0`, else IBM Enterprise COBOL or Micro Focus from their manuals; the choice recorded for 1985 in `docs/conformance/` (one file per entry). If the chosen reading is also 2023's, the 2023 row `SR-14.9.51.3-15` is affected (not judged).
