# PB1686 — OPEN

Filed by the train-70 lander from wave 70 V3's report (implementer of PB1509; `w70v3-PB1509-report.md`, lead 2).

## Finding

**Repro.** `E:\Temp\claude\E--COBOL\73cac64c-391d-42c0-bcdf-c880e3e10305\scratchpad\w70v3\p\sumtr.cob`: a SUM
counter of PICTURE 9V99 accumulating an addend of three decimal places, 1.000 then −0.005. The report prints 1.00;
the same two additions written as `ADD` statements into a 9V99 item give 0.99.

**Rule** (`cite.py --check` OK). §13.18.54.4 3): "The current content of each addend is implicitly added into the
sum counter during execution of GENERATE and TERMINATE statements at specific times defined below. The adding is
consistent with the general rules of the ADD statement …" — ADD forms the exact sum and only then stores it to the
receiver's scale.

**Code site.** `src/Cobol.Net.Compiler/CodeGen/Verbs/ReportWriterEmitter.cs:555` —
`NumericRenderer.Align(num.Render(v, …), sum.Scale, sum.Rounding)` is applied PER ADDEND, inside the addend delegate,
so the fraction below the counter's scale is lost before the addition.

**Mechanism.** The engine's counter (`CobolReport.SumEntry`, Int128 since PB1509) holds the value at the counter's
scale, so the addend delegate aligns first. ADD-consistency needs the addition at the wider of the two scales and the
store (truncation, or GR4's ROUNDED) after it — the engine would carry the addend's own scale or a wider working scale.
