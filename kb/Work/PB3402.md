# PB3402 — the soft cap's STOP-UNIT stops the landing lane

**What happened (2026-10-18, operator session c95c0b86).** The loop's `enqueue` unit (session f62eee93, started 20:02:57)
passed the 200,000-token soft cap at 21:17:53, and the supervisor wrote `STOP-UNIT`
(`orchestrate-20261017-212242.out.log` line 399: "context 200036 > soft cap 200000: STOP-UNIT (the unit starts no new
step and launches no fleet; its running fleet finishes and lands first)"). At 21:18 the unit's same-head re-reviews stamped
two branches CLEAN: `notes-pb3263-pb3386-leads-20261018` at ebbf7b71e (r3) and `inbox-guard-stop-hook-20261018` at
b9831fcf5 (r4). From 21:18:42 the landing lane logged "land lane: STOP-UNIT (E:\COBOL-coord\STOP-UNIT): no landing
starts (a running one finishes)" at every tick, ten times, until the unit ended at 22:04:14. Both branches started landing
at 22:05:08, 47 minutes after they were ready. It was the second time that evening: the previous enqueue unit passed
the same cap at 19:11:27 (line 379), and the lane started nothing until that unit ended at 19:37:52.

**Why.** `STOP-UNIT` carries two meanings with one file. The design doc's table
(`docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-orchestrator-loop.md`, the `STOP-UNIT` row) says the supervisor creates it "for either
context cap and for `STOP`": it asks the RUNNING UNIT to start no new step. PB3103 (design point 2) made it a stop for the
landing lane too: the lane "stops starting attempts once STOP or STOP-UNIT exists", and `land_lane.stop_reason`
(`scripts/orchestrator/land_lane.py`) lists it beside the loop's `STOP`, the owner's global `STOP` and `HALT`. That is right
for `STOP` and the hard cap, where the loop winds down. It is wrong for the soft cap. A landing is its own process
(`enqueue.py land`), it reads nothing from the unit's transcript and adds nothing to its context, and the branches it lands
are already reviewed. The unit's context state then decides how long finished work waits, against the owner's rule of
2026-10-18 12:10 ("We should not be holding finished work this long, continually postponing it").

**The fix (landed; the owner, 2026-10-18 ~23:58: "Stop the soft cap from holding landings").** Every stop meant to
halt the loop already has a file of its own: the loop's `STOP`, the owner's global `STOP`, `HALT`, and the hard
cap's fleet stop `scratch\STOP-loop`, which the supervisor writes beside `STOP-UNIT` for the hard cap and for `STOP`.
The soft cap writes `STOP-UNIT` alone. So `land_lane.stop_reason` now reads the fleet stop in place of `STOP-UNIT`:
- the soft cap, and an operator's bare `STOP-UNIT`, no longer hold a landing;
- the hard cap, `STOP`, the global `STOP` and `HALT` still do, as before.
The supervisor, the units and `dispatch_guard.py` are unchanged: `STOP-UNIT` still tells the running unit to start no
new step and launch no fleet.

The same change updates `land_lane.py`'s module docstring and `decide`'s docstring, the design doc
(`DESIGN-orchestrator-loop.md`: the file table's `STOP-UNIT` and `scratch\STOP-loop` rows, the context-policy table's
soft row, section 4.9's LAUNCHES step, the fleet paragraph and the self-test list) and MANDATORY-PRACTICES O11.
`land_lane.py --self-test` case 7 plants `STOP`, the hard cap's pair (`STOP-UNIT` with the fleet stop) and the global
`STOP`, and each starts nothing. Case 7b plants `STOP-UNIT` alone beside a WAITING TO LAND row and asserts that the
attempt starts. Mutation-checked: with `STOP-UNIT` back in `stop_reason`, case 7b alone goes RED.
