# PB4582 — the next night's adjudicators do not read the verdicts the earlier nights settled

**Sites:** `scripts/fuzz_triage.py#plan_filing` (where a record's family is matched against the existing `upstream` notes), `scripts/fuzz_triage_briefs/common.md` (what an adjudicator is told to read first), `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/fuzz-vet/step1/p/prior_notes.py` (step 1's comparison of night 10's signatures with the earlier nights, outside the repository).

**Observed.** An adjudicator decides each signature from the spec and the repro alone. What nights 1 to 9 decided about the same mechanism is in the register only as a LATITUDE or non-defect line scattered in the notes, so night 10 re-decided it. PB4545 (the DYNAMIC LENGTH clause) is the case that shows it: nights 7, 8 and 9 had each reached LATITUDE on the signature THEY_REJECT:13.18.19, night 10's refuter upheld it as a defect, and only the vetting reversed it (an optional element of A.4.5 1) that cobc does not claim, and feature-requests #109). Step 1 counted three earlier LATITUDE verdicts reversed. PB4519 (LATITUDE for native arithmetic) and PB4551 (device dependence) are the same kind: a settled question decided again from nothing.

**Fix shape.** The input is derived, not hand-kept. `fuzz_triage.py prepare` emits, per signature of the night, the verdicts earlier nights reached on the same signature (read from the fuzz history it already keeps, `scripts/fuzz_history.py`, and from the `upstream` and `process_only` notes that name the signature), and the adjudicator brief says a verdict that reverses an earlier one must cite the new evidence. No new list is opened: the data is the history and the notes already in `kb/Work`.
