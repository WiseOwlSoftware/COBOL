# PB3703 — should the landing lane outlive the supervisor's exit 4 and exit 5?

**Sites:** `scripts/orchestrator/orchestrate.ps1` (`Step-Lane`, `Wait-Until`, the loop top where the circuit breaker exits 4 and an owner question exits 5), `scripts/orchestrator/stop.ps1` (the restart protocol: live lock, exit 3), `docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-orchestrator-loop.md` (section 4.9, the landing lane).

## The question

PB3639 made the lane tick for as long as the supervisor lives (one timer, `Step-Lane`, in `Invoke-Unit`, `Wait-Until` and between
units). The lane is a part of the supervisor process, so when the supervisor ends the lane ends. The circuit breaker (exit 4) and
an owner question (exit 5) end it: every branch that becomes WAITING TO LAND or is re-armed afterwards waits for someone to restart
the loop, although landing needs neither a unit nor a budget.

## Options for the owner

1. Leave as is: a trip or a question stops landing until the loop restarts. No code change.
2. A lane-only linger: on exit 4 or 5 the supervisor keeps ticking `land_lane.py` (no units) until STOP or the restart. It changes the
   restart protocol (a live supervisor lock, exit 3, `stop.ps1` would all meet a process that is up but runs no units) and needs the
   owner's call on what a restart does to a lingering lane, and on whether a breaker trip should keep landing at all (the breaker
   exists to stop a run of failures; the lane lands work that is already reviewed and gated).
3. A separate lane process with its own lock, started by the supervisor and stopped only by STOP.

Shares PB3639's root (landed), which fixed the in-process cases.
