# PB3703 — should the landing lane outlive the supervisor's exit 4 and exit 5?

**Sites:** `scripts/orchestrator/orchestrate.ps1` (`Step-Lane`, `Wait-Until`, the loop top where the circuit breaker exits 4 and an owner question exits 5), `scripts/orchestrator/stop.ps1` (the restart protocol: live lock, exit 3), `docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-orchestrator-loop.md` (§4.9: the landing lane).

## The question

PB3639 made the lane tick for as long as the supervisor lives (one timer, `Step-Lane`, in `Invoke-Unit`, `Wait-Until` and between
units). The lane is a part of the supervisor process, so when the supervisor ends the lane ends. The circuit breaker (exit 4) and
an owner question (exit 5) end it: every branch that becomes WAITING TO LAND or is re-armed afterwards waits for someone to restart
the loop, although landing needs neither a unit nor a budget.

## Options for the owner

1. Leave as is: a trip or a question stops landing until the loop restarts. No code change.
2. A lane-only linger: on exit 4 or 5 the supervisor keeps ticking `land_lane.py` (no units) until STOP or the restart. It changes the
   restart protocol (a live supervisor lock, exit 3, `stop.ps1` would all meet a process that is up but runs no units) and needs the
   owner's call on what a restart does to a lingering lane, and on whether a breaker trip should keep landing at all (the breaker
   exists to stop a run of failures; the lane lands work that is already reviewed and gated).
3. A separate lane process with its own lock, started by the supervisor and stopped only by STOP.

Shares PB3639's root (landed), which fixed the in-process cases.

## Decided (2026-10-24, the owner)

The operator explained the three options (leave it, a lingering supervisor, a separate lane process) and recommended option 3, because a breaker trip stops failing AI units, not the landing of work already reviewed and gated, and because the owner's O11 rule says nothing simple waits. The owner answered at about 23:45 PDT: "Option 3".

**The bar.** `land_lane.py` gains a long-running mode (`land_lane.py run`) holding its OWN lock in the coordination directory (one lane per coordination directory; a second refuses), ticking on the cadence `Step-Lane` uses today. The supervisor starts it if it is not running (`orchestrate.ps1` start, and after `adopt_main` restarts the supervisor) and no longer ticks the lane itself, so there is ONE ticker. Exit 4 and exit 5 leave the lane running; only STOP (`stop.ps1`, the loop's STOP file, the owner's global STOP) and a loop-wide quota stop end it, and `stop.ps1 -Status` reports the lane's state. When the lane's own code changes on main, the supervisor restarts it (the PB2699 rule applied to the lane). Restart rules stay as they are: the supervisor's lock and exit 3 concern the supervisor alone. Self-tests: a breaker trip with a WAITING TO LAND branch still lands it; STOP ends the lane; a second lane refuses; the supervisor never ticks while a lane runs. Update DESIGN-orchestrator-loop section 4.9 and MANDATORY-PRACTICES O11 in the same change.
