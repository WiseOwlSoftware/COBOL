# PB672 — OPEN — two fields of one row disagree, and only one of them is read

Found 2026-09-05 by the `PB323` implementer (report §"New defects" A) while correcting its own row: `GR-14.9.27.4-3`
had `editions` omitting 2023 while its `notes` sentence adjudicated all four editions. The implementer corrected that
one row in its change set (train 10, row 1) and swept the file for the class.

## The shape

`tests/version-matrix/traceability-inventory.json` rows carry two statements of edition reach: the `editions` field,
which the version-matrix machinery and every per-edition burn-down read, and a human-written
`EDITIONS AS ADJUDICATED: …` sentence inside `notes`. Nothing asserts that they agree. The sweep (the repro below)
finds five rows where they do not — every one narrower in the field than in the sentence:

- `GR-14.9.27.4-4` — notes: *"Tables 18 and 20 and the open-mode assignment: 1985 / 2002 / 2014 / 2023"* (PB321's row)
- `GR-14.9.49.4-3` — notes: *"GR3a/GR3b: 1985/2002/2014/2023"*
- `GR-14.9.49.4-6` — notes: *"The rule IN THIS FORM is 2023"* — the field omits the ONLY edition it applies at
- `FMT-14.9.49.2` — notes: *"Formats 1-2: 1985, 2002, 2014, 2023"*
- `GR-14.9.6.4-1` and `GR-14.9.27.4-6` — no `EDITIONS AS ADJUDICATED` sentence at all; a reading is needed before
  either is classed as agreeing or not

`GR-14.9.49.4-6` is the proof that the harm is not cosmetic: a per-edition view of the 2023 compiler does not see a
rule the register adjudicated as 2023-only. This is the same family as [[PB659]] (the MAX/MIN rows' editions column
reaching editions the rule does not) seen from the other side — there the field over-reaches, here it under-reaches —
and the two notes want ONE convention for the column, written down once, and then a guard.

## Repro

```
python - <<'PY'
import json, io
rows = json.load(io.open('tests/version-matrix/traceability-inventory.json', encoding='utf-8'))
for r in rows:
    if r.get('editions') and '2023' not in r['editions']:
        print(r['rule-id'], '|', r['editions'], '|', r.get('state'))
PY
```

## Fix

Not a spec rule — an internal consistency invariant between two fields of the same row. The natural guard is a drift
test beside `SpecTraceabilityInventoryDriftTests` asserting that the edition list parsed out of a row's
`EDITIONS AS ADJUDICATED` sentence is a subset of its `editions` field (the sentence is the adjudicator's statement;
the field is derived from it, never the other way round). Land the guard RED first: fix the five rows through a
`record_verdicts.py` batch, read the two sentence-less rows and give them a sentence, then prove the test fails on a
deliberately narrowed field before committing it green. Settle [[PB659]]'s convention in the same change set so the
guard encodes one rule.
