# PB55 — OPEN (NEW 2026-08-08) — whether a nested numbered item is a catalog ROW depends on how the transcription indented it, not on what the standard says

> ⚙ **flags:** process

> **Found by PB39's segmentation fix, and deliberately NOT ridden in on it** (PB39's contract was
> count-preserving identity repair; this changes WHAT COUNTS, which is a denominator question — the class
> D13 makes an owner decision).
>
> **THE DEFECT.** The standard nests numbered lists inside rules (GR 4 a) 1. 2. 3. …). The transcription
> sometimes indents those items (they are absorbed into the parent rule's catalog text) and sometimes
> flattens them to column 0 (they become their own `L<k>.<n>` catalog rows) — **and the same list can be
> split between the two treatments.** §14.9.13.4 GR4a's six-item list is transcribed items 1–3 indented,
> items 4–6 at column 0: the catalog holds rows `GR-14.9.13.4-L2.4/.5/.6` while items 1–3 are invisible
> inside GR4's text. §12.3.7.4 GR7's list likewise contributes `L3.2–L3.6` with its item 1 absorbed.
> So the denominator counts SOME nested sub-rules as rows and hides others, on no principle at all —
> the deciding input is the transcriber's indentation.
>
> **MEASURED, not inferred:** the PB39-final catalog carries 12 sub-lists that are flattened TAILS
> (first ordinal > 1) — §12.3.7.4 L3 · §13.18.54.4 L2 · §14.9.10.4 L2 · §14.9.13.4 L2 · §14.9.20.4 L2 ·
> §14.9.25.4 L2 · §14.9.33.4 L2 · §14.9.37.4 L2 · §14.9.41.3 L2 · §15.30.3 L2 · §15.31.3 L2 · §15.95.4 L2 —
> each an incomplete row-ification of one nested list. Fully-flattened nested lists (rows for every item,
> e.g. §15.68.3's LOCALE items L2.1–L2.7, adjudicated under PB37 as "the r5b items") and fully-indented ones
> (no rows at all) are both common; no inventory of the fully-indented population exists yet.
>
> **THE QUESTION AN OWNER DECISION MUST SETTLE** (with PB29's batch or after it): are nested numbered items
> inventory rows in their own right, or body text of their parent rule?
> · If ROWS: the transcription's indented lists need harvesting too (extractor change), adding rows and
>   growing the denominator — and ids should say what the standard says ("GR 4 a) 1." is not naturally
>   "L2.4").
> · If TEXT: the flattened lists need re-indenting in the transcription (a transcription repair, verified
>   against the printed page), REMOVING rows — including adjudicated ones (PB37's eight §15.68.3 rows).
> Either answer is defensible; the current mix is not. **The status quo mis-states the denominator in both
> directions at once**: it counts tails without heads and hides whole normative sub-lists (§14.9.13.4 GR4a
> items 1–3 state how EVALUATE pairs selection subjects with objects — semantics PB45 shipped a defect in).
>
> ⚠ Whatever the decision, the count and id churn must be measured first (PB39's text-keyed diff is the
> instrument), and the PB37/PB29 adjudications keyed to affected rows migrate with it.
