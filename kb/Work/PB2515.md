# PB2515 — batched gating (owner trial until Saturday 2026-10-10 10:00 PDT)

## The owner's words (2026-10-07)
16:20 PDT: "I don't think every change, however small, should need to run the entire population. First, a change may
only affect a small part of the population so much of the work may be wasted. That said, it may be difficult to know
what part is affected. However, it seems we could boost thruput by bundling multiple changes, then running the
population test. Optimistically, the one run finds no errors. Worst case, it finds multiple error and we fix them
together rather than one by one."
16:25 PDT, asked "Adopt batched gating as a measured trial …?": **"Yes, trial until Saturday (Recommended)"**.

## The evidence the operator showed (48 implementer verdicts on disk, 2026-10-07)
27 green; 12 test-red found in LEG 2 (the rest of the population), 1 in leg 1; 5 population-not-exact (gate tooling);
3 red with no failing leg recorded. No impact map was recorded on any of them (`map none`), so leg 1 is the change's own
tests plus previous reds. About one change in four breaks something outside its own tests, which the per-change whole
population (PB1708, owner 2026-09-28 "ORDER, DON'T SKIP") was catching. CPU: the machine sits at 100 %; gates are the
work (≈ 6 min quiet, ≈ 15–17 min at cap 3); per 5-change train today ≈ 7–8 whole-population runs, batched ≈ 2–3.

## What the trial changes
1. `build-local.ps1 -Mode implementer` runs leg 1 only — the change's own tests, the previous gate's reds, and the
   cheapest cases the change reaches (whatever the plan already orders first); its verdict line says
   `LEG 1 ONLY (batched-gating trial, PB2515)` so no one mistakes it for a whole-population green. The Linux gate and the
   CI-guard leg stay at the lander.
2. The lander's whole-population leg is unchanged and is now the population check for every cluster in the train. On a
   red, the lander ATTRIBUTES before fixing: re-run only the failing cases on each cluster's branch (a filtered run per
   branch, minutes), then fix a small break in the train (as landers already do) or eject that cluster to a finisher,
   and re-run the whole population. A red no single branch reproduces is an interaction: the lander bisects the train.
3. The trial expires on its own at Sat 2026-10-10 10:00 PDT (the mode reads an expiry, like PB2514's cap), after which
   the implementer gate returns to the whole population unless the owner decides otherwise on the measurement.
4. MANDATORY-PRACTICES I1/I2, the gate skill, the implementer and lander brief templates, `check_practices.py`, plan §9
   and the CI-invariant doc (`kb/Work/PB1957`) say the same thing; nothing else runs "full per change".

## Landed: the trial's mechanism (wave 1033 group V, 2026-10-07, branch `gate-batch`)

- **The switch is a shared setting with an expiry**, beside PB2514's cap in the slot directory's `settings.json`:
  `python scripts/gate_slot.py set-implementer-scope leg1 --until <ISO> [--why TEXT]` (`leg1` is refused without an
  expiry; `whole` is the default). `gate_slot.py status` prints it. After the expiry the implementer gate is the whole
  population again with no one acting.
- **`scripts/run_gate_legs.py`** reads the scope once at the start of an implementer gate. Under `leg1`, when the plan
  has a leg 2, it runs leg 1 only, the population check names every leg-2 case NOT RUN (`not-run-*.txt`), and the
  verdict is `=== BUILD-LOCAL GATE: LEG 1 ONLY (batched-gating trial, PB2515): GREEN|RED — …; leg 2 left to the
  lander's whole-population train gate: … ===` — never `GREEN`, so `GATE: GREEN` never matches it; `verdict.json`
  records `scope`, `scope_until`, `leg1_only`. With no plan the one leg IS the whole population and the verdict is a
  plain GREEN. The lander ignores the scope. Four new self-test arms (seen RED with the scope ignored), asserted by
  `GateLegDriftTests`.
- **The lander attributes before it fixes** (`lander-train-brief.md` step 3, MANDATORY-PRACTICES L12): ONLY the
  failing cases re-run on each cluster alone (base + that cluster's commit, or its implementer branch head when it does
  not cherry-pick alone; one build and one filtered run through `filter_population.py` each); the owner of a red is
  fixed in the train (L9's bar) or EJECTED to a finisher with its failing case names; a red no single cluster
  reproduces is an interaction, bisected over the train's prefixes; then the whole population again. The Linux gate
  (with CI's `guard` leg) stays at the lander; implementers skip it while their verdict says `LEG 1 ONLY`.
- **Every train is measured**: `python scripts/orchestrator/train_measure.py record --train … --gating batched|whole
  …` (lander-train-brief step 9; lander-brief step 3) appends one validated record to
  `<coord>\train-measurements.jsonl`; `train_measure.py summary` prints, per gating mode, changes landed per hour,
  whole-population runs per train, red first runs with the mean attribution time, interaction reds, finishers created
  by ejection, reds fixed in the train, and CI reds. Self-test (4 arms, seen RED on planted defects) asserted by
  `TrainMeasureDriftTests`.
- **Said the same way everywhere**: MANDATORY-PRACTICES I1, I2, I8, L2, L10, L12; the gate skill; the fix-lane and
  implementer briefs and the dispatch-spec template; both lander briefs; `check_practices.py` (requires the
  `LEG 1 ONLY` done-state in the implementer briefs and spec, the attribution step and `train_measure.py record` in
  the lander briefs, and `set-cap`/`set-implementer-scope`/L12 in MANDATORY-PRACTICES); plan §9; DESIGN-test-build-ci
  sections 3.14.1 and 3.14.6; DOC_INDEX; PB1708 (amended, dated) and PB1957 (the CI invariant still holds).
