# PB258

**Open row:** `SR-14.9.4.3-5`.
**Closed on spec-derived goldens (2026-09-09):** `GR-14.9.4.4-1` · `SR-14.9.4.3-9` · `SR-14.9.11.3-1` ·
`FMT-14.9.11.2` · `GR-14.9.12.4-1` · `GR-14.9.12.4-7`.

Seven inventory rows whose stated residue named a fix that had since LANDED, and whose verdict was never
re-derived. The registration wave's grouping fleet proposed flipping all seven — six to CONFORMS, one
(`SR-14.9.11.3-1`) from DIVERGES to CONFORMS — and **every one of those proposals was REFUSED**, on the ground
the contract itself names: a reconciliation must rest on a recorded owner decision or a `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` §4
licence, and "the fix landed, I read the code and it looks right" is neither.

The golden round then paid the debt properly. Every rule was re-quoted from `specs/ISO_COBOL.md` and validated
with `cite.py --check` **before** the expected value was derived; every expected value was written into a
golden's header before the golden was run; every golden was run on a compiler built in the round's own worktree.

## Why refusing was the correct call, and not merely the cautious one

The asymmetry decides it. Recording CONFORMS on an unverified read **over-claims conformance** — the single
failure mode the whole traceability inventory exists to make expensive, and the one CLAUDE.md rule 1 spends its
length on (the legacy oracle, the NIST goldens and the GnuCOBOL differential are regression NETS, never
authority; a verdict is earned against the spec TEXT). Recording the row instead **under-claims**, and costs one
note. The two errors are not the same size.

There was also a mechanical reason the flip could not have landed as proposed even if the reads were right: a
resolving verdict closes a row only with a SPEC-DERIVED covering test, and six of the seven proposals named no
`test-ref` at all. Recording CONFORMS on them would have produced six CONFORMS-but-untested rows — the GAP count
unchanged, the defect count six lower, and nothing on disk any better. That is a burn-down moving on paperwork.

**And the refusal earned its keep on the one row where a read would have been WRONG.** `SR-14.9.4.3-5`'s two
stated residues had indeed landed — and the row is still PARTIAL, because the rule's condition carries an
exception (*"and identifier-2 is not an address-identifier"*) whose arm cannot be written at all: measured on the
built compiler, `CALL "S" USING BY REFERENCE ADDRESS OF W-X` and the `BY CONTENT` spelling are both COBOL0001
parse errors, while §14.9.4.3 SR3 explicitly admits an address-identifier as identifier-2. A read of the two
named residues would have closed the row over a defect nobody had looked for.

## Why this is one note and not seven

One mechanism: **there is no back-link from a landed fix to the inventory rows whose verdict it invalidates.**
A note lands, its rows keep the verdict they had, and nothing anywhere goes red. `kb/Work PB245` records the
same mechanism over fourteen §15 rows, found independently by a different family of the same fleet — which is
the corroboration that this is a structural gap and not seven separate oversights. The golden round measured its
size on this note alone: **six of seven rows were stale, and one of the six had been stale in the direction that
publishes a DIVERGES against conforming code.**

**Half the structural remedy exists**: a note's `inventory_rows` frontmatter is the machine-readable back-link,
and `DefectiveRowCoverageDriftTests` fails the battery when a defective row has no live note claiming it. What
that gate still does NOT do is fire when a note LANDS while its rows stay defective — the exact shape that
produced all seven of these. Whether it should is the open design question this note carries, and it is a change
to the GATE, not a new list.
