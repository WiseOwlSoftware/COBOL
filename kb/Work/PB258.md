# PB258

**Rows:** `GR-14.9.4.4-1` · `SR-14.9.4.3-5` · `SR-14.9.4.3-9` · `SR-14.9.11.3-1` · `FMT-14.9.11.2` ·
`GR-14.9.12.4-1` · `GR-14.9.12.4-7`

Seven inventory rows whose stated residue names a fix that has since LANDED, and whose verdict was never
re-derived. The registration wave's grouping fleet proposed flipping all seven — six to CONFORMS, one
(`SR-14.9.11.3-1`) from DIVERGES to CONFORMS — and **every one of those proposals was REFUSED here**, on the
ground the contract itself names: a reconciliation must rest on a recorded owner decision or a
`docs/CONFORMANCE.md` §4 licence, and "the fix landed, I read the code and it looks right" is neither.

## Why refusing was the correct call, and not merely the cautious one

The asymmetry decides it. Recording CONFORMS on an unverified read **over-claims conformance** — the single
failure mode the whole traceability inventory exists to make expensive, and the one CLAUDE.md rule 1 spends its
length on (the legacy oracle, the NIST goldens and the GnuCOBOL differential are regression NETS, never
authority; a verdict is earned against the spec TEXT). Recording the row here instead **under-claims**, and costs
one note. The two errors are not the same size.

There is also a mechanical reason the flip could not have landed as proposed even if the reads were right: a
resolving verdict closes a row only with a SPEC-DERIVED covering test, and six of the seven proposals named no
`test-ref` at all. Recording CONFORMS on them would have produced six CONFORMS-but-untested rows — the GAP count
unchanged, the defect count six lower, and nothing on disk any better. That is a burn-down moving on paperwork.

## What each row is owed

Per row: re-read the implementation against the spec text (not against the landed note's summary of it), derive
the expected behaviour from the clause, and record the verdict WITH the spec-derived witness that pins it. The
fleet's reads are a starting point and are reproduced below as HYPOTHESES, not findings.

1. **`GR-14.9.4.4-1`** (PARTIAL) — the row's two refuted findings were `ProgramTable.CallProgram`'s `finally`
   restoring `n.Active` but not `n.Instance`, and `BinderDriver`'s containee identification. `kb/Work PB133`
   (landed, DEVLOG 1370) claims both as its wave-A mechanisms (4) and (5), with goldens
   `pb133_recursive_containee` / `pb133_instance_restore`. **Owed:** confirm those goldens cover THIS rule's
   obligation, or write the one that does.
2. **`SR-14.9.4.3-5`** (PARTIAL) — the row's own text says what is still open: "the object-data ban
   (`OoIsObjectData` unreached from CALL) and the once-only-evaluation half". `PB132` built the
   `ScreenCallOperand` chokepoint and `PB133` wave B landed GR3a's once-only identification. **Owed:** a
   negative fixture for the object-data ban reached FROM a CALL, and a positive one distinguishing once-only
   from per-argument evaluation (a subscripted operand whose subscript the statement changes — the same shape
   `GR-14.9.11.4-14` records as the only test that can tell them apart).
3. **`SR-14.9.4.3-9`** (PARTIAL) — the classification half (`CALL … RETURNING` over an item of a CONSTANT
   RECORD binding through `ctx.Refs.Resolve` rather than `ResolveReceiving`). `PB128` landed the
   `ResolveReceiving` routing with `conformance:negative/pb128-call-constant-by-reference`. **Owed:** verify
   the RETURNING position specifically — the negative fixture's name says BY REFERENCE, and a two-arm dispatch
   with only one arm fixed is this repo's most reproducible defect shape (`feedback_two_arm_dispatch`).
4. **`SR-14.9.11.3-1`** (DIVERGES) — "identifier-1 shall not reference a data item of class message-tag,
   object, or pointer", measured as unenforced (`DISPLAY WS-PTR` printing `ManagedPointer.ToString()`).
   `kb/Work PB148` (landed 2026-08-29) claims `ExpressionBinder.ScreenOperandClass` over the ONE classifier,
   called for every DISPLAY operand, with `COBOLNET1694`; `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` §7 Annex A.1 item 56 publishes
   the determination. ⛔ **This is the one row where the STALE verdict is the harmful direction**: if PB148 did
   land it, the inventory is publishing a DIVERGES against a compiler that conforms. Re-measure it FIRST, and
   with a run, not a read.
5. **`FMT-14.9.11.2`** (PARTIAL) — two halves. The END-DISPLAY edition asymmetry is claimed landed by
   `PB134` (an ACTIVE `end-display-2002` row in `constructs.json`, `VersionConformancePass` calling
   `Constructs.EndDisplay2002`); the Format-2 half is the unclaimed A.4.2 screen module, which is the
   `PB259` shape. **Owed:** re-derive the format row against the printed diagram with the A.4.2 posture stated,
   and record the verdict the two halves together earn.
6. **`GR-14.9.12.4-1`** and 7. **`GR-14.9.12.4-7`** (PARTIAL) — DIVIDE's quotient and REMAINDER rules. Each
   row's residue list is partly landed (`PB129`'s per-receiver `GuardedStore` and the checked back-multiply)
   and partly the STANDARD-BINARY clause, which `kb/Work PB198` (landed owner decision, 2026-08-31) declines on
   §4.2.6 processor-dependence with `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` §2 row 2 as the documentation. ⚠ **The declined-mode
   half is NOT a licence to widen the derived-verdict selector.** `tests/version-matrix/inventory-schema.json`
   `derived-verdicts.standard-binary-only` selects rules SOLELY conditioned on the mode; these two have
   reachable content under a mode we DO support and are excluded BY DESIGN. Any disposition here is a per-row
   adjudication, and `DerivedVerdictDriftTests` is what stops it becoming a predicate change.

## Why this is one note and not seven

One mechanism: **there is no back-link from a landed fix to the inventory rows whose verdict it invalidates.**
A note lands, its rows keep the verdict they had, and nothing anywhere goes red. `kb/Work PB245` records the
same mechanism over fourteen §15 rows, found independently by a different family of the same fleet — which is
the corroboration that this is a structural gap and not seven separate oversights.

**The structural remedy now exists and is half of this wave**: a note's `inventory_rows` frontmatter is the
machine-readable back-link, and `DefectiveRowCoverageDriftTests` fails the battery when a defective row has no
live note claiming it. What that gate does NOT yet do is fire when a note LANDS while its rows stay defective.
Closing these seven rows is the occasion to decide whether it should — and if it should, that is a change to the
gate, not a new list.
