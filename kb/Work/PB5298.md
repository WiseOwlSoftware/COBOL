# PB5298 - at --std 85 SEGMENT-LIMIT after PROGRAM COLLATING SEQUENCE IS alphabet-name is refused: the 2002 optional alphabet-name-2 slot of programCollatingSequenceClause takes the word SEGMENT-LIMIT, which the lexer does not know as a reserved word

**Sites:**

- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar/CobolParserCore.g4` (`programCollatingSequenceClause`, `segmentLimitClause`)
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolParserCoreBase.cs` (`segmentLimitAhead`)

**Mechanism.** The 1985 OBJECT-COMPUTER paragraph prints `[PROGRAM COLLATING SEQUENCE IS alphabet-name-1] [SEGMENT-LIMIT IS segment-number]` in that order (§1985:XVI.2.2.2), the SEGMENT-LIMIT clause is `SEGMENT-LIMIT IS segment-number` with IS optional (§1985:XVI.2.3.2), and §1985:XVI.2.2.4 GR3 points to it. `programCollatingSequenceClause` is `PROGRAM? COLLATING? SEQUENCE (collatingForPhrase+ | IS? cobolWord cobolWord?)`, and SEGMENT-LIMIT is recognized by text (`segmentLimitAhead`), not as a token, so the optional second cobolWord (2002 alphabet-name-2) consumes it and the integer is left as an unknown clause. With IS written after SEGMENT-LIMIT some layouts parse, so acceptance depends on prediction.
