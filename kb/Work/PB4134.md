# PB4134 — the recovery's tail discard resynchronizes ONLY at the separator period (SentenceEnd), never at a token the en

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs:160` (`CobolErrorStrategy.BeginUnitTailDiscard`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs:266` (`CobolErrorStrategy.UnitTailDiscard`, `UnitTailDiscard.Resumes`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs:1037` (`CobolErrorStrategy.ResimulationWalk.After`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs:230` (`CobolErrorStrategy.TokensWithin`), `tests/WiseOwl.Cobol.Tests.Unit/ParseRecoveryUnitTailTests.cs`.

Review lead R1-1 (low, round 1) of PB3843 (CobolErrorStrategy.cs:151 on the reviewed branch). The tail discard of a faulted sentence
after a closed construct stops only at the separator period; it never stops at a token the enclosing program can resume with. When the
faulted sentence also lacks its period, the discard runs through `END PROGRAM <name>.` and a true second diagnostic that main reported
(before PB3843) is lost.

**Fix:** include the enclosing program's resumption tokens (END PROGRAM, the next division/paragraph header: `ResynchronizationSet` knows
the follow sets) in the discard's stop set. Pin with a golden: a faulted sentence without its period, followed by a second fault after
`END PROGRAM`.

Source: report `w20261764c-PB3843-report.md`, review lead R1-1; PB3843 (landed).

## Landing (wave 20261780, group F, 2026-10-30)

Re-probe: a faulted sentence without its period followed by `END PROGRAM 123.` drew the true COBOL0306 and lost the second fault
(the malformed end marker), reproduced by switching the new stop off. The discard (`ConsumeUntil` through `BeginUnitTailDiscard`,
the successor of `ResynchronizationSet`) now stops, without consuming, where some rule AROUND the unit, completed, takes the next
two tokens (`UnitTailDiscard.Resumes`, a `ResimulationWalk` positioned at the end of each enclosing rule in its real context): the
ATN's follow, not a list of words, so `END PROGRAM`, the next program's header, `END DECLARATIVES`, the next `name SECTION` and the
`PROCEDURE DIVISION` after a data entry are all resumption points. Two tokens because `END` is also the AT END phrase. A word
before a period the unit itself may hold (`TokensWithin`) is never one: the unit's last operand and its period, or a paragraph
header, cannot be told apart (no Area A in the parser), so a missing period before a paragraph header still costs the header
(pinned by `AMissingPeriodBeforeAParagraphHeader_CostsTheHeaderNotTheNextFault`). The note's "next paragraph header" is therefore
not a stop; the reason is in `DESIGN-frontend-grammar.md` section 3.11.

- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs#UnitTailDiscard` - `Resumes` asks a `ResimulationWalk.After(container)` (a walk positioned at the end of an enclosing rule in its real context) whether the next TWO tokens continue it; REUSE `ResimulationWalk.After` for any 'what may follow this rule here' question. Trap: a paragraph header (`name .`) and a sentence's last operand and period are the same two tokens (no Area A in the parser), so `Resumes` refuses any `word .` the unit can hold (`TokensWithin`); and any parse error inside a NESTED program draws a false COBOL0001 at the host's END PROGRAM (adaptive prediction at `nestedProgram*`, not recovery).
