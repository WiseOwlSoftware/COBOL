# PB5168 - at --std 85 a fixed-form comment-entry paragraph header with no period compiles (AUTHOR, DATE-COMPILED and the other comment-entry paragraphs): CommentEntryHeader writes the header and two periods whatever follows the word

**Sites:**

- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Preprocessor/ReferenceFormatProcessor.LogicalConversion.cs` (`CommentEntryHeader`, `CommentEntryOf`)

**Mechanism.** A 1985 Identification Division paragraph is a paragraph header (a reserved word followed by the separator period) and its comment-entries (§1985:VI.3.2.1.1, §1985:VI.3.4.2). The fixed-form logical conversion `CommentEntryOf` takes an Area-A AUTHOR, INSTALLATION, DATE-WRITTEN, DATE-COMPILED or SECURITY word as the header, and `CommentEntryHeader` writes the word plus two periods whatever followed it, so the header's own period is never asked for: a line holding the bare word AUTHOR or DATE-COMPILED, with no period at all, compiles at --std 85.

**Expected (from the 1985 text, the record's).** a diagnostic at line 3: §1985:VI.3.2.1.1 prints the paragraph as the word AUTHOR and a period, then "[ comment-entry ] …"; §1985:III: "Paragraph Header. A reserved word, followed by the separator period, that indicates the beginning of a paragraph in the Identification and Environment Divisions." AUTHOR with no period is not a paragraph header, and the Identification Division's format admits nothing else there.

**Observed (the record's).** exit 0, no diagnostic (none under --flag-extensions either); the program runs and displays D. The same for a line holding the bare word AUTHOR with no period at all.

**Fix direction (root cause, CLAUDE.md rule 4).** Have CommentEntryHeader keep the source's period, or report its absence, so the parser sees the header as written; the conversion is the one place every comment-entry paragraph passes, so one change covers all five. A missing period is then the parser's COBOL3408 like any other paragraph header.
