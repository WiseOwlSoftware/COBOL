Filed 2026-09-22 by the ORCHESTRATOR on the owner's decision ("do #5 while we wait on scheduled work, as we recoup the
effort quickly").

## Why

The lander's whole-Conformance leg is the campaign's serial bottleneck: ~7,900 cases, measured **9.6 min quiet,
18.5 min at battery #84, 30.6 min in train 48** (DEVLOG, lander-throughput entry). Trains 46, 47 and 48 each hit 1–3
reds on their first gate, and the fixes were overwhelmingly TEST-SIDE (a probe template, a stale test-ref, a pinned
expectation) — yet the re-gate recompiled every program from scratch. Implementers re-gate the same way.

## What to build

A content-addressed cache of each case's COMPILED program, keyed by everything that determines the compiler's
output: the bits of the compiler and runtime assemblies under test (their hashes, not their versions), the case's
source (and every copybook it reads), the edition and every compiler option. A hit reuses the compiled artefact and
still RUNS it (runtime behaviour is what the case checks); a miss compiles and stores. Any compiler/runtime change
therefore misses everywhere — correctness never depends on the cache — while a re-gate after a test-only fix hits on
every case.

## Obligations

1. **Measure first**: where does a case's time actually go (COBOL front end, Roslyn emit, load, run)? If Roslyn
   emit is not the dominant cost, say so and size the cache to what is.
2. The key must be provably complete — a drift test that fails if a compiler input is added that the key omits
   (flip one input, prove a miss).
3. Safe under PARALLEL test hosts and PARALLEL worktrees (atomic writes; per-worktree or content-addressed shared
   dir; a size cap / eviction).
4. Opt-out switch, and the battery / CI run COLD by default unless the owner decides otherwise.
5. Report the measured before/after for a whole-assembly re-gate with no product change.
