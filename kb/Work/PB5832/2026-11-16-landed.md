**Landed (2026-11-16, wave 20262089 group A).** Re-probed on this branch's base at `--std 85` with the note's `intarg.cob`:
`FACT(WN*0.5) 00000001`, `MOD(A/B,3) 00000000`, `RANDOM(WN*0.5) = RANDOM(1)`, `CHAR(SQRT(N)+64) [@]`, `DOI(SQRT(N)+1) 16010102`,
`DAYOI(SQRT(N)+1) 01601002`, exit 0 and no diagnostic; `intctl.cob` (the root quotient) refused with COBOL1627. After the fix all six
statements of `intarg.cob` are refused with COBOL1627 (exit 65), at every edition.

**Mechanism.** The three shapes were three missing arms of one walk, and the root arm that stood for the quotient was a special case of
what the walk should always have been. `CollectAdditive` now carries a RATIONAL factor instead of a sign: `E * c`, `c * E` and `E / c`
(c an exact literal fold, nonzero) scale the spine, so an item's net coefficient is a rational and `ItemWitness` asks the one
granularity question, whether `coefficient x 10^(-scale)` is a whole number. That replaced the root `item / literal` arm of
`NotAlwaysIntegral` (a sibling sweep: `WN / 2 + 1`, `WN / 2 * 3`, `W-S * 2 + 65.5` and `W-I / 2 + 0.5` had failed open the same way, and
`FUNCTION MAX (X, 0) / 2` is the shape that still does). A non-provably-integral term under a non-integral factor clears `RestIntegral`
(half of an integer product is no integer). The witness terms of PB5345 became one mechanism, `WitnessOf`: the power at a unit
coefficient, a quotient of two numeric items that are not one item and cannot share storage (`StorageOverlap`; A at one least value and
B at 3 or 7 times its own, so `MOD (A / B, 3)` is 1/3), and SQRT, LOG, LOG10, EXP, SIN, COS, TAN or ATAN of a bare item of scale >= 0 (the
item at two least values; the proof is on `NonIntegerAtTwoGranules`). The Net now records every numeric item beneath an unreadable term
as opaque (`MarkOpaque`, `NumericItemsBeneath`), because an integer item reached by a fractional factor is a Net leaf.

**Goldens.** Refusals `pb5832-product-by-literal`, `-quotient-in-sum`, `-item-quotient`, `-numeric-function-term`,
`-date-of-integer-function-term`, `-day-of-integer-function-term` (85 2002 2014 2023); the accepted complement
`pb5832_scaled_integral_shapes` (WN * 0.5 * 2, WN / 2 * 2, WN * 0.5 + WN * 0.5, S / 0.1, U * 4 / 2, SQRT (N) - SQRT (N) + 5, A / A, A / AR
over a REDEFINES). Each refusal compiled clean with the fix reverted and was refused with it restored. Two older goldens changed:
`5.5-Integer-operands/pb1526_integer_argument_has_integral_value` and `..._integrality_standard_decimal` wrote `X / 2 + 1`, `X * 0.5`
and `F * 1` as their fail-open argument at 2023; those are provably not always integral and are refused now, so they write
`FUNCTION MAX (X, 0) / 2 + 1` and the like, which the screen cannot prove (same values, same output). In `IntegerArgumentConstantWitnessTests`
the two rows recorded as fail-open (`W-S * 2 + 65.5`, `W-I / 2 + 0.5`) are refused rows now.

**Decision recorded here: the arms are edition-free** (as PB5208's power arm is). PB1526's fail-open rested on the run-time
EC-ARGUMENT-FUNCTION net, which 1985 lacks; §2023:15.3 AR6 "shall be specified" and §1989:A.2.2 AR4 "must be specified" both state the
requirement on the program, so refusing a provably not-always-integral argument is conforming at every edition. The compile-time screen
still refuses only what has a witness valuation; every other shape fails open and the value is tested at run time at 2002 and later.
