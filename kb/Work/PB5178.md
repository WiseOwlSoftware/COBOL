# PB5178 - at --std 85 the ENTER statement's routine-name is a bare token: it needs no separator period, a later-edition verb word in the slot is a syntax error, and the word is never entered in the user-defined word census

**Sites:**

- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar/CobolParserCore.g4` (`enterStatement`, `enterOperand`)
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Bound/StatementBinder.cs` (`BindStatementCore`)
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/UserWordKind.cs` (`UserWordKind`)

**Mechanism.** §1985:VI.6.12.2 prints ENTER language-name-1 [routine-name-1] followed by a separator period, which has the status of a required word (§1985:IV.2.1.6), and §1985:VI.6.12.3 SR2 makes routine-name-1 a COBOL word that may be referred to only in an ENTER sentence. `enterStatement` is an ordinary statement with no period of its own and `enterOperand` one IDENTIFIER token that never reaches the cobolWord rule. So at --std 85 (1) ENTER COBOL followed by another statement in the same sentence compiles; (2) a routine-name spelled as a word a later edition makes a verb (FREE, UNLOCK, VALIDATE, GOBACK) is read as the next statement, a legal program refused (COBOL0001); (3) `UserWordKind` has no routine-name member, so a word used as routine-name-1 and as a paragraph-name or data-name is not refused as two of the disjoint sets of user-defined words.

**Found by.** The 1985 code read (2026-11-10; its records are `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/coderead-85/for-register.json`): each rule below was read against the code sites it names and probed at `--std 85`, and found non-conforming. Verdict, harm, evidence, repro and observed text are the records'; the rows' verdicts are recorded on another branch.

- `1985-FMT-VI.6.12.2` - `§1985:VI.6.12.2` (record verdict: PARTIAL, harm: rejects-legal-source). Checked: §1985:VI.6.12.2: "language-name-1 [ routine-name-1 ] .". Code sites read: src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar/CobolParserCore.g4#enterStatement; src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar/CobolParserCore.g4#enterOperand; src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Bound/StatementBinder.cs#BindStatementCore; src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Validation/VersionConformancePass.cs#VisitEnterStatement; src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolParserCoreBase.cs#NoteGatedOffender. Code read: No kb/Work note owns the fix. §1985:VI.6.12.2 prints ENTER, language-name-1, an optional routine-name-1 and a separator period ("language-name-1 [ routine-name-1 ] ."), and §1985:IV.2.1.6 makes that period part of the format: "The separator period, when used in the formats, has the status of a required word." Implemented at 85: enterStatement is `ENTER enterOperand enterOperand?` with enterOperand one word token (IDENTIFIER, or LINKAGE), StatementBinder.BindStatementCore binds it as a no-op, and VersionConformancePass.VisitEnterStatement is the edition arm, refusing the statement from COBOL-2002 (COBOL0902), so 1985 alone accepts it. Probed at --std 85: ENTER COBOL., ENTER FORTRAN SUB1., ENTER LINKAGE. and lower case compile and run; ENTER with no operand, with a literal and with three words is refused. Not implemented, two arms. (1) The period: enterStatement is an ordinary statement alternative with no period of its own, so ENTER COBOL followed by another statement in the same sentence compiles. (2) Routine-name-1 as a COBOL word: the slot takes only an IDENTIFIER token and does not offer the gated-word reading the definition slots have, so a routine-name spelled as a word a later edition makes a verb (FREE, UNLOCK, RESUME, ALLOCATE, VALIDATE, RAISE, INVOKE, GOBACK; none is a 1985 reserved word, §1985:VI.6.12.3 SR2 "Routine-name-1 is a COBOL word") is read as the next statement and the sentence is refused at its period; the same word as language-name-1 is accepted, because there the error token is the word itself and NoteGatedOffender frees it. Arm (2) follows from arm (1): with the period required after the operands, the word could not begin another statement. Both arms stand while ENTER with another language-name is accepted at 85; the owner question recorded on 1985-SR-VI.6.12.3-1 may remove that acceptance. The statement is inert, so neither arm changes what a program computes. The witness is ENTER COBOL. between two DISPLAY sentences.

Code read: No kb/Work note owns the fix. §1985:VI.6.12.3 SR2: "Routine-name-1 is a COBOL word and it may be referred to only in an ENTER sentence." Implemented: enterOperand takes one word token for routine-name-1, so a literal or an integer is a syntax error, and because BindStatementCore binds ENTER as BoundNop and declares nothing, a word that is only a routine-name resolves nowhere else: GO TO, PERFORM, MOVE, CALL and DISPLAY of it are refused with COBOL1639 (the witness is the GO TO). Not implemented, both because routine-name-1 is a bare token that never reaches the cobolWord rule or the user-defined word census: (1) routine-names are one of the disjoint sets of user-defined words (§1985:IV.4.2.2.1.1: "All user-defined words, except segment-numbers and level-numbers, can belong to one and only one of these disjoint sets"), but UserWordKind has no routine-name member, so a word written as routine-name-1 and also declared as a paragraph-name or a data-name is referred to outside the ENTER sentence with no diagnostic, although the same check refuses a data-name that is also a paragraph-name (COBOL2692); (2) "is a COBOL word": VisitCobolWord asks the 30-character limit of §1985:IV.4.2.2.1 (COBOL1567) and the reserved-word screen only of cobolWord, so a routine-name of 31 or 38 characters and the reserved words LINKAGE and COBOL are accepted as routine-name-1 at 85 (so is a 31-character language-name-1). The verdict stands while ENTER is accepted at 85; the owner question on 1985-SR-VI.6.12.3-1 may remove the statement.
