**Rule it breaks:** §15.44.4 r1 (INTEGER is the greatest integer <= the argument), §15.49.4 / §15.42.4 (INTEGER-PART and FRACTION-PART are defined through it), §15.4.1 r1 (the value equals its EAE). CobolDec.ToUnscaled's own contract (CobolDec.cs:751-759) reserves the low-order-digit keep for a final transfer to a store, and ToUnscaledIntermediate exists for value-semantics consumers (kb/Work PB69).

**Scenario:** Under ARITHMETIC IS STANDARD-DECIMAL, IntrinsicRenderer routes FUNCTION INTEGER(10 ** 40) to CobolIntrinsics.FloorDec. FloorDec calls a.ToUnscaled(0, Truncation). The digit count plus the shift is 41 > 38, so the unchecked arm keeps sig % 10^0 = 0 and the function returns 0, where §15.44.4 requires 10^40, which the SDIDI holds exactly (Sig 1, Exp 40). INTEGER(-10 ** 40) returns -1: the truncated 0 compares unequal to the negative argument and is decremented. FRACTION-PART(10 ** 40) returns 10^40 where the EAE gives 0. A 34-digit value of order 10^40 keeps only its low 31 significand digits. MOD/REM on non-integer or past-Int128 SDIDI operands go through FloorDec / TruncDec of the quotient, so a quotient of 10^38 or more gives a wrong remainder. Nothing is raised, and the value flows on into comparisons and expressions.

**Measured how:** R2 batch finding `runtime-values-1--code--f1#3` (pair runtime-values-1--code), read in the pinned tree at 8be23006851a; skeptics rule upheld, scenario upheld, site upheld.

**Spec:** §15.44.4 "The returned value is the greatest integer less than or equal to the value of argument-1." (`cite.py --check` passed at collection); §15.42.4 "(argument-1 - FUNCTION INTEGER-PART (argument-1))" (`cite.py --check` passed at collection)

**Target:** Floor and truncate ON the decimal: an operand with Exp >= 0 is already an integer and is returned unchanged; otherwise drop the -Exp fraction digits of the significand (DivRemPow10) with the sign-aware floor adjustment. No Int128 landing at all, so no magnitude limit applies, and MOD/REM's fallback inherits it. Pin INTEGER, INTEGER-PART and FRACTION-PART of +/-10 ** 40 under STANDARD-DECIMAL with a golden.

**Design reference:** PB2365.

## Landed (wave 1042 group E, 2026-10-08)

Re-probed on a fresh build before the change: under `ARITHMETIC IS STANDARD-DECIMAL`, `INTEGER(10 ** 40) / 10 ** 38`
answered 0 (100 is owed) and `FRACTION-PART(10 ** 40) / 10 ** 38` answered 100 (0 is owed). Fixed at the root: the one
integer-part rule is now `CobolDec.TruncateToInteger` / `CobolDec.FloorToInteger` (an SDIDI with a non-negative exponent
is already an integer and comes back unchanged; any other value drops its `-Exp` fraction digits through the existing
`DivRemPow10`, with the sign-aware floor adjustment), and `FloorDec`, `TruncDec`, so `FractionPartDec`, `ModDec` and
`RemDec`, read it. The SECOND COPY of the rule, `CtNumeric.IntegerPart` (the compile-time ISO 7.3.6.3 GR3 truncation, which
had the right shape), is deleted and its two callers read the same method. Cites checked with `cite.py --check`: 15.44.4
r1, 15.49.4 r1 (SIGN(x) * INTEGER(ABS(x))), 15.42.4 r1, 7.3.6.3 r3. Tests: golden `conformance:2014/pb2630_integer_family_beyond_int128`
(INTEGER / INTEGER-PART / FRACTION-PART of +/-10 ** 40, a 34-digit significand at 10 ** 6, MOD and REM of a quotient past the
carrier, and the fraction controls); `unit:CobolDecIntegerPartTests` (BigInteger-derived floor and truncation over 21 shapes).
Sibling sweep: every `ToUnscaled(` / `FloorDec` / `TruncDec` caller in `src/` was read; the only other value-semantics landing
of an integer part was `CtNumeric.IntegerPart`. MOD(10 ** 40, 3) is 1000000 by the equivalent arithmetic expression (the
34-digit quotient 3.33...E+39 times 3 is 9.99...E+39), pinned in the golden.

## Landing

- `src/Cobol.Net.Runtime/Values/Numeric/CobolDec.cs#TruncateToInteger` / `#FloorToInteger` own the SDIDI integer part; REUSE them (never `ToUnscaled(0, ...)`, which keeps only low-order digits past 10^38). `CobolIntrinsics.Dec.cs#FloorDec` / `#TruncDec` are one-line readers of them. The compile-time copy `CtNumeric.IntegerPart` is deleted.
- Trap: MOD(10 ** 40, 3) is 1000000 by the 34-digit equivalent arithmetic expression, not 1 - an exact-remainder expectation is the wrong oracle.
