# PB5858 - at --std 85 UNSTRING with no POINTER phrase from a zero-size sender runs ON OVERFLOW

**Ruling: UPHELD (a), harm wrong_answer.** The row `1985-GR-VI.6.27.4-17` (a re-read after the fix of kb/Work PB5816) does not close on its inherited verdict. At `--std 85` an UNSTRING with NO POINTER phrase from a zero-size sender runs ON OVERFLOW, where the 1985 text gives NOT ON OVERFLOW. The fix of kb/Work PB5816 carried the ruling made for WITH POINTER to the form with no POINTER, and leg C of its witness expects the wrong answer. The checker's confidence: high. Cause (a): wrong at 85 only, right at 2023 (its zero-length guard). Severity MAJOR: a wrong-answer defect is at least MAJOR.

**The wrong witness, as the checker names it.** `conformance-suite/cases/VI.6.27@1985-UNSTRING-STATEMENT/pb5816_unstring_zero_length_sender_overflow.cob` and its `.out`, leg C: it expects `C OVERFLOW` on "the implicit pointer 1". Legs A and B are right.

**The earlier ruling for WITH POINTER stands.** The refuter is right, and the earlier ruling (batch 6, mechanism 5) stands: that ruling, the note kb/Work PB5816 and its repro are about WITH POINTER 1 only (a value in identifier-7 of 1 is greater than size 0). The fix extended it, with no ruling, to the form with no POINTER (`long ptr = 1` feeds the same initiation test).

**The checker's evidence (rulings.json, copied).** GR17a's condition is "the value in the data item referenced by identifier-7"; with no POINTER phrase there is no identifier-7, and GR13a says only that examination begins "with the leftmost character position"; 1985 states no implicit pointer value (GR15 speaks only of "an UNSTRING statement with a POINTER phrase"). GR17b needs unexamined characters, which a zero-size sender lacks. Neither GR17 condition holds, so GR20 sends control to NOT ON OVERFLOW. Folio check: VI-136 to VI-139 carry no RECONSTRUCTED marker (all 8 markers of the 1985 transcription are in Section XIII).

**Mechanism 3: what the fixer must also rule (an open question of this note).** The checker left one point at medium confidence: whether, at 1985, an UNSTRING with no POINTER from a zero-size sender also space-fills the first receiving item and adds 1 to the TALLYING item (`§1985:VI.6.27.4 GR13` and its item g). Today the compiler leaves both unchanged. By GR13 (the transfer at initiation) and 13g ("repeated", so the first pass is unconditional) the first receiver may take the zero characters examined by MOVE (space fill) and TALLYING count it (GR7), so `WX=[QQQ] T=00` may be wrong too. The fix decides it from the text and its witness says which.
