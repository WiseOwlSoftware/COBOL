# PB5855 - `scripts/semgrep/verify.py` fails locally on "unreadable files" whose number varies by run

**Sites:**
- `scripts/semgrep/verify.py#unreadable_files` (the files semgrep's grammar cannot read at all, from a scan with a throwaway AST pattern)
- `scripts/semgrep/verify.py#main` (prints `FAIL: N unreadable file(s) unnamed, M stale exception(s).`)

Source: the report of wave 20262062a (kb/Work PB5822), `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/reports/w20262062a-PB5822-report.md`,
its Gate section and its last Follow-up; observed by that implementer on Monday 2026-11-16 between 00:20 and 01:55
PST in the worktree of branch `worktree-agent-a77965f7fc492c8b0`. Nothing here was re-measured by the session that
files it.

kind: defect. process_only: true. No harm flag. Severity: MINOR. area: tooling/semgrep. related: PB5822.

## What was observed
`python scripts/semgrep/verify.py` ended `FAIL: 2 unreadable file(s) unnamed` on one run and
`FAIL: 3 unreadable file(s) unnamed` on others, in one worktree, with no file under `src/` different from
`origin/main`. The implementer identified the files as `IntrinsicArgumentRules.cs`, `VersionConformancePass.cs` and
(on the runs that counted three) `DiagnosticCatalog.cs`. The check is not one of the gate's audits, so the branch
gated GREEN with it red.

## Why it matters
The review skill's first step is this check ("the mechanical checks are free"): six architectural invariants are
enforced by it and by nothing else. A check that fails for a reason of its own, on files that vary by run, is
skipped by whoever runs it, and an invariant violation behind it would go unseen.

## What a fix must decide (not decided here)
- Why semgrep cannot read those files (their size, a parse timeout, a memory limit under a loaded host), and why
  the count varies.
- Whether "unreadable" is a failure of the check or of the file: a file the rules could not be applied to is a
  failure to verify and must stay loud, but it must name the file and the reason.
- Whether the check belongs among the gate's audits once it is deterministic.
