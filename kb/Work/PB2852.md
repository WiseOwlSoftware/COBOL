# PB2852 — units and landers that do not fit

Owner, 2026-10-08 ~21:55 PDT, after asking why branches were left over: "Every time we hit a context limit it is a
failure of design, planning, or implementation. There may be the very rare exception."

## Measured (2026-10-08)

- Loop units over the 200 K soft cap (`logs/orchestrate-20261008-*.out.log`, "context … > soft cap 200000: STOP-UNIT"):
  12:04, 13:12, 16:11, 20:14, 21:15 — every land unit that day.
- Train landers at the 220-turn cap: train 1045 (19:10, nine commits to 974322c12, gate 2 red fixed, needed a resume
  lander at 19:11) and train 1047 (21:15, resumed itself when the Linux gate returned).
- Overnight: wave 1038's unit passed the 150 K cap at fleet launch and eight implementers returned SPLIT (PB2597's
  fix raised the cap; per the owner, raising a cap is not the fix).
- Consequence: units stop before their LAST steps — branch cleanup, records, prune — so lander worktrees, superseded wave
  branches and pushed note branches were left behind (cleaned by hand 21:55, PB2708), and one worktree was removed with
  an uncommitted path (PB2851).

## What closes it

1. **Land unit = one train.** A land unit plans one train (4–6 clusters), supervises its lander, records, and hands off;
   a second train is the next unit.
2. **Lander phases.** Merge → gate → review/fix → Linux gate → oracle → DEVLOG/push as checkpointed phase agents (each a
   fresh context reading the train's STATUS.md), sized from measured turns per cluster per phase.
3. **Deterministic tail work.** Cleanup (worktree/branch removal local and remote, PB2708, PB2851), ledger publish
   notices, lead filing from a structured report: scripts run by the supervisor at each unit START, not model turns at a
   unit's end.
4. **Every cap hit is a defect.** The supervisor, on a soft-cap or hard-cap wind-down, posts a mailbox `info` and the
   next unit files (or appends to) a kb/Work note naming the unit, what it was doing and why it did not fit.
5. Measure: units per day hitting a cap, target zero.

## Train 1052: one landing cut into phases by brief step (2026-10-10)

Train 1052 carried 13 clusters (every branch WAITING TO LAND, O11), more than twice the 4-6 band. The land unit cut the
one landing at the lander brief's own steps, each phase a fresh lander resuming from the last one's branch and
STATUS.md (`E:\COBOL-coord\scratch\t1052\phases.md`):

| phase | brief steps | work | turns (about) | ended |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | 1-2 | clusters 1-7 brought in and verified | 60 | done |
| 1b | 1-2 | clusters 8-13, one in-train fix (51F's call-ABI pin), 7 lead notes | 105 | done |
| 2 | 2b, 3, 3b, 5b | the lease, two whole-population gate runs (Windows and Linux), their reds attributed and fixed, a four-shard review with 5 fixes in the train and one fix withdrawn | 195 | SPLIT before push-main (turn cap) |
| 2 resumer | 3, 3b, 3c, 4-9 | the withdrawn fix recorded (PB2917), gate run 3, semgrep, the oracle, DEVLOG 1951, plan section 0, push-main, train_measure | about 75 to push-main | reached push-main (the cluster bring-in and both fix loops were already done) |

**Finding.** Phase 2 carried BOTH loops that grow with the cluster count: the gate-and-fix loop (attribute each red per
cluster, fix, re-gate both hosts) and the review-and-fix loop (four shards, each confirmed finding fixed and re-gated,
one fix withdrawn after it turned a Linux race test red). Either alone fits a phase; together they did not. A
13-cluster train therefore needs them as SEPARATE phases: (2a) lease + gates + red attribution and fixes, (2b) review
and its fixes + the re-gate, (2c) oracle + DEVLOG + push + measure. Sized from these measurements: bring-in costs about
8-17 turns per cluster (1a: 60/7; 1b: 105/6 with the lead notes), and phase 2's 195 turns split roughly evenly between
its two loops on 13 clusters, so about 7-8 turns per cluster per loop. The resumer's turns above are the cost of the
tail alone (one gate run on a clean head, oracle, DEVLOG, push). Phase planning should derive each phase's cluster
count from these per-cluster rates against the 190-turn split line rather than from the brief's step boundaries alone.
