# PB5098 - the self-test HANG stop leaves the test's child processes running

**Sites:** `scripts/self_tests.py` (`Runner._exec`, `self_test`), `scripts/process_job.py` (whole: new module), `scripts/gate.py` (`_Live`, `Host._captured`, `Host.run_host`), `scripts/gate_slot.py` (`GateSlots.acquire`; `bind_process_tree` deleted).

**Found (2026-11-09).** When a self-test runs past `HANG_AFTER_S` (3,600 s), `subprocess.run` kills the self-test's own
process (the `pwsh` running `test_orchestrate_*.ps1`) and nothing else. On Windows a kill does not reach a process's
children, so the 21 `orchestrate.ps1` test supervisors those self-tests had started (PB3032's hang) ran on with their land
and review lane ticks; two `self_tests.py --reuse` runners sat a further ~15 minutes past the stop, and the orphans were
still alive at 21:50, an hour after their parents, until the Mythos session stopped them by command line (only those with
`-ClaudeExe` naming `testdata/fake-claude.ps1` and a `-CoordDir` under a self-test scratch dir). An orphan left this way
can hold the runner's output pipes and keeps taking the host's processors from every later gate.

**Fix.** Start each self-test in its own process group (a Windows job object, or `taskkill /T /F` on the tree) and stop the
WHOLE tree at the HANG stop and on the runner's own exit; on Linux a new session and `killpg`. Sweep the other places that
stop a child with `timeout=` and leave its tree (gate.py's detached gate, the audits' runners). Its self-test plants a
self-test that starts a grandchild and sleeps, with a tiny hang limit, and checks that no process of the tree survives.

**Second witness (2026-11-10).** The PB5109 gate (run 000001 of fix-same-file-hold) sat 87 minutes. Its
`test_orchestrate_account.ps1` case `acct1` ran an `orchestrate.ps1 -MaxUnits 1` test supervisor that never exited; at
3,600 s the HANG stop killed the test's `pwsh` alone, and the runner then waited forever in `subprocess.run`'s post-kill
`communicate()` (no timeout on Windows) on the stdout pipe the orphaned supervisor still held (py-spy: every worker idle,
one in `Popen._communicate`). Killing the orphan by hand let the verdict through as HANG. So the defect was worse than
filed: the hang detector itself hung.

**Fix (landed with this note).** New `scripts/process_job.py`, the one held process tree: a root starts SUSPENDED, joins
a fresh kill-on-close Job object, then resumes (Windows), or leads a new session (POSIX); `kill` ends the whole tree, an
orphan whose parent already exited included, which `taskkill /T` cannot reach. `self_tests.Runner._exec` runs each
self-test as a ProcessJob with its output in FILES, never pipes; the HANG stop kills the whole tree, and a self-test that
exits leaving processes running has them killed and named on its output. The siblings: `gate.py`'s audits
(`_captured`, now a file, not a pipe) and test hosts (`run_host`) are ProcessJobs, so `_Live.stop_all` kills their whole
trees; `gate_slot.bind_process_tree`'s private Job-object code is deleted and the slot holder calls
`process_job.bind_current` (one copy of the Win32 job code). Self-tests: process_job (an orphaned grandchild is killed;
a root past its wait is TimeoutExpired and killed with its child), self_tests (a hung self-test's grandchild dies with
it; an orphan left by a passing self-test is named and killed), gate_slot, gate.

**Still open, elsewhere.** Why the `acct1` case's supervisor never exited is not this note's (the evidence went with the
self-test scratch); PB3032 is the open note for the orchestrate self-tests' parallel failures, and the runner now names
such a leftover instead of hanging on it.
