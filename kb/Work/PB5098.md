# PB5098 - the self-test HANG stop leaves the test's child processes running

**Sites:** `scripts/self_tests.py` (`Runner._exec`: `subprocess.run(..., timeout=self.hang_after_s)`).

**Found (2026-11-09).** When a self-test runs past `HANG_AFTER_S` (3,600 s), `subprocess.run` kills the self-test's own
process (the `pwsh` running `test_orchestrate_*.ps1`) and nothing else. On Windows a kill does not reach a process's
children, so the 21 `orchestrate.ps1` test supervisors those self-tests had started (PB3032's hang) ran on with their land
and review lane ticks; two `self_tests.py --reuse` runners sat a further ~15 minutes past the stop, and the orphans were
still alive at 21:50, an hour after their parents, until the Mythos session stopped them by command line (only those with
`-ClaudeExe` naming `testdata/fake-claude.ps1` and a `-CoordDir` under a self-test scratch dir). An orphan left this way
can hold the runner's output pipes and keeps taking the host's processors from every later gate.

**Fix.** Start each self-test in its own process group (a Windows job object, or `taskkill /T /F` on the tree) and stop the
WHOLE tree at the HANG stop and on the runner's own exit; on Linux a new session and `killpg`. Sweep the other places that
stop a child with `timeout=` and leave its tree (gate.py's detached gate, the audits' runners). Its self-test plants a
self-test that starts a grandchild and sleeps, with a tiny hang limit, and checks that no process of the tree survives.
