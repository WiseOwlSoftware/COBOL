# PB4984 - real-word misprints in the printed 1985 standard

**Sites:** `specs/ANSI_COBOL_1985.md` (the Errata section: its finders paragraphs, C20 to C30 and their in-place flags), `scripts/spec/audit_batch.py` (`PROMPTS`, `EST_OUTPUT_TOKENS`, `EFFORT`, `VERDICTS`, `plan_misprints`, `misprint_leads`, `prompt_parts`, `parse_answer`, `spaced`, `misprints_error`, `build_parser`, `main`), `scripts/spec/test_audit_batch.py` (`main`), `docs/rearchitecture/DESIGN-spec-conformance-review.md` (section 10.3), `docs/rearchitecture/spec-rule-catalog-1985.json` (regenerated), `tests/version-matrix/inventory-1985/` (ten records restated). `scripts/spec/verify_publishable.py#check_corrections` only validates the Errata's flags and entries; it is not a misprint finder.

**Report.** PB4972 corrected 19 misprints of the printed 1985 standard (each read on the rendered page) and ended the transcription with a section listing them. The scan that located candidates finds only misprints that
make a NON-word (BEFCRE, fopting, thac and the like). A misprint that makes another real word (for example a wrong-but-valid word in a rule) is invisible to it. Finding those needs a reader: a page-by-page
read of the rendered 1985 text against its sense and against the 2002/2023 counterpart clause. Analysis to decide: what is the cheapest reader that covers the normative clauses (the audit_batch map/route machinery, PB4971, already pairs 1985 clauses with 2023 counterparts and could flag wording differences), and what residual risk the Errata section's completeness claim should state.

## Decision (wave 20261937 B)

**Re-probe.** The audit state of the 1985 run (`state-k15`: 2,253 rules, 1,251 SAME pairs verified by a reader that saw both texts) carries no verdict that names a misprint of the 1985 text: the only "typo" it contains is a 2023 Addendum note quoted in a source. The verify prompt never asked the question, so the silence is no evidence of absence.

**Cheapest reader.** The map/verify machinery (`scripts/spec/audit_batch.py`, PB4971) already puts each 1985 rule next to its 2023 counterpart in front of a reader, 1,789 pairs in the SAME and DIFFERS arms. Adding one question to that pass ("does a word of the 1985 sentence read as a misprint of the sense the 2023 sentence carries?") costs no new retrieval and no new corpus. It covers only the rules the map paired with a 2023 counterpart (SAME 1,251 + DIFFERS 538 = 1,789 of the 2,253). The 464 rules the map labelled NONE have no 2023 sentence to compare against, so this reader cannot see a real-word misprint in them; they need a separate read of the rendered page. Prose, general-format diagrams and the index are outside the catalog and stay uncovered.

**Residual risk, stated.** The Errata section now says its list is not known complete, why (a real-word misprint is invisible to both finders), and what a user does (check a wording that disagrees with 2023 against the rendered page). The statement is the landed part of this note.

**Open at the decision (wave 20261937 B):** the extra question, its run, and a read of the prose outside the catalog.

## Landed (wave 20261943 A)

**The reader is a pass of its own, not a question in the verify pass.** The decision above put the question into the
verify pass. Implementation showed why that is the wrong shape: the verify pass reads only the SAME answers (1,323 on
the current run, `state-route`), its stored answers feed every rule's disposition and change record, and adding a field
to its answer would have made `withdraw` re-ask all of them and could move upheld verdicts. So `audit_batch.py` has a
ninth pass, `misprints`, planned from the 1985 catalog AS IT NOW READS (the map's items carry the text from before
PB4972's corrections) with each rule's map-paired 2023 rules as counterparts, or none. It covers the NONE rules too,
read for their own sense alone. The answer is MISPRINT, with each misprint's printed words, intended words and context,
or CLEAN; `misprints_error` holds each misprint to the rule's text (tags, escapes, box sides and line breaks dropped, the
printed words whole inside the context), so a lead always points at words the page prints. `misprint-leads` writes
`DIR/misprint-leads.json`. Design section 10.3 records the pass; the self-test plants the plan (a repaired rule and a rule
the map never saw), the prompt, the answer shape (seven refusals) and the leads.

**The run** (`E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/audit-1985/state-route/misprints`, account 1, batches `msgbatch_0152VWdRqzZ86gxga9uhUuRq`
(canary, 20) and `msgbatch_01WCvsYQkBEZq74pf73aGkty`, $5.57, no reply refused): 2,255 rules, 1,863 with a counterpart; CLEAN
2,230, MISPRINT 25 (26 leads).

**The page read.** Every lead was located on its PDF page and the plausible ones rendered (pages 263, 282, 300, 329, 330,
336, 339, 360, 363, 392, 421, 445, 475, 476, 572, 586, 614). Eleven are printed misprints whose sense is unambiguous and
are corrected in place as Errata C20 to C30 (kind `word`): `explain`, `a entry`, `referenced modified` (twice; the
standard writes `reference modified` six times), `participate` (the BEFORE item beside it prints `participates`, and 2023
does too), `assumed`, `to be right`, `modules`, `If not exception`, `medium reference by`, `printer line`. Two printed
misprints are left as printed because Amendment 2 corrects them in its own list of changes, so the amended standard
already reads right: `had been execution` (§1985:XI.4.4.4 GR12; page 934) and `current page group` (§1985:XIII.3.10.8 GR4;
page 936). The rest are the standard's requirement or wording, not misprints, and stay as printed: the SET rule's
`index-name-1` where the sense wants index-name-2 (§1985:VI.6.23.4 GR2: a normative operand, for the 1985 adjudication,
never a transcription correction), the MOVE category list without alphanumeric (§1985:VI.6.19.4 GR3, printed so),
`multiplier` (§1985:VI.6.20.4 GR1: the BY operand is the multiplier), REPLACE's `is not replaced` (§1985:XII.3.4 GR7,
which Amendment 2 rewrites), `for the RECORD IS VARYING` (page 475, plain English). Eight leads were judged from the
reconciled text without a render, because each is the standard's wording, repeated or deliberate, and no reading of
the page could make it a misprint: the three `output` file readings of SAME RECORD AREA (one sentence printed alike in
three I-O modules), `specific conventions` (printed alike in three modules), `next more significant key` (twice),
`whenever ... literal`, `reel or file`, `data-name` in the REDEFINES rule. The 1985 catalog is regenerated (ten rule texts) and the ten 1985 change
records stored against the old text are restated against the corrected text through `record_verdicts.py --edition
1985`, their decisions unchanged.

**What stays unread, stated in the Errata.** The reader read the catalog (its rules and 210 general formats). The prose
outside it (introductions, Function paragraphs, appendices) and the index have had no such read, and a rule with no
counterpart was read for its sense alone. The Errata says so and keeps its instruction to check a wording that
disagrees with 2023 against the rendered page. No further reader is owed by this note.
