# PB4887 - one citation format, every edition, the edition mandatory

**Why.** CLAUDE.md rule 1 requires an exact, mechanically checked citation for every semantics question. The repository
now holds the 2023 standard, and the 1985 and 2002 standards are being transcribed (PB4812, PB4813). A citation does not say
which edition it means:
- about 123,000 `§N.N` occurrences in the tree, most of them bare 2023 clause numbers (`§2023:13.18.35.4 GR1`);
- the same `§` marks the repository's own document sections ("plan §0", "DESIGN-x §2023:4.4");
- other editions are free text ("COBOL-85 §4.3.3", "ISO 1989:1985 8.3.1.2"). The citations audit cannot check them; it
  skips them through a hand-kept word list (`NOT_THE_STANDARD` in `scripts/spec/audit_code_citations.py`);
- `cite.py --edition` (PB4815) exists, but the citation text cannot carry the edition to it.
A 1985 citation written with `§` today is read as a 2023 clause, and the audit calls it a phantom (2026-11-06: PB4885's
"§1.5.2").

**The format** (owner 2026-11-06; the edition is MANDATORY: "Mandatory (Recommended)"):

    §<edition>:<locator>[ <rule>]

| Edition token | Document | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| `2023` | ISO/IEC 1989:2023 | clause number: `13.18.35.4` |
| `2014` | ISO/IEC 1989:2014 | clause number (validated once a transcription exists) |
| `2002` | ISO/IEC 1989:2002 | clause number |
| `1985` | ANSI X3.23-1985 | Roman section, then the section's own paragraph number: `VI.5.3.2` (Section VI, Nucleus). Paragraph numbers restart in each section. A printed page with no paragraph: `p.VI-20`; an appendix: `B.…` |
| `1989` | ANSI X3.23a-1989 (the amendment) | its own numbering |
| `1993` | ANSI X3.23b-1993 (the amendment) | its own numbering |

- **Rule:** `SR n` | `GR n` | `Format n` | `Table n`, then an optional rule path written glued as today (`GR6d.2`).
- **Examples:** `§2023:13.18.35.4 GR1`, `§2002:14.8.4 SR2`, `§1985:VI.5.3.2 GR3`, `§1985:I.1.5.2`, `§1985:p.VI-20`.
- **Disambiguation.** `§` immediately followed by an edition token and a colon is a standard citation, and nothing else is.
  The repository's own documents keep their `§` sections. `NOT_THE_STANDARD` is deleted, because the grammar now separates
  the two cases.

**The work.**
1. **One parser**, `scripts/spec/citation.py`. Its docstring is the format's one specification: the grammar, the edition table
   and the examples. It provides parse, render and validate. `cite.py --check` takes a whole citation
   (`cite.py --check "§1985:VI.5.3.2 GR3" "<text>"`) and validates it against that edition's transcription (`spec_pdf`).
   `audit_code_citations.py`, `audit_doc_citations.py`, `fuzz_triage.py` (`CLAUSE_IN_TEXT`) and every other reader of a
   citation use it, and no other regex for a citation survives (sweep with `git grep`).
2. **The rewrite.** Rewrite every existing citation of the 2023 standard from `§N`… to `§2023:N`… with one script, run once and
   then deleted. It rewrites only what the parser classifies as a standard citation: the audit's current clause universe
   decides, and a repository-section `§` is left alone. Free-text citations of other editions ("COBOL-85 §4.3.3") are
   converted to their edition's form where the locator is certain, and listed for hand repair otherwise. Then the citations
   audit must be GREEN on the rewritten tree. Under CLAUDE.md rule 4 there is no compatibility reading of a bare `§N`
   afterwards: the audit refuses a bare standard citation.
3. **User-visible text.** Compiler diagnostics that cite the standard ("ISO §2023:12.4.5.2 SR4" in the binder's messages) use the same
   format, and their goldens change in the same landing. Citing the rule of the edition the program was compiled under
   (`--std`) is a separate improvement; file it as its own note if it is wanted.
4. **Docs.** CLAUDE.md rule 1, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, the conformance-finding issue form, the `spec-lookup` skill and
   MANDATORY-PRACTICES name the format and point at `citation.py`. `docs/DOC_INDEX.md` lists `citation.py`.
5. **Drift test.** `citation.py --self-test` holds planted cases for every edition, every locator shape and each rejection:
   no edition, an unknown edition, a phantom clause, a 1985 paragraph outside its section.

**Landing.** The rewrite touches thousands of files, so it lands ALONE as one change, while no other branch is in flight on
the files it touches (memory tree-wide-rename-lands-alone). Coordinate the timing with the operator lane through the mailbox,
and land items 1 and 3 to 5 with it, or before it as long as the parser accepts both forms only until the rewrite lands
(one change set; no lasting alias).

**Sites:** `scripts/spec/citation.py` (new), `scripts/spec/cite.py` (`main`, `check`), `scripts/spec/audit_code_citations.py` (`CITE`, `NOT_THE_STANDARD`, `_phantoms`, `phantom_scan`), `scripts/spec/audit_doc_citations.py` (whole: its citation reader), `scripts/fuzz_triage.py` (`CLAUSE_IN_TEXT`), `CLAUDE.md` (whole: rule 1), `CONTRIBUTING.md` (whole), `src/` (whole: diagnostic message text and comments, rewritten by the script).

**Phase 1 (wave 20261889, landed with this note still open).** `scripts/spec/citation.py` is the format's specification and
its one parser: `parse`, `scan`, `render`, `validate` against each edition's transcription (2023 and 1985 today; 2002 once
`specs/ISO_COBOL_2002.md` exists; 2014, 1989 and 1993 parsed only), the region builders of both transcription shapes (ISO
clause headings; the 1985 Roman sections, their own paragraphs, printed pages and appendices), and `bare_standard`, the
classifier of a bare `§N`. Every reader parses through it: `cite.py --check` takes a whole citation and also checks an ISO
rule's ordinal; `audit_code_citations.py` lost `CITE` and `NOT_THE_STANDARD` (the document-name screen is
`citation.DOCUMENT_SECTION`, the other-edition one `citation.other_edition`), validates an edition-form citation against
its own edition (PHANTOM), refuses a malformed one (CITATION), carries the phase-2 BARE refusal switched off, and its
ordinal family no longer reads another edition's clause number as 2023's; `audit_doc_citations.py` embeds
`citation.PATTERN` and checks a 1985 quotation against the 1985 transcription; `fuzz_triage.py` lost `CLAUSE_IN_TEXT`;
`work.py`, `where.py`, `phase_b_batch.py` and `docchecks/vcr.py` read through it. `BARE_MEANS_2023 = True` keeps the
unrewritten tree green. `scripts/spec/rewrite_citations.py --dry-run` counts 94,480 bare 2023 citations in 6,302 files (a document name counts only as a whole name right against the `§`, review R1-1: the first screen, a substring anywhere in 45 characters, took "EVALUATE" for "EVAL" and called 2,658 citations a document's section; the whole-name screen leaves 1,400 of those as real document sections, so about 1,258 were genuine citations that would have stayed bare);
on a scratch copy `--apply` then a second `--apply` (0 changes) and `BARE_MEANS_2023 = False` left both citation audits
GREEN, the bare form refused.
