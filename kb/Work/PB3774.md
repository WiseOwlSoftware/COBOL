# PB3774 — route a bounce to the lane that dispatched the branch

**Sites:** `scripts/orchestrator/land_lane.py` (the bounce and CONFLICT handling, the conflict brief, the queue watch's alerts), `scripts/orchestrator/units/enqueue.md` (the finisher and re-arm steps), `scripts/orchestrator/plan_wave.py` (the dispatch ledger: each group's wave, slug and scratch), `scripts/orchestrator/mailbox.py` (the `to-mythos` inbox).

## What happens

On 2026-10-25 two lanes dispatched implementers: the loop (account 2) and the Mythos lane (account 1, waves from 20261500, scratch `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/mythos`). The landing lane lands every CLEAN branch of both, which is intended, but it names "the enqueue unit's finisher" for every bounce and posts every stall to the operator. So at 13:27-13:30 the loop's enqueue unit, the Mythos lane and the operator each re-armed the same 10 outage bounces, and at 14:06 the lane assigned PR #602 (worktree-wf_1341dc87-1c4-14, a Mythos branch) to the loop's finisher. The owner had the lanes split ownership by origin (memory lane-split-2026-10-25); the operator had to repeat that split to each new loop unit by message, because no unit reads it.

## Fix direction

Ownership by construction: the dispatch ledger already records which wave (and scratch) dispatched each branch's group. The lane reads the owning lane from it (a wave in the Mythos lane's range, or a scratch under its directory, or an explicit `lane` field the renderer records) and sends that lane's bounces, conflict briefs and stalls to its own inbox (`mailbox.py send --to mythos`); the enqueue unit acts only on the loop's. Self-tests: a Mythos-lane branch's CONFLICT goes to `to-mythos` and no loop finisher is planned; a loop branch's still goes to the enqueue unit.
