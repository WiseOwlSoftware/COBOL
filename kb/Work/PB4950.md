# PB4950 - a diagnostic cites its edition's rule

**The decision (owner, 2026-11-07).** "Yes a diagnostic cite the rule of the edition the program was compiled under."
PB4887 raised this as a separate improvement once the citation format carried the edition. The owner has now decided it,
and added: "an error with a still valid 1985 construct compiled for 2023 must cite the 2023 version of that error". **The
rule is symmetric: the citation ALWAYS follows the compilation's edition, never the edition a construct comes from.** A
1985 construct that is still valid in 2023 and is compiled with `--std=2023` cites the 2023 rule. The same construct
compiled with `--std=1985` cites the 1985 rule. A construct's origin never picks the citation.

**What is true today.** `DiagnosticDescriptor` (`src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Editions/Diagnostics/DiagnosticDescriptor.cs`) carries ONE
citation, `IsoSection`, "the ISO/IEC 1989:2023 § ... this diagnostic enforces". Some messages also interpolate a § at the
call site. So a program compiled with `--std=1985` is pointed at a 2023 rule, and its numbering differs from the 1985 rule
that governs it: for the SAME clause, `§2023:12.4.6.4.3 SR2/SR3/SR7/SR9` against `§1985:VII.2.13.3 SR1-SR3c` (lead of
w20261917a). Where 1985 has no such rule, the diagnostic must not cite one.

**The design (to be written into the diagnostics deep-dive design doc before any code).**
- The descriptor carries the citation per edition, `IReadOnlyDictionary<Edition, string>` or an equivalent typed
  structure, each value in PB4887's edition form and validated mechanically by `cite.py --check` in a drift test. It is
  never a parallel hand-kept table.
- The message renders the citation of the compilation's edition (`--std`). A diagnostic with no rule in that edition (an
  edition gate's "not in 1985" refusal, say) cites the edition gate's own source, `VERSION_CHANGE_REFERENCE`'s row and
  the edition where the construct begins, never a rule the edition does not have.
- Call sites that interpolate a § go through the same rendering, so no site spells a 2023 citation for a 1985 compile.
- **The data comes from the 1985 audit (PB4886).** Its mapping pass ties each 1985 rule to its 2023 counterpart (SAME or
  DIFFERS). Reversed, that gives each diagnostic's 1985 citation mechanically. A rule the mapping marks NONE has no
  1985 counterpart, and the diagnostic follows the edition-gate arm above. 2002 and 2014 follow when their transcriptions
  and audits exist (PB4813).
- Goldens: every `--std=1985` diagnostic golden re-pins to the 1985 citation in the same change. A drift test refuses a
  1985 golden that carries a `§2023:` citation for a rule the 1985 map knows. The same drift test refuses the reverse:
  a `--std=2023` golden that carries a `§1985:` citation. Its planted cases include one construct valid in both
  editions, compiled once under each, each run citing its own edition's rule.
