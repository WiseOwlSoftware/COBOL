## What the standard prints

Rendered off the canonical PDF, not read from the transcription — `python scripts/render-spec-page.py 784`
→ PDF page 784 / printed folio 754, §14.9.41.2 START general format:

```
START file-name-1
  ┌ FIRST                                                                    ┐
  │ KEY relational-operator { data-name-1 | record-key-name-1 } [ WITH LENGTH … ] │
  └ LAST                                                                     ┘
```

On the render `KEY` is underlined and `relational-operator` is **not underlined and not bracketed**. The one
plain bracket in the group is the outer FIRST/KEY/LAST stack. §5.2.6.2 —
`cite.py --check 5.2.6.2 "Brackets, [ ], enclosing a portion of a general format indicate that the syntax
element contained within the brackets or one of the alternatives contained within the brackets may be
explicitly specified or that portion of the general format may be omitted."` → **OK** — only a *bracketed*
portion may be omitted, and §5.2.1 —
`--check 5.2.1 "The words, phrases, clauses, punctuation, and operands in each general format shall be written
in the compilation group in the sequence given in the general format"` → **OK** — requires everything else to
be written. So once `KEY` is written, `relational-operator` shall be written.

`IS` alone is not a relational operator. §8.8.4.2.2 Format 1 (General-relation) stacks its operator
alternatives in **braces**, and every alternative carries at least one underlined word or a relational
character: `--check 8.8.4.2.2 "Each of the three brace groups requires exactly one of its enclosed
alternatives"` → **OK**; `--check 5.2.2 "Keywords are reserved words or context-sensitive words. They are shown
in uppercase and underlined in general formats."` → **OK**, and `--check 5.2.3 "Optional words are reserved
words or context-sensitive words. They are shown in uppercase and not underlined in general formats."` → **OK**
— `IS` is the *optional word* of every alternative, never an alternative of its own.

Three independent confirmations that the operator is required and that no default fills in for it:

- `--check 14.9.41.3 "In the KEY phrase, relational-operator is a relational operator specified in the
  general-relation format of 8.8.4.2"` → **OK §14.9.41.3 3)**. SR3 speaks of the operator unconditionally,
  where the neighbouring SR5 and SR6 both say "data-name-1, **if specified**".
- `--check 14.9.41.4 "If the KEY phrase is not specified, the behavior is the same as if KEY IS EQUAL TO
  data-name-1 or record-key-name-1 had been specified"` → **OK §14.9.41.4 15)**. EQUAL is implied when the
  **phrase** is absent — never when the phrase is present and only the operator is missing. GR8 says the same
  for relative files ("If the KEY phrase is omitted").
- The external GnuCOBOL corpus (`tests/external/gnucobol/tests/`) never writes the operator-less spelling:
  a regex sweep for `START <file> KEY [IS] <name>` with no operator returns 4 hits, **all four in comment
  lines**. There is no vendor-extension demand here to dialect-gate (`docs/dialect-strictness.md`), so the
  leniency axis is not the answer either.

definition (`scripts/spec/work.py`: an item that does nothing to a user's program) — the compiler's behaviour
is correct, the artefact is a test. ⛔ That also means **`work.py next` will not surface it**
(`feedback_measure_the_selectors_complement`); it reaches a lander through the battery #57 attribution, not
through the ranked list. It is MAJOR because a red gate on main blocks every landing behind it.

IS K` compiling and silently behaving as EQUAL, a spelling §14.9.41.2 does not print — and it would have to

## The fix

One source line, `tests/CobolSharp.Tests.Integration/FileIOTests.cs:536`:

```
-                START IX-FILE KEY IS IX-KEY.
+                START IX-FILE KEY IS EQUAL TO IX-KEY.
```

The three assertions at lines 548–550 (`00`, `BBB222`, `CCC333`) are unchanged and were measured to still
hold — the test keeps verifying exactly what its name says, positioning for READ NEXT. (`START IX-FILE.`
with the phrase omitted would also be legal and would mean the same thing by §14.9.41.4 GR15, but it would
stop exercising the KEY phrase this test exists to exercise.)

**The process root, which is the part that generalizes.** `OptionalWordSubsetDriftTests` proves the power set
of a format's OPTIONAL words compiles — it measures what the grammar must ACCEPT and can say nothing about
what a tightening just made it REJECT. The complement was never measured
(`feedback_measure_the_selectors_complement`), and the only suite that compiled the removed spelling is
`CobolSharp.Tests.Integration`, which the per-commit wave-local gate does not run (only `scripts/guard.sh`
does), so the stale test survived to the battery. A commit that removes a spelling from the grammar should
grep the corpus for that spelling in the same change set; the mechanical form of that check belongs beside
`scripts/spec/audit_grammar_optional_words.py`, which already knows which words a rule requires and could diff
the required-word set against the previous revision and sweep the corpus for the difference.
