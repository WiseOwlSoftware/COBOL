# PB6114 - hint 0c' claims a section header as a second CORRESPONDING receiver

PB6096's hint 0c' (`CorrespondingReceiverMessage`, called from `GuessCobolIntent`) reads the MOVE, ADD or SUBTRACT CORRESPONDING statement the
parser completed just before the offending token and reports the token as a second receiver. At every edition without areas (2002, 2014, 2023,
and free form) it does so also when the token, with the word after it, writes a section header:

```
MOVE CORR G1 TO G2
S2 SECTION.
```

The statement is complete and a period is missing. Main reports that as COBOL0307 "A period may be missing"; hint 0c' now says the
statement has a second receiver, which is the wrong mistake and hides the right one.

**Site.** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolErrorStrategy.cs` (`CobolErrorStrategy.GuessCobolIntent`; hint 0c' is at line 1870 of PB6096's branch, which is not on main until it lands, so re-derive the line). The guard excludes only the 1985 area-A opening (`PeriodIsMissingBeforeAnAreaAOpening`). It
does not exclude a token that begins a header: `OffendingPosition.WritesAHeader` (the token is SECTION or DIVISION, or the word before one).

**Why it is the same family as PB4706, PB4770 and PB4845.** Each of those found a closed-format reader that took a header's words for a clause or
a name; `WritesAHeader` is the exclusion they added, and each new arm over a completed statement has to apply it. Sweep every arm of
`GuessCobolIntent` that reads `StatementThatEndedBefore` or a completed-statement prefix for the same missing guard (hint 0h's
`APeriodLetsTheSentenceFollow` is the model).

**Next.** Add the `WritesAHeader` exclusion (and the paragraph-name case the message already states as ambiguous) to hint 0c', with a refusal
golden per edition without areas: `MOVE CORR G1 TO G2` then `S2 SECTION.` expects COBOL0307.
