# PB4108 — the order of a SYMBOLIC CHARACTERS group's ordinals decides whether it compiles

## What happens

The ordinal list is refused when a literal ordinal comes before a constant-name ordinal in the same group; the reversed
order compiles. IS/ARE present or absent makes no difference. Probed with the built compiler at main (`K1`, `K2`, `K3`
integer constant-names):

| probe | std | spelling | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| RT01 / RT02 / RT03 | 2023 | `SYMBOLIC CHARACTERS SC1 SC2 [ARE / IS] 66 K2` | rc=65 COBOL1670 "2 symbolic-character name(s) against 1 integer(s)" |
| RT04 | 2023 | `... SC1 SC2 SC3 66 K2 68` | rc=65 COBOL1670 (3 against 1) |
| RT07 | 2023 | `... SC1 SC2 IS 66 K2 SC3 68` | rc=65 COBOL1670 |
| RT09 | 2023 | `... SC1 SC2 SC3 66 67 K3` | rc=65 COBOL1670 (3 against 2) |
| RT11 / RT12 | 2014 / 2002 | `SC1 SC2 66 K2` / `SC1 SC2 ARE 66 K2` | rc=65 COBOL1670 |
| RS02, RT05, RT10 | 2023 | `SC1 SC2 K1 67` (constant first) | compiles, gives the right characters |

## Why it is legal (each checked with `cite.py --check`, OK)

- §5.5 1): an integer operand "refers to a fixed-point integer literal that shall be unsigned and nonzero unless
  otherwise specified in the associated rules".
- §13.10.3 2): "constant-name-1 may be used anywhere that a format specifies a literal of the class and category of
  constant-name-1". The compiler already accepts constant ordinals (kb/Work PB1948).
- §12.3.7.3 16) b): "The relationship between each symbolic-character-1 and the corresponding integer-1 is by position
  in the SYMBOLIC CHARACTERS clause"; 16) c): "There shall be a one-to-one correspondence between occurrences of
  symbolic-character-1 and occurrences of integer-1".

`{ integer-1 } ...` repeats per occurrence, so `66 K2` is two integer-1 occurrences; the only parse of
`SC1 SC2 [ARE] 66 K2` the format admits is one group of two names and two ordinals. GnuCOBOL 3.2 has no constant-name
ordinals at all, so it has nothing to say on the order; the ISO rules settle it.

## Mechanism and sites

- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolSpecialNames.g4#symbolicCharacterEntry`: alternative 1,
  `cobolWord+ (IS|ARE)? integerLiteral+`, ends the entry at the last literal, so a trailing constant-name ordinal starts a
  new alternative-3 entry (`cobolWord+`).
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/DataBinder.Switches.cs#SwitchBindSymbolic` (about line 2262:
  `sc.symbolicCharacterEntry().SelectMany(SymbolicEntryGroups)`) and `#SymbolicEntryGroups` (about line 2327) regroup one
  entry at a time, so `([SC1,SC2],[66])` then `([],[K2])`.
- Fix direction (the implementer's design): regroup the clause's entries as ONE source-order stream of names and
  ordinals, then pair by §12.3.7.3 16) b) / c).

**Lead for the fix's adjudication, not claimed as a defect.** RT06 `SC1 SC2 66 SC3 67 68` gives COBOL1670: the compiler
pairs per group ("positional per ENTRY", DataBinder.Switches.cs about line 2241), while 16) b) / c) speak of positions and
occurrences across the whole clause (3 names, 3 ordinals). GnuCOBOL 3.2 rejects it too ("invalid SYMBOLIC clause"). The
one-stream walk above decides it either way; rule on it in the same change.

Do not add the PB3752 audit marker `audit:optional-word IS ARE decided-by DataBinder.SymbolicEntryGroups` until this
lands: it would name the member that mis-decides RT03 (kb/Work PB4110).

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/DataBinder.Switches.cs` (`SwitchBindSymbolic`, `SymbolicClauseOperands`, which replaced `SymbolicEntryGroups`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Validation/IntegerOperandPass.cs` (`LiteralOnlySlots`, its reason text names the new member), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolSpecialNames.g4#symbolicCharacterEntry` (comment only), `docs/DATA_MODEL_DESIGN.md`.

## Landing (2026-10-30, wave 20261780 group E)

Re-probed on the built compiler: RT04, RT06, RT07 and `SC1 SC2 SC3 66 K2` all gave COBOL1670 before the fix, each followed
by a false COBOL1639 "not defined" at every use. Root cause as above: the grammar ends the entry at the last literal, so a
constant-name ordinal after a literal one opens a new entry, and the binder paired names with ordinals entry by entry. The
entry is no unit of the rule: §12.3.7.3 SR16 b) pairs "by position in the SYMBOLIC CHARACTERS clause" and c) counts
occurrences. Fix: `SymbolicClauseOperands` reads every entry's tokens as one stream and `SwitchBindSymbolic` pairs the two
lists once.

**Ruling on the lead (RT06 `SC1 SC2 66 SC3 67 68`): accepted.** The ISO text pairs across the clause (3 names against 3
integers: SC1=66, SC2=67, SC3=68), the format admits it as two repeats of the braced group, and no rule makes a group the unit
of the one-to-one count. GnuCOBOL 3.2 refuses it ("invalid SYMBOLIC clause"); the precedence is ISO first.
Also fixed on the way (I9): a refused count no longer leaves the clause's names undeclared. Goldens:
`12.3.7-SPECIAL-NAMES/pb4108-symbolic-literal-then-constant-ordinals` (accept 2002, where CONSTANT begins) and the refused
`pb4108-symbolic-clause-count-mismatch`.
