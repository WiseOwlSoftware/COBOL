**Landed (2026-11-14, wave 20262031 group F).** Re-probed on a fresh build: `q19d` (COPY LQ19 REPLACING ==XX== BY == / D QQ==) was COBOL7061 at --std 85 with the mode, exit 65; `q19e` printed `[Q]`. Now `q19d` prints `[Q]`.

**The mechanism differs from the note's hypothesis.** The note blamed `OriginWriter.Append` giving the replacement the replaced word's origin. A dump of the resultant text showed the cause one step earlier: pseudo-text-2 begins with the debugging carrier, which COPY keeps, and `ApplyReplacements` wrote it MID-LINE, so the resultant line was `DISPLAY "[" <carrier> QQ`. The lexer makes every token after a carrier on its line debugging text, and `ReportDebuggingLinesBreakingTheProgram` hides by resultant line, so `DISPLAY "["` was hidden with `QQ` and `"]".` was left to parse alone. The origin is not consulted by the check. The text after the match was already moved to a line of its own (`resultEndsDebugging != endLineDebugging`); the text before it was not.

**Fix.** `CopyProcessor.ApplyReplacements` (whole-text arm): a replacement that begins with a carrier (`StartsWithCarrier(introduced, 0)`, which `IntroducedText` leaves only where the replaced line does not decide) after non-blank text on its line starts a resultant line of its own (`sb.NewLine`). The carrier is dropped wherever the replaced line decides (`IntroducedText`), so the new break is the only other place a carrier can land mid-line. The LEADING and TRAILING arm cannot reach it at --std 85 (COBOL0900, §2002 phrase), and the debugging-line check runs below 2002 only.

**Goldens.** `pb5472-copy-debugging-line-replaces-mid-line` (accept 85, prints `[Q]`) and the refusal `pb5472-copy-debugging-line-replacement-needed` (the debugging line holds the MOVE operand: COBOL7061 still). Probes `p20a` (the same), `p20b` (no mode: prints `[]`), `p20d` (REPLACE statement, decided path: `[Q]`).
