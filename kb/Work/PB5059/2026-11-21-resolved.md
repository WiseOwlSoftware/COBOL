## Resolved (wave 20262131 group s, branch train85b-g3-cite)

**Meaning decided.** A `§1985:` citation names the standard as amended: the transcription applies both amendments in
place in the base text (kb/Work PB5045), so a 1985 locator names the amended rule. What only an amendment prints is cited
by that amendment's own token, `§1989:` (Amendment 1: Section A and its pages) or `§1993:` (Amendment 2's pages). The rule
is in `scripts/spec/citation.py`'s format docstring.

**One resolver.** `citation.regions_1985` ends the standard at the first `# AMENDMENT` heading, so every reader of it
(`_loaded`, `element_standing.py`, `extract_rule_catalog.py`, `rule_catalog_1985.py`, `conformance_1985.py`) shares the cut;
`amendment_regions` still reads each amendment from its own heading. `python scripts/spec/citation.py "§1985:p.B-16"`
now prints `FAIL: the 1985 standard has no locator p.B-16` (it printed OK before), and the self-test plants a folio after an
amendment heading and checks the real universe holds no `p.A-` or `p.B-` folio.

**`--find` searches the amendments.** `cite.py --find --edition 1985` searches the standard and each amendment printed
with it (`citation.printed_with`), citing every hit by its own token: the ALL-subscript sentence is found at
`§1989:p.A-28` and `§1989:A.2.2`, never as a 1985 page. A failed `--check` of a 1989 or 1993 citation names
`--find --edition 1985`.

**The wrong-edition citations it exposed** (13 sites, DEVLOG left as history, PB5043's body names the old form in
backticks): each 1985 page citation of an Amendment 1 page took the amendment's token (`§1985:p.A-9` became `§1989:p.A-9`; each re-checked with `cite.py --check` and its quotation where it
has one: p.A-9, A-28, A-29, A-64; A-34 by locator), the one `§1985:p.B-46` became `§1993:p.B-46`.
`extract_rule_catalog.py --edition 1985 --check` stays `✓ no findings`.
