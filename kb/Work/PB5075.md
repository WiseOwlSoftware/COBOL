# PB5075 - the composite-of-operands limit is enforced at 31 digits at every edition and counted over all operands: at --std 85 a 19-to-31-digit composite compiles in ADD, SUBTRACT, MULTIPLY and DIVIDE, and MULTIPLY F18 BY R18 (a 18-digit composite of receivers) is refused

**Sites:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Validation/StatementValidation.cs#CheckComposite` (the cap is `EditionContext.MaxDigits`; `CompositeMembers.ReceivingItems85`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Procedure/Verbs/ArithmeticBinder.cs#BindMultiply` and `#BindDivide` (every call passes `CompositeMembers.ReceivingItems85`), `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Editions/Diagnostics/DiagnosticCatalog.cs#ArithmeticCompositeTooWide`, `docs/NUMERIC_DESIGN.md` (D3 and the Edition gating bullet), `tests/version-matrix/inventory/SR-14.7.7-2.json` (notes).

**Mechanism.** 1985 caps the composite of operands at 18 digits and defines it per statement: ADD and SUBTRACT format 1 use all operands, MULTIPLY and DIVIDE use the RECEIVING items only (DIVIDE except the REMAINDER item). CheckComposite caps at 31 (the 2023 limit) for every edition and composes it from all operands. The cap is wrong in both directions: 19 to 31 digit composites pass (under-reject), and a multiply of a V9(18) operand by a 9(18) receiver, whose 1985 composite is 18 digits, is refused with COBOL0805 (rejects legal source). The code comment rests the 31 on CCVS-85 NC101A (MULTIPLY of 9(3)V9(3) by 9(18)); that program's composite is its one 18-digit receiver and conforms to the 18-digit rule, so the refutation applies the 2023 composite to a 1985 statement.

**Found by.** The 1985 `--std=85` draft triage (kb/Work PB4995 item 3): each rule below is a conformance-suite draft that did not MATCH at `--std 85`, and its adjudicator decided COMPILER-DEFECT. Harm and observed text are the records'; nothing here was re-derived.

**Inventory.** The traceability inventory carries no 1985 rows yet, so `inventory_rows` is empty; the rows this note covers are the 1985 rule ids listed below (catalog `docs/rearchitecture/spec-rule-catalog-1985.json`).

**Rules (5; every citation checked with `scripts/spec/cite.py --check`).**

- `1985-SR-VI.6.4.4-2` - `§1985:VI.6.4.4` (record harm: under-rejects, slice-05): §1985:VI.6.4.4, item (2) (the catalog's SR2; the paragraph has no Syntax Rules heading): "The maximum size of each operand is 18 decimal digits. The composite of operands, which is a hypothetical data item resulting from the superimposition of specified operands in a statement aligned on their decimal points ... must not contain more than 18 decimal digits." Which operands are 'specified' is each statement's own rule; for ADD format 1 it is §1985:VI.6.6.3 SR3a: "In format 1 the composite of operands is determined by using all of the operands in a given statement." `ADD A TO B` with A PIC 9(18) and B PIC V9 superimposes 18 integer positions and 1 fraction position: 19 digits. A program that breaks the rule is not a conforming source program (§1985:I.1.6: "A conforming source program is one which does not violate the explicitly stated provisions and specifications of Standard COBOL."). The draft is right.
- `1985-SR-VI.6.6.3-3` - `§1985:VI.6.6.3 SR3` (record harm: under-rejects, slice-05): §1985:VI.6.6.3 SR3: "The composite of operands must not contain more than 18 digits" and its item a: "In format 1 the composite of operands is determined by using all of the operands in a given statement." `ADD A TO B` with A PIC 9(10) and B PIC V9(9) superimposes 10 integer and 9 fraction positions: 19 digits (§1985:VI.6.4.4: "aligned on their decimal points"). A program that breaks the rule is not a conforming source program (§1985:I.1.6: "A conforming source program is one which does not violate the explicitly stated provisions and specifications of Standard COBOL."). The deciding words: "The maximum size of each operand is 18 decimal digits. The composite of operands, which is a hypothetical data item resulting from the superimposition of specified operands in a statement aligned on t" (§1985:VI.6.4.4).

**Observed (copied from `1985-SR-VI.6.4.4-2`).** cobol t.cob --std 85 --source-format fixed --flag-extensions -o p.dll: exit 0, no diagnostic; the program runs (exit 0) and displays: 'DONE'. ## Landing (wave 20261973 group A)

**Re-probe (own build, `--std 85`).** `ADD A10 TO F9` (9(10), V9(9)), `MULTIPLY 2 BY A10 F9`, `DIVIDE 2 INTO C18 D2` compiled clean (composite 19 to 20); `MULTIPLY F18 BY R18` (V9(18), 9(18)) was COBOL0805, "spans 36 digits". All as the note says.

**Fix.** `CheckComposite` caps at `EditionContext.MaxDigits` (18 at 85, 31 from 2002, the one ceiling the PICTURE clause already uses) and, at 85, superimposes the RECEIVING items alone for MULTIPLY and DIVIDE (`CompositeMembers.ReceivingItems85`, passed by the six MULTIPLY and DIVIDE call sites; DIVIDE REMAINDER keeps its quotient-only receiver list). ADD, SUBTRACT and the CORRESPONDING per-pair check already passed every operand, as §1985:VI.6.6.3 SR3 a/b/c and §1985:VI.6.26.3 SR3 prescribe. The message names the 1985 clause at 85. The NC101A justification was wrong for 85 (its composite there is the one 9(18) receiver) and is deleted with `docs/NUMERIC_DESIGN.md`'s matching claim.

**Witnesses.** `conformance:cases/_unassigned/pb5075_composite_receivers_only_85` (accept 85) and wiseowl diagnostic `pb5075-composite-over-18-digits-85` (COBOL0805 at 85). They sit in `_unassigned` with `rules: []` because the only inventory rows are 2002+ (SR-14.7.7-2, SR-14.9.26.3-4) and the 1985 rows carry no verdict yet.
