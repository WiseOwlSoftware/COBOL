# PB686 — OPEN — a boundary decided in a comment

Found 2026-09-05 by the `PB673` implementer (report §"New defects" 2), which deliberately kept its LINAGE goldens
away from the boundary so they would not pin a contested reading.

## The two readings

§14.9.51.4 GR26 (`cite.py --check 14.9.51.4 "an end-of-page condition occurs when the lines written by a WRITE
statement do not fit within the"` → OK): arm a) as printed says the page end is reached *"when the associated
LINAGE-COUNTER is equal to or exceeds the page size"*; arm b) requires the counter *"equal to or exceeds the footing
start and is less than the page size"*. Read literally, `counter == page size` is arm a) (overflow). The code
(`src/Cobol.Net.Runtime/IO/SequentialConnector.cs#AdvanceLinageCounter`) reports OVERFLOW only when the post-advance
counter is GREATER than the page body, so `counter == page size` is a FOOTING end-of-page — justified in its comment
by §13.18.34.4 GR3's inclusive footing area (`--check 13.18.34.4 "footing area"` → OK) plus GR26's lead sentence,
which is coherent if both comparisons are read against the counter as it stands when the fit is decided.

**Repro of the difference:** `LINAGE IS 4 LINES WITH FOOTING AT 3`, four `WRITE … AFTER ADVANCING 1 LINE`; the fourth
(counter 4) reports a footing end-of-page where a GR26 a)-literal reading reports overflow. Pinned by
`LinageConformanceTests.Gr26ab_CounterEqualsBody_IsFooting…`, stated in no adjudication and no CONFORMANCE row.

## What settles it

Derive the reading from the whole of §13.18.34.4 (GR3's footing area, GR7's counter semantics: is the counter the
line ABOUT to be written or the line just written at the moment GR26 is evaluated?) and from D.19's concepts text if
it speaks; survey GnuCOBOL and one more implementation on the exact program above (spec first, the survey only where
the standard leaves latitude). Record the determination as an adjudication paragraph and, if it is implementor-
defined, a CONFORMANCE.md row; then re-verdict `GR-13.18.34.4-2`/`-3` with a golden at the boundary. If the literal
reading wins, the fix is one comparison in `AdvanceLinageCounter` and the unit test flips. Not a dispatch for an
implementer until the reading is settled.
