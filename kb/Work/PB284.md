# PB284 — the denominator, and four ways `extract_rule_catalog.py` lost part of it

A sibling of [[PB55]]. **The GAP count is the definition of v1.0** (owner decision D13), so a rule the extractor
never emitted is not merely untracked — it is a rule the burn-down asserts does not exist, and no verdict, no
selector and no gate can ever contradict that. `cat['parse_gaps']` is `[]` in every case below: **the extractor's
guards fire on a block that yields ZERO rules and on an unrecognised heading spelling, and none of these four
failures looks like either.**

⛔ **DO NOT FIX THE CATALOG IN THE CHANGE SET THAT FOUND THIS.** A denominator change re-runs
`extract_rule_catalog.py`, rebuilds `traceability-inventory.json`, moves every clause total in the generated
`kb/Conformance` view and shifts the number `session-probe` reports — with adjudicated verdicts riding along.
That is its own landing, with its own battery, and mixing it into a verdict batch would make both unreviewable.

## (1) One general format lost to a one-word heading variant — FMT-14.9.7.2

`specs/ISO_COBOL.md:23078` reads `##### 14.9.7.2 General forms` — **"forms", plural** — and it is the **only**
such heading in the standard against **322** titled "General format". The extractor keys on the exact title
(`re.fullmatch(r"general formats?\.?", …)` matches the block, but the surrounding harvest keys the FMT block on
the singular spelling) and dropped exactly this one. Confirmed structurally: catalog sections under §14.9.7 are
`['14.9.7.3', '14.9.7.4']` with **no `.2`**, while §14.9.36 has all three.

**It is not a curiosity — it made a live selector look safe.** `commit-and-rollback-only`'s first draft excluded
general formats by matching the transcription's `^<pre`, a RENDERING proxy for a KIND predicate that leaks on
**56 of 322** FMT rows. §14.9.7.2's format is written as an inline backticked `` `COMMIT` `` rather than a `<pre>`
block, so had it been extracted, the proxy would have stamped COMMIT's format DOCUMENTED-NON-SUPPORT and left
ROLLBACK's alone — **a conformance split decided by transcription markup.** The proxy is now `excludes-kinds`,
but the row is still missing.

## (2) Thirty rules truncated by a markdown heading — and every truncated block looked healthy

The transcription writes the format band as plain text ("FORMAT 1") in most places but as a **markdown heading**
(`## FORMAT n`) in exactly **eight**, across **four** clauses, and `extract_rule_catalog.py`'s `ANY_HEADING`
(`^#{1,6}\s+\S`) closes the rule block there. Measured catalog-vs-spec top ordinals:

| clause | extracted | printed | LOST | what was lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| §8.3.3.6.4 | 3 | 10 | **7** | the ZERO / SPACE / HIGH-VALUE / LOW-VALUE / QUOTE / ALL / symbolic figurative-constant semantics — **core mandatory COBOL** |
| §13.18.38.4 | 13 | 18 | **5** | OCCURS format 4 — the A.4.4 dynamic-capacity module we **CLAIM** |
| §14.9.1.4 | 12 | 25 | **13** | the entire ACCEPT-screen format-3 general-rule set |
| §14.9.22.4 | 18 | 23 | **5** | INSPECT formats 3 and 4 |
| | | | **30** | |

Two independent measurements (a deriver and its refuter, working separately) agree on all four numbers and on the
total. `cite.py --check 14.9.1.4 "The ACCEPT screen statement causes the transfer…"` **passes** while
`GR-14.9.1.4-13..25` exist in neither the catalog nor the inventory — the text is in the standard and the rows are
not. A **TRUNCATED** block is invisible to both existing guards: §14.9.1.4 yielded 12 rules and looked perfectly
healthy.

**The repair is two parts, and the second is the one that matters.** Normalise the eight `## FORMAT n` lines to
plain text; then add a **completeness guard** that compares each block's printed top-level ordinals against what
it extracted, because the existing guards cannot see truncation *by construction* and this class will recur on
the next transcription edit.

## (3) Three whole clauses with no rows at all — §9.1.16, §9.1.17, §9.1.18

No catalog rule has a section beginning `9.1.16` (record locking), `9.1.17` (logical unit of work) or `9.1.18`
(**Commit and Rollback**), and `parse_gaps` is empty. A.4.3 item 1's cross-reference target — the concept clause
that defines the facility and, at §9.1.18.7, **sets EC-FLOW-APPLY** — is therefore unrepresented in the
traceability inventory and unreachable by any selector. Of the four failures here this is the largest coverage
hole, and unlike (1) and (2) the mechanism is not yet identified: **find it before repairing, and check every
other §9.1.x clause in the same pass** (`feedback_scan_all_similar`).

## (4) The printed FORMAT band is not a field — and the text it should scope is polluted instead

The standard scopes rules three ways: by clause, by text, and by the **printed FORMAT band**. The catalog carries
the first two. For the third, the extractor **glues the heading onto the PRECEDING rule's text** —
`SR-13.18.63.3-35` ends `"… is 88. FORMAT 5"` — so the scope is lost *and* the text a predicate reads is
polluted. **176 rules** carry a stray `FORMAT n` today. A `formats` field exists only on FMT rows and is a bare
count.

**The measured cost, today:** twelve rules are solely conditioned on a declined module, are text-silent, and no
sound predicate can reach them, so they sit outside the derived-verdict lane and outside its drift test —

- **A.4.14** (blank, no verdict at all): `SR-13.18.41.3-1` (PRESENT WHEN, under "FORMAT 2" = validation),
  `SR-13.18.63.3-36` (*"Syntax rules 10, 11, 13, 17, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, and 31 above apply."*),
  `SR-13.18.63.3-38`, `SR-13.18.63.3-39`, `GR-13.18.63.4-24` (*"General rule 18 applies."* — in full).
- **A.4.2** (carrying hand verdicts from [[PB260]] that the drift test cannot hold): `SR-14.9.1.3-5`,
  `SR-14.9.11.3-3`, `GR-14.9.11.4-12`, `GR-14.9.11.4-17`, `GR-14.9.11.4-18`, `GR-14.9.11.4-L2.1`,
  `GR-14.9.11.4-L2.2`.

⚠ **A `format` field alone would not have closed all of them, and that is the design lesson.** §12.3.7.3 carries
no format bands whatever, and its CURSOR / CRT STATUS operand rules are scoped by **OPERAND NAME** — a third
mechanism nobody had named. Those five were reached on 2026-09-02 by a section+pattern AND-gate arm, which is
also how the COLUMN/LINE format-2 pair was reached. So the extractor should carry the band, and the selector
shape should keep the operand arm: neither subsumes the other.

## What "fixed" looks like

`extract_rule_catalog.py` emits a `format` field on every SR/GR row that sits under a band, stops gluing the
heading into `text`, and grows a completeness guard on printed-vs-extracted top ordinals. Then
`inventory-schema.json` gains a `formats` arm field, the twelve rows above join their selectors, and
`$format-band-residue` in the schema is deleted rather than edited. Until then that `$comment` block **is** the
record of what the shape cannot see, which is the difference between a known limit and a silent one.

⚠ **Land the catalog rebuild together with [[PB291]].** That note carries the OTHER catalog-text defect found the
same day — 43+ rules whose text spells the standard's hyphen and minus with typographic look-alikes (U+2011,
U+2212) — and its repair is the same `extract_rule_catalog.py` rebuild over a repaired transcription. Two
denominator moves in one landing is one before/after row count to reconcile instead of two. They are distinct
defects: this note is rules the extractor LOST or truncated; PB291 is rules it kept faithfully and whose TEXT is
wrong.
