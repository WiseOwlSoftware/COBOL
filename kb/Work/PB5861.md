# PB5861 - at `--std 85` `IF A > 1 AND NOT NOT < 5` is refused, where the 1985 text reads the first NOT as the logical operator

**Ruling: UPHELD (a), harm rejects_legal_source.** At `--std 85` `IF A > 1 AND NOT NOT < 5` is refused (COBOL0001), where the 1985 text reads the first NOT as the logical operator and `NOT <` as the relational operator. The same condition written `NOT IS NOT < 5` compiles and gives the right answer. At 2023 the refusal is right. The row `1985-SR-VI.6.3.3-2` does not close on its inherited verdict. The checker's confidence: high. Cause (a): wrong at 85 only. The origin is the landed kb/Work PB5123, which made the refusal; a landed note is not reopened.

**Mechanism 3: the text.** `§1985:VI.6.3.3 GRL2.1` tests the WORD that follows NOT against six words: when it is one of them "then the NOT participates as part of the relational operator" (cite.py OK); otherwise, by GRL2.2, "The NOT is interpreted as a logical operator" (cite.py OK). In `AND NOT NOT < 5` the word after the first NOT is NOT, which is none of the six, so the first NOT is the logical operator and the second, followed by `<`, is part of the relational operator. The expansion is `A > 1 AND NOT (A NOT < 5)`, which the table of permissible sequences admits: its bar on the pair NOT NOT is a bar on two LOGICAL operators (`§1985:VI.6.3.2.3`; the checker's quotation, cite.py OK). The landed kb/Work PB5123 made the refusal and its landing file calls it correct; for 1985 it is not. At 2023 the text itself bars the form (the note under `§2023:8.8.4.12.4 GR1`: "NOT cannot immediately precede an extended-relational operator", cite.py OK). A lead beside it: at 2023 this form gets the generic COBOL0001 where its sibling `NOT IS NOT <` gets the specific COBOL0902.

**Where:** `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Parsing/CobolParserCoreBase.cs#logicalNotBeforeRelationalOperator`: its look-ahead of two tokens excludes `CobolParserCore.NOT`; it should admit NOT when the third token is GREATER, `>`, LESS, `<`, EQUAL or `=`. The reasoning comment at `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Frontend/Grammar/Core/CobolExpressions.g4#negatedAbbreviatedRelation` ("and NOT, which no reading lets follow NOT") carries the error. `VersionConformancePass.VisitNegatedAbbreviatedRelation` would then give 2002 and later the COBOL0902 it gives the IS form.

**The checker's evidence (rulings.json, copied).** Read by its words: the format is relation-condition {AND|OR} [NOT] [relational-operator] object (rendered page 293 confirms both brackets), and IS NOT < (IS optional) is one relational operator (`§1985:VI.6.3.1.1`). GRL2.1 tests the WORD immediately following NOT against a closed list of six (GREATER, `>`, LESS, `<`, EQUAL, `=`); for the first NOT that word is NOT, which is not on the list, so by GRL2.2 the first NOT is the logical operator; the second NOT is followed by `<`, so it is part of the relational operator NOT `<`. The expansion (A > 1) AND (NOT (A NOT < 5)) has the elements simple-condition AND NOT simple-condition, which table 1 admits (SR2's compliance test); table 1's "NOT NOT is not permissible" ranges over logical-operator elements, and the second NOT is not one. The dispatch's paraphrase ("NOT followed by a relational operator is part of the relational operator") is not the text: the rule lists six words and NOT is not among them. The compiler's own acceptance of `NOT IS NOT <` (same parse, IS written) confirms the semantics are implemented and only the predicate refuses. 2023 half: `§2023:8.8.4.12.2` (rendered page 233) admits ONE of NOT, simple-relational-operator or extended-relational-operator before object-1, and the GR1 note says NOT cannot immediately precede an extended-relational operator; IS NOT < is extended (`§2023:8.7.5.1`), so the refusal at 2023 is right. **Correction to the dispatch (the checker's):** `AND NOT IS NOT < 5` does NOT compile at 2023; it is refused COBOL0902 (correct), and `NOT NOT <` draws the generic COBOL0001 instead of that diagnostic. PB5123's landing file asserts "`NOT NOT > C` ... stay COBOL0001 at 85, correctly"; that assertion is what this row overturns.
