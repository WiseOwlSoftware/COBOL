# PB5791 - 1985-GR-IV.4.4.2-L2.1 and -L2.2: the 1985 text leaves the flow of control undefined where the last statement of a declarative section is reached with no statement in control, so no program can witness either rule and neither row takes a verdict until the list trailer is attached to its items

**Decision (registered 2026-11-15 from the adjudication of the twenty rows and the refuter's second pass).** `1985-GR-IV.4.4.2-L2.1` and `-L2.2` take NO verdict and NO witness. The only consequence 1985 states for either item is the list's trailing sentence, so no output can be expected of a program that reaches either antecedent, and neither an output case nor a refusal case can fail on it. A `CONFORMS` with an empty witness computes the GAP and records nothing true, so the rows stay open.

**The text (each citation checked again with `python scripts/spec/cite.py --check`, verdict OK).**
- `§1985:IV.4.4.2 GRL2.2`: "The last statement in a declarative section when the statement is in the range of an active PERFORM statement executed in a different section"
  `OK  §1985:IV.4.4.2 GRL2.2  (4.4.2 Explicit and Implicit Transfers of Control)  the text is in GRL2.2`
- `§1985:IV.4.4.2 GRL2.1`: "The last statement in a declarative section when the paragraph in which it appears is not being executed under the control of some other COBOL statement"
  `OK  §1985:IV.4.4.2 GRL2.1  (4.4.2 Explicit and Implicit Transfers of Control)  the text is in GRL2.1`
- `§1985:IV.4.4.2 GRL2.5` (the trailer of the list, which the catalog files under GRL2.5 alone): "When there is no next executable statement and control is not transferred outside the COBOL program, the program flow of control is undefined"
  `OK  §1985:IV.4.4.2 GRL2.5  (4.4.2 Explicit and Implicit Transfers of Control)  the text is in GRL2.5`
  The trailer's exception (an implicit EXIT PROGRAM) applies only "in the nondeclarative procedures portion of a program under control of a CALL statement", so it never applies to a declarative section.
- `§1985:VII.4.6.4 GR3`: a declarative section "may be referenced in a different declarative section or in a nondeclarative procedure only with a PERFORM statement"
  `OK  §1985:VII.4.6.4 GR3  (4.6.4 General Rules)  the text is in GR3`
- `§1985:VI.6.21.4 GR11`: "The range includes all statements that are executed as the result of a transfer of control by CALL, EXIT, GO TO, and PERFORM statements in the range of the PERFORM statement"
  `OK  §1985:VI.6.21.4 GR11  (6.21.4 General Rules)  the text is in GR11`

**Why no program witnesses L2.2.** The antecedent is populatable by a conforming program: a nondeclarative `PERFORM D1` where declarative paragraph D1 does `GO TO D2`, the last paragraph of the same declarative section (`§1985:VII.4.6.4 GR3` allows the PERFORM, the GO TO stays inside the section, and D2 is in the PERFORM's range by `§1985:VI.6.21.4 GR11` but is not its exit). What follows D2's last statement is "undefined" by the trailer, so the source is legal and nothing can be expected of it. Observed on the pin by the adjudicator (probe `open20/probes2/decl-l22.cob`, not re-run because it loops): control falls to the next paragraph of the dispatcher and the program prints BEFORE / D1 / D2 without bound. "Undefined" permits that.

**Why L2.1's "cannot be reached" argument fell.** The first draft recorded L2.1 `CONFORMS` by derivation on the arm `unpopulatable-antecedent` ("no conforming 1985 program populates the antecedent"). The refuter overturned it with a counterexample that is conforming 1985 source, compiles clean at `--std 85` and runs on the pin: two declarative sections (`USE ... ON INPUT`, `USE ... ON OUTPUT`); the nondeclarative M0 does `PERFORM A1` (allowed by `§1985:VII.4.6.4 GR3`); A1 does `GO TO A2` inside its own section; A2 is the last paragraph of section A (an L2.2 situation). Control then falls into section B and executes B1's statements, the last statements of a declarative section in a paragraph entered by no PERFORM and no USE, which is L2.1's antecedent. Output: `M0 ENTRY 1`, `A1`, `A2 LAST OF A`, `B1 REACHED: LAST STATEMENT OF B NOT UNDER CONTROL`. The derivation itself conceded this route and dismissed it because it follows an undefined flow; that makes the antecedent reachable only after an undefined step, not unpopulatable, so the named arm is false. (Under a reading of `§1985:VI.6.21.4 GR11` that puts B1 inside PERFORM A1's range, the program is L2.2 again, which also defeats the closed-set claim: the classification is itself unsettled.) The honest record is L2.2's: L2.1's consequence is the trailer's "undefined".

The counterexample, `E:/COBOL-coord/scratch/my-20261115/open20-refute2/probes/l21fall.cob`:

```cobol
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. L21FALL.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  CNT PIC 9 VALUE 0.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       DECLARATIVES.
       A-SEC SECTION.
           USE AFTER STANDARD ERROR PROCEDURE ON INPUT.
       A1.
           DISPLAY "A1".
           GO TO A2.
       A-MID.
           DISPLAY "A-MID".
       A2.
           DISPLAY "A2 LAST OF A".
       B-SEC SECTION.
           USE AFTER STANDARD ERROR PROCEDURE ON OUTPUT.
       B1.
           DISPLAY "B1 REACHED: LAST STATEMENT OF B NOT UNDER CONTROL".
           STOP RUN.
       END DECLARATIVES.
       M-SEC SECTION.
       M0.
           ADD 1 TO CNT.
           IF CNT > 2
               DISPLAY "M0 REENTERED TWICE, STOPPED"
               STOP RUN
           END-IF.
           DISPLAY "M0 ENTRY " CNT.
           PERFORM A1.
           DISPLAY "BACK".
           STOP RUN.
```

**What closes the rows.** Either (i) the derivation checker treats a list's trailing "undefined" sentence as governing each item of that list (here L2.1 to L2.5, the items of "There is no next executable statement when ..."), which closes both on the undefined-in-rule arm citing GRL2.5's sentence; or (ii) the rows stay open with this reason. The structural defect is in the catalog: the trailer is the consequence of every item, and the catalog filing it under GRL2.5 alone leaves L2.1 to L2.4 without it (CLAUDE.md rule 5: make the next such list automatic). Neither row's compiler behavior is in question.
