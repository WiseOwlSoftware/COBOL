# PB5442 - at --std 85 COBOL6877 refuses a PERFORM whose named span holds a section of another segment that no execution of the range reaches

**Sites:**

- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Procedure/Segmentation.cs#CheckProcedures` (reads `PcRange.SpannedPcs`, the physical span)
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Procedure/Segmentation.cs#RangeAllowed`
- `src/WiseOwl.Cobol.Compiler/Binding/Procedure/Verbs/ControlFlowBinder.cs#BindPerform` (the PERFORM call site; the SORT and MERGE procedure call sites share `CheckProcedures`)
- `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` section 3.8, row "The procedures a PERFORM, SORT or MERGE names": its first sentence, "§1985:XVI.3.3.2, XVI.3.3.3 and XVI.3.3.4 are asked of the procedures the statement NAMES (procedure-name-1 through procedure-name-2, in either physical order)", and its "Rejected: the closure over the procedures they perform in turn (the range of §1985:VI.6.21.4 GR11)". The adjudication shows the framing wrong: the text makes the executed range the rule, and what the compiler chooses is which violations it diagnoses. The fix rewrites the row.
- The doc comment of `Segmentation.CheckProcedures`, which states the same reading and points at section 3.8.

**Mechanism.** `CheckProcedures` collects the segment-number of every pc in `range.SpannedPcs()`, the procedures that lie physically between procedure-name-1 and procedure-name-2, and refuses the statement (COBOL6877) when those numbers mix segments as `RangeAllowed` forbids. A section inside that span that no execution of the range can reach, because a GO TO inside the range jumps over it, still counts. The executed range then lies wholly in one permitted set of segments and the program is legal, but it is refused.

**What the text says.** The restriction is printed twice, at `§1985:XVI.3.3.2` ("in addition to any declarative sections whose execution is caused within that range, only one of the following") and at `§1985:VI.6.21.4 GR15` ("can have within its range, in addition to any declarative sections whose execution is caused within that range only one of the following"). The rule list that holds GR15 defines the range: "The range of a PERFORM statement consists logically of all those statements that are executed as a result of executing the PERFORM statement" and "The range includes all statements that are executed as the result of a transfer of control by CALL, EXIT, GO TO, and PERFORM statements in the range of the PERFORM statement" (`§1985:VI.6.21.4 GR11`). The permitted sets are `§1985:XVI.3.3.2 SR1` ("Sections and/or paragraphs wholly contained in one or more non-independent segments"), `§1985:XVI.3.3.2 SR2` ("Sections and/or paragraphs wholly contained in a single independent segment"), and for a PERFORM in an independent segment `§1985:XVI.3.3.2 SRL2.1` ("Sections and/or paragraphs wholly contained in one or more non-independent segments") and `§1985:XVI.3.3.2 SRL2.2` ("Sections and/or paragraphs wholly contained in the same independent segment as that PERFORM statement"). A program that breaks the rule has an undefined result: "The results of violating the formats or rules of Standard COBOL are undefined unless otherwise specified in this standard" (`§1985:I.1.6`), so the compiler may choose which violations to diagnose. A program that keeps it must be accepted: "An implementation must accept the syntax and provide the function for all Standard COBOL language elements" (`§1985:I.1.5.2.2`). Every citation here passed `python scripts/spec/cite.py --check`.

**The choice that stays (set 6 adjudication).** The compiler diagnoses the procedures the statement names and their fall-through, and does not follow nested PERFORMs: CCVS-85 SG102A and SG103A, written to be accepted, perform fixed-portion paragraphs from inside an independent segment's performed range, which breaks the executed-range rule by its letter, with an undefined result (`§1985:I.1.6`). GnuCOBOL 3.2 diagnoses none of it. So accepting such a program conforms, and so does diagnosing a span the named procedures fall through. Only the refusal of a section the range cannot reach is wrong.

**Fix direction (root cause, CLAUDE.md rule 4).** In `CheckProcedures`, take the sections the range can reach from procedure-name-1 by fall-through and GO TO up to the end of procedure-name-2, not the physical span, and leave nested PERFORM ranges to their own check. `ProcedureReach.Closure` (`docs/CONTROL_FLOW_DESIGN.md`, the dynamic-range paragraph) does not follow a GO TO, on the ground that a syntax rule that followed it would refuse statements no execution reaches; here following the GO TO only narrows the set and removes a refusal, so the fix states that difference in the design doc. Rewrite the `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` section 3.8 row in the same change: the range is GR11's executed range; the compiler diagnoses the named procedures' fall-through span and leaves nested PERFORM ranges undiagnosed (a violation there is undefined, `§1985:I.1.6`; CCVS SG102A and SG103A); a section in the span that no path of the range reaches is not in the range. Pin s1 to s5 as goldens (accepted, with their output) beside the existing COBOL6877 refusals.
