METHOD. A prose line of each in-scope paragraph (sections I, II, IV, VI-XVI, A and Appendix C; general formats, tables,
`<pre>`, editorial notes, folios and running headers skipped) is split into sentences. A sentence of more than 25
characters with normative vocabulary (`must`, `shall`, `cannot`, `is required`, `is undefined`, `unpredictable`, ...) is
covered when its normalised text is a substring of some row's normalised `text`; otherwise it is a hole.

SPOT CHECK of the earlier measurement: of 10 holes read by hand, 7 were real unrowed rules and 3 restated a rule a row
already holds in other words, so the 198 overstates the real hole by roughly a third (substring coverage cannot see a
paraphrase). KNOWN UNDERCOUNT in the other direction: a requirement stated without normative vocabulary, and any text
inside a table, is not measured. The number is therefore a measurement of the method, a lower bound on unrowed text
that uses modal words and an upper bound on its paraphrase-free part; step 2 (adjudicating each hole) fixes the true figure.

2002, 2014 and 2023 are `not measured` by the tool until their readers are wired in; the verdict says so for them. 2014
is also not held by the inventory schema, so its inventory and code-read parts are `not measured` as well.
