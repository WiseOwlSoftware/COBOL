# PB6012 - a seventh derivation arm: informative

**Sites:** `tests/version-matrix/inventory-schema.json` (`derivation.arms.informative`, `$refusals` 10);
`scripts/spec/inventory_schema.py#Derivation._arm_refusals` (check `informs`);

## The question
PB5453's read fleet and its second read (slice s-99) left eleven 1985 rows DOUBTFUL, both readers agreeing that no program
can show them and that none of the six arms of DESIGN-spec-conformance-review section 1.1 fits:

- ten describe the STANDARD'S OWN TEXT: 1985-GR-IV.1-p1 (the section's scope, boxes, flowcharts "are not meant to dictate
  implementation"), 1985-SR-IV.2.1.1-p1 (what a general format is made of), 1985-GR-IV.2.1.5-p1 (the ellipsis in prose),
  1985-GR-IV.3.1-p1 and 1985-GR-IV.3.2-p1 (what a syntax rule and a general rule are), 1985-GR-VI.6.3.4-p2 (figures 1 to 4),
  1985-GR-VI.6.4-p1 (what paragraph 6.4 holds), 1985-GR-XIII.3.10.2-p1, -p3 and 1985-GR-XIII.3.10.3-p1 (the presentation
  rules tables, their columns and their shorthand);
- one EXPLAINS a requirement stated elsewhere: 1985-GR-IV.4.3.7-p2 (why alignment on natural boundaries exists: "additional
  machine operations in the object program may be required").

`definitional` is barred for all eleven: its claim is a property of the USER'S PROGRAM, and for IV.4.3.7 p2 the subject is the
implementation, which the arm refuses by rule.

## Options weighed
1. **A `not-a-rule` class** (`informative`) in the catalog dispositions. Rejected. Section 3 point 3 of the design closes that
   door on purpose ("There is no other way out of the denominator"; "a free `not-a-rule` reason such as 'definition' would
   remove a row more cheaply"): every `not-a-rule` class has a MECHANICAL test, and no textual test separates informative
   prose from a rule (the measured precision was 73 to 77 %). The two guards would also refuse four of the eleven on their
   words alone ("may be required", "may also either impose", "might possibly affect", "that may appear"), so the class would
   need a hand list of exemptions: a hand-maintained list where a structure belongs (CLAUDE.md rule 5). And the rows are
   minted and refuter-upheld as rows; un-minting them is a second mint to reverse a decision that was not wrong.
2. **Widen `definitional`** to "defines a term of the standard". Rejected: six of the eleven define nothing (they describe
   where figures are, what a paragraph holds, how a table is laid out, or give a reason), and one arm would then carry two
   claims with one check.
3. **A seventh arm, `informative`.** CHOSEN. It is what the design already says of a unit that obliges nothing observable: it
   is a ROW and closes on a signed, refuter-upheld derivation, "never by leaving the denominator".

## The arm
| arm | what it claims | how it is CHECKED |
|---|---|---|
| `informative` | every sentence of the rule INFORMS the reader of the standard and requires nothing of a program or of an implementation: it describes the standard's own text (its notation, organization, figures, tables, kinds of rule, the scope of a section), or it explains a requirement that another rule states; no behaviour of any compiler, a defective one included, and no program makes it false | **REVIEWED ARGUMENT, shape-checked.** The `Names` cell takes one of two forms, each ending in a quotation in double quotes, of four or more words, that lies inside the row's own catalog text in its edition (the comparison `undefined-in-rule` uses): `describes the standard's <what>: "<quotation>"` (`<what>` at least ten characters), or `explains <rule-id>: "<quotation>"`, the rule-id existing in the row's own edition's catalog and not the row's own. The quotation is the words that make the document, or the reason, the sentence's subject. |

**Bounds, each encoded as a refusal or as the refuter's brief, none left to prose:**
- EVERY sentence of the row must be informative. A row with one sentence a program or a compiler can violate takes a test for
  that sentence and no derivation (the refuter reads the whole row, sentence by sentence, and says so in its record).
- The refuter's job, as on `unfalsifiable-under-determination`: describe a behaviour of a compiler, or a program, that would
  violate the sentence. If one exists the derivation is OVERTURNED and the row takes a test.
- A sentence that RESTATES a requirement (an example that is itself a rule elsewhere, as "qualification is not allowed in
  level 1 of the Nucleus" in IV.1 p1) is informative here only because the requirement has its own row: the argument names it.
- The arm is never mechanical: a `claude:` signature needs the UPHELD refuter record (refusal 8), an `owner:` one is the read.
- It is edition-local like every arm: the 2002 and 2023 texts have such prose too (2023 clause 5, "Description techniques"),
  and each such row is argued and refuted on its own edition's words when its mint reaches it. That is why this is an arm and
  not a list: the next row of this kind closes the same way with no change to any tool.
- Refusals 1, 2 and 3 of section 1.1 apply unchanged (a row with a spec-derived test, a verdict that does not resolve, a DOC
  row the register does not document).
