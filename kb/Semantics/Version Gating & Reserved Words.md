# Version Gating & Reserved Words — design alternatives

**Why COBOL.NET recognizes the *union* of all four editions in one grammar and resolves "which edition is this
legal in?" in a pass + data tables — rather than by version-gating the grammar or shipping four grammars.** This is
the *decision record*; the *implementation* lives in [[kb/Semantics/Passes]] (the pass, its two arms, the funnel).

## The problem has two axes, and both are nastier than "keywords were added over time"

1. **Syntactic deltas** — a construct exists in some editions, not others (e.g. the boolean-shift tier is 2023; a
   parameter-description form is 2002+). Sometimes the delta is a *whole* rule; more often it is *one optional clause
   inside* an otherwise-shared rule.
2. **Reserved-word deltas** — whether a *word* is reserved is per-edition, and the reservation vector is
   **non-monotonic**: `RECEIVE`/`SEND`/`END-RECEIVE` are reserved @85, **user words** @2002/2014, **re-reserved**
   @2023; `MESSAGE` runs the other way (reserved @85/2002, user word @2014/2023).

Both axes are also **context-sensitive independent of version**: many words (`AUTO`, `COLUMN`, `LENGTH`…) are a
keyword in one grammatical position and a legal user-defined name in another *within the same edition* — the
[[kb/Spec/Lookup/Grammar|`cobolWord`]] context-sensitive-words funnel. The lexer, being context-free, can never make
the keyword-vs-name call; **position decides, and position is a parser concept.** Version is a *second* axis layered
on top of that already-context-sensitive decision.

## The chosen design — superset parse + one pass + data tables

- **Grammar recognizes the union of all editions** (effectively the 2023 superset; default `--std` is 2023). It
  carries just **two** `{isYYYY()}?` predicates, and both are *forward-detect disambiguators*, not edition gates:
  `{is2023()}? LOCATION` (control flow) and a `{is2002()}?` parameter-description form (data). Everything else parses
  edition-blind.
- **Edition identity is recovered from the parse/bound node** and checked in the **sole** gate,
  `VersionConformancePass`, whose ~88 sites all route through one `ConstructRegistry.Check(edition, …)` funnel. Two
  arms: the **parse-tree arm** gates *recognition* (drop-proof — a below-edition construct that also fails to bind
  keeps its parse node), the **bound-tree arm** gates genuinely *semantic* facts (MOVE category, USAGE/organization
  conditions).
- **Reserved-ness is data, not grammar.** `ReservedWords.Table.cs` (generated by `gen-reserved-words.ps1` from the
  ISO §8.9 list + the GnuCOBOL 85/2002/2014 word lists; canonical form `reserved-words.json`; drift-tested both ways)
  exposes `IsReservedAt(edition)`. The **same** table backs both the parser's `facilityWord()` recognizer predicate
  and the pass's reserved-word funnel, so recognition and reservation **can never diverge**. A per-row `Confidence`
  flag lets low-confidence entries be *present but inert* — the conservative "**never reject a valid program**"
  policy.
- Keyword tokens are **hard, edition-agnostic tokens**; `RECEIVE` is *always* the `RECEIVE` token. The `cobolWord`
  funnel admits it as a user name wherever unreserved; `facilityWord()` gates the keyword-led *statement* arm on the
  table; and using a reserved word as a name is rejected *semantically* in the pass — where a precise diagnostic is
  possible.

## Alternatives considered

### A. Per-rule grammar predicates — `{is2002()}?` on every edition-specific rule
One of the four ruled-out patterns (see [[kb/Context/Doctrine & Anti-Patterns]]). Predicates are re-evaluated per
occurrence and drop out of ANTLR's DFA cache; scattered across the grammar they become a second, hand-maintained copy
of the edition data. **Kept only for the two irreducible forward-detects above** — where the choice is a genuine
parse-time lookahead disambiguation, not an edition policy.

### B. Lexer keyword-toggling — make `RECEIVE` lex as a keyword or `IDENTIFIER` per edition
Expressible (`RECEIVE : 'RECEIVE' {is85() || is2023()}? ;` with fall-through to `IDENTIFIER`) — non-monotonicity is
**not** the blocker; a disjunction of `isYYYY()` handles any vector. It loses on mechanics and correctness:
- Predicated lexer rules lose DFA caching; "predicate fails → fall through to `IDENTIFIER`" leans on end-of-rule
  placement and maximal-munch tie-breaking — fragile at the scale of the reserved list.
- It reintroduces **token-level keyword/identifier duality** — the exact shape that poisoned ALL(\*) boolean-factor
  prediction and regressed `COMPUTE R = B-NOT A.` (DEVLOG 903). Hard token + `cobolWord` funnel keeps *one* stable
  token and moves reservation to a semantic check.
- It can't express "recognize but don't reject" (the `Confidence`-inert policy), and it degrades diagnostics to a
  silent demotion instead of "`RECEIVE` is reserved under `--std 2023`."

### C. Four grammars over a shared file (one per edition, 100%-overlap factored out)
The textbook "N dialects" design, and it has **real** strengths: no version predicates; four static, fully-cached
ATNs; non-monotonicity becomes *structural* (the token is simply present in the 85/2023 grammars, absent from
2002/2014); and the per-edition lexer token sets are **generatable** from `reserved-words.json`. The syntactic deltas
are genuinely small (only ~2 rules differ today), so most rules *do* factor into the shared file. It loses on three
things that don't depend on inflated counts:

1. **Recognize-then-diagnose vs. fail-to-parse — the deciding axis.** The mission is four compilers that *diagnose
   precisely* with a conservative rejection policy. A strict per-edition grammar is a *recognizer*: a 2002-only
   construct under `--std 85` yields "syntax error near INVOKE" — the 85 grammar has no concept of the construct to
   say anything better. Producing "INVOKE is a COBOL-2002 feature, unavailable under --std 85" requires *recognizing*
   it first, i.e. running a superset and rejecting in a pass. And "recognize but don't reject (yet)" can't be
   expressed structurally at all. The moment you want good diagnostics you are maintaining the 2023 superset — at
   which point the other three grammars are redundant.
2. **ANTLR import-override is whole-rule, not additive.** For a rule that differs by one optional clause, you cannot
   share the common 95% and override the 5% — the whole rule is copied into each diverging edition, and the shared
   portion then drifts independently. Small today; the *derivative* is bad — every future edition-specific clause
   duplicates rather than gaining a gated clause in place.
3. **It splits version logic in two rather than removing it.** Edition differences are partly syntactic and partly
   *semantic* (`VersionConformancePass` handles constraints that are not syntax), and `Permissive` is an orthogonal
   dialect axis. Four grammars address only the syntactic slice, so the version-aware pass and the permissive knob
   survive regardless — now version logic lives in *both* grammar-selection and the pass (×2 again for permissive).
   Plus 4× ANTLR codegen / generated C# / ATN memory (bounded, but real).

## Honest scorecard

| Concern | A. Per-rule predicates | B. Lexer toggle | C. Four grammars | **Chosen: superset + pass + table** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Non-monotonic vectors | ✓ (disjunctions) | ✓ (disjunctions) | ✓ (structural) | ✓ (data table) |
| DFA caching | ✗ per-predicate | ✗ per-keyword | ✓ (×4 ATNs) | ✓ (one static ATN) |
| Single source of edition data | ✗ scattered | ~ (if generated) | ~ (if generated) | ✓ one table + registry |
| Recognize-then-**diagnose** | ~ | ✗ silent demotion | ✗ fail-to-parse | ✓ precise, edition-aware |
| "Recognize but don't reject" (Confidence) | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ inert low-confidence rows |
| Build / binary cost | 1× | 1× | **4×** codegen | 1× |
| Removes the semantic version pass? | no | no | no | n/a (owns it) |

**When C would win:** if the goal were a pure per-edition *recognizer* (accept/reject only, no edition-aware
diagnostics, no conservative-inert policy), four grammars over a shared file would be very competitive — possibly
cleaner than the chosen design. It loses here specifically because the goal is *diagnosing* compilers. It is the
better answer to a slightly different question.

## Key facts

- Reserved-word table: **462** rows — **296** reserved in all four editions (need no version logic at all), **166**
  edition-varying (the only ones any gate would touch), **4** truly non-monotonic. So "a predicate on *every*
  reserved word" overstates the surface ~3×.
- Grammar version predicates: **2**, both forward-detect disambiguators — *not* the edition-gating mechanism.
- `VersionConformancePass`: ~88 gate sites, one `ConstructRegistry.Check` funnel, HALT-on-error before emit.
- Deciding factor: **recognize-then-diagnose** + conservative **Confidence**-inert rejection ⇒ a superset you gate,
  not exact grammars you fail against.
