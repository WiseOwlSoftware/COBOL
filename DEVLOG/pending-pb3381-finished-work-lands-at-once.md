## Finished work lands at once: the rolling wave's same-file hold is deleted, the review rounds converge, a host-I-O group meets the Linux legs at its own gate (PB3381)

The owner, 2026-10-18 ~12:10 PDT: "We should not be holding finished work this long, continually postponing it. Something in our process is flawed", and on the Mythos lane's proposed changes: "Make the changes now and land the clean links as they come."

**The evidence.** Wave 1118 (the PB3330 engine core, workflow wf_14a1e62b-326) ran seven hours with six groups DONE and none landed. PB3331 was DONE at 06:22, went through three whole-branch review rounds (5, 2 and 1 findings, 2.3 h), then was HELD from 08:43 to land "through" its successor; the round cap turned one LOW finding (a test theory's length class) into a DECISION row. PB3332 was DONE at 07:39 on its predecessor's unfixed head, so its reviewer restated the predecessor's findings. PB3333 was DONE at 08:50, reviewed only at 10:10 after its successor returned, CLEAN at 11:07, and refused at the landing at 11:26 because 31 NIST RL programs differ on Linux, which no earlier gate had run. Under the hold rule nothing in a six-link chain lands until the last link is clean.

**The root.** The hold ("the predecessor's branch is HELD and lands THROUGH the successor") is from the train era, when two same-file branches had to ride one train. Since PB3023 and PB3024 each branch is its own pull request in the merge queue, so the hold saved nothing and only postponed. Three rules compounded it: a held predecessor was reviewed only after its successor returned; every review round re-reviewed the whole branch and every finding blocked, so the rounds never converged; the Linux legs ran first at the landing. A fourth sat in the branch survey: `prune_worktrees.contained()` read a CLEAN predecessor inside its successor as IN FLIGHT, so the landing lane would have held it even with the wave's hold gone.

**The changes.**
- `wf_rolling_wave.js`: `held`, `landAlone`, `hasSuccessorQueued`, the HELD arm of `onResult` and the final `held` loop are deleted. Every finished result is reviewed in its slot the moment its implementer returns and is `ready` when CLEAN, predecessor or not. A successor starts once its predecessor is `settled()` (its review loop ended, or it returned unfinished) and is told the predecessor's FINAL branch, head, report and verdict, the review finisher's when one ran. A predecessor that ended FINDINGS at the cap keeps those findings for its next-wave finisher, and the successor's reviewer does not record them again. Round 2 and later review only `git diff <reviewed_head>..HEAD` and the report's answers. The wave passes `--blocking critical,high,medium` until the round after its last finisher, which passes `critical,high`, and it returns the reviews' leads.
- `review_findings.py`: a finding requires a `severity` (critical, high, medium, low, info). `verdict --blocking` is CLEAN when no blocking finding was recorded. The rest are LEADS: `verdict` prints them, `show` marks them, and the stamp lists each once under the report's `## Review findings`, where the enqueue unit's clerk files it. A legacy finding with no severity blocks.
- `prune_worktrees.py`: `contained()` now carries only a branch with no clean stamp at its tip (a DECISION with no report or a FINDINGS stamp, or a finished report WAITING FOR REVIEW: an unreviewed checkpoint never lands alone), so a predecessor reviewed CLEAN keeps WAITING TO LAND. A new `carrying_findings()` is the safety arm: a WAITING TO LAND branch containing another note's unlanded FINDINGS-stamped tip reads IN FLIGHT (`waits_on`) until that note's finisher has landed it. `owned()` excludes it.
- `make_dispatch_specs.py`: `host_io` (a file under `src/Cobol.Net.Runtime/IO/`), `group_files` (the `file_set`, else the notes' declared set, shared by the render and the ledger record) and the two `LINUX_GATE` paragraphs. A host-I-O group's spec runs `bash scripts/linux-gate.sh --legs unit,conformance --nice` after its Windows gate and blocks on the verdict. The template's paragraph is now `{linux_gate}`, and `check_practices.py` refuses a spec whose paragraph is not its group's.
- Docs, current in the same change: the workstream skill §2, MANDATORY-PRACTICES I8, I10, O2 and O10, the review skill's branch form, `cobol-reviewer.md`, `units/wave.md`, `units/enqueue.md`, DESIGN-test-build-ci §3.17.2 steps 2 and 3, and DESIGN-orchestrator-loop's dependency successors.

- `landing_check.py`: the file-set partition stops a landing only for INDEPENDENT earlier work. An in-flight branch that descends from the landing branch (their merge-base lies beyond origin/main: a successor, a finisher or a carrier that merged some head of it) and a worktree with no commit beyond origin/main block nothing. Wave 1118's successor -326-13 had merged PB3333's older head 3dadec3a3 and so stopped PB3333's reviewed -326-20 three times (12:48, 13:18, 13:21, both gates GREEN); an empty finisher worktree in mid-merge did the same.
- PB3380 (the successor chain never carries review fixes forward; its note is on the operator's unlanded branch `notes-pb-loop-drain-ownership-20261018`) is fixed by this change: a successor merges its predecessor's final, reviewed branch only once that review loop has ended. The operator's clerk flips PB3380 to landed once both are on main.
