## An index-name inside a compound subscript and a reference-modified inline invocation are screened like their data-item twins

Two reference-resolution screens admitted what the standard forbids (kb/Work PB6292, PB6304, from the R2 review of reference resolution, PB1754).

**PB6292.** §2023:8.4.2.3.2 writes a subscript as ALL, arithmetic-expression-1, or index-name-1 [{+|-} integer-1]; §2023:8.8.1.1 names no index-name among the operands of an arithmetic expression (the 2002 and 2014 texts say the same at §2002:8.8.1 and §2014:8.8.1, checked with cite.py). At 2002, 2014 and 2023 strict, `T (IX * 2)`, `T (J + IX)`, `T (IX + J)`, `T (IX ** 2)`, `T (IX / 1)`, `T (- IX)`, `T ((IX))` and `T (IX + 1 + 1)` compiled clean and read the occurrence number, where `COMPUTE N = IX * 2` is refused COBOL1637. Re-probe on this tree before the fix: all eight shapes compiled with no diagnostic. The direct walk of the position (`SubscriptBinder.ResolveSubscriptName`) returned the index cell wherever it met the name, and the D18 route bound under the index-name window, which admits an index-name at any operand. The fix is one shape screen on the written position, `SubscriptBinder.ScreenIndexNameSubscriptShape`, called at the head of `BindPosition` for a subscript before either route: an index-name is admitted as the whole subscript or as the left operand of one `+` or `-` and an unsigned integer literal; any other index-name operand takes the R29 disposition (COBOL1637 strict, a warning and the occurrence number under `--permissive`). The question is the symbol table's, diagnostic-free (`IdentifyIndexName(report: false)`), so the resolution reports are unchanged. The 1985 gate (`VisitSubscriptPart`, PB5074) already refuses these shapes, so the screen asks only where the 2002 subscript is available; at 85 the probe still draws one COBOL0900 per line and no COBOL1637. After the fix: nine COBOL1637 errors at 2002 and 2023 for the nine illegal lines and none for `T (IX)` and `T (IX + 1)`; nine warnings under `--permissive`.

**PB6304.** A reference-modified inline method invocation looped `RefModOf` over every written modifier, so `obj :: "m" (1:5) (2:2)` compiled, and `obj :: "m" (20:5)` over a PIC X(8) result compiled with checking off; a user-defined function result skipped the literal range screen the same way (the Revisit line of PB1707), and `IntrinsicBinder.FinishIntrinsic` carried a third copy of the SR3 count. `RefModBinder.RefModOf` now takes the written modifier list and runs the one sequence the data-reference path runs: `ScreenRefModCount` (now asked of a count and the node it reports at, so the data reference, the function result and the inline invocation share it), `RefModExclusion`, `ReadRefMod`, `ScreenRefModLiterals` (now over any node, internal), `RefModView`. `IntrinsicBinder.FinishIntrinsic` calls the count screen instead of its copy, and `ResultRefMod` reads its own modifier and applies the literal screen to a user function's result item. The intrinsic function result has no item; its size is the catalog's and stays unscreened (registered in the report). After the fix: COBOL1630 for the second modifier, COBOL2670 for `(20:5)`, `(0:2)` and `(3:7)` over the PIC X(8) item, and the in-range edges `(1:3)`, `(6:3)`, `(8:1)` compile and run.

**Goldens.** `8.4.2.3-Subscripts/pb6292_index_name_subscript_forms` (accepted from 2002; values derived from GR1c) and `pb6292-index-name-compound-subscript` (refused at 2002, 2014, 2023, COBOL1637); `8.4.3.3-Reference-modification/pb6304_inline_invocation_ref_mod_in_range` (accepted from 2002), `pb6304-inline-invocation-ref-mod-twice` (COBOL1630), `pb6304-inline-invocation-ref-mod-out-of-range` and `pb6304-function-result-ref-mod-out-of-range` (COBOL2670). The rows FMT-8.4.2.3.2, SR-8.4.3.3.3-3 and GR-8.4.3.3.4-5 were already CONFORMS and take the new witnesses; no row changed verdict.
