## PB5125: a relative WRITE of a key outside the file's boundaries answers 24 below 2023 and 34 from 2023

Re-probe on the Debug build (random WRITE of RK = 9999999999 into a dynamic-access relative file): `--std 85`, 2002, 2014 and 2023 all printed `WRITE BEYOND STATUS 34` with neither INVALID KEY phrase run. §1985:VIII.1.3.4 item (3) c) and §1985:VIII.1.3.5 require 24 and the invalid key condition at 85. The 2002 transcription (§2002:14.8.47.3 GR25 b) and GR29 b)) and the 2014 FCD (GR26 b) and GR30 b)) are the same: the '34' sentence of §2023:14.9.51.4 GR29 b) ("less than 1 or greater than the highest relative record number permitted") exists only in 2023, so the note's 85-only framing was too narrow; the edge is 2023 and 2002 and 2014 were wrong too. The note's citation §1985:VIII.4.9.4 GR14 b) is the sequential-file rule; the relative rule is §1985:VIII.1.3.4 item (3) c) (checked with `cite.py`).

Fix: a new `DialectBehavior.RelativeWriteKeyOutOfRangeIsPermanentError` (changed in 2023, VCR row 7.56, `compiler-facts.json`, PublicAPI). `RelativeConnector.Write` asks it for the one key-range test it already had: from 2023 `FileStatusCode.PermanentBoundary`, below it `FileStatusCode.BoundaryViolation`. Key 0, for which 1985 states no result, takes the same arm (the boundaries DOC-A.1-107 defines exclude it); `docs/CONFORMANCE.md` DOC-A.1-107, FILES_DESIGN and DESIGN-runtime-library say so. Sibling arms checked: `FileStatusCode.PermanentBoundary` has three producers (this one, `FileRegistry.ReadExceedsRecordMaximum` for an ODO READ, `ForWriteFailure` for a sequential medium boundary) and only this one is the relative key range; REWRITE and DELETE of an out-of-range key find no record and answer 23.
