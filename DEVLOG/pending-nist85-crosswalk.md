## The NIST-85 crosswalk: 7,101 units, 350 mapped to 1985 rows by NIST's own references, the rest owed

PB6049 (suite 85 Stage 0) needed the NIST-85 unit list and its mechanical map to the 1985 catalog's rows. `harness/crosswalk.py` now reads the 459 CCVS85 programs and writes `tests/nist/crosswalk/<program>.json`, and `suite_status.py --edition 85` reads its `unmet(85)` for the NIST term.

What the units are. A paragraph-name unit is a distinct `MOVE "<name>" TO PAR-NAME` literal of a program with its blanks trimmed: 6,884 in 389 programs (the design's 6,929 counted spacing variants of one name apart). A flag unit is a `Message expected for above statement:` comment: 40 OBSOLETE in 14 programs and 177 NON-CONFORMING STANDARD in the 17 FIPS programs, which hold no paragraph unit and are `excluded` (`fips-pub-21-1`, decision 11). Seven PAR-NAME moves name no literal (DB105A, NC107A, NC210A, OBIC1A, OBIC3A: a table-driven name); they are listed `unenumerated` and counted owed, never dropped.

What the re-probe showed. The ANSI-REFERENCE settings resolve in the held text, but NIST's numbering is not the held text's from the ENTER statement on: NC's MULTIPLY units cite `VI-106 6.19.4 GR1`, which the held text gives to MOVE (its MULTIPLY is 6.20.4, one page on), and its EVALUATE cites 6.12, the held ENTER. A mapping that trusted the number would have credited MULTIPLY units to MOVE rows. So a reference maps a unit to rows only when it names every rule it cites (GR1, `(5)`, `GR1-5`, `6.15.3/4`; `V1` and `1V` read as `VI`, `IV`) and the cited section's subject begins or continues a word of the unit's FEATURE or paragraph name. A format-keyed name adds the statement's FMT row. Any other unit is `open`: `ambiguous` when the literal reference or the one section of the cited chapter whose subject names the unit offers candidate rows, `unmapped` otherwise. A flag unit maps through Appendix C.1: the catalog marks each obsolete row `obsolete: C.1(n)`, the flagged statement names the element, and `audit_elements` reads the 16 items' headings back from the transcription.

Result: 350 units `rows`, 4,126 `open`/`ambiguous`, 2,448 `open`/`unmapped` (2,385 of them in programs that set no ANSI-REFERENCE: Segmentation, Debug, Communication and the file-I/O programs), 177 `excluded`. `unmet(85)` counts the open units, the unenumerated sites and the `rows` units with a row no cleared test witnesses (row level; the points are PB6050's): 6,931. The status line reads `=== SUITE 85: INCOMPLETE — rows 2961 · points UNMEASURED · NIST 6931 · second compiler 211 · transcription UNMEASURED ===`. `obligations.facts_of_repository` is now cached per process so the rows term and the NIST term share one generator run.

The audit (`crosswalk.py --check`) holds each file to its program (every unit present once, its lines, feature and reference as the program says), to the catalog (every row exists) and, for `basis: "mechanical"` entries, to what the tool now maps, while a later adjudicated entry is held only to the program's facts and the catalog's rows. The self-test `selftests/crosswalk/test_crosswalk.py` plants each arm: the units and the references in effect, the reference tokens, mapped, shifted-numbering, unmapped and annex units, the flag arms, the audit's problems, the term and the loud failures.

Review round 1 (two blocking findings). A CCVS init paragraph may move PAR-NAME before its own ANSI-REFERENCE and FEATURE; the parser credited such a unit with the previous test's reference (92 units, 11 of them wrongly `rows`). A unit's reference and feature are now those in effect at its test paragraph header (self-test case 16; the 459 files rebuilt: 350 `rows`, 4,126 ambiguous, 2,448 unmapped). `crosswalk.py --check` ran in no gate; it is now the gate audit `NIST-85 CROSSWALK` (scripts/gate_audits.py), a CI step and a battery.sh step.
