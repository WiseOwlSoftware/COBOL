## COBOL-85 comment-entry headers need their period, a hyphen cannot continue an entry, and `*>` is a text-word below 2002

Three notes of one root, the §2023:6.5 logical conversion and the text-word scanner it feeds (PB5168, PB5169, PB5470), fixed in one change.

**PB5168 and PB5169 (the comment-entry paragraphs).** Re-probed at `--std 85 --source-format fixed`: `AUTHOR JOHN SMITH.`, a bare `AUTHOR`, and an AUTHOR entry continued by a hyphen in column 7 all compiled and ran. `CommentEntryHeader` wrote the paragraph word and two periods whatever the source held, and `CommentEntryOf` answered a continuation line inside an open entry as one more body line. Now a fixed-form header with no separator period after its word is COBOL7166 (§1985:III, §1985:IV.2.1.6), and a hyphen in the indicator area of an entry line is COBOL7165 (§1985:VI.3.2.1.2 SR1, §1985:VI.3.4.3 SR1 for DATE-COMPILED), asked in the converter and, for a candidate the resolver decides late (D-CE2), in `ConvertCommentEntryCandidate`. My first shape for PB5168 wrote the word alone and let the parser refuse; that drew a COBOL0307 at the next paragraph and two COBOL1971 on the program's own lines, so the header is still written whole and the diagnostic carries the refusal. Free form keeps D-CE1's optional period. The old unit test `CommentEntryParagraph_KeepsItsHeader` had encoded the bug (its entry was continued by a hyphen); its third line is now a plain line.

**PB5470 (`*>` in COPY pseudo-text).** The note's `q3b` reproduced COBOL0900, COBOL2495 and COBOL2449; its `q3a` line overruns margin R (the closing period sits in column 73), so a copy cut to fit stood in. COBOL-85 has no floating comment, so `*>` is an ordinary text-word (§1985:III), but the conversion and the scanner read it as the 2002 comment at every edition. The shape: comments are removed by the logical conversion alone, so below 2002 fixed form the conversion leaves `*>` in the text (one predicate, `ReferenceFormatDiagnostics.FixedFormReadsFloatingComments`), the scanner reads no comment of its own (it skips only a carried comment-entry line, and `HoldsComment` asks for a character-string holding the indicator), and the 2002 introduction gate (COBOL0900, COBOL2495) is asked once of the finished text by `SurvivingFloatingComments`, after COPY and REPLACE, so only a `*>` that survives them is refused. Free form always reads `*>` as a comment. Two `TextManipulationTextWordTests` had fed text manipulation unconverted source with comments in it; they now run it through the conversion, as `Frontend.Normalize` does. `ForbiddenIn` stays edition-neutral: an introduced `*>` at 85 is refused either way.
