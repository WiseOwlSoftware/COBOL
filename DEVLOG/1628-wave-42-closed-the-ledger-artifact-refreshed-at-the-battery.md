**PB950 — `push-main.sh` orphans a `ci/<sha>` verification branch, and its one cleanup cannot report its own failure.** Found by looking at why `ci/*` was useless as a liveness signal while watching train 43. Nine branches survived; six were **not ancestors of `origin/main` at all** although their subjects named landed trains. ⚠ My first hypothesis — a caller cut off by the command timeout resuming past the cleanup — is WRONG, and the script disproves it: `DELETE_BRANCH=1` is the default (`:34`) and the delete at `:168-172` is deliberately unconditional with a comment saying why. The real mechanisms are two. **A:** `BRANCH` is computed from HEAD **at invocation** (`:55-56`), so a lander that rebases between attempts lands `sha2`, deletes only `ci/<sha2>`, and orphans `ci/<sha1>` permanently — no future invocation will ever compute that name. **B:** the delete is `git push --quiet origin --delete "$BRANCH" 2>/dev/null && say "…deleted…"`, so a failed delete produces no output and no read exit code, and the landing prints its ✅ either way.

**Mechanism A then reproduced under observation, ninety minutes after filing.** Registrar #8 landed first, moving main to `9172f1a74`; battery #82 had already pushed `ci/410280c17f6e`, rebased onto the moved main, and landed `e3902c8f3` — same subject, different sha, and `merge-base --is-ancestor` says the branch is an orphan. One nine-agent wave produced one new permanent orphan. The counter-observation is equally load-bearing and is written into the note: train 43's own `ci/c774def44463` **was deleted correctly** in the same window, so mechanism B is INTERMITTENT, not universal — which is the argument for deleting the `2>/dev/null` BEFORE attempting any sweep, because the silencing is what makes B's condition unreproducible.
