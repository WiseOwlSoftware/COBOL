## ACCEPT of a numeric item skips a non-digit that stands ahead of a dropped high-order digit (PB3573)

`CobolNum.ParseDeviceDecimal` (the reader of the decimal device form, PB3513) rejected any non-digit in the integer run, including one ahead of an integer digit the item cannot hold, and returned zero. GnuCOBOL 3.2.0, whose rules the reader reproduces, counts the integer digits and then advances over characters until the surplus digits are consumed, so a non-digit among them is never examined. Re-probed on this build against `cobc -x -std=default`: `Y123` into `PIC 9(2)V99` holds 23.00 (we gave 0), `-X123` into `PIC S9(2)V99 SIGN LEADING SEPARATE` holds -23.00, and a COMP or COMP-3 receiver given `Y9` plus 38 integer digits holds the low-order 678.50. The note named the character-form arm only; the binary and packed arm had the same defect behind a wider threshold. GnuCOBOL converts a character-form item into itself (surplus = digits past its own integer positions) and a binary or packed one through an intermediate 38 integer digits wide (measured: `X` plus 38 digits is invalid, `X` plus 39 is accepted), so a fix that skipped the junk for every form would have accepted `12X345` into a COMP item, which GnuCOBOL refuses. The reader now has the two stages: the first-stage capacity is the item's integer positions for a character-form item and `DeviceReadDigits` (38) for the rest, the surplus characters are skipped by position, and a second cut reduces the integer to the item's positions. Tests: two unit facts in `NumericDisplayDecimalTests`, an end-to-end `AcceptDifferentialTests.Device_NumericReceiver_SkipsANonDigitAheadOfADroppedHighOrderDigit` whose expected text is GnuCOBOL's own output, and DOC-A.1-1 extended. No inventory row claimed; PB3573 landed. Self-review (architecture, full code, performance, duplication): the only reader of the form is this function, the `digits` form never rejects a character, and the added loop runs only when digits are dropped.
