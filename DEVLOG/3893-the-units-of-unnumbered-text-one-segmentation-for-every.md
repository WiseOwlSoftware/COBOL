## Entry 3893 — 2026-11-21 02:06 PST — The units of unnumbered text: one segmentation for every edition, every unit pending, and the audit of their dispositions (kb/Work PB5449, first landing)

The rule catalogs held a row for every numbered rule and for the 18 PB284 paragraphs of record locking, and nothing for
the rest of the standard's unnumbered prose, its rule blocks' lead-ins and its tables. Decision PB5448 makes the margin
paragraph the unit of that text; this landing builds the mechanism (PB479 step 1) and changes no row.

**Re-probe.** Before the change `cite.py --check "§2023:8.4.2.1" "The same name shall not be used both as the name of an
external record"` answered only "the text is in the clause", the session probe printed 2023's 4,214 rules as the whole
denominator, and `prose_paragraphs` read §2023:8.4.2.1 as two paragraphs (no closing parenthesis after a stop) and cut
§2023:14.6.2.2's dash list from its lead.

**The mechanism.** `scripts/spec/text_units.py` segments a clause's unnumbered text: a sentence ends in a stop and any
closing brackets; an unterminated unit continues across a blank line into a lowercase or continuation-word opening (the
42 such breaks of the 2023 text are its case data); a colon unit owns its dash or lettered list and the list's closing
tail; a numbered prose item is a unit by its printed number; a NOTE stays inside its unit and a general format is none;
a table's units follow its kind (entries and matrix by body row, data whole). Units are keyed by clause, the digest of
their normalized words and their order. The ISO reader (`iso_units`, replacing `prose_paragraphs`) and the 1985 reader
(`rule_catalog_1985.units`) feed it; each manifest's `paragraph-blocks` now holds a disposition per unit, regenerated
by the extractor (a new unit enters pending, a pending entry that lost its unit leaves, a decided one stays for the audit
to refuse), with the 18 PB284 rows and 2002's 8.8.4.1.5.2 migrated from the positional `kind`/`kinds` maps.

**The audit.** It refuses a unit with no entry, an entry no unit has, a moved unit, a kind the clause does not take, a
`restates` naming no existing rule, a `not-a-rule` failing its class test (lead-in, cross-reference, data) or the verbal
forms (`scripts/spec/data/verbal-forms.json`) or the shape guard derived from every row's sentence openings, either one
without an UPHELD refuter record of its digest, per-class counts unequal, and (once a manifest sets
`not-rules-refuted`) a `not-rules` block without its record. Each refusal fires on planted data in
`scripts/selftests/spec/text_units/test_text_units.py` (10 cases, 46 expectations).

**What moved.** Every catalog's `rules` array regenerated byte-identical; each `outside-denominator` gained
`pending-units`: 2023 2,298, 2002 1,766, 1985 911. `inventory_view.pending`, the one reader of an edition's bound (the ledger train's, kb/Work PB5994), counts them beside the
pending clauses and the untyped headings: the session probe prints them as lower bounds and `edition_status` counts them
in the edition's inventory GAP, so no edition with a pending unit reads complete. `cite.py` answers
`(unnumbered prose, pending)` for prose in no row (kb/Work PB222's repair) and reads `p<n>` and `Table <t> row <r>`;
`audit_catalog_coverage.py` treats as admitted only a clause whose every unit is a row. A prose item's `item <n>`
designator was tried and withdrawn: six existing citations use `item <n>` for lists that are no unit, and they are
re-pointed first.

**The move and the split (item 6).** `scripts/spec/rule_moves.py` holds the two operations a regeneration asks of a
store, applied by `record_verdicts.py` as batch records. A move (`{"rule-id": new, "move-from": old}`) carries a record
only when its `rule-digest` is the text the new id holds now, so a chain of renumbered paragraphs moves each record once;
a split checks that the parent's old text is its text now plus the carved paragraphs in order, writes no record for a
carved row, and keeps the parent's record only through an UPHELD recheck of the remaining text (its digest re-stamped,
its notes marked), withdrawing it otherwise. Both re-point the older editions' change records that name the ids; a split
re-stamps one only when it was decided against the parent's old text, and one already stale, or whose `reason` quotes
eight consecutive words of carved text, is left STALE for adjudication (the branch review's R1-1 found the first guard
missing: an already stale record would have been made fresh unread). The split is a batch record type, as a withdrawal
is, not the separate command the design first named. A
regeneration that relabels a decided row writes the move batch itself (`text_units.moves`). Each refusal fires on
planted data in `scripts/selftests/spec/rule_moves/test_rule_moves.py` (7 cases, 33 expectations).

**The 1985 margin levels (item 3, its 1985 half).** `scripts/spec/measure_margin_levels.py` measures the first printed
line of every paragraph a 1985 rule's text holds after the rule's own, against the page's margin, with level 0
calibrated on the block lead-ins (1985 indents every first line, 17.8 pt; 75 of 102 lead-ins measure level 0). Of 381
such paragraphs, 48 print at level 0, 243 deeper, 18 left of the indent, 9 ambiguous and 63 not found
(`scripts/spec/data/margin-levels-1985.json`). The 1985 reader makes each level-0 paragraph a pending unit, numbered
after its clause's lead-ins, and the paragraph stays in its rule's text while pending, so the 1985 `rules` array
regenerated byte-identical (2,275 rules) and the 1985 pending units went from 911 to 956. A measurement found one
defect of method on the way: the commonest left edge of a page was its margin in the design-time script, and on page 707
(nine indented lines, four at the margin) it was the indent; the margin is now the leftmost edge two long lines share.
