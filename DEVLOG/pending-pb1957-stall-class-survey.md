## The stall-class survey: sixteen orphaned notes join PB1957, and PB3639 files the lane that sleeps between units

After the operator landed four stranded branches by hand on 2026-10-24, a nine-agent read-only survey measured every
mechanical stop that left finished work idle since 2026-10-12, from the landing records, the 491 lane attempts, the
supervisor logs and the register. The classifier proposed 32 classes. Opus refuters re-opened the raw records and kept
16, overturning the three largest on their numbers. The landing lane really does sleep between units, but the minutes
first charged to it belonged to owner STOPs, survey misreads and the WSL disk outage. The record is
`docs/rearchitecture/evidence/fleet-optimization/2026-10-24-stall-class-survey.md`.

The kept classes mostly have notes already. Those notes were open but in no running campaign, so no lane could plan
them, which is the gap PB3636 reports. PB3389, PB3145, PB3476, PB3509, PB3118, PB2665, PB3367, PB3386, PB3404, PB3157,
PB3113 and PB3446 join PB1957 at tier 2, and the one-line fixes PB3250, PB3474, PB3409 and PB3135 at tier 3. PB3389
and PB3145 gain today's witnesses: PR #493's stale flag and PB3112's 59.8-minute exit-6 wait. PB3639 is new: the
supervisor ticks the tokenless landing lane only while an LLM unit runs.

Whether the lane may land under an owner STOP is the owner's decision, not the operator's, and stays open.
