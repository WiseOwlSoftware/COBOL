## Entry 3517 — 2026-11-11 17:12 PST — Segmentation under --permissive: the number's range and the ALTER rule are asked where the meaning is kept (PB5223, PB5224)

Under `--permissive` at 2002 and later a section segment-number is a warned removed construct whose COBOL-85 run-time meaning (an independent segment's initial state, §1985:XVI.1.4.3) is kept, but the two rules that make that meaning well-defined ran only at COBOL-85. PB5223: `SECTION 100` passed the range screen and reached the emitter's `__SegOf` byte table, failing the generated C# with CS0029. PB5224: an ALTER from another segment of an independent segment's GO TO (§1985:XVI.3.3.1) compiled and was silently undone by the reset. Both notes' mechanisms were read in the code on a fresh tree before the fix.

Fix: one predicate, `Segmentation.MeaningKept(edition)` (COBOL-85, or `--permissive`), asked by `CheckAlter` and by the section and declarative range screens (`ScreenSegmentNumber`). SEGMENT-LIMIT's screen and the PERFORM / SORT / MERGE rules (`CheckProcedures`) have no run-time meaning to protect and stay COBOL-85 only; strict 2002 and later refuse the number (COBOL0902) and ask neither. CONTROL_FLOW_DESIGN D4 and the `EmitSegmentState` doc say where "the reset never undoes an ALTER" holds. Tests: `SegmentationPermissiveTests` (11 cases: three editions each of the refused ALTER, the own-segment ALTER running `A ALTERED`, the three-digit number, plus the strict negative); the conformance-suite diagnostics file cannot express `--permissive`, so the COBOL-85 goldens of PB5084 remain the suite half. No inventory row closed. Sibling sweep: the only readers of `SegmentNumbersRemoved2002` availability are `MeaningKept`, `RulesInForce` and the SEGMENT-LIMIT screen.
