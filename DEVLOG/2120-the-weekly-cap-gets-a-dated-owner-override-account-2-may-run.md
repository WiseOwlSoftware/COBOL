## Entry 2120 — 2026-10-16 16:21 PDT — The weekly cap gets a dated owner override: account 2 may run to 99 % until its Saturday 10:00 reset (PB3305)

At 15:23 on 2026-10-16, day 7 of account 2's week, the meter read 92 %. The owner said: "We can run to 100% today, without triggering aborts, as the reset is 10 AM tomorrow and we won't need to work tonight." The loop had no way to do that. On day 7 the weekly cap (97 %) is the binding limit, `budget.py` answers `stop-week` at the cap, and a stop-week ends the loop under both of the owner's per-run levers, `-OverrideHold` and `-BorrowDays`. A per-run cap argument would not have reached the planner's headroom or a unit's own budget call, because those read the quota table themselves.

So the special case is now a row in that table. `model_rules.json` gains `accounts.list[].quota_overrides`: dated entries with an `until`, the quota keys they set and the owner's words in `_source`. `Account.quota_at(now)` is the one resolver, and `budget.py`, `next_unit.py` and `ledger_plan.py` all read it, so an override governs every reader at its next call without a loop restart. It lapses by itself at its `until`. `account.py` refuses a malformed entry rather than ignoring it. The first entry lifts account 2's cap to 99 until 2026-10-17 10:00, not 100. The cap acts only between units, and the last point covers the running unit and the estimate's drift; the 15:23 reading had been 2.6 points above the estimate.
